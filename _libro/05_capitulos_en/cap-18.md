## Chapter 18 — Creativity, Originality and Taste

Chapter 17 described how to think: the difference between stupid thinking and genius thinking, building one's own philosophy, questioning, starting from the problem, the gap between knowing and understanding, and strategy. This chapter asks what that thinking is for. In Koe's system the answer is creation. A mind that has learned to hold ideas open, to question them and to frame them by a problem is a mind that can produce something that did not exist in that form before: a solution, a perspective, a concept, a product, a body of work. Chapters 14 through 16 dealt with the inputs (learning, skill, reading, notes, AI as a tutor); Chapter 17 dealt with the processing; this chapter deals with the output and with the qualities that make an output worth anyone's attention.

The chapter is built in six steps. Section 18.1 starts with what creativity is, which in Koe's corpus is a surprisingly unstable question. He rejects the idea that creativity is a job title or a "creative skill" such as design, and he defines it in several ways over five years: as creating clarity from experience, as achieving any goal with the knowledge and skill available, and, in 2026, as a natural state of consciousness in which one "notices the unnoticed." The same section presents his broader claim that to be human is to be a creator. Section 18.2 describes the process: collecting, connecting and creating with "the dots," deconstructing wholes into parts and rebuilding them like "mental Legos," and the family of metaphors (the DJ, the blacksmith, the idea worker) through which he describes the creator as someone who remixes ideas. Section 18.3 deals with originality: Koe's repeated claim that "nobody has original ideas," the five ways he nonetheless offers to think originally, and the central practical concept of this chapter, the **novel perspective**. Section 18.4 presents the person who does this work for a living: the **synthesizer** and the **value creator**, and, in his 2025–2026 material, the "synthesizer of truth" and the strategist who can hold paradox. Section 18.5 turns to **taste**, which Koe calls "the new intelligence" in a world where AI can produce anything, and Section 18.6 to **articulation**, the ability to say clearly what one has understood, which Koe treats as something built through a body of work rather than a gift.

Three earlier threads carry into this chapter. From Chapter 6 comes the goal as a lens: what the mind notices depends on what it is pursuing, and this will reappear as the explanation of both perspective and taste. From Chapter 5 comes entropy and order in consciousness: Koe's oldest definition of creativity is "bringing order to consciousness to embrace chaos." From Chapter 1 comes intelligent imitation and "create or be created": this chapter returns to imitation and asks when it becomes original. The chapter prepares Part VII, where the conclusion that the creator's perspective is the differentiator becomes "you are the niche," and Part VIII, where these ideas become the craft of writing.

### 18.1 The Creative Nature

#### What "creative" does not mean

Koe's discussion of creativity almost always begins with a negation. In ordinary usage, "a creative" is a person with a certain kind of job or skill: a designer, a filmmaker, a painter, a writer, a photographer. Koe rejects this usage explicitly. In a 2024 video on leverage he says: "A creative is not someone with a specific skill that a robot will outprice and outcompete," and "a creative is not a designer, filmmaker, painter, writer, photographer or any specific limiting identity." The phrase "specific limiting identity" matters. It connects the topic to the model of identity built in Part II: a job label is one more identity that, once adopted, defines what a person believes they can and cannot do, and it also makes them vulnerable, because a skill defined narrowly enough can be automated or underpriced.

**Source:** How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md

The same distinction appears two years earlier, in a 2022 video on making money as a creative online. There Koe contrasts "what people think creativity is" with "true creativity." People think creativity depends on a skill like design or art. Those skills do require some creative output, he grants, "but that is not true creativity." His evidence is economic. There are designers still working in what he calls low-skill labor jobs, earning a fixed amount, "with AI coming to take over their jobs." Meanwhile, "the visionaries, strategists, leaders, innovators" who earn the most "aren't necessarily reliant on these creative skill sets, they are reliant on their nature as a creative being." **True creativity**, in this sense, is not a skill but a nature: the capacity of a human being to create, which a job may or may not use.

**Source:** How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md

Later in the same video Koe adds an observation that will matter in Section 18.4 and in Part XI: "a design job isn't inherently valuable, your design skill isn't inherently valuable" unless it is applied to help people improve and reach an outcome they want. His examples are modest on purpose. Someone cannot design their own profile picture; someone wants a design job and you help them learn faster than a college degree would. The skill becomes valuable when it is attached to another person's problem.

#### Mere technicians: Krishnamurti's warning

In a 2024 video presenting a sequence of steps for staying relevant over the next decade, Koe reaches the step he calls "creativity for unique solutions" and opens it with a long quotation from Jiddu Krishnamurti: "Unfortunately most of us are becoming mere technicians. We pass examinations, acquire this or that technique in order to earn a livelihood, but to acquire technique or develop capacity without paying attention to the inner state brings about ugliness and chaos in the world. If we awaken creative beauty inwardly, it expresses itself outwardly, and then there is order."

**Complementary context:** Jiddu Krishnamurti (1895–1986) was an Indian-born speaker and writer on the nature of the mind, education and conditioning. Koe quotes him in several places in the corpus, usually as an opening authority for ideas about conditioning and education.

Koe's own commentary is cautious about definitions: "What is creativity? That is an extremely difficult question to answer when you peel beyond the surface of skills and identities labeled as creative, like designers and filmmakers. But those creatives can still follow mechanical, stupefying processes in their work." The point is that a "creative" job can be performed mechanically, and a person with a creative title can be a technician in Krishnamurti's sense: someone who has acquired a technique without attending to the inner state from which the work comes. Koe draws the consequence directly: "As Krishnamurti said before, most of us are becoming technicians, robots. It's no wonder why people are so afraid of losing their jobs and being replaced. They lack creativity. They are not creators." The fear of replacement, in this reading, is a symptom. A person who is a technician can be replaced by a better technician, human or machine; a person who creates solutions cannot be replaced in the same way, because the solution comes from them.

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md

The same idea appears as a principle in the official summary of *The Art of Focus* (2024). Principle 16 reads: "The modern world favors productivity but neglects creativity; this leads to robotic and efficient machines that are no longer human. Create the efficient systems that your mind loves, but don't hesitate to evolve beyond them and discover the next problem that must be solved." The formulation is balanced in a way that is easy to miss. Koe does not reject efficiency; the systems "your mind loves" are part of his method (Chapter 12 is built on them). What he rejects is efficiency as the highest value. A system is a tool for one stage; once it works, the creative act is to see the next problem. The two principles that follow in the same list extend the thought. Principle 17: "The mind makes sense of the world through storytelling; life unfolds in chapters and phases; it is an unfolding of problems, calls to adventure, curiosity, exploration of the unknown, struggle, insight, transformation, solution and climax — flow with them all." Principle 18: "Success cannot exist without failure; failure is a reference point that reveals a problem from which you generate a goal to discover a lesson." Read together, the three principles describe creativity as the movement between problems: the efficient system solves one, the story of life presents the next, and failure is the reference point that reveals it. Chapter 39 returns to the idea of life in chapters.

**Source:** The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md

#### The true creative: a problem-solving orientation

If creativity is not a job, what is a creative? Koe's positive definitions in the 2024 leverage video are two. First: "a creative is someone who engages with the process of life reflected in their work." Second: "a creative is someone who solves problems with whatever combination of skill, knowledge, experience and inspiration necessary in the pursuit of a goal, usually in the form of a project." The word **creative** is therefore used in a sense of his own. It names an orientation, not a profession: a person facing a self-chosen goal, treating what stands in the way as problems, and drawing on whatever resources solve them. The mention of the project links this definition to Chapter 9, where the project is the unit through which a life's work advances.

**Source:** How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md

From this definition Koe derives a provocative consequence. "If you work a job that you hate as something like a designer, then I would argue that you are not a true creative, even if your job title and identity say you are." The reasoning is a direct application of the definition: "Creativity or being a creator is about creating solutions to problems, and if you can't identify the job that you hate as a problem that is solved with a creative solution, then you are not a creative." The designer who hates their job has, in Koe's description, "grown too comfortable in the routine"; their mind "doesn't want to break out" and dedicate "at least one hour a day to high leverage work that will solve the problem of a job that you hate." He goes further: "You aren't creative enough to realize that life is a series of problems that need solving in order for your mind to expand and for you to evolve." And he concludes: "A creative and a creator are those that take control of their future by solving all of the problems standing in the way of their vision for that future."

The argument is worth reading carefully, because it is easy to misread as an insult to designers. Its structure is: (1) creativity is creating solutions to problems; (2) the most pressing problem in this person's life is the job they hate; (3) they do not treat it as a problem to be solved, which shows in the absence of even one hour a day spent on solving it; (4) therefore, whatever their title, they are not exercising creativity where it matters most. The concept of the **true creative** is thus a test that applies to anyone, not a ranking of professions. It connects with the anti-vision of Chapter 7 (the hated situation as material for direction) and with the "one hour a day" of high-leverage work that recurs in Chapter 12.

#### Creativity as achieving any goal with what is available

In March 2024, in a video on the "future-proof skill stack," Koe gives a definition that pushes the problem-solving orientation as far as it goes. His last point is that people should stop worrying about which career skills AI will make obsolete and worry instead about whether they are training, or being trained, to make "ideal-future-aligned decisions," to be open-minded and perceptive, and to be self-motivated. Then: "Everyone is creative, and creativity is about achieving any goal with the knowledge and skill available to you, not fancy art or designs." The consequences he draws are practical: learn to set self-generated goals; use the internet, mentors and abundant information to educate yourself; embrace trial and error, self-reflection and experiment to create your own path. And the warning: "Creativity gives you the power to do whatever you want in life; those who don't realize this gift are going to be doomed to the assignments of others."

**Source:** The Future-Proof Skill Stack (How Average People Become Millionaires).md

Two points should be noted. The first is that this definition makes creativity depend on goals, and specifically on self-generated goals. That is the link to Chapter 1, where the central idea of the book is that one always pursues goals, either chosen or assigned: here, the person without creativity is "doomed to the assignments of others." The second is a matter of attribution. The video belongs to the period in which Koe was working closely with ideas from the writer Devon Eriksen (spelled in various ways in the transcripts), and the boundary between Eriksen's framing and Koe's own in this passage is not clear in the source. The definition is presented here as Koe's, with that reservation.

#### Creativity as a state: noticing the unnoticed

In March 2026, in a video titled "If you've lost your creative genius, watch this," Koe offers a different kind of definition. The earlier ones described creativity as a process or an ability directed at results. This one describes it as a state of consciousness.

He begins with what the state feels like. "Even if you think you aren't a [creative] person, you can tap into this incredibly enjoyable state of consciousness. It's similar to the flow state, but it's potentially more potent. You aren't focused on breezing through a set of tasks; you're more so noticing things for the first time. You're seeing a deeper layer of reality that you either forgot was there or you didn't know was there in the first place." His image is a dog seeing grass for the first time: "What is going on?" Its "dopamine is going crazy." He contrasts this **creative state**, which he also calls **open state creativity**, with "narrow state stressed focus": "we're trying to accomplish this open state creativity." The comparison with flow (Chapter 10) is precise. Flow, in Koe's treatment, is absorption in a task at the edge of one's ability; the creative state is less about executing and more about perceiving.

**Source:** If you've lost your creative genius, watch this.md

Then comes the definition. His first point is that "you don't have ideas because there's too much interference." He admits that he is annoyed by the sentence "I'm not a creative person," which he used to say himself, because it makes creativity seem like mere talent. He grants that "in a way" creativity is a skill: "It's a skill to be able to sustain a creative state over an extended period of time. So if you aren't in that creative state… you just haven't practiced the skill of getting back to that state." But at the deeper level: "At its core, creativity is just a natural way of being. It's a state of consciousness. It's a capacity that everyone has. But that capacity gets buried over time… with anything that narrows your mind." And: "Creativity is a very open, relaxed state where you see connections, patterns, and possibilities that aren't immediately obvious. It's the act of **noticing the unnoticed**, which is not… creating something from nothing. That nothing is already there. It's already raw material. You're creating with what's in reality, and that's a lot of stuff."

The logic of this definition has three parts. Creativity is natural and universal (everyone has the capacity). It is buried by **interference**, by whatever narrows the mind. And it is recovered by practice, which here means the practice of returning to an open state, not the practice of a technique. In the same video Koe names what narrows the mind ("the three narrowers of the mind": conditioning, productivity as a priority, and infinite input with zero processing time); those are treated in Chapter 11, where the environment that consumes attention is the subject. Here the relevant point is the shift in where creativity is located: not in the output and not in a skill, but in the quality of attention that precedes both.

This 2026 definition has a precedent in 2022. In "The Matrix Is Real (How To Break Free With Your Mind)," Koe describes creativity as bringing findings from the unknown into one's creations, and asks: "When you think of a creative… do you think they're just brooding over some political election? No, they're in a deep restful state and they're letting their creative abilities come into play." The Matrix, in that video's vocabulary (Chapter 1), keeps people trapped in surface-level thoughts; to reverse its effect, one must tap into and explore the unknown. The 2022 version already contains the two elements of 2026: a restful, open state, and an object of attention that lies outside the usual surface concerns.

**Source:** The Matrix Is Real (How To Break Free With Your Mind).md

#### How the definition changed

Koe's definitions of creativity are not fully consistent, and he does not reconcile them. The sequence is clearer in a table.

| Year | Definition | Emphasis | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | "True creativity" is one's nature as a creative being, not a creative skill; "creativity equals create plus clarity" (Section 18.2) | Nature; clarity from experience | How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md |
| 2022 | The creative is in "a deep restful state," not brooding on surface concerns | State | The Matrix Is Real.md |
| 2023 | Creativity is entering chaos and creating clarity (Section 18.2) | Process | Most People Are NPCs.md |
| 2024 (March) | "Achieving any goal with the knowledge and skill available to you, not fancy art" | Results; self-generated goals | The Future-Proof Skill Stack.md |
| 2024 (May) | The creative "solves problems… in the pursuit of a goal, usually in the form of a project" | Problem-solving orientation | How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md |
| 2024 (July) | "Bringing order to consciousness to embrace chaos": collect, connect, create with the dots, including "something from nothing" (Section 18.2) | Process; order from chaos | Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md |
| 2026 (March) | "A natural way of being… a state of consciousness"; "noticing the unnoticed," explicitly "not creating something from nothing" | State of perception | If you've lost your creative genius, watch this.md |

The most visible tension is literal. In July 2024 Koe lists "something from nothing" among the transformations that creativity achieves; in March 2026 he says that creativity "is not creating something from nothing; that nothing is already there." The reason he gives for the 2026 framing is that the phrase "I'm not a creative person" makes creativity seem like mere talent, and he wants to show that the capacity is universal. He does not mention or correct the earlier formulation. One reasonable reading, which the corpus permits but does not state, is that the definitions describe different phases of one cycle: a state (open perception), a process (collecting and connecting), and a result (a solution to a goal's problem). The 2024 "something from nothing" can also be read rhetorically, as one item in a list of paired opposites (sense from nonsense, life from death) rather than as a metaphysical claim. But the reader should know that Koe moved from treating creativity mainly as an ability oriented to outcomes to treating it mainly as a state of consciousness, and that he did not say so.

#### You are a creator: the human as toolbuilder

Beyond the definition of creativity lies a stronger claim about human nature. In a 2025 video on AI and the creator economy, Koe insists that by "creator" he does not mean "content creator" but "the essence of your being": you are human, therefore a creator. He frames this historically, with explicit hedging. We are not "those measly puny little beings of the past who look to the gods as the creators." At some point that switched — "maybe this was during the Enlightenment" — and we realized that we can acquire knowledge, solve problems and build tools so that we do not die in the cold or burn. The dating is speculative by his own wording ("maybe"), and it should be read as a story about a shift in self-understanding, not as a historical thesis. What follows is the practical point: many people have yet to adopt the mindset that "you can just create things, you can just solve problems." It is difficult, he grants, but that is where meaning and purpose come from, and one only finds it by leaning into the pain and realizing that struggle in specific situations can be fun.

**Source:** The creator economy is dying thanks to AI.md

The fullest version of the argument appears in October 2025, as step four of a sequence on escaping the Matrix: "become a value creator." It starts from a picture of the human condition. "Humans are toolbuilders." Facing problems like cold, hunger and predators, we created fire, shelter and weapons; once those were created, better tools became possible, and that accumulation produced today's society. Koe adds a deliberately unsentimental premise: "Nature is brutal. It's not this kind and loving thing that many hippies believe." Without transforming the earth to make it hospitable, humans would be extinct or drastically underdeveloped. From there he moves to the good life: becoming a creator is central to it because "happiness and enjoyment are the combination of progress being made and a contribution to something greater than yourself. Both are accomplished by solving problems for yourself and others. Problems are solved through creativity." And to work: "The intersection of purpose and profit lies in creating solutions to problems you deem interesting and passing those solutions down to contribute to humanity." Finally, a trajectory: one solves more complex problems as one gains knowledge, skill and resources, and "life gets better as problems shift from shallow to meaningful."

**Source:** You're Stuck In The Matrix, Here's How You Escape.md

The chain is worth stating in order, because each link depends on the previous one: nature presents problems; humans survive by building tools that solve them; tools compound; happiness consists of progress plus contribution; both are achieved by solving problems; problems are solved through creativity; therefore creativity is not a luxury but the mechanism of a good life, and a career that solves interesting problems for others is where purpose and profit meet. Chapter 39 develops the formula of happiness as progress plus contribution; Chapter 31 develops value as the solution of problems.

Two earlier formulations say the same thing from other angles. In a December 2023 video titled "The Future Of Work Is Play," Koe states: "Evolution is about solving problems with technology. That's what we do as humans; that is our edge." That edge is what sets humans apart from other beings and leads to skyscrapers, libraries, sports and "the infinite internet." We build, create and solve our own problems "with a plan, focus and creativity," and he concludes that "arguably the meaning of human existence is to create." The word "arguably" is his; he offers the claim as a position, not as a demonstrated fact. In a 2024 guide to the one-person business he makes a comparative argument. Chimpanzees use tools, but no other being has constructed the world with its mind: the school system, government, religious beliefs, language, all built "from nothing but images and symbols." "Humans are the only being that can have an idea and make it real." We had consciousness, attention, focus and the tools to connect ideas, make them tangible, communicate and form relationships. Koe pauses to flag that these constructions have traps and downsides, since they control many people's lives, and defers that discussion; the critique of institutions is the subject of Chapter 2. His conclusion is that one key to a high quality of life is "leaning into your nature as a creator, as a builder, and actually building things rather than watching other people build things."

**Source:** The Future Of Work Is Play (How To Create A Digital Career).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The phrase **nature as a creator** thus covers two ideas that should be kept apart. One is anthropological: humans, uniquely, turn ideas into reality. The other is normative: a good life consists in exercising that capacity rather than watching others exercise it. The second does not follow automatically from the first; Koe bridges them with the claim about happiness as progress and contribution.

#### Creators create their own jobs

Koe applies the creator thesis to the future of work with two pieces of evidence that he presents without sources. The first is a poll. "When kids were polled for what they wanted to do when they grow up, the top answer was become a YouTuber." He gives no source or figures. Most people, he says, will see this as delusional ("silly kids, go and get a real job"). "I see it as natural and potentially the only option in the future, not just like a YouTuber but a creator": "being human, doing what you want to do, studying your curiosities, leaning into what makes you you, and then distributing that value to the world through your unique mind." He also rejects the reflex of labeling the wish for influence and attention as vanity, on the ground that motives cannot be reduced to a single "why."

**Source:** People Dumber Than You Are Making Millions.md

The second is a statistic that he repeats in at least two videos from 2022: "sixty percent of new jobs haven't been created yet." He uses it to argue that creators, not only content creators but "creators as a whole," those who lean into their nature as creators and piece together thoughts, create their own jobs. Again no source is given. Both data points should be read as rhetorical support for a position, not as evidence the author has verified. The position itself, that the creator is a general human role newly made viable by the internet rather than a fringe career, is the one that Section 18.4 develops under the names "synthesizer" and "value creator."

**Source:** How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md

#### The pleasure of creating

Koe also claims that creation is pleasurable in a way that is a signal to do it more. In a 2023 video he quotes, with obvious enjoyment, "the wise words of Arnold": Schwarzenegger's famous comparison of the muscle "pump" in the gym to sexual climax ("to me the pump… just feels fantastic"). Koe uses the analogy to frame the creative flexing of the mind as similarly pleasurable, as a bridge to his own formula, "creativity is mental bodybuilding" (developed in Chapter 15, where the mind is treated as a body that grows under load). The source here is a third party, used as an analogy, and the claim it supports is experiential: creation feels good, and that feeling is a reason to do it more often.

**Source:** Get Mad At Where You Are In Life (A Deep Explanation).md

#### Creativity is not inherently valuable

One last qualification prevents the creator thesis from becoming romantic. In the 2022 video on making money as a creative, Koe says that one can spend six figures on a degree in "artistic basket weaving," or any creative field, and it is not inherently valuable "until you understand how a select group of people perceives value." "What is deemed valuable is up to perception," and perception depends on what a person has been made conscious of and conditioned to believe. It differs completely between people: one sees "a hunk of garbage" where you see "a diamond." Creativity, then, is a human capacity that does not by itself produce value for others. It produces value when it meets a group of people who perceive it as solving something for them. This is why the rest of the chapter moves from creativity to perspective, from perspective to the value creator, and from there to taste and articulation: each step adds a condition under which a creative act becomes valuable to someone. Chapter 31 develops "value is perception" as part of Koe's theory of value.

**Source:** How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md

### 18.2 Collect, Connect, Deconstruct and Remix

Section 18.1 described what creativity is. This section describes how it works, in the sense of what the mind does when it creates. Koe gives two families of answers. The first is spatial: experience is a map of dots, and creativity is collecting, connecting and creating with them. The second is mechanical: creativity is taking wholes apart and putting the parts together differently, whether those parts are one's own or other people's. Around the second answer Koe builds a set of metaphors for the creator (the DJ, the blacksmith, the idea worker) that all say the same thing in different registers: the creator is someone who gathers material and remixes it into something that has their mark.

#### Collect the dots, connect the dots, create with the dots

In the 2024 video where he quotes Krishnamurti, Koe gives his own definition right after the quotation: "Creativity in my eyes is the process of bringing order to consciousness to embrace chaos: collect the dots, connect the dots and create with the dots, to create certainty from uncertainty, sense from nonsense, clarity from chaos, focus from distraction, signal from noise, something from nothing, success from failure, meaning from struggle, life from death, positive from negative."

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md

The definition has three parts. The first ties creativity to Chapter 5: consciousness tends toward disorder (psychic entropy), and creativity is one way of bringing order to it. The phrase "to embrace chaos" matters: the order is not created by avoiding chaos but by going into it. The second part is the three-phase process that gives the definition its name, **collect / connect / create with the dots**. One collects experiences, information and ideas (the dots); one connects them, seeing relations that were not obvious; and one creates something with the connected dots, a solution, a piece of content, a product. The third part is the list of paired opposites, each describing the same movement from a less ordered to a more ordered state. As noted in Section 18.1, "something from nothing" in this list is the item that Koe's 2026 definition contradicts.

#### Creativity equals create plus clarity: the dots on a map

The dots metaphor is older than 2024. In June 2022, in "How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet," Koe presented it with a graphic. The graphic itself is not visible in the transcript, so its exact layout cannot be reconstructed, but its components can be recovered from what he says.

At the top of the graphic is a formula: "creativity equals create plus clarity." Below it is a circle. Koe says the circle is "the unknown," or alternatively the experience one gains over time, or what one is "made conscious of," and that it should be thought of as a map. Inside the map are dots, some filled and some unfilled. The human task, as he describes it, is "to trek into the unknown, uncomfortable, unpredictable aspects of life and gain awareness of what's on the map"; as one explores, unfilled dots become filled. Filled dots stand for what one has been made conscious of. Creativity is then taking that experience, the stimuli and information stored in the subconscious (which is more than one is directly aware of), and creating clarity from it. Connecting the dots produces "a creative solution to a modern problem." He adds the economic point that this is how innovators "make the big bucks."

**Source:** How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md

| Element of the graphic | What it stands for |
|---|---|
| "Creativity = create + clarity" | Creativity is the act of creating clarity from accumulated experience |
| The circle | The unknown; the experience gained over time; what one has been made conscious of; a map |
| Unfilled dots | Parts of reality not yet explored or experienced |
| Filled dots | What one has been made conscious of through exploration |
| Connecting the dots | Producing a creative solution to a modern problem from that experience |

Two features of the 2022 version deserve attention. The first is that the material of creativity is experience, and specifically experience gained by going into the uncomfortable and unpredictable. A person who stays within the known has few filled dots to connect. The second is that much of the stored material is subconscious: one has taken in more than one can name, and creativity draws on that reservoir. Both features recur below.

#### Entering chaos and creating clarity

In May 2023, in a video about NPCs (the people who live on autopilot, in the vocabulary of Chapter 1), Koe describes the same process as a sequence of inner moves. NPCs, he says, don't realize that "the magic or the discovery or the zest of life comes from the unknown, from the uncertain," and from going into it, exposing oneself to chaos and creating clarity from it. "That's what creativity is: being in a chaotic state, making sense of it, creating order, being okay, calming down, zooming out, gathering the pieces and putting them back together into a tool or resource that you can use to launch yourself into the future."

**Source:** Most People Are NPCs (Don't Waste Your Life On Autopilot).md

The sequence is almost a procedure: (1) be in the chaotic state rather than fleeing it; (2) make sense of it; (3) create order; (4) be okay, calm down; (5) zoom out; (6) gather the pieces; (7) reassemble them into a tool or resource; (8) use it to move forward. The emotional steps (being okay, calming down) are not decoration. They correspond to the "deep restful state" of the 2022 Matrix video and the "open, relaxed state" of 2026: in all three versions, the creative act requires that the person not be gripped by stress while facing disorder. The output is a tool, which links this description back to the toolbuilder of Section 18.1.

#### Nothing happens, then everything happens

The dots metaphor also explains a pattern Koe observes in careers. In a 2022 video on seven failed online businesses, the first lesson is "nothing happens, then everything happens," or "nothing makes sense and then everything makes sense." The unconventional path is unpredictable; courses can teach aspects of it, but most of it depends on one's ability to self-educate, iterate, pivot and pick up specific knowledge. "Exploring the unknown, you're collecting dots until you can connect them, and once you connect them, things take off." His own case is the example: seven failed businesses preceded the success.

**Source:** 7 Lessons From 7 Failed Online Businesses (And Making 7 Figures).md

Chapter 15 treats "nothing happens, then everything happens" as the shape of the path to mastery. Here the same phrase receives a mechanism. Progress appears non-linear because the connecting phase cannot begin until enough dots have been collected; during the collecting phase, nothing visible happens, and once a critical connection is made, many dots become usable at once.

#### The missing dot

A corollary appears in a January 2023 video on the "niche of one" (Chapter 19). After presenting an idea that he expects some viewers will not yet grasp, Koe says that not understanding it is "a good thing," and explains why with the dots: the reason is lack of experience. "There's a dot here and there's a dot here and you're missing the dot here that allows those to connect and give you that aha moment." The **missing dot** is the piece of experience that would let two existing ones connect.

What to do about it is equally concrete. Start anyway. Immerse yourself in information that has the potential to solve the problem: read books, buy courses, listen to podcasts. "Drown yourself in the information," to the point where, when you do nothing, zoom out or go on a walk, ideas won't stop coming, because "your subconscious mind is working to solve that problem." This is the practical side of the subconscious reservoir of the 2022 graphic: one deliberately loads the subconscious with material relevant to a problem and then gives it the unstructured time in which connections surface. The walk, which appears repeatedly in this chapter and in Chapter 13, is the standard setting for that surfacing.

**Source:** The Most Profitable Niche Is You (Create Your Niche Of One).md

The method needs a qualification that Koe makes elsewhere (Chapter 14): immersion in information without application becomes "tutorial hell." The two positions are compatible because the immersion here is directed by a problem one is actually trying to solve; the information is collected for the sake of a connection, not as a substitute for action.

#### The candle in a dark room

A related image explains how skills, rather than ideas, connect. In a 2023 video on the "$1 million skill stack," Koe draws on screen what he calls the gradual awareness of the domain. "When you learn one skill, a field of awareness opens around it." Picture the skill and its domain as a candle in a dark room. You use that skill to navigate, and its light makes you aware of other skills and opportunities you could learn or take on. Learn the next one, and a field opens around that; now there are two connected fields, then three, four, and eventually "you full circle," with awareness of a much larger domain in which you can act and solve problems by connecting your skills. Understanding (not necessarily mastering) one skill after another, together with one's interests and life experience, expands the **field of awareness**, and "that is where you can solve creative problems, that is where you're going to make money." The drawing itself is only partly reconstructable from the transcript; the sequence of candles and overlapping fields is what the narration supports.

**Source:** The $1 Million Dollar Skill Stack (Learn In This Order).md

The candle image adds something to the dots. The dots are pieces of experience; the candles are capacities that let one see more dots. Learning a skill does not only add a dot; it lights up a region where new dots become visible. Creativity in the sense of this section is greatest where several fields overlap, because there one can connect things that people with a single field cannot see together. This is the root of Koe's preference for the generalist (Chapter 20) and of the "connect disciplines" method in Section 18.3.

#### Idea space: a map of light and dark

In December 2025, in "The Most Important Skill To Learn Right Now," Koe returns to the map image at a more abstract level. "Think of idea space or the unknown as a universal map with light and dark spots. The light spots are areas you've explored and the dark spots are where your potential lies." The map is a surface area of ideas to discover and test against reality. When results do not move you toward your goal, or move you away from it, a problem is revealed and you must "error correct toward the goal."

**Source:** The Most Important Skill To Learn Right Now.md

The passage is part of a longer argument about human capabilities, and the third capability he names here is **variation**. Is there a limit to the new ideas we can generate? With computation we can navigate the whole space; with agency we can take any step and "eventually stumble across a good idea after many bad ones"; with creation we can move through it uniquely, "like flying over a forest rather than walking through it." The conclusion is expansive: "we can understand anything, create anything, and discover an infinite set of new ideas to solve an infinite string of problems." Koe adds that an artificial general intelligence could do the same, and that both humans and AGI are bound by the laws of nature. The passage may draw on a source he does not name; it is presented here as Koe's.

**Complementary context:** The vocabulary of this passage (humans as universal explainers, progress through error correction, no limit to the ideas that can be discovered within the laws of physics) resembles that of the physicist David Deutsch in *The Beginning of Infinity*. The transcript does not mention Deutsch, so the resemblance is noted only as orientation, not as an attribution.

The evolution of the metaphor is continuous. The 2022 map of filled and unfilled dots becomes in 2025 a universal idea space with explored and unexplored regions. What changes is the scale: the 2022 map is an individual's experience; the 2025 map is the space of all possible ideas, with the individual as one navigator and AI as another. What stays is the idea that creative potential lies in the unexplored region and that problems (results that do not match the goal) are the signals that guide exploration.

#### Deconstruct wholes into parts, reconstruct parts into wholes

The second family of answers is mechanical. In an October 2022 video, "The Rise Of The Value Creator," later reproduced in a 2024 compilation guide, Koe explains how to explore what he calls a "crevice of reality": "you deconstruct wholes into parts, then you reconstruct those parts back into a whole."

**Source:** The Rise Of The Value Creator (A Career Path For Polymaths & Self-Improvers).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

He gives the method a philosophical grounding, briefly and with the note that he will not go deeper. The universe, God, or infinite intelligence (he uses the three terms together) "has divided itself into infinite different parts; it is a whole and it is a part," and parts exist because of distinctions. "The only reason they exist is because of relationship… because of distinction." His example is himself: "I am not that camera, so I am a human being." If you were floating in a void, you would cease to exist, because you would have nothing to compare yourself to. The claim is metaphysical and he presents it as a philosophy, "metaphorically and philosophically," not as a demonstrated fact.

The relevance of the philosophy to creativity is the following. If things exist as parts of larger wholes and are defined by their distinctions and relations, then understanding something means taking it apart into its parts, and creating something means arranging parts into new relations. The whole–part language anticipates the "holon" vocabulary Koe takes from Ken Wilber, which appears in Part XIV.

**Complementary context:** In Ken Wilber's integral philosophy, a holon is something that is at once a whole in itself and a part of a larger whole (a term Wilber takes from Arthur Koestler). Koe's 2022 passage uses the whole/part idea without naming Wilber.

#### Mental Legos and deconstructing everything

In January 2023 Koe compresses the method into an image: "That's what creativity is: it's either taking one thing like an idea and then deconstructing it into parts and then reconstructing it like mental Legos," or taking those parts from other people and reconstructing them into your own. The two options in the definition should be kept apart. In the first, one works with a single idea, taking it apart and building it back in a new form. In the second, one takes parts from many people and assembles them into something that is one's own. The second is the basis of everything Koe says later about synthesis, and also of his defense of "stealing" ideas (Section 18.3).

**Source:** The Most Profitable Niche Is You (Create Your Niche Of One).md

In a November 2022 video of lessons from growing a one-person business, the fourth lesson is "deconstruct everything." "Deconstructing everything is the key to creativity": break a whole into parts and turn the parts into something new — "not necessarily new, but a new perspective, solution or way of thinking." He gives the steps: (1) expose yourself to more experience; (2) have accurate perception of the information you consume; (3) question it, to break it down and gain a holistic perspective; (4) piece things together and apply them to a different goal, yours or your audience's. His example, developed in the next part of the same video, is taking principles deconstructed from spirituality and applying them to making money.

**Source:** 11 Lessons From Growing A 7-Figure One Person Business.md

The four steps connect the mechanical method to the rest of the book. The first step is the collecting of dots. The second, accurate perception, echoes the thinking of Chapter 17 (seeing what is there rather than what one expects). The third, questioning, is the "Questioning" section of Chapter 17 applied to an object of study. The fourth is transfer: the parts become useful when applied to a goal other than the one for which they were created. That last step is where the novelty usually lies. Spiritual principles are not new and neither is advice about money, but the principles of one applied to the other produce "a new perspective." The phrase "not necessarily new" anticipates Section 18.3, where Koe argues that originality is mostly a matter of perspective.

#### Creators are DJs with ideas

Koe's favorite metaphor for this remixing is the DJ. It appears three times in the corpus with different nuances.

In December 2022 he says that he thinks of creators as "idea junkies," like DJs. He muses aloud whether DJ stands for "disc jockey" or "junkie," and settles, half-jokingly, on junkie. DJs, he says, do what creators do, but with music. Electronic dance music is relatively new, yet new genres and subgenres are created every week, unlike traditional music (jazz, R&B, country, rock). Technology opened the ability to be infinitely creative, and minor distinctions are how people separate themselves from the crowd. You have the information to become an idea junkie and synthesize your own philosophy into internet content. "Ideas are the sounds, and then when you mix them together, the content are the songs. So create your mix, play it loud, and attract an audience."

**Source:** You Have A $100,000 Product In Your Head (One Person Business Series).md

The 2024 compilation guide reproduces this passage in the context of the "way of the synthesizer" (Section 18.4), with the abbreviation "IJs" (idea junkies) and with examples of EDM subgenres (riddim, "zap step," dubstep, grime step). The point he draws from the subgenres is explicit: the distinctions between them are minor, "but that's how people separate themselves from the crowd."

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

In July 2023, in "The Value Creator," Koe develops the metaphor with more care under the phrase **DJs with ideas**. DJs play sets, mix synths and sounds and remix things; they pull many things together and turn them into something new. Technology let DJs, especially in electronic music, manipulate and piece sounds together seamlessly in a way physical instruments could not, and new genres sprang up. Koe says he values classical music but finds it silly to deny electronic music's creativity and evolution, and that the same pattern can be seen in every industry. In the creator economy, people talk about new topics in education, "creating this tree of content for people to explore; we're documenting the collective consciousness online." Then the parallel: "With software and skill, DJs can blend together sounds into songs, and with software and skill, creators can blend together ideas into content." Creators collect their own ideas, other people's ideas and remixes of their favorites, and weave them with the evergreen skills (writing, speaking, marketing, sales); over time they attract an audience that invests attention in those ideas, and with that attention they benefit humanity and earn a living doing what they enjoy.

**Source:** The Value Creator (A New Internet Career Path For Intelligent People).md

| Element | For the DJ | For the creator |
|---|---|---|
| Raw material | Sounds (one's own, others', samples) | Ideas (one's own, others', remixes of favorites) |
| Tools | Software and skill | Software and the evergreen skills (writing, speaking, marketing, sales) |
| Output | Songs, sets | Content |
| Source of distinction | Minor differences that create new subgenres | Minor differences in perspective that separate one from the crowd |
| Result | An audience that comes to hear the mix | An audience that invests attention in the ideas; income from doing what one enjoys |

The metaphor carries a defense of remixing. A DJ is not accused of lacking creativity because the sounds already existed; the creativity is in the selection, combination and distinction. Koe asks that ideas be judged the same way. It also carries a claim about technology: in both cases, new tools expanded what could be combined, and expansion produced more genres, not fewer. Koe's "tree of content" and the "collective consciousness online" anticipate the Part VIII view of the internet as a place where minds are documented.

#### The mental blacksmith

In the same 2023 video Koe offers a second metaphor, which he recommends as the way to start: "becoming a **mental blacksmith**." In a video game, blacksmiths mine ore to fuel their creations, smelt it and turn it into armor or weapons. Creators do the same with ideas. They mine a crevice of reality they are interested in, keeping the holistic aspect (if one is interested in health, one is probably also interested in business), consume information of interest, and extract lessons or ideas to turn into something new.

**Source:** The Value Creator (A New Internet Career Path For Intelligent People).md

The two products of the forge correspond to two kinds of value:

| Product | What it is | Example or form |
|---|---|---|
| Ore | Ideas and lessons extracted from a crevice of reality | Information consumed in a domain of interest |
| Armor | "The perspective or worldview that helps people navigate reality in a better way" | A book that brings awareness to a potential one didn't know existed; ideas, motivation and inspiration that shape perspective and identity |
| Weapons | "Tools for battle": advice that teaches how to do things and encourages action | Actionable steps and solutions, crafted with writing, speaking, marketing and sales |

The distinction between armor and weapons is useful beyond the metaphor. Armor is perspective: it changes how a person sees and protects them as they move through reality. Weapons are tactics: they tell a person what to do. A creator who only gives weapons offers advice without a frame; one who only gives armor offers inspiration without action. The same tension reappears in Section 18.3 as the shift between "how-to" and "novel perspective." Koe ties the crafting to his skill stack and closes with a phrase that compresses his theory of value: "marketing is perception, but value is perception."

#### Idea workers

The most recent version of the remixing idea appears in June 2026, in "If You Have Multiple Interests, Start A One-Person Business." Koe describes a type of person: they "read weird books. They find themselves in esoteric rabbit holes on the internet. They curate idea sources they love and save them in a safe place. They question what most people think, gather multiple perspectives, and synthesize a unique one. They jot down thoughts like mad scientists. They are all **idea workers**. They hunt for ideas and share the best ones, because 80% of writing comes from research. The other 20% is structuring and writing it the way that only you could."

**Source:** If You Have Multiple Interests, Start A One-Person Business.md

The 80/20 ratio is a heuristic, not a measurement, and it should be read alongside the "research of 100 to create a content of one" in Section 18.3. Both say that the bulk of creative work is gathering, and that the distinctive part is small but essential: the structure and voice "only you could" give. The description also gathers almost every step of this section into one portrait: reading outside the mainstream (collecting), curating sources (the commonplace book of Chapter 16), questioning (deconstructing), gathering multiple perspectives and synthesizing (connecting), and writing (creating with the dots).

### 18.3 Original Thinking and Novel Perspectives

If creativity is collecting, connecting and remixing material that already exists, what becomes of originality? Koe's answer has two layers. At the level of ideas, he repeatedly says that nothing is truly original. At the level of perspective, he says that originality is not only possible but necessary, and that it is the main thing a creator offers. Most of this section is about the second layer: what a **novel perspective** is, why it captures attention, how it is generated, and how naming one's perspectives turns them into durable assets.

#### Nobody has original ideas

Koe's skeptical position appears in its bluntest form in a December 2024 video on building a one-person business: "Pro tip: nobody has original ideas, absolutely nobody." His reasoning is a claim about how the mind works: if one understands how the mind soaks in information and produces ideas, one sees that other ideas are needed for any new idea to exist. Writing online that seems like "an original deep thought," he guarantees, is combining ideas from sources one is not yet aware of.

**Source:** Build A One-Person Business As A Normal Person (From $0 To $10K).md

In January 2025, in "You're Not Boring: How To Become An Original Thinker (Fast)," he develops the position into an argument. It begins with a qualification of skepticism: "Becoming a true skeptic… will open your mind to less limited thinking, but it doesn't guarantee that you will have original ideas; it's just a starting point." (Skepticism and questioning are the subject of Chapter 17.) Then the chain: "Original thinking is perception; perception is dependent on identity; identity is dependent on conditioning, values, beliefs, conscious or unconscious goals and the rest." Originality, on this view, is not a property of an idea but a relation between an idea and a perceiver. Whether something seems original depends on who is looking.

His example is his own book. Some readers of *The Art of Focus* thought it was incredible; others left negative reviews saying "it's nothing new, I've read this all somewhere before." He thought it fairly original, "or maybe not even original but at least unique… multiple ideas were strung together." The lesson: "certain identities at certain levels of development will see your ideas as original and some won't, and that's fine." He extends it to the thinkers he admires: "Do you actually think all of Peterson's and Watts's ideas have never been said before, or are they simply articulating a good idea in a way that a specific group of people perceive as great?" The question anticipates Section 18.6, where articulation, not novelty, is what Koe admires in those thinkers.

**Source:** You're Not Boring How To Become An Original Thinker (Fast).md

The argument has a practical function. It releases the writer from a demand that cannot be met (to say something no one has ever said) and replaces it with one that can (to say something in a way that a specific group perceives as new and valuable). It also explains why criticism of the form "nothing new" is not decisive: it reports the reader's position as much as the work's.

#### The evolution of the originality question

Koe's position on originality is not perfectly stable, and the change is worth laying out because he does not reconcile it.

In 2022 he says: "Nothing is original, but there are original perspectives, and that's where you come in" (a passage in which he calls the person a "perspective vessel," developed in Chapter 3). In 2024 he says "nobody has original ideas, absolutely nobody." In January 2025 original thinking is "largely a myth," yet he offers five ways to think originally (below). In February 2026, in a framework of "levels of thinking" adapted from developmental psychology (treated in Chapter 38), the highest level, which he calls "generative," is described as one in which "you create original perspectives that didn't exist before, or you come to ideas without outside influence." The 2026 description admits something the 2024 one denied: ideas reached without outside influence. Koe adds in that framework that levels are not strictly sequential and that one can "tap into level four occasionally."

**Source:** How To Think Like A Strategic Genius (5-Dimensional Thinking).md (2026); Build A One-Person Business As A Normal Person (From $0 To $10K).md (2024)

The change can be read as a shift of emphasis rather than a reversal. From 2022 to 2025 the claim is that originality is relative to perspective and perception; in 2026 the generative level is presented as rare and occasional. Both readings are consistent with the practical advice of this section, which never asks the creator to produce ideas from nothing and always asks them to produce a perspective that is theirs. But the reader should note that the absolute denial of 2024 and the generative level of 2026 cannot both be literally true.

#### Five ways to think originally

Having said that original thinking is largely a myth, Koe nevertheless asks: "How do we discover, articulate and create new ideas that few people have said before? Because that's the key to your success as a creative, especially going into the world of AI domination." He answers with five steps.

**Source:** You're Not Boring How To Become An Original Thinker (Fast).md

| Step | Instruction | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Say the same thing from your own perspective | Originality is exploring existing ideas in a fresh way, which takes mental labor |
| 2 | Read what other people haven't read | If you consume what everyone consumes, you will say what everyone says |
| 3 | Connect ideas from different disciplines | Write about one domain, study another; the connection is the new perspective |
| 4 | Use frameworks | Creativity thrives within self-set constraints |
| 5 | Experiment | Original thinking is the byproduct of experience |

**Step 1: say the same thing from your own perspective.** Koe starts from a complaint he hears constantly while teaching writing (in his 2 Hour Writer course and in the Cortex Discord community): "Most writers struggle because they feel as if they're saying what's already been said." His reply: "Originality is less about saying something new and more about exploring existing ideas in a fresh way. This takes effort, mental labor, endurance running for your psyche. You need to set aside time to think through an idea without distractions — go on a walk." The phrase "endurance running for your psyche" is characteristic: freshness is not a matter of inspiration but of sustained thinking about the same idea until a new angle appears. The walk is again the setting.

**Step 2: read what others haven't read.** This step and step 4 are developed in other units of the same video, whose full treatment belongs to other chapters, and are summarized here so the framework is complete. Koe says that the best ideas he has found are "collecting dust in little-known books, podcasts, videos and posts," and that most people who seem original are "just talking about ideas you haven't seen before." If one consumes only mainstream books and algorithmic feeds, it is no wonder one feels one is saying the same thing. He recommends long podcasts with a few thousand views and going down "more rabbit holes," while separating the intention of entertainment from that of learning. Chapter 16 develops what and how to read.

**Step 3: connect ideas from different disciplines.** "I get my best ideas when I want to write about one topic like business but study something completely different like philosophy, and in fact these usually turn into my most memorable writing and videos. People like my business videos because I bring a philosophical depth to it." His examples are two of his own concepts. The advice to "niche down" combined with ego development theory became "you are the niche," which targets people at different levels of development (Chapter 19). The marketing concept of levels of awareness (problem aware, solution aware, product aware) combined with Anthony de Mello's book *Awareness* produced what he calls "a really unique and potentially original perspective to writing about writing and teaching about the levels of awareness." The prescription: "Stop only studying what you're good at… complement that with a complementary domain to bring a new dimension to it," so that one is not pigeonholed in a specialist area "so that an AI specialist can go and replace it." If you are into marketing, study psychology or spirituality; if into philosophy, study business or social media; and "write about the connections that you make."

**Complementary context:** The "levels of awareness" come from Eugene Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966), a foundational text of direct-response copywriting; Anthony de Mello (1931–1987) was a Jesuit priest whose posthumous book *Awareness* presents spiritual teaching on seeing through conditioning. Koe's originality, by his own account, lies in putting the two side by side.

**Step 4: use frameworks.** Koe says that creative people are "allergic to frameworks for no good reason," because they think frameworks ruin their authenticity or freedom, but "creativity thrives within constraints, specifically constraints that you set." "You can't think outside of the box if there is no box." Chapter 17 (thinking from the problem) and Part VIII (structure) develop the frameworks he uses, chiefly "pain and process."

**Step 5: experiment.** "Experimentation or direct experience is going to give you the most unique viewpoints on things because they're your experience and you're unique from everyone else." The reasoning is a chain: "since most thinking starts with a problem and experimentation, not prescription, is how you solve problems for good, then original thinking is the byproduct of experience." Koe then widens the scope: "You live in your body, you live with your mind, you live with other people and you live with responsibilities; because of that, it should be considered your full-time job to learn about them, practice skills that improve them and master those domains." He is bothered by people who say "I don't know what to do" while their life is a mess: "you're over here studying this esoteric crap rather than just fixing what's right in front of you, because that's going to teach you more."

The criticism of "esoteric crap" sits in tension with other parts of the corpus, where Koe recommends metaphysics, *The Kybalion* and Hermetic philosophy as foundations of reality (around the same period, in 2024 and March 2025). He does not reconcile the two. The most plausible reading, supported by the context of each, is that the criticism targets people who use esoteric study to avoid concrete problems, and the recommendation targets people who are already acting and want general principles. The point of step 5 survives either way: one's own problems, worked on directly, are the most reliable source of a perspective no one else has.

The five steps share a structure. Steps 1 and 3 work on the connecting phase (seeing existing ideas from a new angle or in a new combination); steps 2 and 5 work on the collecting phase (gathering dots others do not have, either from obscure sources or from direct experience); step 4 supplies the constraint within which the creating phase can happen. The framework is therefore a practical elaboration of collect–connect–create.

#### Novelty catches attention

Why does perspective matter so much? Koe's first answer is about attention. In an August 2022 video on writing as a high-income skill, step two of his writing method is "brainstorm a unique perspective," and the reason is that "novelty catches attention; people like new things." His examples are simple. Going out into nature and thinking "wow, this is so beautiful" is novelty. Reading someone's unique perspective that completely makes sense and changes how you think produces dopamine and excitement, "caused by a novel perspective."

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Thrive In The Next 10 Years.md

In May 2023, in "I Made $800,000 In One Year Writing 2 Hours A Day," he restates step two and adds a defense of attention itself. "Novelty catches attention, and attention is necessary for people to read what you write." Against those who say that catching attention is dirty or "cheap dopamine," he argues: "you have to catch attention… that's what you're doing at any given second of the day." When you put on cologne to go out, you want to attract someone; when you slick your hair in the morning, "you're not doing it for yourself"; if you were the only person on Earth, you would not keep those grooming habits. So "you might as well make that conscious and do something good with it." And: "Novel perspectives are how you differentiate yourself through experience with the things that you write about."

**Source:** I Made $800,000 In One Year Writing 2 Hours A Day.md

The argument removes a moral objection by showing that the objector already does what they object to. Everyone seeks attention in daily life; the question is not whether but how, and to what end. The answer "consciously, and for something good" connects to the theme of making unconscious behavior conscious that runs from Chapter 1 onward. It also distinguishes the attention Koe defends from the attention he criticizes in Chapter 11: not attention as such, but attention captured without value behind it.

#### The good dopamine hit

In a December 2023 video on the "Build Teach Earn" method, Koe lists novel perspectives as the third element of content that holds attention. Most people regurgitate the same ideas and never provide a new way of looking at things. "Novel perspectives are the main way to provide a good dopamine hit that aids in learning, understanding and action." Most creators "dish out dopamine like candy," hoping for a loyal readership. The phrase "good dopamine" is deliberate. Chapter 11 distinguishes cheap dopamine (stimulation without progress) from the dopamine of meaningful pursuit; here Koe places novel perspectives on the good side, because the excitement they produce is attached to understanding something and being moved to act.

**Source:** How Intelligent Creators Will Make Money In 2024 (The Build Teach Earn Method).md

How are they created? "Through **hunting in the unknown**": through personal experience, pursuing one's own goals and self-reflection, and researching information in the depths of books, podcasts, articles and social media accounts. "People don't want just another idea, they want a unique point of view." His illustration is the introduction to the same video, on the future of work: he had to stop and think about the idea he wanted to convey, and paired personal experience with the philosophical ideas that shaped his worldview. The conclusion is a recipe in one sentence: combine topics, beliefs and novel perspectives acquired over time "to craft writing that holds attention without annoying tactics." The "unknown" here is the same territory as the unfilled dots of Section 18.2.

#### A research of 100 to create a content of one

In November 2023, in "The Most Important High-Income Skill To Learn (In The Next 10 Years)," Koe adds a principle about inputs. Novel perspectives "require getting the bulk of your ideas from long form": newsletters, articles, podcasts, books, talks. In short form it is hard to create a novel perspective because one does not get the whole perspective. Novelty raises dopamine and brings more clarity to an idea that has been talked about over and over. "If you're getting all of your content from Twitter while trying to grow on Twitter, you will probably end up with the same perspective as everyone else." Metaphors, personal experiences and creating new concepts are how one achieves novelty. And then the ratio: "Content consumption or research is as important as creation: you need a research of 100 to create a content of one."

**Source:** The Most Important High-Income Skill To Learn (In The Next 10 Years).md

The example he gives in the same video shows the method at small scale. He read in an article that a glass of water creates an acre of fog. The fact was unrelated to his topics (mindfulness, business, human potential), but it offered a unique perspective. He asked himself how to tie it in, and wrote: "The amount of water needed to create an acre of fog is one drinking glass. Remember this the next time your mind is fogged with worry — but in reality the problem is as insignificant as a glass of water." The steps are visible: collect an unrelated fact from long-form reading; notice that it carries a structure (a small quantity producing a large obscuring effect); map that structure onto a problem in one's own domain (worry); state the result as a metaphor. It is a concrete case of the "connect disciplines" step and of the mental Legos: a part taken from physics and reassembled into a piece of practical psychology.

**Source:** The Most Important High-Income Skill To Learn (In The Next 10 Years).md

#### How to generate a unique perspective: the brain dump

Koe's most detailed method for producing a perspective appears first in August 2022 and is repeated, almost word for word, in his 2024 compilation guide; a variant appears in January 2024. In the 2022 version, after explaining why novelty matters, he gives the how. Based on your topic, brain dump: (a) common problems associated with it; (b) the benefits of overcoming those problems; (c) common goals associated with it — "what are you helping people achieve," which is where how-to advice comes in, since you are helping people reach a goal through the problems in the way; and (d) your personal experiences related to it. Then write it as if you were explaining to a friend how to get to that goal, with all that context in mind: "I'm trying to get to this goal, but the problem standing in the way is this, so how am I going to tell someone how to get there?"

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Thrive In The Next 10 Years.md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The mechanism is stated in a phrase that the lexicon of this book treats as one of Koe's coinages: **"a problem creates the perspective."** Everyone has different problems, and those problems determine "what reality or what angle are you looking at this goal from and achieving this goal from." The perspective is the angle from which a particular person approaches a shared goal, and the angle is set by the obstacles that person has faced. Two people writing about the same goal (getting fit, writing well, earning online) will produce different perspectives if they have faced different problems, and each will be most useful to the readers who face the same ones. The phrase is a close relative of "a problem creates a goal" (Chapter 8): there the problem generates direction; here it generates the angle from which the direction is seen.

In January 2024, in "This High-Income Skill Will Make You Irreplaceable," the method reappears with two changes. The brain-dump list gains an item: common problems, benefits of overcoming them, common goals, **roadblocks** to those goals, and personal experiences. After the dump, one pieces everything together, orders it so that it makes sense, and explains it as one would to a friend, using the result as a structure. The second change is in the framing: "Forget everything you know about value": actionable advice and platitudes "don't sell as well as they did when the internet was first budding." Novelty catches attention; attention is a given if one wants readers; and "novel perspectives are the best and most replicable way to capture attention without using sleazy tactics."

**Source:** This High-Income Skill Will Make You Irreplaceable (Full Guide).md

| Element of the brain dump | 2022 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Common problems with the topic | Yes | Yes |
| Benefits of overcoming them | Yes | Yes |
| Common goals (where how-to advice comes in) | Yes | Yes |
| Roadblocks to those goals | — | Yes |
| Personal experiences | Yes | Yes |
| Final step | Explain it to a friend | Order it so it makes sense and explain it to a friend, as a structure |
| Status of how-to advice | The core of the perspective | Demoted: "actionable advice and platitudes don't sell as well" |

The demotion of actionable advice is part of a longer oscillation in the corpus. In 2022 the how-to is at the heart of the perspective. In 2023, in a conversation with Dickie Bush, Koe says he has moved toward "pithy big picture stuff" because tactical content is already documented, while in the same year he also says that "actionable advice is how you build an audience at the start." By January 2024 he tells his audience to "forget everything you know about value." In 2025 and 2026 the movement partly reverses: in conversations with the founders of Stan he accepts that abstract posts "sound like a philosophical quote" and don't lead to sales, and in 2026 he criticizes "fortune cookie tweets" written by people who "act like you're Marcus Aurelius." The reasons he gives change with the period: the market saturated with generic advice (2024); tactics already documented (2023); abstraction doesn't sell (2025). The positions can apply to different situations (concrete advice for the beginner without a reputation and for the feed; perspective and depth for the creator with an engaged audience and for long form), but Koe does not state that resolution himself. Chapters 23 and 32 return to this tension in the context of structure and persuasion.

#### People don't follow information, they follow perspectives

In October 2024, in "The Death Of The Personal Brand," Koe compresses the argument into a sentence: "People don't follow information, they follow perspectives." Ten people can talk about the same topics, but one says it in a way that brings entirely new meaning. "Perspectives are infinite": sometimes the new perspective is not a different idea at all but the same idea "from a deeper lens." He credits this for his own success. He takes ideas that perform well and seem shallow, senses that there is something deeper in them, writes to dissect them, and forms a deeper philosophy; people tell him they like that his content leans toward depth. The method: find ideas that are already working; save ideas from books, social media, podcasts and videos; turn them into an original idea by changing the perspective. To change the perspective one must "learn how to think with impact" (the subject of Chapter 17).

**Source:** The Death Of The Personal Brand (& The Future Of Creative Work).md

The sentence about what people follow evolves in later material. In 2024 Koe also says that people are "following the content," not the profile; in January 2025 that "they want your perspective on information"; in mid-2025 that "people don't follow ideas, they follow people who share ideas"; and in August 2026 that "people don't follow you for your content anymore," but for a point of view that comes from a worldview, with the reason that Google and AI make information available at a click. The drift is from content, to perspective, to person and worldview. The versions are compatible if one reads them as successive deepenings: information is available to everyone, so the differentiator is the angle; the angle comes from the person; and the person's angle comes from their worldview. Chapter 27, on the personal brand, takes up the last of these.

#### Popular means useful: say it from your goals

A heuristic from August 2024 removes the anxiety about repetition from the other side. "Any idea that you could see and you want to put under your brand in the form of content, save it for later and then post it. If it's already been said or taught by someone else, good. Say it from your own point of view, in alignment with your own goals, because goals shape how you perceive situations." Think about your goals or vision and how the idea relates to them, "and boom, you have a unique piece of content." Moreover, "if it's already been said or taught and is popular, that means it's going to be useful for your followers, too."

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

The justification "goals shape how you perceive situations" is the goal-as-lens of Chapter 6 applied to content. A popular idea passed through the lens of a particular person's goals comes out bent in a way that is theirs. The heuristic also inverts the usual worry: that an idea has been said before is evidence that it is useful, not a reason to avoid it.

#### The translator of ideas

In January 2026 Koe gives the most explicit account of how he himself turns others' ideas into his own. When thinking of ideas, content or products, "filter them through your story or through your worldview, which kind of comes naturally once you stop trying. You become a **translator of ideas**, right? Because no idea is really original. We disguise stealing like an artist as research." His process: pick a topic for the newsletter or the video; look at what others have said about it; think about books he has read or is reading; "pick and choose ideas and kind of tie them into something new, and say them in my own words, and that alone is unique enough."

**Source:** If you have multiple interests, do not waste the next 2-3 years.md

He adds a clarification that matters. Filtering through one's story does not mean talking about oneself all the time. Personal anecdotes, such as the story of his own "tutorial hell" with which he sometimes opens, are relatable, "but that's not necessarily what I mean. I mean translating." The distinction resolves a tension visible elsewhere in the corpus. In 2023 Koe advised talking about yourself in replies because it produces profile clicks, and in 2024 described his writing as a public journal; in 2025, guests he interviewed proposed writing for one's younger self while warning that "me me me" content fails. The 2026 passage separates two uses of the self: the self as the subject of the content, which should be limited, and the self as the filter through which content passes, which is unavoidable and is the source of uniqueness.

**Complementary context:** "Steal like an artist" is the title of a 2012 book by Austin Kleon, which popularized the idea that all creative work builds on what came before. Koe uses the phrase without naming its author.

Elsewhere in the corpus Koe pairs the phrase with a saying he attributes to Wilson Mizner, that taking from one source is plagiarism and taking from many is research, and he uses Seneca's image of gathering pollen from many flowers and digesting it into one's own honey for the same point (Chapter 22).

The phrase "we disguise stealing like an artist as research" places the translator within a longer history of Koe's views on "stealing" validated ideas. In 2022 he said that "people need to be reminded more than they need something new" and that all content is one's version of what others say; he treated stealing high-performing ideas as valid but "overused," and warned that the long-term solution was synthesis (Section 18.4), because copying without perspectives doesn't last. In 2023 he said he had changed the saying to "people need to be reminded with something new." In 2024 he advised taking ideas that already work and posting them under one's brand, while also saying, for the process of a single post, "don't even watch the video" (below). In 2025 he returned to "sell what's already selling with your own twist" and to the original form of the reminder saying; in 2026 he called validated content "the secret," while drawing the line that unique concepts and categories do belong to someone. The arc goes from stealing as a tolerated practice with a risk of commoditization to stealing as a central principle, while the demand for one's own perspective grows alongside it. The resolution the corpus supports, though Koe never states it in one place, is that what may be taken is the topic and the structure, and what must be one's own is the angle, the process, the wording and any coined concept. Chapter 26 treats validated content in detail.

#### Signal in the AI firehose

The 2026 version of the argument for novel perspectives is driven by AI. In the same January 2026 video, idea six is simply "content is novel perspectives." "The internet is just continuing to become more and more of a firehose of information, and AI isn't helping with that. Anyone can generate anything. But what that means is that trust and signal are more important than ever." Signal is the opposite of noise: the reaction "Ooh, exciting, important. Need to pay attention to that." The justification has changed from 2022 (novelty catches attention) to 2026 (when content is infinite, only signal is scarce), but the conclusion is the same, and Section 18.5 will make the scarcity argument explicit under the heading of taste.

**Source:** If you have multiple interests, do not waste the next 2-3 years.md

#### Memorable, even in a job interview

Koe does not limit the value of novel perspectives to content creators. In a May 2022 video titled "Stop Trying To Be Unique… Do This Instead," he says that beyond followers or buyers, intelligent imitation (the conscious copying discussed in Chapter 1) "is what's going to get you hired over the next person." You stick in an employer's mind by causing a rise in dopamine through a novel perspective: "that guy really made sense, that guy changed my mind on this issue." Being remembered in this way "lowers the necessity of having to actually build your value," because the impression does part of the work. The example is useful because it shows that a novel perspective is not a format of online content but a general mechanism of memory and persuasion: in any situation where someone must choose among people, the one who changed their mind is the one they remember.

**Source:** Stop Trying To Be Unique... Do This Instead.md

#### Name your own concepts: the positive mind virus

The last step from perspective to asset is naming. In a December 2022 video on the one-person business roadmap, reproduced in the 2024 compilation, Koe describes a sequence: note patterns from your experience, connect the dots in long-form content, and create your own versions of concepts, processes and philosophies. His example is the Eisenhower Matrix, a priority matrix that sorts tasks by what to pay attention to, what to forget, what to delegate and so on. It is "just a random name with a valuable idea or concept" that grew until people talk about it constantly. If he had a genuinely helpful system for prioritizing his day, he could give it his own name and distribute it through content or a book; if others talk about it, "that's a contagious idea," it sticks, and his ideas and brand grow "without me doing anything" — "like a **positive mind virus**." He closes the passage with a production note: take pieces of the long-form content for short-form content.

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

**Complementary context:** The Eisenhower Matrix sorts tasks into four quadrants by urgency and importance. It is named after Dwight D. Eisenhower, to whom a remark about the urgent and the important is attributed, and was popularized by Stephen Covey's *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People*.

The mechanism has three parts. A valuable idea alone spreads slowly because it has no handle; a name gives it a handle that others can repeat; and repetition by others spreads the idea and, with it, the reputation of the person associated with it. The "virus" is positive because what spreads is useful.

Koe illustrates with his own case in the same video. He did not come up with the concept of the one-person business, which meant operating a business alone with internet technology. "I didn't come up with the concept of one person business, but I took it and I gave it an entirely new meaning": productizing yourself, self-monetization, you as the brand, doing what you love for a living backed by systems that get results. That reinterpretation, he says, makes him stand out. He has also named processes of his own: "intelligent imitation," "tactical stress," "the unknown" and others (he does not define them in that video). He explains where these came from: applying his interests (spirituality, philosophy, neuroscience) to his expertise (online business, marketing, branding), with enough exposure to all those domains, because he is not a specialist, to notice common patterns and create long-form content that is difficult to replicate. This is the "connect disciplines" step and the candle-in-the-dark-room image in a single biographical example. In a 2023 conversation he says the same thing about the one-person business ("isn't my concept, but I like to bring new meaning to it"), which Section 18.4 takes up.

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md

In September 2024 he gives a method for making a process unique before naming it. The more unique one's process (the steps to overcome a pain point), the better. Choose a pain point, or find a high-performing topic, such as a YouTube video doing very well. To create your own version, start with the pain point and "don't even watch the video": do not adopt the simple solution everyone has already talked about; think through which steps one through five actually help people overcome the pain point in a unique way. Then, "if you want to make it even more powerful, name that process" — like "the one-person business" or "the 4-hour workday" — something tied to you, so that you get the authority for it. The instruction not to watch the successful video is a safeguard: it keeps the validated topic (which tells you people care) while preventing the validated solution from replacing one's own thinking.

**Source:** 4 Frameworks To Write Follow-Worthy Content (+ Kortex Template).md

**Complementary context:** "The 4-Hour Workday," the name Koe gives to his own method (Chapter 12), plays on the title of Tim Ferriss's *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007), which is an example of the same mechanism: a familiar phrase given a new meaning and attached to a person.

The section's argument can now be summarized as a chain. Nothing is wholly original, so the creator's contribution is a perspective. A perspective is created by the problems one has faced and the goals one pursues, and it is enriched by long-form research, obscure sources, other disciplines and direct experience. A novel perspective captures attention and produces a "good" dopamine response that helps people learn and act. Named and repeated, a perspective becomes a concept that spreads on its own and attaches authority to its author.

### 18.4 The Synthesizer

The previous sections described creativity, remixing and perspective as activities. This section describes the person who makes them a way of life and a way of earning a living. Koe uses two overlapping names for that person, the **synthesizer** and the **value creator**, and in 2025–2026 adds a third, cognitive version, the "synthesizer of truth" and the strategist, which concerns not a career but a level of thinking. The three are related by a single capacity: taking material from many places, holding it together and producing an integrated result that is more useful than any of its sources.

#### Why synthesizers: reality is not compartmentalized

In the October 2022 video "The Rise Of The Value Creator," reproduced in the 2024 compilation guide, Koe recounts watching a lecture by Actualized.org that pointed out that the school system and government are compartmentalized: a biology department, a chemistry department, a business department, and you are limited to studying within them, "when reality is not compartmentalized."

**Complementary context:** Actualized.org is a self-development and philosophy channel run by Leo Gura. Koe cites it several times in the corpus as a source of ideas he then adapts.

Koe's adaptation goes beyond the critique of institutions. Business is part of life, and all of one's life plays into it. "We need more synthesizers" to research their interests and "create an entirely new category," understanding and connecting the full picture to help people achieve goals. He admits that this is difficult, but says it is "fun" and "10 times more rewarding" to research what one wants and embrace the unconventional, uncomfortable path. He also defends the clichés that point in this direction, like "break out of your comfort zone": they have lived a long time because they are very important, even if conditioning has made them sound boring.

**Source:** The Rise Of The Value Creator (A Career Path For Polymaths & Self-Improvers).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The argument has the form of a mismatch. Institutions divide knowledge into departments; problems in real life do not respect those divisions; therefore someone must reconnect the pieces, and that person creates value precisely by working across the divisions. The phrase "create an entirely new category" is important: the synthesizer does not compete inside an existing field but defines a new one at an intersection. Chapter 20 develops the critique of specialization on which this rests.

#### The way of the synthesizer

In December 2022 Koe names the career path: "The future belongs to those that can deliver holistic, nuanced, but yet interesting information" that is valuable in this century. This is "the way of the synthesizer," the career path for self-improvers or polymaths: study all angles of one or multiple interests and talk about the findings, what is valuable, "the signal among the noise." Three adjectives define the product. **Holistic**: it considers the whole, not one department. **Nuanced**: it does not reduce the whole to one rule. **Interesting**: it holds attention, which brings back the novel perspective of Section 18.3. The DJ metaphor of Section 18.2 appears in the same passage as the image of how the synthesizer works.

**Source:** You Have A $100,000 Product In Your Head (One Person Business Series).md

The 2024 compilation guide shows what the way of the synthesizer looks like in practice, through Koe's own experiments in fitness and diet, presented as step four of building an information product: "experiment for holistic understanding." "My own way is implementing the best teachings from various sources until I have a better way of doing it or a unique way of doing it." In training he started with StrongLifts 5x5 to begin lifting; then studied Greg O'Gallagher, who trains only two or three days a week; then Wendler's 5/3/1, a powerlifting program; then super-high-volume bodybuilding splits. Each gave him data and the understanding that there are billions of ways to reach one result. If he found a way he genuinely enjoys and made it his own program, he says, he could package and sell it; personal brands are dynamic, without a fixed niche, as long as one makes one's interest interesting. In diet he tried If It Fits Your Macros, ancestral eating, veganism, keto and Greg Doucette's low-calorie approach (he eats Doucette's "anabolic French toast" often); from the ancestral approach he kept liver (for its nutrient density), ground beef and eggs. This big-picture understanding, he says, lets him create "10 times more valuable information than the people that are just trapped in one ideology."

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The example has a clear structure. The synthesizer does not choose one school and defend it; they implement several, keep what works for them, and combine it into something that is theirs. The value of the result comes from two sources: the breadth of the experiments (more dots) and the absence of ideological commitment (freedom to connect them). The multiplier "10 times" is rhetorical, but the contrast it expresses is the one between the synthesizer and the person "trapped in one ideology." This is a concrete case of the step "experiment" in the five ways to think originally, and of the self-experimentation of Chapter 15.

#### Synthesis as a passion: transcend and include

In a March 2023 conversation with Dickie Bush, Koe speaks about synthesis in the first person. He could see himself writing ten, twenty, thirty books on different topics (health, the creator economy), "like how Alan Watts wrote a bunch… he just picks a topic and muses on it." He likes "to create concepts like one person business; isn't my concept, but I like to bring new meaning to it… fill in the blanks." His way of educating is not "here's these steps that I've been told are the steps"; "it's more so here's the steps that I've learned from this specific perspective, and here's some other perspectives to help back that… synthesis is my passion." The form follows: "you find the vessel," whether a book or, in a project he describes, a "mastery facility" as a kind of "physical synthesis." And the next step, to avoid saturation: "you transcend and include… you integrate… you create something new by combining them together, and that unlocks new lenses to go and create from."

**Source:** Dan Koe & Dickie Bush On One-Person Businesses, Creative Workflows, and Lifestyle Design.md

**Complementary context:** "Transcend and include" is Ken Wilber's formula for development: each higher stage goes beyond the previous one while keeping what was true in it. Koe uses the phrase in many contexts (see Part XIV); here he applies it to ideas and to creative work.

The application of "transcend and include" to saturation is an interesting move. A creator who has exhausted a topic, or a market saturated with similar content, can be escaped by integrating several positions into a new one that contains them; the new position is a new "lens" from which further work can be created. Synthesis is thus not only a way of producing content but a way of renewing one's creative capacity when a subject has been worn out.

#### Synthesis as protection against commoditization

In the December 2022 roadmap video (reproduced in 2024), synthesis becomes a stage in a business plan. At stage three, with high follower counts and income, the creator should reposition as a "synthesizer": develop big ideas that stick in people's heads, which Koe calls "the mental monopoly." He says one can do this from the start, but at this stage it becomes critical so as not to become a commodity, because people copy successful creators. When something of his "pops off," the next day he sees around ten similar posts, slightly reworded, so that it is not plagiarism; "they're not going to make it," he says, because they lack the creative ability to consistently produce novel perspectives. This is best done in long form (newsletters, YouTube, podcasts).

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

His method for becoming a synthesizer at this stage has four steps: pursue what interests you; study the big-picture ideas, not the minor details ("when you read a book, what's the five huge ideas that they wrote that entire book around?"); sit with those ideas, go on a walk, experience life; and connect the dots through experience, writing and note-taking. He mentions building software for "content synthesis" (Chapter 16 tells the story of that software).

The passage qualifies an earlier step of the same roadmap, where stealing high-performing ideas is accepted. Stealing works, but it is available to everyone, and what everyone can do becomes a commodity. The copier can reproduce the post but not the perspective-generating capacity behind it; the synthesizer's defense is to make that capacity the product. The "five huge ideas" of a book anticipate the "inner album of greatest hits" of Section 18.6: in both cases, the unit of value is a small number of big ideas, thought through deeply, rather than many details.

#### What makes creative work succeed in the age of AI

In February 2025, in "How To Thrive With Multiple Interests," Koe applies synthesis to the question of AI. "It doesn't matter if AI can write a book or build a software or design a website," because there is a human behind it anyway, and the ability to produce the thing is "not what makes creative work successful" or what makes people care, which is necessary for the work to be valuable. "What makes a book, a brand, a software and any other creative pursuit work is not the ability to create that thing but how you synthesize multiple various ideas from different disciplines in a way that nobody could have thought of." A book on how to focus takes a completely different shape "when you're at the pillar of it with your personal experience" and merge philosophy, psychology, metaphysics, spirituality or whatever one's interests are. He proposes an exercise: think of a random weird interest you have and how it could fit into a book called *How to Focus*; "it probably can."

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

The argument separates two things that are easily confused: the capacity to produce an artifact, which AI has made cheap, and the combination of ideas that makes an artifact matter to people, which depends on who is doing the combining. Section 18.5 picks up the same video's argument about taste, which is the faculty that selects among the combinations.

#### The value creator: definition and four traits

In the same October 2022 video in which he speaks of synthesizers, Koe introduces "what I call a **value creator**, or in some cases a synthesizer." He frames it as one way of doing the one-person business, among infinitely many (digital products, info products, courses, coaching and consulting are not the only models; the point is "turning yourself into a business"). It is a new career path the internet created; he places it among the "sixty percent of new jobs" yet to be created (Section 18.1). The value creator, or educator, "takes it on as their responsibility to research a specific crevice of reality that nobody has explored, or at least explored in that combination": explore many interests, synthesize and connect them, educate others, and play a role in the shift away from a traditional education system that cannot keep up with technology. Near the end of the video he says: "becoming a value creator, that may be my name for all of this," and calls it "a very viable and purposeful career path."

**Source:** The Rise Of The Value Creator (A Career Path For Polymaths & Self-Improvers).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The term **crevice of reality** recurs throughout the corpus: the patch of reality that intrigues a person most and that they explore, ideally a combination that no one has explored in quite that way. The qualifier "at least explored in that combination" is the originality argument of Section 18.3 restated: the crevice may not be new, but the combination of interests through which it is explored can be. The domains he lists as examples are self-improvement, marketing, business, spirituality, metaphysics, philosophy and email marketing. Value creators dedicate themselves to exploring the unknown aspects of life and their curiosity as their life's work.

He gives the value creator four traits, which the 2022 video presents as a list and the 2024 compilation repeats:

| Trait | Description | Key phrase |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Earn with the mind, not with time | They can remain a one-person business without limiting their income | "Value creators are the people that earn with their mind, not their time" |
| 2. A one-person media company | They research their obsessions and distill what they learn through writing, video and other internet content; they build their name on social media with media (videos, writing, graphics) and sell a product from that traffic | One-person media company |
| 3. Paid for aggregated knowledge | They research, "leave out breadcrumbs in the form of content and media," then aggregate the information into a step-by-step system sold as a digital product or service; "everyone should have some form of digital product" | Breadcrumbs; aggregated knowledge |
| 4. Focus on educating | Educating the audience instead of posting memes, starting drama or commentating; it does not take flashy looks to build an audience, but education about what one is learning and becoming expert in | Education over entertainment |

**Source:** The Rise Of The Value Creator (A Career Path For Polymaths & Self-Improvers).md

Each trait answers an economic question. How does a single person earn without trading time for money? By earning with the mind (trait 1). How do they reach people? By being their own media (trait 2). What do they sell? The aggregated, systematized version of what they have been giving away in pieces (trait 3; the "breadcrumbs" are the free content that leads to the product). Why do people come and stay? Because they are being taught something (trait 4). Parts X and XI of this book develop the business side of each trait.

In July 2023 Koe remade the video, with some repetition, and compressed the first trait into a phrase: "one man's labor cannot scale, but their creative ability can." He also revised the fourth trait. Memes or joke posts are fine — "you're supposed to entertain, you're supposed to be yourself" — but "you need different pillars of your brand"; one cannot be only a meme account and expect to be valuable and paid; one must help people get results, through education.

**Source:** The Value Creator (A New Internet Career Path For Intelligent People).md

The revision is a small instance of a larger change. In 2022 the value creator educates "instead of" memes, drama or commentary; in 2023 memes are acceptable as one pillar among others; later in 2023 Koe contrasts the synthesizer with "general influencers who attract the masses with looks rather than depth" (below); in 2024 he says "a creator, not an influencer"; and in 2025 he describes a value creator who knows that "if they don't capture attention, that meaning won't be transmitted," and recommends merging attention-capturing content with meaningful content. The direction is from opposing education to entertainment to integrating the mechanics of attention in the service of meaning. What is rejected throughout is attention without meaning, not attention as such, which agrees with the defense of catching attention in Section 18.3. Chapter 26 treats the levels of content involved.

#### The value creator graphic

In the 2022 video Koe also shows a graphic of the value creator, which the 2024 compilation reproduces. The visual layout is not recoverable beyond the order of its components, but the transcript gives all four stages and the skills each requires.

|---|---|---|

| 2. Creating your unique perspective | — | Critical thinking, open-mindedness, self-improvement |

At stage two he promotes his free challenge "Seven Days to Genius Ideas" (described in Chapter 16). At stage three he notes that the skills let one understand how to structure a persuasive message. His advice is to screenshot the graphic and research those skills generally, to get the big picture of what they are and how to do them well.

The graphic is a map of this book. Stage one is Part VI's learning and reading (Chapters 14–16). Stage two is the thinking of Chapter 17 and the perspective of this chapter. Stage three is the writing of Part VIII and the value and persuasion of Part XI. Stage four is the audience and distribution of Part IX. The sequence also explains why perspective comes before value: interests alone do not help anyone, and they become value only after they have been processed into a perspective that can be applied to another person's goal.

#### The synthesizer as a digital Renaissance man

At the end of 2023, in "The Future Of Work Is Play," Koe gives the synthesizer its most expansive description. A synthesizer is "a digital Renaissance man," a constant self-educator who acquires the knowledge and skill to actualize their goals and lets self-created goals, not those assigned by society, be their "interest compass." Synthesizers pursue their goals, collect ideas to achieve them creatively, and distribute a valuable message to readers. They are "decentralized media companies." He contrasts them with "general influencers who attract the masses with looks rather than depth for the sake of reinforcing their ego," and who "may be unconsciously leading society toward a dead end." "The world needs more synthesizers," he says, and he sees it as one of the main career paths of the future: having one's own distribution, being one's own media company, attracting attention to one's work and selling it as an individual. Personal brands, creators and synthesizers are "the same thing but different nuance and impact." At the time, his school Cortex University promised to make students "a synthesizer in 90 days," a "synthesizer of experience."

**Source:** The Future Of Work Is Play (How To Create A Digital Career).md

The phrase "interest compass" ties this version to the first stage of the graphic and to the goal-directed learning of Part VI: interests are not random hobbies but directions set by self-chosen goals, and they determine what the synthesizer collects. The "digital Renaissance man" anticipates Chapter 20, where Koe speaks of a second Renaissance favoring the polymath.

#### The value creator in later formulations

Koe's definition of the value creator kept accumulating layers. They are best presented together, because each adds something without cancelling the previous ones.

In November 2022, referring back to "The Rise Of The Value Creator": "a value creator's job is to explore the unknown and make new discoveries by researching the crevice of reality that they feel the most compelled to." If one is researching fitness, one researches that little domain, and one's job is to connect all the dots by pushing the boundaries of what one thinks one knows.

**Source:** The Matrix Is Real (How To Break Free With Your Mind).md

In January 2023, describing how he arrived at the idea of the niche of one, Koe states the job of the creator as a process: "that's what you do as a creator: you take problems and you think about them, you contemplate, you research, you write your own stuff, you pull from your direct experience," and then you deliver a solution. The niche-of-one idea itself, he says, was the result of doing this with a problem that kept recurring for him. The order matters: the creator's work begins with a problem, not with a topic, which is the problem-first thinking of Chapter 17.

**Source:** The Most Profitable Niche Is You (Create Your Niche Of One).md

In October 2023: "A value creator is someone who has intention behind their inputs and outputs." Such a person treats life like a science project, in five steps that, he notes, mirror the chapters of his book: (1) identify problems in their life; (2) educate themselves with the internet's infinite resources; (3) test the solution on themselves; (4) distribute their experiences through writing; (5) package the most helpful, streamlined solutions as a product or service, which is how they make a living by living with purpose. He adds two formulas: "becoming a creator is the cure for overconsumption," and "writing is the vessel for distributing value."

**Source:** If Your Life Sucks, Here's How To Reprogram Your Mind.md

The phrase "intention behind inputs and outputs" connects the value creator to Chapter 11 and Chapter 16: the consumer takes in without intention and produces nothing, while the value creator chooses inputs for a problem and turns them into outputs for others. The five steps are the self-experimentation of Chapter 15 extended into a business.

In December 2023, in the "Build Teach Earn" video, Koe gives a criterion: "Not only does teaching build authority, but it turns you into a value creator. If value equals positive behavior change, then most creators aren't valuable." When one starts "a micro education business… disguised as a content creator," one sets oneself up for hyper-profitability. The definition "value equals positive behavior change" (developed in Chapter 31) is demanding: content that entertains or informs without changing anyone's behavior does not count as value in this sense, and teaching is the activity most directly aimed at changing behavior.

**Source:** How Intelligent Creators Will Make Money In 2024 (The Build Teach Earn Method).md

In May 2024: "Today's vessel for beginning creatives is to become a creator, not an influencer; a creator, a value creator to be exact." "A value creator is someone who becomes a **fountainhead of value** based on the interests you love by making them interesting to other people." "You are to become the source of your interest to attract an audience to them." And: "value creators are those who dedicate themselves to their interests, take it on as their life's work to explore them and distill the information into education; this is the style of one-person business that I teach." The image of the fountainhead makes the creator the source from which value flows on a particular subject, and it places the creative work in a specific act: making one's interests interesting to others, which requires perspective (Section 18.3) and articulation (Section 18.6).

**Source:** How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md

In April 2026, in "I'm begging you to start writing essays," the value creator acquires a civilizational role. The world doesn't need more rage-bait posters, mainstream news, people competing to be the most productive, or the next billion-dollar AI company; it needs "ordinary people who make sense of their own minds and document them in public." He calls them value creators, distinct from the typical influencer or personal brand: a person who chooses a positive trajectory for their life, cares deeply about the interests and skills that move the needle toward it, and passes their journey down, from their point of view, to others who can relate and benefit. He considers the path future-proof and deeply meaningful. Such a person isn't making a tangible product or holding "a real job," but causes "a **greater cascade**": they provide the root, the information that influences identity, which influences behavior, which influences the flourishing or destruction of civilization. "Even building rockets isn't that impactful." He says he "stumbled upon" this path rather than chose it, and found it meaningful.

**Source:** I'm begging you to start writing essays (even if you hate writing).md

The causal chain of the greater cascade (information → identity → behavior → civilization) is the model of Part II read from the producer's side. If identities are built from the information a person absorbs (Chapter 3), then those who produce information are shaping identities, and through them behavior. The claim that this is more impactful than building rockets is an opinion, stated for emphasis, but it follows from the premise.

#### The synthesizer of truth: second-tier thinking

In July 2025, in "This mental model will make you think like a strategic genius," the word "synthesizer" reappears in a different register. The video presents stages of development adapted from Ken Wilber's work and from Spiral Dynamics (Part XIV develops them). The highest grouping, which Koe calls "second tier," is described by a capacity rather than a career. The second-tier person "can look here… here… and here," not isolated to one stage, which "opens up a world of complexity, systems thinking and awareness." "It's less about a black and white 'I'm right,' and it's more about a nuanced: the best solution comes from a synthesis of all. So this guy is a **synthesizer of truth**. That's what we should all become." He likes the name: "Sounds like a very kingly title."

**Source:** This mental model will make you think like a strategic genius.md

**Complementary context:** In Spiral Dynamics, developed by Don Beck and Christopher Cowan from Clare Graves's research, "first tier" value systems each regard themselves as the correct one, while "second tier" systems can see the partial validity of all the previous ones. Koe uses the tier vocabulary as a basis for his own term.

The second-tier thinker knows that authority, merit and equality (the values Koe associates with earlier stages) are all important and must all be taken into account. "Most people can't hold those contradictions in their minds long enough for them to be reconciled." Genius thinkers also understand that these "are stages of development… not static identities." The phrase "synthesizer of truth" should be kept distinct from the career "synthesizer." The career synthesizer integrates ideas from many fields into content; the synthesizer of truth integrates the partial truths of many worldviews into a judgment. The second is the cognitive capacity on which the first depends at its best.

In the same video Koe gives an example of how this works. "Second tier thinkers often act as translators between the stages… They can speak the language of all of them." Faced with a business problem, they "put on the cap of the CEO" and solve it better than a premodern "Bible thumper" who would say "I trust God to take this business and do great things for me" and "get nowhere." Faced with a relationship problem, "mansplaining about female psychology probably isn't going to help very much. Or telling them that their hormones are all messed up": the modern scientific approach fails. The second-tier thinker "can set aside a lot of what you know to be true about science and rationality and go more of the irrational empathetic route. It all depends on the context." The **translators between the stages** choose the perspective that the context requires, rather than applying one perspective everywhere. The connection with Section 18.3's "translator of ideas" is a matter of family resemblance: both translate, one between sources and one's own worldview, the other between worldviews.

**Source:** This mental model will make you think like a strategic genius.md

#### The strategist stage and the capacity to hold paradox

In August 2026, in "The Art of Strategic Thinking," Koe returns to the same capacity under a new name, the **strategist** stage, which he adapts from ego development theory and Spiral Dynamics. He lists its qualities:

| Quality | Koe's description |
|---|---|
| 1. Systems thinking | Strategists "see competing systems and perspectives simultaneously, while achievers, who are the self-helpers or the businessmen, operate within a single system of success and achievement" |
| 2. Comfortable with paradox | They "hold contradictions like being both pro and anti-AI, rather than resolving them into clear or false answers" |
| 3. Process over outcome | "They care about how things happen and the principles at play, not just the results" |
| 4. Aware of constraints | "They see how their assumptions and frameworks shape what they perceive" |
| 5. Meta-awareness | They "can observe their own meaning-making and not just deploy expertise or navigate social dynamics" |
| 6. Principled flexibility | "Strong values but adapt methods fluidly," as opposed to rigid rules or social conformity |
| 7. Constructions seen as choices | "Strategists see their own constructions as choices: their identity, their values, their worldview" |

**Source:** The Art of Strategic Thinking (How to Become Wildly Successful at Anything You Do).md

**Complementary context:** In Susanne Cook-Greuter's model of ego development, the "Strategist" (sometimes called "Autonomous") is a late post-conventional stage following the "Individualist," characterized by systems thinking and the ability to hold multiple frames. Koe's list adapts the stage's characteristics in his own words.

Koe calls the ability to hold paradox the most important trait. "The ability to hold paradox, or to hold multiple frameworks or perspectives… not engaging in black and white thinking, which is the lowest form of intelligence. And this is one massive trap of social media. Every time you log on, that's all you see, and you react to it, and you get addicted to it without even knowing it, and you slowly degrade your mental capacity to hold paradox." The solution is to "become a **curator**. You need to carefully protect your mind and curate your own sources of information." In the video this advice leads into a promotion of a reader application (Eden) with a method attached: save and highlight sources, organize them in boards, use a feed of top-performing posts to save, reverse-engineer and synthesize into one's own work, "building your garden for ideas." Setting aside the product, the method is the commonplace book of Chapter 16 placed in service of the capacity described here.

**Source:** The Art of Strategic Thinking (How to Become Wildly Successful at Anything You Do).md

The link between paradox and creativity is direct. Section 18.2 defined creativity as connecting what was not connected; Section 18.3 located originality in combining disciplines. Both require the ability to keep two ideas in the mind without immediately choosing one and discarding the other. Black-and-white thinking does exactly that discarding. The warning about social media is therefore a warning about creativity as well: a feed that trains the reflex to react to binary positions erodes the capacity on which synthesis depends. The prescription to "become a curator" leads directly into Section 18.5, where curation becomes taste.

#### Musashi's nine precepts and the problem of "nothing useless"

In the same 2026 video Koe presents nine precepts from Miyamoto Musashi's *The Book of Five Rings*, "nine precepts or principles every strategist should follow":

1. Don't think dishonestly.
2. Training is the way itself.
3. Get acquainted with every art.
4. Know the ways of all professions.
5. Understand gain and loss in worldly dealings.
6. Develop intuitive judgment about everything.
7. Perceive what can't be seen.
8. Attend even to small things.
9. Do nothing useless.

**Source:** The Art of Strategic Thinking (How to Become Wildly Successful at Anything You Do).md

**Complementary context:** Miyamoto Musashi (c. 1584–1645) was a Japanese swordsman and strategist; *The Book of Five Rings* (*Go Rin no Sho*) is his treatise on strategy, written near the end of his life. The wording of the precepts above follows Koe's paraphrase as it appears in the transcript, not a particular translation.

Koe says these precepts "define what this strategist or master strategist looks like on the outside," while the stage qualities above describe the inside. The video had promised "nine traits of a master strategist," and it does not make explicit whether the nine traits are Musashi's precepts or something else; the seven stage qualities are not nine. The mapping between the two lists is ambiguous in the source. Several precepts fit this chapter closely: "get acquainted with every art" and "know the ways of all professions" are the synthesizer's breadth; "perceive what can't be seen" is close to "noticing the unnoticed"; "attend even to small things" is close to the details in which taste appears (Section 18.5). Chapter 15 treats Musashi's related idea of going so deep into one discipline that one discovers what is universal.

The ninth precept creates a tension with Koe's own account of creativity five months earlier. In the March 2026 video on lost creative genius, he says that if your life isn't centered around efficiency and optimization, everyone thinks you're useless — "in other words, if you aren't a robot, you're useless. But that's exactly what creativity demands. It demands useless wandering, true boredom, or creating space for the right idea to emerge," and "people who schedule every hour of their day don't stumble onto anything." In August 2026 he presents "do nothing useless" as a principle for strategists and, in a related passage, as an operating rule for a year. He does not reconcile the two. A reading that the corpus allows is that they operate on different planes: what is "useless" by the standard of productivity (wandering, boredom) may be exactly what is useful by the standard of creative strategy, so that "do nothing useless" excludes the trivial and the reactive, not the unstructured time in which ideas arrive. But that is an interpretation; the source leaves the two statements side by side. Chapter 11 develops the case for boredom and wandering in its treatment of the narrowers of the mind.

**Source:** If you've lost your creative genius, watch this.md; The Art of Strategic Thinking (How to Become Wildly Successful at Anything You Do).md

### 18.5 Taste

Sections 18.2 to 18.4 described how ideas are collected, combined and turned into perspectives and careers. All of that presupposes a faculty that decides which ideas are worth collecting, which combinations are worth pursuing and which results are worth releasing. Koe calls that faculty **taste**. It is a late theme in the corpus: almost all of the material is from 2025 and 2026, and its rise is tied explicitly to generative AI. When anyone can produce anything, the scarce capacity is no longer producing but choosing.

#### Taste is the new intelligence

In August 2025, in "You Have About 36 Months To Make It," Koe's second step is "become a filter for ideas." Ideas are not the only thing that matters, he says, but they matter more now than before. In a world overflowing with information, where anyone can create anything, "choosing what ideas deserve to be executed deserves the first seat at the table." And the formula: "**Taste is the new intelligence, and curation is the new IQ test.**" The reason is a change in the cost structure of creation: "It's never been easier to create something mediocre." Those pumping out maximum content with ChatGPT and automations will lose or lack longevity, because things change, and they will not understand the principles that keep producing results.

**Source:** You Have About 36 Months To Make It.md

The claim that ideas now matter more than execution reverses a common piece of entrepreneurial advice (that ideas are cheap and execution is everything). Koe's reversal is conditional on the present: when execution is cheap, the selection of what to execute becomes the bottleneck. The phrase "curation is the new IQ test" should be read as a statement about what distinguishes people in this environment, not as a claim that taste replaces intelligence in a psychometric sense.

The formula belongs to a longer series of statements about intelligence in the corpus, which never quite settle. In late 2024 Koe uses two definitions side by side: a pragmatic one (iterating, persisting, getting what you want) and a holistic one (pattern recognition across the whole, zooming out until boundaries dissolve). In an interview at the end of 2024, Devon Eriksen argues that IQ-type intelligence is biological and cannot be increased, and says explicitly that Koe disagrees because he defines intelligence "more broadly"; both agree that agency, not intelligence, is the bottleneck. In 2025 Koe adopts a pragmatic definition he takes from Naval Ravikant; in August 2025 he says "taste is the new intelligence"; at the end of 2025 he lists intelligence as one of three ingredients of success (with agency and opportunity) and says that with low intelligence one will never fully benefit. These are layers rather than replacements, and the tension between a fixed, narrow intelligence and a broad, expandable one is not resolved in the source. "Taste is the new intelligence" sits most comfortably with the broad definition: whatever else intelligence is, in Koe's 2025 view the form of it that now differentiates people is discernment.

#### The mind as a signal-to-noise filter

In the same video Koe explains what taste requires. "Your mind must become a finely tuned **signal to noise filter**." The mind interprets reality based on identity and worldview, values and beliefs, "the code you live by," one's level of development, and the vision one is actualizing. "Your brain releases dopamine when information is useful toward your main and overarching goals." Therefore, to develop taste, one needs a cultivated point of view of one's own — one's own philosophy and vision — because that is what frames the mind to notice the unique ideas worth putting into the world under one's philosophy and brand.

**Source:** You Have About 36 Months To Make It.md

This passage is the bridge between taste and the rest of the book. The filter is not a separate faculty added to the mind; it is the mind's ordinary way of perceiving, shaped by everything Parts II and IV built: identity, values, development and vision. Chapter 6 described how the goal acts as a lens, and how attention and dopamine respond to what serves the goal. Here the same mechanism becomes the basis of taste. A person without a vision has no stable filter, so everything and nothing seems important; a person with a clear vision and philosophy notices what serves it. Taste, on this account, is not mainly aesthetic preference; it is the perceptual consequence of having a direction. The claim about dopamine and usefulness toward goals is Koe's framing of the neuroscience he uses elsewhere (Chapter 11); he presents it as a mechanism, not as a cited finding.

#### Taste as discernment: the infinite library and the infinite monkeys

In February 2026, in "The Future Of Work (& The New High-Income Skill Stack)," Koe defines taste as "the skill of discernment" and justifies it with two thought experiments, which he uses without naming their origins. The first is the **infinite library**: a library containing every possible permutation of words would be meaningless and worthless, with "no opinion worth looking at." The second is the **infinite monkeys**: infinite monkeys typing for a billion years eventually produce Shakespeare, but what is it worth? It took a billion years, and the monkeys don't know what they wrote; "it doesn't mean anything." The insight he draws from both: "**curation matters more than creation**" — selecting what matters and sharing it with another person.

**Source:** The Future Of Work (& The New High-Income Skill Stack).md

**Complementary context:** The infinite library recalls Jorge Luis Borges's story "The Library of Babel" (1941), in which a library containing every possible book is useless because the meaningful books cannot be found among the meaningless ones. The infinite monkey theorem is a classical illustration from probability theory, associated with Émile Borel, that a random process given unlimited time will eventually produce any given text.

The two experiments make the same point from opposite sides. In the library, everything already exists and nothing has value, because no one has selected. In the monkey case, a masterpiece is produced and has no meaning, because the producer does not know what it produced. Value appears only when someone who understands selects and shares. The relevance to AI is obvious, though Koe lets the reader draw it: a model that can generate unlimited text is closer to the library and the monkeys than to an author, and the human contribution is selection with understanding.

How is taste developed? Koe's answer in the same passage is: by building. Create, curate, put things out and see whether they work; make decisions (agency), see what works and what doesn't, iterate. Taste is not acquired by consuming good work; it is acquired by making work and judging the results against reality.

#### Taste as knowing what is broken

In June 2026, in "How To Become So Valuable AI Can Never Replace You," Koe adds a definition from the side of the maker. "Taste represents the ability to know what is broken and not be able to pull yourself away from it. It's the ability to spot the chink in the armor and polish it up." It is not only knowing what good looks like; to create good work, one must know what isn't right and make it right — "that is your edge."

**Source:** How To Become So Valuable AI Can Never Replace You.md

The definitions accumulate without contradicting each other: taste as a standard that exceeds one's own work (2025, below), as discernment and curation (February 2026), as knowing what is broken and being unable to leave it alone (June 2026), and as "the experience to know what's worth putting out into the world" (June 2026, below). The 2026 formulation adds a motivational element. Taste is not only a judgment; it is a kind of discomfort that does not let go until the flaw is fixed. That compulsion is what turns a judgment into improvement.

#### The taste gap: Ira Glass

In February 2025, in "How To Thrive With Multiple Interests," Koe introduces taste as the second of three differentiators (below) with a quotation he says he "resonated hard with," from Ira Glass. People who do creative work get into it because they have good taste, but for the first couple of years what they make "isn't so good." It has ambition, but their taste, "the thing that got you into the game," is good enough to tell them that their work is a disappointment. "A lot of people never get past that phase… they quit." Everyone who does interesting creative work went through years of this. "The most important possible thing you could do is do a lot of work. Put yourself on a deadline so that every week or every month you know you're going to finish one story, because it's only by actually going through a volume of work that you're actually going to catch up and close that gap." It takes a while, it is normal, and "you just have to fight your way through that." Koe calls it a quote "you store forever and you come back to."

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

**Complementary context:** Ira Glass is the host and creator of the American public radio program *This American Life*. The passage Koe quotes comes from a widely circulated interview about beginners and creative work, often referred to as "the taste gap" or "the gap."

The idea is from Glass, and Koe uses it as he finds it. Its place in his system is clear, though. The taste gap explains why taste can be discouraging: it arrives before skill, so for a period it serves mainly to show the creator how far their work falls short. The prescription (volume under a deadline) fits Koe's own advice about progressive practice (Chapter 15) and his habit of daily writing (Section 18.6). It also explains why taste is developed by building, as the 2026 passage said: the gap closes not by refining taste but by raising skill to meet it, and that only happens through a volume of finished work.

#### How taste is blunted and how it is cultivated

In the same 2025 video Koe argues that "most people had their taste blunted by schools and jobs." People with multiple interests have "a major advantage," because they did not let school prevent them from studying beyond what was defined; they love to explore the unknown. But "you need to practice it." His own story is the example. When he started using Photoshop, he had good taste and could see what others were creating, but when he tried, "it just didn't work, it didn't look that good"; he would post it anyway, somewhat confident. Over time, "as you gradually learn these little tiny things, the details is where things start to stand out. That's when you start to really cultivate it."

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

Two points follow. Taste is an innate sensitivity that can be dulled by environments that reward conformity (the critique of school and employment in Chapter 2), and it can also be cultivated, but only through practice. And it shows in the details: the difference between work that looks "not that good" and work that stands out is often a collection of small things, each learned separately. This is the Ira Glass gap seen from inside one person's experience: good taste, disappointing work, and a slow closing through attention to detail.

#### A thousand people, one book

In the same video Koe offers a thought experiment that shows where taste enters even when AI does the production. Imagine writing a book called *How to Focus* with AI. He describes the process in detail. Open Claude (he names Claude Sonnet 3.5, the model current at the time); give it a purpose as a ghostwriter; give it your writing style, tone and voice; give it the target audience; have it create a table of contents based on the audience's pain points; for each chapter, include a relevant story, a main insight, actionable steps and a novel concept (like time blocking). Study other books for patterns — "this one has a metaphor" — to create "building blocks" the AI can use for a compelling narrative. Give it ideas you have previously written about focus. Create and refine the outline chapter by chapter. Finish and refine to your liking.

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

Then the turn: "Now take 1,000 different people and have them write a book on the same topic: every single final output will be different." "One book can be written a thousand different ways, and 1,000 different people can interpret that book in 1,000 different ways." The same applies to software or design. "Do you see where taste is starting to come into play?"

The example is instructive because nearly every step of the process involves a choice: which style, which audience, which pain points, which stories, which patterns to borrow from other books, which earlier ideas to include, what to refine and when to stop. The AI executes; the person decides. Even with an identical procedure, the decisions differ, and so do the books. Taste is the sum of those decisions. The argument also answers a common fear about AI and creative work: if a procedure can be followed by anyone, does it erase the differences between people? Koe's answer is that the procedure only moves the differences from execution to selection.

#### Generalism, taste and agency: the three differentiators

The 2025 video is organized around what Koe calls "**the three differentiators** right now, which is agency, taste and generalism" (later recapped in the order "generalism, taste, and agency"). After presenting them, he explains how to turn multiple interests into a path: a career, an income, a way to sustain one's interests and share them with others.

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

The relation between generalism and taste is the key point of the video. "Multiple interests are merged with taste": anyone can create anything; a person has multiple interests and goals, sees their life going many ways, and can test many of them "because technology has made doing things so accessible. You can embrace your nature as a generalist and tie things together with taste." "It's your multiple interests that come together to create that thing that is unique." In this formulation, generalism supplies the material (many interests, many fields of awareness, many dots), agency supplies the action (actually testing, building and shipping), and taste supplies the binding (deciding how the interests come together into a coherent thing). Without taste, a generalist produces a scattered collection; without generalism, taste has little to choose from; without agency, neither produces anything. Chapter 20 treats generalism and Chapter 35 agency; this chapter's concern is the middle term.

#### The five ingredients and the difference between assets and hits

In June 2026, in "How To Become So Valuable AI Can Never Replace You," Koe presents what he calls the **five ingredients of success**, which allow a person to do meaningful work even if, hypothetically, all jobs were replaced:

| Ingredient | Koe's definition |
|---|---|
| 1. Agency | "The ability to just do things without permission," to see an opportunity and act when nobody asked |
| 2. Taste | "The experience to know what's worth putting out into the world" |
| 3. Persuasion | The skill of getting people to care about what you do — "not to be confused with manipulation" |
| 4. Persistence | "The understanding that mistakes do not equal death and that they are necessary" |
| 5. Iteration | "The process of error correcting toward a goal based on feedback": if it doesn't work, learn and pivot until it does |

**Source:** How To Become So Valuable AI Can Never Replace You.md

The ingredients must be combined and used together. Koe reduces them to two skills: "the ability to figure it out and the experience to know what needs to be done." The first summarizes agency, persistence and iteration; the second summarizes taste and, in part, persuasion. Taste appears here with a definition that stresses experience: it is not a gift but the accumulated knowledge of what works.

To explain why these matter, Koe plays a clip from the David Senra podcast. He says he never watched the full podcast, and the speaker is not named in the transcript. The guest says that AI is great at asset creation, but hit creation isn't asset creation: "**Asset creation is a necessary but insufficient condition for hit creation.**" Anyone could make a video game five years ago; the technology is commoditized; thousands of mobile games come out every year, and there are from zero to five hits, which, the speaker says, are made by his team. Koe's interpretation: anyone can build or write anything, so the barrier to entrepreneurship, which he calls "the antidote to wage slavery," is lower than ever — "but that doesn't matter."

**Source:** How To Become So Valuable AI Can Never Replace You.md

**Complementary context:** David Senra hosts the podcast *Founders*, in which he studies biographies of entrepreneurs, and has also interviewed founders on other shows. Since the speaker in the clip is unnamed in the transcript, the quotation is attributed here only to "a guest" as Koe presents it.

The distinction between assets and hits is the economic version of the infinite monkeys. Producing assets (a game, a book, a video, an app) has become cheap; producing hits (the few that matter to many people) has not. The ingredients that make the difference are the human ones: knowing what is worth making, making people care, persisting and iterating.

The five ingredients are the latest step in a long evolution of what Koe calls the skill stack. From 2023 to 2024 his lists were of market skills: the "$1 million skill stack" (evergreen skills of writing, speaking, marketing and sales, plus interests, experience and the internet), the "future-proof skill stack" (marketing and sales, writing and thinking, entrepreneurship, technical know-how). In 2025 he spoke of three superpowers (learning, persuasion, execution). In February 2026 he proposed a post-AI hierarchy — agency, taste, perspective, persuasion, technical know-how — "more of a skill hierarchy than a list of skills." In June 2026 the five ingredients replace perspective and technical know-how with persistence and iteration. The direction of change is from external, career-oriented skills to traits and judgment that are not tied to any career; Koe's reasons are that AI absorbs the technical layer, which changes every few years, while human principles persist. Taste enters the list in 2026 and stays. In the same June 2026 video, agency, which he had called "the most important skill" in late 2025 and "the meta-skill" in early 2026, becomes one ingredient among five; he calls "high agency" a tech-bro buzzword and says "it's not the only one thing." He does not explain the demotion beyond noting, elsewhere, that his "most important skill" statements should be read metaphorically. Chapter 36 treats the skill stack in the context of the AI economy.

#### Becoming a scarce resource

The final group of passages in this section explains why taste and perspective become more valuable, not less, as AI spreads.

The first is an argument about commodities. In the February 2026 video Koe takes up the "dead internet" objection: if AI floods the internet, doesn't content become worthless? "Yes, it becomes a commodity." But that increases what can be paid for something human — not necessarily something made without AI, since everyone will work with AI assistance, but "a human perspective, a human point of view, the curation, the taste." AI can produce five billion beautiful words, "but which ones are you going to select to stick around?"

**Source:** The Future Of Work (& The New High-Income Skill Stack).md

The second is a framework, presented in the same video: **five things AI cannot replicate**.

| Element | Koe's explanation | Key phrase |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Perspective | AI can analyze what it is like to be, for example, a first-generation immigrant building a creative career, but cannot be one; perspective is shaped by beliefs, experiences, "your wounds, your wins"; AI has none because it has no stakes | "AI can think about your perspective but it cannot think from it" |
| 2. Energy signature | The human ability to select what to focus on and why it matters to you; two people writing on the same topic feel completely different | "AI can mimic the style but it cannot mimic care" |
| 3. Sensemaking | AI processes information but cannot decide what it means, frame it, prioritize it, or say "this matters and this doesn't"; sensemaking requires stakes | "AI does not die" |
| 4. Trajectory | A person has a past, present and future, a story arc; AI has no trajectory, no mortality and no "temporal compression" | — |
| 5. Evolving taste | When he tells AI to write like him and reads the result, he no longer wants to write about that; his taste has instantly changed | — |

**Source:** The Future Of Work (& The New High-Income Skill Stack).md

Two elements need commentary. On trajectory, Koe adds a reflection on mortality: even if we solved immortality, this moment would still pass and could not be redone, so he does not think immortality would make life meaningless; when one creates, one creates from right now, and then it is gone. The point is that the human creator is situated in time, and that situation is part of what they offer. On evolving taste, his example is almost comic: if an AI imitated him perfectly, the imitation would change his taste, and an AI that tried to follow that change would loop infinitely "and then it'd explode." The serious point is that taste is not a fixed target that can be copied; one grows, changes, looks back and thinks one's earlier work is bad. That movement is itself part of what a human creator is.

In the same passage, Koe mentions a related concept he had published as a Substack prompt: the **intellectual signature**, "what ideas are the most important to you that you can pull together, connect, synthesize to create this unique style of content." It is the intellectual counterpart of the energy signature and is treated in Section 18.6.

The third passage is from a February 2025 video on AI and the creator economy. "The real kicker: AI lacks a coherent vision and philosophy." People don't follow creators for one piece of content but for "their **body of coherent work**." Viewers find value in his videos, he says, not because each is the first they have watched but because of "the entire philosophy I've strung together over the past 3 to 4 years," helping creative individuals make sense of their place in the world; that value compounds the more one watches. Good writers and creators won't be replaced; they will become more powerful and dynamic, because AI lets one person create more, faster, inside a company or not, "but only if they value agency and self-development." He recalls a tweet of his: "the ultimate goal is getting paid to be yourself."

**Source:** The creator economy is dying thanks to AI.md

The body of coherent work is the cumulative product of taste and perspective over time. A single piece can be imitated; a body of work held together by a philosophy cannot, because the coherence comes from one person's vision applied repeatedly. Section 18.6 shows that the same body of work is also what makes articulation possible.

The fourth group of passages states the scarcity thesis directly. In March 2026: "If you're starting a business or you're running a business… your most valuable asset is your ability to think… the ability to be creative." "In today's world, your creativity is the most scarce resource. Anyone can build anything today. Anyone can think anything. Anyone can write anything. The people who will win in business, writing, art, and just general quality of life, as always, will be those who can take the most creative path, the path that nobody else considered to take."

**Source:** If you've lost your creative genius, watch this.md

In December 2024, in "A Daily Routine To Work Less, Earn More & Enjoy Life," Koe gives the economic form: "The quality of your work depends on the quality of your ideas; therefore **the future of productivity is creativity**." The economy "continues to bias creators and founders who leverage AI and technology." Anyone can type ChatGPT into a browser and get "a book, newsletter, marketing asset, piece of art, code for a to-do app or legal document, and yet there aren't a million bestseller-quality books or production-level software. Work has become easier, but the fact that most people lack agency remains. If people weren't already motivated to build their own thing in the past 10 years, they're still not going to be, and they may be taken advantage of by those who are." "If productivity is through the roof… the thing that matters more than ever is creativity, because productivity is nothing without creativity." He adds that the video is "for those who want leverage," not for those set on traditional work.

**Source:** A Daily Routine To Work Less, Earn More & Enjoy Life.md

This argument closes a loop opened in Section 18.1. There, the modern world favored productivity over creativity and turned people into "robotic and efficient machines." Here, AI has made productivity nearly free, so the old priority inverts: what was neglected becomes what is scarce. The absence of "a million bestsellers" despite universal access to generation is the same observation as the hit-and-asset distinction, stated in the publishing domain.

Finally, in May 2025, in "The Deep Work Routine That Changed My Life," Koe states the most provocative consequence: "Understand that as a creative and heading into the future of work, the people who work the least will end up on top, because your story, synthesis of ideas, and experience are the only moat when AI can do almost everything." (The transcript reads "mode"; the context indicates "moat.") The implication is that time spent outside work — living, walking, reading, experiencing — is where the competitive advantage is generated, because that is where story, synthesis and experience accumulate. The claim is an opinion about the future, framed as such; it is consistent with Chapter 12's 4-Hour Workday and with the "useless wandering" passage discussed in Section 18.4, and it is in visible tension with the productivity culture Koe criticizes.

**Source:** The Deep Work Routine That Changed My Life.md

The section's argument can be put in a sequence. When production is cheap, selection is scarce. Selection depends on a filter, and the filter is shaped by identity, values and vision. That filter, cultivated through building and through attention to detail, is taste; it runs ahead of skill for years (the gap) and closes only through volume. Taste binds multiple interests into a unique result, and together with agency, persuasion, persistence and iteration it distinguishes the rare hits from the abundant assets. What AI cannot replicate — perspective, care, sensemaking with stakes, a trajectory in time, a taste that keeps evolving, a coherent body of work — is what remains valuable.

### 18.6 Articulation

The last capacity in the sequence is the ability to say clearly what one has understood. A creator may have collected the right material, connected it into a novel perspective and selected well, and still fail to transmit any of it if they cannot put it into words. Koe treats **articulation** as a word with a sense of his own: organizing one's thinking and putting it into words, so that the structure of the expression carries the idea. This section follows his account of articulation from his own early frustration, through the claim that simplifying complex topics is the great skill of the age, to the concrete method he proposes in 2025: building an "inner album of greatest hits" on top of a body of work.

#### Envying the articulate

Koe tells the story of his relationship to articulation several times, from 2023 to 2026, with consistent content. In a September 2023 video on reprogramming the mind and building intelligence, he says that when he was young he "always envied those that were more intelligent and articulate" than him. He wondered how they organized their thoughts and put them into words that affected him through books, videos and lectures; how they said what he had been thinking for years but "couldn't string into words"; and how they held high-level conversations, for example on podcasts, where the gap in knowledge and understanding was so large that he did not really understand what they were saying, yet he knew "that it means something," that it was "deeper or higher" than his current level. He always wanted to reach that point. He names the qualities he admired: "the poeticism of Watts, the pithy advice of Seneca, the crypticism of Nietzsche, the elegance of Peterson." There was "a gravity to their words" that stayed at the back of his mind, and he believes the wish to become intelligent influenced all the actions he took up to the present and led him to where he is.

**Source:** How To Reprogram Your Mind & Build Your Intelligence.md

In a July 2025 video he names his models again, each with a quality: "I've always admired the articulation of Jordan Peterson, regardless of what people think of him… the magic behind Alan Watts and the clarity of Daniel Schmachtenberger." Articulation, magic, clarity: three distinct qualities that, together, describe what he wanted his own expression to have.

**Source:** This mental model will make you think like a strategic genius.md

**Complementary context:** Jordan Peterson is a Canadian clinical psychologist known for long-form lectures on psychology, mythology and meaning; Alan Watts (1915–1973) was a British writer and speaker who popularized Eastern philosophy in the West; Daniel Schmachtenberger is a researcher and speaker on civilizational risk and collective sensemaking. Seneca (c. 4 BC–AD 65) was a Roman Stoic philosopher known for his letters; Friedrich Nietzsche (1844–1900) a German philosopher known for his aphoristic and often enigmatic style.

The story has a function in the system. It locates the origin of Koe's career in a desire (to become able to say what he thought), which he later describes as having guided his choices. The admired thinkers are models in the sense of intelligent imitation (Chapter 1): he does not want to repeat their ideas but to reach their capacity to express ideas.

#### Why he could not: a jumbled mess of half-baked ideas

Two later versions of the story add the diagnosis. In January 2025, at the opening of the video on original thinking: "When I was a teenager I would watch Jordan Peterson lectures and Alan Watts lectures, and I would just listen to them in awe at the way that they articulated their points. Why couldn't I do the same? Was I not smart enough? Was I not old enough yet?" No matter how much information he consumed, "it never seemed to help. I had all of the thoughts in my head, but when it came time to articulate them, it all came out just a jumbled mess of half-baked ideas… after all of the stuttering." When he first started writing, he would stare at the blank page waiting for ideas; when none came, "negative thoughts would start to flood in about how I'm not meant to be a writer." His diagnosis: "I didn't have a structure to help frame my thoughts… I had no idea what I was writing or what I was writing about, but I had some idea that I felt I needed to share with the world."

**Source:** You're Not Boring How To Become An Original Thinker (Fast).md

In December 2025, at the opening of "How To Articulate Your Thoughts Intelligently (Talk Like This)," he tells the story once more with a different emphasis. As a youth he was drawn to people who "sounded intelligent" — Watts, Peterson, Schmachtenberger — "who could explain deep ideas in an exciting yet palatable way. Most of the time I didn't understand what they were saying, either because I was too young and dumb or they were just overcomplicating… but they sounded smart and articulate. So I gave them my respect." He believed they were inherently more intelligent. "I was a smart kid in school, but being good at taking tests is a lot different from being good at stringing together coherent thoughts and articulating them to someone else. I felt like I had to memorize entire books' worth of information so that I could recite it on the spot, because I was trained to learn that way." Over a decade later, "without really trying to become articulate, people ask me how I write and speak so well." Over six years, "millions of people have chosen to hit the follow or subscribe button." And then a characteristic self-deflation: "I'm not an entertainer. I'm not that funny. I'm actually quite boring, which I like… I wouldn't even say that my content is eloquent or revolutionary." He attributes his success "to being able to articulate valuable ideas in a way that people are drawn to, and that's not very difficult to do."

**Source:** How To Articulate Your Thoughts Intelligently (Talk Like This).md

Taken together, the three versions identify two causes of inarticulateness and one remedy. The first cause is the absence of a structure to frame thoughts: the ideas were there but had no form to go into. The second is a training in memorization rather than in connecting: school taught him to store and recite, not to string coherent thoughts together. This connects to the critique of schooling in Chapter 2 and to the difference between knowing and understanding in Chapter 17. The remedy, which the next passages describe, was writing. Note also what the story does not claim: Koe does not say he became eloquent or entertaining. His claim is narrower: that he learned to articulate valuable ideas in a way people are drawn to, and that this is learnable.

#### Writing every morning: the meathead and the habit

In July 2026, in "The Writing System That Saved My Brain," Koe gives the remedy its most personal form. He never thought he would become a writer and still doesn't call himself one. "I don't look like a writer. I'm just a big dumb meathead who fell in love with deep ideas." Writing as a simple morning habit altered the direction of his life and mind. As a kid he looked up to very articulate people, Peterson and Watts, and thought his brain didn't work that way, until he started writing and "could actually feel myself getting smarter and more articulate." His entire business was built on one to two hours of writing every morning, "because how do you get customers in today's world? Writing."

**Source:** The Writing System That Saved My Brain (Learn Faster & Think Clearly).md

In December 2022 the same habit appears as an example of a "boring fundamental": "this guy Dan, he writes for 30 minutes every morning." The point there is that a repetitive action that looks boring from the outside is what he calls "mastery over misery" (Chapter 15): repetition until the activity becomes automatic and frees energy for the next level.

**Source:** How To Get Ahead Of 99% Of People (In 6-12 Months).md

The figures for his writing time vary across the corpus, and they are worth stating because the variation is a matter of data, not of principle. In late 2022 he describes 30 minutes each morning; in 2023 a 90-minute block, "2 hours a day," one to two hours, and two hours in the morning within two to three hours of total work; in mid-2023 "30 minutes to an hour," explaining that at first he needed more time because he lacked skill; in 2024 the first two to three hours of the day; in 2025 "two hours of writing in the morning, that's my entire routine"; in 2026 "1–2 hours every morning" and "about 60 minutes a day." His recommendations for beginners are lower (two or three days a week, or blocks of 30 to 60 minutes). The principle is constant: morning writing is the base of both his thinking and his business. The figures move with the period, and the only explanation he gives is growing efficiency.

There is also a small tension in how he names himself. In 2022 he did not know his title ("maybe… brand advisor"); in 2024 he said "I'm not a writer"; in 2025 and 2026 he described himself as a writer and brand adviser; and in July 2026 "I still don't call myself a writer." He does not explain the inconsistency. It may depend on whether he is speaking of his professional identity or of the stereotype of the literary writer; the "big dumb meathead" line suggests the second.

#### Simplifying complex topics: the sense makers

Koe's account of why articulation matters beyond his own career appears first in October 2022, in "The Rise Of The Value Creator": "Simplifying complex topics will be the greatest skill of the next century." We live in an information age: there are so many ideas that people are overwhelmed by what to do and by their options in career and life. People make new discoveries and create new knowledge "left and right" and distill it as internet content; what is needed is people who organize and simplify that information.

**Source:** The Rise Of The Value Creator (A Career Path For Polymaths & Self-Improvers).md

In July 2023, in "The Value Creator," he restates it with a reason: "Simplifying complex topics [is] the greatest skill of the 21st century, because value creators are **sense makers**: your job is to make sense of your life, the world, and bring clarity to the actions that others should take" — those who follow your vision. This comes with time and action, he adds; one won't know whether one's creations are valuable if one never gets eyes on them.

**Source:** The Value Creator (A New Internet Career Path For Intelligent People).md

The sense maker joins the threads of this chapter. Creativity was defined as creating clarity from chaos (Section 18.2); the value creator as someone who explores a crevice of reality and educates (Section 18.4); taste as the filter that selects what matters (Section 18.5). The sense maker is all three seen from the receiver's side: someone who has done the work of turning confusion into clarity and hands the result to people who need it in order to act.

The phrase "the greatest skill" deserves a note. The 2022 version says "of the next century," the 2023 version "of the 21st century." More importantly, simplifying complex topics is only one of several things Koe calls the greatest skill at different moments: writing (2022), "the ability to order consciousness," "decreasing the time between idea and execution," persuasive communication as "the base layer" (2023), persuasion (2024), agency (2025) and "human nature" (2026). He himself says that such statements should be read metaphorically, as a way of "getting the point" across, rather than as rankings. The candidates are compatible: they all describe forms of turning disorder into a clear message that moves people, with writing as the vehicle and persuasion as the function.

#### The written word and sustained attention: Schmachtenberger

In April 2026, in "I'm begging you to start writing essays (even if you hate writing)," Koe builds an argument about articulation on a quotation from Daniel Schmachtenberger; every quotation in that video, he says, is from Schmachtenberger. "The written word as the primary type of media was probably required for democracy to work, because it required the capacity to pay attention to an idea for long enough to understand it." Koe's first point in the video is that "the internet isn't dead, but it's killing us," and his thesis is that social media and AI are "quite literally a threat to civilization," though people see them as a little app. How could doom-scrolling Reels, a person dancing on TikTok, or a 280-character polarizing opinion about Trump on X ruin the world? Because one focuses only on oneself, one's "own little bubble," and doesn't think about the consequences of the small unconscious actions one has picked up; once one zooms out beyond oneself, it is hard to unsee.

**Source:** I'm begging you to start writing essays (even if you hate writing).md

The quotation is Schmachtenberger's, and Koe uses it as a frame. Its relevance to articulation lies in the mechanism it names: the written word requires and trains sustained attention to a single idea, long enough to understand it. Articulation, in this light, is not only a personal advantage; it is the other half of a capacity a society needs. A person who learns to articulate ideas in writing learns to hold an idea long enough to understand it, and offers readers an occasion to do the same. Chapter 21 develops the case for writing as the base skill, and Chapter 11 the case against the formats that erode attention.

#### Articulation more than marketing

A 2023 passage shows that Koe does not confine articulation to writing. In "Value Creation: The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business," he applies his method of value creation to two other situations. In a course curriculum, one should not stop being persuasive once students are inside: capture attention in each module and give steps to overcome the problem presented in each section — "that's how you get them to act." In everyday conversation, start with a problem you are facing and let the conversation carry on — "that's how you articulate your points; this is articulation more than it is just marketing."

**Source:** Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md

The structure is the same in all three settings (content, course, conversation): open with a problem, then follow the path that solves it. This is the problem-first thinking of Chapter 17 and the "pain and process" framework mentioned in Section 18.3, applied to speech. Calling it articulation rather than marketing makes the point that the structure is not a sales trick but the natural form of a clear explanation: people follow an idea when they know what problem it addresses.

#### The inner album of greatest hits

Koe's most developed method for articulation appears in December 2025, in "How To Articulate Your Thoughts Intelligently (Talk Like This)." Before any method of speaking, he says, "you need something to articulate. So first we need to build our **inner album of greatest hits**. If you want to articulate yourself intelligently, you need a pool of 8 to 10 of your biggest ideas that can be connected to almost any topic. Then, when it's time to write or speak in any situation, you have a starting point that you've already thought through hundreds of times before."

**Source:** How To Articulate Your Thoughts Intelligently (Talk Like This).md

He illustrates with his own output. After two books, hundreds of newsletters and YouTube videos, and thousands of social posts, "it's obvious which ideas have led to the most DMs of people telling me that that idea changed their life. It's obvious that I really only have eight to 10 big ideas that illustrate the value that my brand provides." He writes for two hours every morning, and "it usually has something to do with those 8 to 10 big ideas. Those are the ideas people want to hear… that introduce new listeners to who I am." He lists the uses: standing out as a new creator, podcasts without a script, company meetings, sales calls, CVs, arguments, "or… just want to be a more interesting person."

The concept inverts a common assumption about articulate people. They seem to produce brilliant thoughts on the spot; Koe's claim is that they are mostly returning to a small set of ideas they have already thought through many times, and connecting them to whatever topic arises. Articulation is thus mostly preparation. The "greatest hits" metaphor, from music, is apt: an artist's greatest hits are the songs that have been played many times and that audiences recognize, and they are few. The criterion for identifying one's own hits is empirical: which ideas produced the strongest responses ("DMs of people telling me that that idea changed their life"). This connects to the "five huge ideas" of a book in Section 18.4: whether reading or writing, the unit of value is a small number of big ideas.

In the same video he describes how these ideas exist in his practice. "You can think of these big ideas as tweets." His content pillars are the one-person business model, lifestyle design, how to get what you want in life, how to master your mind and, more recently, AI. "For each of those content pillars, I have just a handful of tweets that I've written that hit hard and have been validated by the market. They get a lot of engagement and they're just good ideas." The big ideas, then, are not abstractions held in mind; they are concrete formulations, short enough to fit in a post, whose strength has been tested by audience response. Chapter 24 treats content pillars as part of the content ecosystem.

**Source:** How To Articulate Your Thoughts Intelligently (Talk Like This).md

#### Articulation requires a body of work

The album is not built in a day. In the same video Koe uses Jordan Peterson as an example. "Jordan Peterson, regardless of your opinion, is known for his articulation. It's captivating when you listen to him. Why? If you look at his body of work, it's obvious he has a body of work. If you wish to articulate yourself and you do not have a body of work that is continuing to be refined and continues to grow, then you have a lot of work to do before you can actually be naturally articulate."

**Source:** How To Articulate Your Thoughts Intelligently (Talk Like This).md

The **body of work** here has the same role as the "body of coherent work" in Section 18.5, seen from the creator's side. For the audience, the body of work is what they follow and what gives a single piece its meaning; for the creator, it is what makes natural articulation possible, because it is where ideas have been thought through, written, revised and connected many times. The word "naturally" is important: the articulate person sounds spontaneous because the work was done earlier.

The **intellectual signature**, which Koe mentions in the February 2026 video discussed in Section 18.5, is a related concept: "what ideas are the most important to you that you can pull together, connect, synthesize to create this unique style of content." He says he published a prompt on Substack to help people find theirs, aimed at those on the content creator or "thought leadership" route (he says he hates the term) who like deep ideas and want to share them. The intellectual signature and the inner album describe the same thing from two angles: the album is the set of ideas, the signature is the distinctive way those ideas are connected. Both are the cumulative result of the perspective of Section 18.3 exercised over years.

**Source:** The Future Of Work (& The New High-Income Skill Stack).md

#### When articulation fails: the podcast problem

Koe does not present himself as having solved articulation. In the same December 2025 video he describes his difficulty as a podcast guest. As a beginner, podcasts were conversations with friends who were building their own shows, a way to make connections. Now he is invited to "the big leagues," where hosts spend "thousands to tens of thousands of dollars on the production quality" and "hundreds of thousands of people may be tuning in," which is nerve-wracking. "I'm not the best podcast guest yet. Not by a long shot." His YouTube videos are more articulate, he explains, because "Devon's in control of the editing… I'm in control of the key points… It's kind of premeditated," whereas on podcasts "you're just **riffing**." Listening back, "I'm just kicking myself, because I knew that I could have responded to certain questions better."

**Source:** How To Articulate Your Thoughts Intelligently (Talk Like This).md

He then names the specific obstacle. "That's my biggest mental hurdle. I don't want to sound like I'm repeating myself. So… I avoid saying the things that I've already said, [and] my mind kind of goes blank, and I feel like I have to force something out just to avoid having an awkwardly long pause… I want to somehow have this mind-blowing idea on the spot, when I know that's not how ideas work. Ideas require time to dissect and explore. They have to be a part of you."

The story is the clearest illustration of why the inner album matters. Koe's failure on podcasts comes precisely from refusing to use it: to avoid repeating his big ideas, he tries to produce new ones on the spot, and finds nothing. The lesson he draws is the principle of the whole section: "ideas require time to dissect and explore. They have to be a part of you." Articulation in real time depends on ideas that were developed beforehand, through the slow work of writing and revision. It also returns to the beginning of the chapter. The 2026 definition of creativity located it in an open, relaxed state; the podcast story describes the opposite, a narrow, pressured state in which the mind "goes blank." Pressure to be original on demand works against both creativity and articulation, and the remedy for both is the same: time, a body of work, and ideas that have become part of the person.

### Exercises

1. **Test the "true creative" argument on yourself.** Koe argues that a designer who hates their job is not a true creative, because they do not treat the hated job as a problem to be solved, which would show in at least one hour a day of high-leverage work. Identify the most pressing problem in your own current situation (work, health, a relationship, money). Do you treat it as a problem to be solved creatively, by Koe's test? If not, what has made the routine comfortable enough that your mind does not want to break out? Then evaluate the argument itself: is there a case in which someone could reasonably not treat a hated job as a problem to solve, and still be creative in Koe's sense?

2. **Reconcile the definitions of creativity.** Section 18.1 presents definitions from 2022 to 2026: creating clarity from experience, entering chaos and creating order, achieving any goal with what is available, solving problems toward a goal, and a natural state of "noticing the unnoticed" that is "not creating something from nothing." Write a single account that integrates them, explaining how each would fit into one cycle, and state where the integration fails (for example, the literal conflict about "something from nothing"). Which definition would you use to teach a ten-year-old, and which to advise a professional who fears being replaced by AI? Why?

3. **Build a novel perspective from an unrelated fact.** Following the "acre of fog" example in Section 18.3, take a fact from a field you know little about (physics, biology, history, a sport). Identify the structure it carries, map that structure onto a problem in your own domain, and write the result in no more than three sentences. Then do the brain dump from the same section for your domain (common problems, benefits, goals, roadblocks, personal experiences) and check whether the perspective you produced fits one of the problems you listed. Which step was hardest, and what does that tell you about where your dots are missing?

4. **Audit an idea you think is original.** Choose an idea you hold that feels like your own. Trace it back: list every source, conversation, book or experience you can identify that contributed to it. Using Koe's chain ("original thinking is perception; perception is dependent on identity; identity is dependent on conditioning"), explain to whom the idea would seem original and to whom it would seem "nothing new." Then consider his 2026 description of a "generative" level of thinking that produces "ideas without outside influence." Is there any part of your idea that you cannot trace? Argue for or against the claim that "nobody has original ideas."

5. **Design your value creator map.** Using the value creator graphic in Section 18.4 (genuine interests, unique perspective, value creation, value distribution), fill in each stage for yourself: your two or three genuine interests, the perspective that your problems and goals create on them, the people it could help and with what outcome, and where they would find it. For each stage, identify which of the listed skills you lack. Then apply the 2023 criterion "if value equals positive behavior change, then most creators aren't valuable": what behavior, specifically, would your work change, and how would you know?

6. **Hold a paradox.** Section 18.4 presents two statements by Koe five months apart: creativity "demands useless wandering, true boredom," and Musashi's precept "do nothing useless." Write the strongest case for each, then write a formulation that holds both without reducing either to the other, in the manner of the "strategist" who holds contradictions "like being both pro and anti-AI." Finally, identify one paradox in your own work or life that you have been resolving too quickly into a single answer, and describe what holding it open would require in practice.

7. **Locate your taste gap.** Following the Ira Glass passage and Koe's Photoshop story in Section 18.5, pick a creative skill you care about. Describe, as precisely as you can, what your taste tells you is wrong with your current work (the "chink in the armor"). Is the gap one of skill, of detail, of volume, or of a missing point of view (the "signal to noise filter" that requires a philosophy and a vision)? Design a volume-and-deadline plan to close it. Then question the model: is there a case where the gap is a sign that the taste, not the work, needs to change?

8. **Draft your inner album of greatest hits.** List the eight to ten ideas you have thought about most and could connect to almost any topic (Section 18.6). For each, write a one-line formulation short enough for a post. Mark which ones you have already tested on other people, and how they responded. Then pick a topic you have not prepared for (a question someone might ask you in an interview, a meeting or a podcast) and practice answering it by connecting it to one of your ideas. What did you learn about the difference between "riffing" and articulation, and about which of your ideas are not yet "a part of you"?
