## Chapter 18 — Creativity, Originality and Taste

Chapter 17 described how to think: the difference between stupid thinking and genius thinking, building one's own philosophy, questioning, starting from the problem, the gap between knowing and understanding, and strategy. This chapter asks what that thinking is for. In Koe's system the answer is creation. A mind that has learned to hold ideas open, to question them and to frame them by a problem is a mind that can produce something that did not exist in that form before: a solution, a perspective, a concept, a product, a body of work. Chapters 14 through 16 dealt with the inputs (learning, skill, reading, notes, AI as a tutor); Chapter 17 dealt with the processing; this chapter deals with the output and with the qualities that make an output worth anyone's attention.

The chapter is built in six steps. Section 18.1 starts with what creativity is, which in Koe's corpus is a surprisingly unstable question. He rejects the idea that creativity is a job title or a "creative skill" such as design, and he defines it in several ways over five years: as creating clarity from experience, as achieving any goal with the knowledge and skill available, and, in 2026, as a natural state of consciousness in which one "notices the unnoticed." The same section presents his broader claim that to be human is to be a creator. Section 18.2 describes the process: collecting, connecting and creating with "the dots," deconstructing wholes into parts and rebuilding them like "mental Legos," and the family of metaphors (the DJ, the blacksmith, the idea worker) through which he describes the creator as someone who remixes ideas. Section 18.3 deals with originality: Koe's repeated claim that "nobody has original ideas," the five ways he nonetheless offers to think originally, and the central practical concept of this chapter, the **novel perspective**. Section 18.4 presents the person who does this work for a living: the **synthesizer** and the **value creator**, and, in his 2025–2026 material, the "synthesizer of truth" and the strategist who can hold paradox. Section 18.5 turns to **taste**, which Koe calls "the new intelligence" in a world where AI can produce anything, and Section 18.6 to **articulation**, the ability to say clearly what one has understood, which Koe treats as something built through a body of work rather than a gift.

Three earlier threads carry into this chapter. From Chapter 6 comes the goal as a lens: what the mind notices depends on what it is pursuing, and this will reappear as the explanation of both perspective and taste. From Chapter 5 comes entropy and order in consciousness: Koe's oldest definition of creativity is "bringing order to consciousness to embrace chaos." From Chapter 1 comes intelligent imitation and "create or be created": this chapter returns to imitation and asks when it becomes original. The chapter prepares Part VII, where the conclusion that the creator's perspective is the differentiator becomes "you are the niche," and Part VIII, where these ideas become the craft of writing.

### 18.1 The Creative Nature

#### What "creative" does not mean

Koe's discussion of creativity almost always begins with a negation. In ordinary usage, "a creative" is a person with a certain kind of job or skill: a designer, a filmmaker, a painter, a writer, a photographer. Koe rejects this usage explicitly. In a 2024 video on leverage he says: "A creative is not someone with a specific skill that a robot will outprice and outcompete," and "a creative is not a designer, filmmaker, painter, writer, photographer or any specific limiting identity." The phrase "specific limiting identity" matters. It connects the topic to the model of identity built in Part II: a job label is one more identity that, once adopted, defines what a person believes they can and cannot do, and it also makes them vulnerable, because a skill defined narrowly enough can be automated or underpriced.

**Source:** How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md

The same distinction appears two years earlier, in a 2022 video on making money as a creative online. There Koe contrasts "what people think creativity is" with "true creativity." People think creativity depends on a skill like design or art. Those skills do require some creative output, he grants, "but that is not true creativity." His evidence is economic. There are designers still working in what he calls low-skill labor jobs, earning a fixed amount, "with AI coming to take over their jobs." Meanwhile, "the visionaries, strategists, leaders, innovators" who earn the most "aren't necessarily reliant on these creative skill sets, they are reliant on their nature as a creative being." **True creativity**, in this sense, is not a skill but a nature: the capacity of a human being to create, which a job may or may not use.

**Source:** How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md

Later in the same video Koe adds an observation that will matter in Section 18.4 and in Part XI: "a design job isn't inherently valuable, your design skill isn't inherently valuable" unless it is applied to help people improve and reach an outcome they want. His examples are modest on purpose. Someone cannot design their own profile picture; someone wants a design job and you help them learn faster than a college degree would. The skill becomes valuable when it is attached to another person's problem.

#### Mere technicians: Krishnamurti's warning

In a 2024 video presenting a sequence of steps for staying relevant over the next decade, Koe reaches the step he calls "creativity for unique solutions" and opens it with a long quotation from Jiddu Krishnamurti: "Unfortunately most of us are becoming mere technicians. We pass examinations, acquire this or that technique in order to earn a livelihood, but to acquire technique or develop capacity without paying attention to the inner state brings about ugliness and chaos in the world. If we awaken creative beauty inwardly, it expresses itself outwardly, and then there is order."

**Complementary context:** Jiddu Krishnamurti (1895–1986) was an Indian-born speaker and writer on the nature of the mind, education and conditioning. Koe quotes him in several places in the corpus, usually as an opening authority for ideas about conditioning and education.

Koe's own commentary is cautious about definitions: "What is creativity? That is an extremely difficult question to answer when you peel beyond the surface of skills and identities labeled as creative, like designers and filmmakers. But those creatives can still follow mechanical, stupefying processes in their work." The point is that a "creative" job can be performed mechanically, and a person with a creative title can be a technician in Krishnamurti's sense: someone who has acquired a technique without attending to the inner state from which the work comes. Koe draws the consequence directly: "As Krishnamurti said before, most of us are becoming technicians, robots. It's no wonder why people are so afraid of losing their jobs and being replaced. They lack creativity. They are not creators." The fear of replacement, in this reading, is a symptom. A person who is a technician can be replaced by a better technician, human or machine; a person who creates solutions cannot be replaced in the same way, because the solution comes from them.

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md

The same idea appears as a principle in the official summary of *The Art of Focus* (2024). Principle 16 reads: "The modern world favors productivity but neglects creativity; this leads to robotic and efficient machines that are no longer human. Create the efficient systems that your mind loves, but don't hesitate to evolve beyond them and discover the next problem that must be solved." The formulation is balanced in a way that is easy to miss. Koe does not reject efficiency; the systems "your mind loves" are part of his method (Chapter 12 is built on them). What he rejects is efficiency as the highest value. A system is a tool for one stage; once it works, the creative act is to see the next problem. The two principles that follow in the same list extend the thought. Principle 17: "The mind makes sense of the world through storytelling; life unfolds in chapters and phases; it is an unfolding of problems, calls to adventure, curiosity, exploration of the unknown, struggle, insight, transformation, solution and climax — flow with them all." Principle 18: "Success cannot exist without failure; failure is a reference point that reveals a problem from which you generate a goal to discover a lesson." Read together, the three principles describe creativity as the movement between problems: the efficient system solves one, the story of life presents the next, and failure is the reference point that reveals it. Chapter 39 returns to the idea of life in chapters.

**Source:** The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md

#### The true creative: a problem-solving orientation

If creativity is not a job, what is a creative? Koe's positive definitions in the 2024 leverage video are two. First: "a creative is someone who engages with the process of life reflected in their work." Second: "a creative is someone who solves problems with whatever combination of skill, knowledge, experience and inspiration necessary in the pursuit of a goal, usually in the form of a project." The word **creative** is therefore used in a sense of his own. It names an orientation, not a profession: a person facing a self-chosen goal, treating what stands in the way as problems, and drawing on whatever resources solve them. The mention of the project links this definition to Chapter 9, where the project is the unit through which a life's work advances.

**Source:** How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md

From this definition Koe derives a provocative consequence. "If you work a job that you hate as something like a designer, then I would argue that you are not a true creative, even if your job title and identity say you are." The reasoning is a direct application of the definition: "Creativity or being a creator is about creating solutions to problems, and if you can't identify the job that you hate as a problem that is solved with a creative solution, then you are not a creative." The designer who hates their job has, in Koe's description, "grown too comfortable in the routine"; their mind "doesn't want to break out" and dedicate "at least one hour a day to high leverage work that will solve the problem of a job that you hate." He goes further: "You aren't creative enough to realize that life is a series of problems that need solving in order for your mind to expand and for you to evolve." And he concludes: "A creative and a creator are those that take control of their future by solving all of the problems standing in the way of their vision for that future."

The argument is worth reading carefully, because it is easy to misread as an insult to designers. Its structure is: (1) creativity is creating solutions to problems; (2) the most pressing problem in this person's life is the job they hate; (3) they do not treat it as a problem to be solved, which shows in the absence of even one hour a day spent on solving it; (4) therefore, whatever their title, they are not exercising creativity where it matters most. The concept of the **true creative** is thus a test that applies to anyone, not a ranking of professions. It connects with the anti-vision of Chapter 7 (the hated situation as material for direction) and with the "one hour a day" of high-leverage work that recurs in Chapter 12.

#### Creativity as achieving any goal with what is available

In March 2024, in a video on the "future-proof skill stack," Koe gives a definition that pushes the problem-solving orientation as far as it goes. His last point is that people should stop worrying about which career skills AI will make obsolete and worry instead about whether they are training, or being trained, to make "ideal-future-aligned decisions," to be open-minded and perceptive, and to be self-motivated. Then: "Everyone is creative, and creativity is about achieving any goal with the knowledge and skill available to you, not fancy art or designs." The consequences he draws are practical: learn to set self-generated goals; use the internet, mentors and abundant information to educate yourself; embrace trial and error, self-reflection and experiment to create your own path. And the warning: "Creativity gives you the power to do whatever you want in life; those who don't realize this gift are going to be doomed to the assignments of others."

**Source:** The Future-Proof Skill Stack (How Average People Become Millionaires).md

Two points should be noted. The first is that this definition makes creativity depend on goals, and specifically on self-generated goals. That is the link to Chapter 1, where the central idea of the book is that one always pursues goals, either chosen or assigned: here, the person without creativity is "doomed to the assignments of others." The second is a matter of attribution. The video belongs to the period in which Koe was working closely with ideas from the writer Devon Eriksen (spelled in various ways in the transcripts), and the boundary between Eriksen's framing and Koe's own in this passage is not clear in the source. The definition is presented here as Koe's, with that reservation.

#### Creativity as a state: noticing the unnoticed

In March 2026, in a video titled "If you've lost your creative genius, watch this," Koe offers a different kind of definition. The earlier ones described creativity as a process or an ability directed at results. This one describes it as a state of consciousness.

He begins with what the state feels like. "Even if you think you aren't a [creative] person, you can tap into this incredibly enjoyable state of consciousness. It's similar to the flow state, but it's potentially more potent. You aren't focused on breezing through a set of tasks; you're more so noticing things for the first time. You're seeing a deeper layer of reality that you either forgot was there or you didn't know was there in the first place." His image is a dog seeing grass for the first time: "What is going on?" Its "dopamine is going crazy." He contrasts this **creative state**, which he also calls **open state creativity**, with "narrow state stressed focus": "we're trying to accomplish this open state creativity." The comparison with flow (Chapter 10) is precise. Flow, in Koe's treatment, is absorption in a task at the edge of one's ability; the creative state is less about executing and more about perceiving.

**Source:** If you've lost your creative genius, watch this.md

Then comes the definition. His first point is that "you don't have ideas because there's too much interference." He admits that he is annoyed by the sentence "I'm not a creative person," which he used to say himself, because it makes creativity seem like mere talent. He grants that "in a way" creativity is a skill: "It's a skill to be able to sustain a creative state over an extended period of time. So if you aren't in that creative state… you just haven't practiced the skill of getting back to that state." But at the deeper level: "At its core, creativity is just a natural way of being. It's a state of consciousness. It's a capacity that everyone has. But that capacity gets buried over time… with anything that narrows your mind." And: "Creativity is a very open, relaxed state where you see connections, patterns, and possibilities that aren't immediately obvious. It's the act of **noticing the unnoticed**, which is not… creating something from nothing. That nothing is already there. It's already raw material. You're creating with what's in reality, and that's a lot of stuff."

The logic of this definition has three parts. Creativity is natural and universal (everyone has the capacity). It is buried by **interference**, by whatever narrows the mind. And it is recovered by practice, which here means the practice of returning to an open state, not the practice of a technique. In the same video Koe names what narrows the mind ("the three narrowers of the mind": conditioning, productivity as a priority, and infinite input with zero processing time); those are treated in Chapter 11, where the environment that consumes attention is the subject. Here the relevant point is the shift in where creativity is located: not in the output and not in a skill, but in the quality of attention that precedes both.

This 2026 definition has a precedent in 2022. In "The Matrix Is Real (How To Break Free With Your Mind)," Koe describes creativity as bringing findings from the unknown into one's creations, and asks: "When you think of a creative… do you think they're just brooding over some political election? No, they're in a deep restful state and they're letting their creative abilities come into play." The Matrix, in that video's vocabulary (Chapter 1), keeps people trapped in surface-level thoughts; to reverse its effect, one must tap into and explore the unknown. The 2022 version already contains the two elements of 2026: a restful, open state, and an object of attention that lies outside the usual surface concerns.

**Source:** The Matrix Is Real (How To Break Free With Your Mind).md

#### How the definition changed

Koe's definitions of creativity are not fully consistent, and he does not reconcile them. The sequence is clearer in a table.

| Year | Definition | Emphasis | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | "True creativity" is one's nature as a creative being, not a creative skill; "creativity equals create plus clarity" (Section 18.2) | Nature; clarity from experience | How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md |
| 2022 | The creative is in "a deep restful state," not brooding on surface concerns | State | The Matrix Is Real.md |
| 2023 | Creativity is entering chaos and creating clarity (Section 18.2) | Process | Most People Are NPCs.md |
| 2024 (March) | "Achieving any goal with the knowledge and skill available to you, not fancy art" | Results; self-generated goals | The Future-Proof Skill Stack.md |
| 2024 (May) | The creative "solves problems… in the pursuit of a goal, usually in the form of a project" | Problem-solving orientation | How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md |
| 2024 (July) | "Bringing order to consciousness to embrace chaos": collect, connect, create with the dots, including "something from nothing" (Section 18.2) | Process; order from chaos | Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md |
| 2026 (March) | "A natural way of being… a state of consciousness"; "noticing the unnoticed," explicitly "not creating something from nothing" | State of perception | If you've lost your creative genius, watch this.md |

The most visible tension is literal. In July 2024 Koe lists "something from nothing" among the transformations that creativity achieves; in March 2026 he says that creativity "is not creating something from nothing; that nothing is already there." The reason he gives for the 2026 framing is that the phrase "I'm not a creative person" makes creativity seem like mere talent, and he wants to show that the capacity is universal. He does not mention or correct the earlier formulation. One reasonable reading, which the corpus permits but does not state, is that the definitions describe different phases of one cycle: a state (open perception), a process (collecting and connecting), and a result (a solution to a goal's problem). The 2024 "something from nothing" can also be read rhetorically, as one item in a list of paired opposites (sense from nonsense, life from death) rather than as a metaphysical claim. But the reader should know that Koe moved from treating creativity mainly as an ability oriented to outcomes to treating it mainly as a state of consciousness, and that he did not say so.

#### You are a creator: the human as toolbuilder

Beyond the definition of creativity lies a stronger claim about human nature. In a 2025 video on AI and the creator economy, Koe insists that by "creator" he does not mean "content creator" but "the essence of your being": you are human, therefore a creator. He frames this historically, with explicit hedging. We are not "those measly puny little beings of the past who look to the gods as the creators." At some point that switched — "maybe this was during the Enlightenment" — and we realized that we can acquire knowledge, solve problems and build tools so that we do not die in the cold or burn. The dating is speculative by his own wording ("maybe"), and it should be read as a story about a shift in self-understanding, not as a historical thesis. What follows is the practical point: many people have yet to adopt the mindset that "you can just create things, you can just solve problems." It is difficult, he grants, but that is where meaning and purpose come from, and one only finds it by leaning into the pain and realizing that struggle in specific situations can be fun.

**Source:** The creator economy is dying thanks to AI.md

The fullest version of the argument appears in October 2025, as step four of a sequence on escaping the Matrix: "become a value creator." It starts from a picture of the human condition. "Humans are toolbuilders." Facing problems like cold, hunger and predators, we created fire, shelter and weapons; once those were created, better tools became possible, and that accumulation produced today's society. Koe adds a deliberately unsentimental premise: "Nature is brutal. It's not this kind and loving thing that many hippies believe." Without transforming the earth to make it hospitable, humans would be extinct or drastically underdeveloped. From there he moves to the good life: becoming a creator is central to it because "happiness and enjoyment are the combination of progress being made and a contribution to something greater than yourself. Both are accomplished by solving problems for yourself and others. Problems are solved through creativity." And to work: "The intersection of purpose and profit lies in creating solutions to problems you deem interesting and passing those solutions down to contribute to humanity." Finally, a trajectory: one solves more complex problems as one gains knowledge, skill and resources, and "life gets better as problems shift from shallow to meaningful."

**Source:** You're Stuck In The Matrix, Here's How You Escape.md

The chain is worth stating in order, because each link depends on the previous one: nature presents problems; humans survive by building tools that solve them; tools compound; happiness consists of progress plus contribution; both are achieved by solving problems; problems are solved through creativity; therefore creativity is not a luxury but the mechanism of a good life, and a career that solves interesting problems for others is where purpose and profit meet. Chapter 39 develops the formula of happiness as progress plus contribution; Chapter 31 develops value as the solution of problems.

Two earlier formulations say the same thing from other angles. In a December 2023 video titled "The Future Of Work Is Play," Koe states: "Evolution is about solving problems with technology. That's what we do as humans; that is our edge." That edge is what sets humans apart from other beings and leads to skyscrapers, libraries, sports and "the infinite internet." We build, create and solve our own problems "with a plan, focus and creativity," and he concludes that "arguably the meaning of human existence is to create." The word "arguably" is his; he offers the claim as a position, not as a demonstrated fact. In a 2024 guide to the one-person business he makes a comparative argument. Chimpanzees use tools, but no other being has constructed the world with its mind: the school system, government, religious beliefs, language, all built "from nothing but images and symbols." "Humans are the only being that can have an idea and make it real." We had consciousness, attention, focus and the tools to connect ideas, make them tangible, communicate and form relationships. Koe pauses to flag that these constructions have traps and downsides, since they control many people's lives, and defers that discussion; the critique of institutions is the subject of Chapter 2. His conclusion is that one key to a high quality of life is "leaning into your nature as a creator, as a builder, and actually building things rather than watching other people build things."

**Source:** The Future Of Work Is Play (How To Create A Digital Career).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The phrase **nature as a creator** thus covers two ideas that should be kept apart. One is anthropological: humans, uniquely, turn ideas into reality. The other is normative: a good life consists in exercising that capacity rather than watching others exercise it. The second does not follow automatically from the first; Koe bridges them with the claim about happiness as progress and contribution.

#### Creators create their own jobs

Koe applies the creator thesis to the future of work with two pieces of evidence that he presents without sources. The first is a poll. "When kids were polled for what they wanted to do when they grow up, the top answer was become a YouTuber." He gives no source or figures. Most people, he says, will see this as delusional ("silly kids, go and get a real job"). "I see it as natural and potentially the only option in the future, not just like a YouTuber but a creator": "being human, doing what you want to do, studying your curiosities, leaning into what makes you you, and then distributing that value to the world through your unique mind." He also rejects the reflex of labeling the wish for influence and attention as vanity, on the ground that motives cannot be reduced to a single "why."

**Source:** People Dumber Than You Are Making Millions.md

The second is a statistic that he repeats in at least two videos from 2022: "sixty percent of new jobs haven't been created yet." He uses it to argue that creators, not only content creators but "creators as a whole," those who lean into their nature as creators and piece together thoughts, create their own jobs. Again no source is given. Both data points should be read as rhetorical support for a position, not as evidence the author has verified. The position itself, that the creator is a general human role newly made viable by the internet rather than a fringe career, is the one that Section 18.4 develops under the names "synthesizer" and "value creator."

**Source:** How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md

#### The pleasure of creating

Koe also claims that creation is pleasurable in a way that is a signal to do it more. In a 2023 video he quotes, with obvious enjoyment, "the wise words of Arnold": Schwarzenegger's famous comparison of the muscle "pump" in the gym to sexual climax ("to me the pump… just feels fantastic"). Koe uses the analogy to frame the creative flexing of the mind as similarly pleasurable, as a bridge to his own formula, "creativity is mental bodybuilding" (developed in Chapter 15, where the mind is treated as a body that grows under load). The source here is a third party, used as an analogy, and the claim it supports is experiential: creation feels good, and that feeling is a reason to do it more often.

**Source:** Get Mad At Where You Are In Life (A Deep Explanation).md

#### Creativity is not inherently valuable

One last qualification prevents the creator thesis from becoming romantic. In the 2022 video on making money as a creative, Koe says that one can spend six figures on a degree in "artistic basket weaving," or any creative field, and it is not inherently valuable "until you understand how a select group of people perceives value." "What is deemed valuable is up to perception," and perception depends on what a person has been made conscious of and conditioned to believe. It differs completely between people: one sees "a hunk of garbage" where you see "a diamond." Creativity, then, is a human capacity that does not by itself produce value for others. It produces value when it meets a group of people who perceive it as solving something for them. This is why the rest of the chapter moves from creativity to perspective, from perspective to the value creator, and from there to taste and articulation: each step adds a condition under which a creative act becomes valuable to someone. Chapter 31 develops "value is perception" as part of Koe's theory of value.

**Source:** How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md
