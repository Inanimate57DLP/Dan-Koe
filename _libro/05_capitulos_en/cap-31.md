## Chapter 31 — What Value Is

Part X ended with a business that has an audience, a service and a roadmap toward products. All of it rests on a word the book has used dozens of times without examining it: *value*. Creators are told to "provide value," to "become high value," to "deliver value up front." Dan Koe noticed, in 2022, that nobody using the word could say what it meant. This chapter reconstructs the answer he built over the following years. Most of it was assembled between 2023 and 2024, and it was refined until 2026.

The answer comes in layers, and the chapter follows them. Section 31.1 gives the deepest layer: value is the reversal of entropy. Koe applies this one idea to biological and technological evolution, to the price of a product, to the slow disappearance of labor work and to spirituality. Section 31.2 adds the second layer: value only exists when someone perceives it, and perception depends on the era, on the identity of the person looking and on how aware that person is of their problem. Section 31.3 turns the first two layers into method. It covers the "value creation" framework of 2023, the "Legos" of persuasive writing and the 2024 "Value Equation," with its macronutrients and micronutrients of value. Section 31.4 gives the practical test of value: behavior change. A brand, a piece of writing and a product are valuable to the degree that they move people. Section 31.5 applies everything to the product Koe considers the natural starting point: the education product. It sets out his defense of it against the objections he heard over five years, and includes his own story as a buyer of courses.

The chapter depends on earlier material. From Chapter 5 it needs entropy and psychic entropy, because the central definition of value is built on them. From Chapter 4 it needs the *level of mind*, because the perception of value depends on where a person's mind is. From Chapters 6 and 8 it needs the goal as a lens and the triad of goal, path and problem, which reappears here as the structure of every valuable message. From Part X it needs the one-person business and "solve your own problems and sell the solution," because this chapter explains why that formula produces something people pay for. Chapter 32 then takes the second layer (perception) and develops it as persuasion, and Chapter 33 takes the third (the frameworks) and turns it into a product.

### 31.1 Reversing Entropy: One Law Behind Evolution, Value, Work and Spirit

#### The question nobody answered: "you need to become high value"

The chapter's starting point is a confession. In October 2022 Koe opens a video with the kind of post that circulates on self-improvement social media, "hey bro you need to become high value," and admits he has written tweets like it himself. Then he asks what "high value" or "valuable" actually means, and he lists candidate answers without settling on any. Is it acquiring a certain number of marketable skills? Is it how you combine those skills? Is it the result the other person receives, and how that result shapes what you do for them? Is it how you look, so that others perceive you as high value? Is it your personality?

He then extends the question beyond skills. He asks about objects: is a Ferrari more valuable than a Kia Soul to someone who loves their Kia Soul? He asks about people: is an NFL cheerleader more valuable than the person you passed on the road without looking twice? His conclusion is blunt: "I don't think anybody knows the real answer to the question of what is value." The word makes intuitive sense. But the culture demands that people become high-value individuals with high-value skill sets and deliver value to others in order to "make it," and in that culture, Koe argues, one should understand value "to the point of being able to create it." He adds: "I haven't seen many if any people bring value to the word value."

**Source:** Why Cheap Dopamine Prevents You From Becoming High Value.md (2022-10-09)

The examples already contain, in unformed state, the answer he would give later. The Kia Soul owner shows that value depends on who is looking. The cheerleader and the stranger show that it depends on what is noticed. The list of candidates (skills, combination, result, appearance, personality) mixes what a thing *is* with how it is *perceived*. Koe's later framework separates those two dimensions and then reconnects them. In 2022, however, the question is presented as open, and the chapter records it as such. It also records the change of position: the open question of 2022 becomes, by March 2024, a closed and layered definition (developed below). Koe gives no explicit reason for the change. The most plausible reading is that the entropy framework he was building in those years supplied the answer, since the definition he eventually gives is stated in terms of entropy.

#### Value requires development over time: the pearl in the oyster

The same 2022 video contains the first firm statement Koe makes about value, and he calls it "a universal principle": "value requires development over time." His image is a pearl. A pearl forms inside an oyster that is closed off to the world, and only after some time does the oyster open and reveal it. People believe value is "so readily available," Koe says, because of the attention game. Social media shows the tips, the conclusions and the polished results, but not the time spent developing the depth that produced them.

**Source:** Why Cheap Dopamine Prevents You From Becoming High Value.md (2022-10-09)

The principle matters for the rest of the chapter in two ways. First, it explains why the attention economy distorts the perception of value. What is visible is the pearl, so viewers underestimate the closed oyster. The same distortion returns in Section 31.5, where free online content is criticized for showing the high-performing topics and hiding the boring fundamentals. Second, it sets a condition on the person who wants to create value: depth comes first. This links the chapter to the "build to learn" process of Chapter 14 and to the realistic timelines of Chapter 30. When Koe warns buyers of education products that results take "three to five years" (Section 31.5), he is applying the pearl principle to his own customers.

#### Entropy as the driving force of evolution

The answer Koe eventually gives to "what is value" starts from a law that Chapter 5 introduced: entropy. In July 2023 he states it in its simplest form. "Entropy is that everything tends towards disorder": one day you will die, the sun will die out, the universe will die. Anything tangible eventually decomposes, unless humans attempt to reverse entropy. "That's all we're really doing here," he says, "is trying to solve the problems that lead to things falling apart." We try to extend our life on Earth. Even something as small as organizing a bookshelf after it gets dirty is done, in his reading, with the larger survival goal in the back of the mind: "we need to live as long as possible, we need to become immortal, and that's what really everyone is [trying] to do."

**Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Life (In 6-12 Months).md (2023-07-25)

**Complementary context:** In physics, the second law of thermodynamics states that the total entropy of an isolated system does not decrease over time. Living beings and human artifacts maintain local order only by consuming energy and exporting disorder to their surroundings. Koe uses the law as a general metaphor and organizing principle, as Chapter 5 explained. He does not present it as a technical claim about thermodynamics, and the bookshelf example should be read in that spirit: as an interpretation of human motivation, not as a measured fact.

In February 2024 Koe draws the strongest conclusion from the law. "Entropy is the driving force of evolution": since everything tends toward death, and we are aware of that, "out of fear we are pushing towards something related to immortality or youth extension, and problem solving is the way we do that." He goes further. Disorder is not just an obstacle to purpose; it is the condition of purpose. "If things didn't tend toward disorder we would not be in the situation we are today. We wouldn't have any motivation to do anything, we wouldn't have a reason to be here, we wouldn't have a purpose."

**Source:** Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md (2024-02-18)

This inversion is the key move of the section. Entropy usually appears in Koe's work as the enemy: the chaos of an unfocused mind, the decay of a neglected routine (Chapters 5 and 13). Here it becomes the source of meaning. A world without decay would offer no problems, and a world without problems would offer no purpose. Chapter 8 defined purpose as the most urgent problem, and this passage gives that definition its cosmic foundation. Problems exist because things fall apart, and solving them is what living beings do.

#### "Even to remain still we must advance": the unattributed quote and the tractor

Koe supports the idea with a quotation he reads aloud twice: in "The 4-Hour Workday" (January 2023) and again in a March 2024 compilation that reproduces the same passage. The transcript does not attribute it to anyone. "Evolution is forced on us by the fact that systems fall apart with time unless they become more efficient. We can't stop and remain in the same place; even to remain still we must advance." He uses the quote as "another reason why this productivity system is important." Standing still is not a neutral option. A system either becomes more efficient or decays, and that holds for a person, a routine and a business alike. It is the same corollary Chapter 5 drew from the entropy law: there is no such thing as staying the same.

He then adds an argument of his own. Humans have "a natural desire to know more, the curse of knowledge," which he describes as "an endless thirst to learn things that will aid in our survival." With unique minds and knowledge, "we build systems to solve problems that lead to better outcomes in all domains of life": society, culture and personal life. His example is agriculture. Carrying water and plowing fields took enormous physical energy, so humans "used our creativity to create a solution like a tractor and a system to use that energy more efficiently."

**Source:** The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md (2023-01-01); Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md (2024-03-21)

Two clarifications are needed. The first concerns attribution. The quotation is a third party's words, and the corpus never names its author. The argument about the thirst for knowledge and the tractor is Koe's own. The second concerns terminology. Koe uses "the curse of knowledge" in an idiosyncratic sense, and the corpus does not use it consistently. In these passages it names the desire to know more, a neutral or even positive drive. Elsewhere in the same 2024 compilation it names something negative: learning without questioning what one learns, and so absorbing the conventional game. The corpus never resolves whether "curse" is meant pejoratively. A reasonable reading is that the same drive serves both ends. The thirst to learn can condition a person, and it can also equip them to build their own systems.

**Complementary context:** In cognitive psychology and economics, "the curse of knowledge" usually names a bias: once people know something, they find it hard to imagine not knowing it, which makes experts poor at explaining to beginners. Koe's usage is unrelated to that standard meaning.

#### Speculation: the next phase of evolution

In July 2023 Koe pushes the idea toward the future, and he marks it explicitly as speculation. He says he has been "down rabbit holes," and recommends a short story, "The Gentle Seduction," as something that opens the mind to the potential of the future. He is "not sure what the next phase of evolution is," but he is "pretty sure" it will be chemical enhancement, as discussed in the biohacking community, which would bridge humans toward becoming cyborgs and living forever. For now, he says, the goal seems to be prolonging health with longevity practices, to the point of reversing aging. The sentence that ties this to the chapter is: "That's all we're doing with evolution: we're trying to reverse entropy and the problems that come with entropy." He then sets the topic aside "for another time."

**Source:** Focus Is A Superpower (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md (2023-07-11)

**Complementary context:** "The Gentle Seduction" is a science-fiction short story by Marc Stiegler (1989). It follows a woman who gradually adopts transformative technologies over a lifetime. The transcript does not name the author.

The passage is an opinion about the future, adapted from fiction and from a subculture. It is not a thesis Koe develops or defends. It belongs in the chapter because it shows how far he extends the entropy principle. Longevity, biohacking and the tractor are, for him, instances of the same activity: solving the problems that decay creates.

#### The 2024 answer: value is not labor

The pieces come together in a March 2024 video titled "The Value Equation." Koe begins with what value is *not*. The labor theory of value ("I worked one year on this, you owe me $100,000") is "not how value works." Instead, "value works by me giving you something useful, but if you don't see it as useful through persuasion then it's not valuable."

**Source:** The Value Equation How To Become A High-Status Individual.md (2024-03-17)

**Complementary context:** The labor theory of value belongs to classical political economy (Adam Smith, David Ricardo) and to Karl Marx. In its classical forms it holds that the value of a commodity is determined by the labor required to produce it. Koe does not engage with that technical tradition. He uses the name for an everyday attitude: the belief that effort entitles one to payment. Chapter 34 shows the same target in his critique of the employee mindset (payment by the hour). This book reports his use of the term and does not correct his reading of the economic theory.

The definition has two parts, and they become the two layers of the rest of the chapter. Something must be *useful*, which is a matter of substance. And it must be *seen as useful*, which is a matter of perception, achieved "through persuasion." Koe names them immediately. The *macronutrients of value* are the perspective, meaning how the value is framed and constructed. The *micronutrients of value* shape how the value is perceived. Section 31.3 reconstructs both. The dietary metaphor fits the definition: macronutrients are the bulk of a meal, and micronutrients are small additions without which the meal does not nourish.

#### Value as structured communication, sense-making and a signal of meaning

Koe then states what the framework lets a person do. It lets you change your life, help others for money, write, speak and think clearly, and create content, landing pages or marketing that sells. It lets you navigate relationships in a mutual way, and understand and move past emotions. The breadth of the list follows from the definition he gives next: "Value in this case is structured communication or sense making to the mind." Value is how a mind makes sense of the world, packaged and given to someone else. It is also a framework for creating solutions to problems, and he connects it to his picture of the good life: solve one problem, then the next, contributing to evolution as you go. He calls it the foundation of human behavior, business and communication.

The passage ends with a string of equivalences: "Value is truth, value is story, value is progress, value is transformation, value is reversing entropy, value is a signal of meaning in your psyche." He presents these terms as "synonymous or heavily related."

**Source:** The Value Equation How To Become A High-Status Individual.md (2024-03-17)

The list is better read as a set of angles on one object than as a set of strict synonyms. Koe does not argue that truth and story are identical. Each term ties the definition to a part of the book. *Truth* and *sense-making* tie it to the thinking of Chapter 17. *Story* ties it to the mind as a story machine (Chapter 23). *Progress* and *transformation* tie it to behavior change (Section 31.4). *Reversing entropy* ties it to Chapter 5. *A signal of meaning in your psyche* ties it to the subjective experience Koe calls "the click," treated in Section 31.4. A reader can test a claim of value against any of these angles. Does it make sense of something? Does it move someone from one state to a better one? Does it reduce disorder in someone's mind or life?

#### The value equation: reversing entropy by creating systems

The video closes with the formula that gives it its title: "Value is reversing entropy. Entropy is the decline into chaos. Entropy is reversed by creating systems to achieve goals by solving problems. That's the value equation."

**Source:** The Value Equation How To Become A High-Status Individual.md (2024-03-17)

The formula is a chain with four links: chaos is reversed through *systems*, which serve *goals*, which are reached by solving *problems*. Each link connects to earlier chapters. Systems connect to Chapter 12 (systematizing) and to the tractor example above. Goals connect to Chapters 6 and 8, where the goal is a lens that turns information into relevance. Problems connect to Chapter 8's definition of purpose. The formula therefore unifies the personal system of the book (anti-vision, vision, goals, projects, focused work) with the economic system (offers, products, marketing). Reversing entropy in one's own life and creating value for others are the same operation, applied to different targets.

Koe attaches a lifestyle to the formula, which he calls the *lifestyle of the 1%*. All high-level individuals, he says, reverse entropy daily. They pursue goals, solve problems, hypothesize, test, and create solutions to distribute. This demands constant learning, building and distributing value, and he calls it "literally the one thing that will set you apart." The "1%" here is a rhetorical marker of rarity, not a measured statistic.

**Complementary context:** Alex Hormozi, whom the architecture of this book lists among Koe's references on offers, popularized a different "value equation" in *$100M Offers* (2021): perceived value rises with the dream outcome and the perceived likelihood of achievement, and falls with time delay and effort. The units gathered for this chapter do not connect Koe's formula to Hormozi's. The two share a name and an interest in perceived value, but Koe's equation is a definition in terms of entropy, systems, goals and problems, not a ratio. Some of Hormozi's factors resemble Koe's micronutrients (proof raises perceived likelihood; risk reversal lowers perceived effort), but the corpus does not draw that parallel.

#### Evolution phases out labor work

The entropy definition has a consequence that Koe develops at length, and it explains part of his view of work and AI. If evolution means solving the problems that cause decay, and repetitive labor is one of the problems people hate most, then evolution will keep eliminating labor. The idea appears first in June 2022, in a plainer form: "Humans are lazy; they tend to phase out what they don't like doing." This is why, Koe argues, the people who make the most money or see the most success are creative people, not people in manual low-skill labor. He says the pattern shows across human evolution. When people got tired of plowing fields by hand, a creative person innovated and made a tractor, "expanding their mind until the imaginary idea of a tractor became an actual tractor," pulling together all available resources. Over time, things keep getting phased out.

**Source:** How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md (2022-06-25)

The tractor appears in both lines of argument, the 2023 productivity argument and the 2022 labor argument, and it does the same work in each. It is the model of a system that reverses entropy by spending less energy for the same result, and the model of a creative act that makes a whole class of labor unnecessary.

In the same video Koe gives a present-day example. He saw a tweet complaining about automated touch-screen ordering at McDonald's and other fast-food chains, which phase out cashiers because they save the company money. Many people see this as bad. But people who believe in themselves, or know they have higher potential and can learn new skills through self-education ("the endless ocean of resources online"), spending a few hours a day, are not worried, or are worried only a little at first. The lesson he draws: "consistently improve yourself and acquire high value skills and become a student of life." Doing so opens room for creativity in yourself, and your creative solutions open more opportunity and creativity for others. The creator economy is his example.

He then cites a source and adopts it. He has been studying Naval Ravikant and finds his philosophy "very aligned with mine." Naval's tweet reads: "If the work doesn't require creativity, delegate it, automate it, or leave it." Koe's interpretation: take personal responsibility for your future and increase your skill set, so that you are not phased out by "human nature's continuous creation of a better future."

**Source:** How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md (2022-06-25)

**Complementary context:** Naval Ravikant is an entrepreneur and investor (co-founder of AngelList) whose aphorisms on wealth, leverage and specific knowledge, collected in a 2018 tweetstorm titled "How to Get Rich (without getting lucky)," are among the most adapted sources in Koe's corpus (see Chapters 20, 25 and 34).

#### "A gradual erosion of human labor"

In April 2023 Koe gives the idea a name borrowed from a third party. He reads a tweet from Zero HP Lovecraft, an account he finds interesting but does not follow. The tweet says that the real AI singularity, if it happens, will not look like a science-fiction doomsday. The real, and probably inevitable, form is "a gradual erosion of human labor."

**Source:** Self Improvers Are Creating Their Own Careers (The New Economy).md (2023-04-16)

**Complementary context:** Zero HP Lovecraft is the pseudonym of an online writer of speculative fiction and commentary, known mainly on Twitter/X. The phrase is his. The use Koe makes of it, as a description of the evolutionary process he has been describing, is Koe's.

In the same video Koe states his own position, and in 2023 it is enthusiastic: he "only see[s] good" in automation and AI. "A decline in labor work means an incline in creative and knowledge work for people that choose to continue working." He considers universal basic income (UBI) and is skeptical of the assumption behind it, that people with free time will naturally self-actualize: "either they'll have more time to self-actualize or they'll have more time to destroy their lives." People who want control can use the moment. Between the two futures he prefers "the AI route" to UBI, "because it forces people to get creative if they want to survive; it forces people to be human."

**Source:** Self Improvers Are Creating Their Own Careers (The New Economy).md (2023-04-16)

The phrase "it forces people to be human" assumes a definition of the human that the next units make explicit: to be human is to create. The preference for pressure over provision mirrors Koe's ideas about tactical stress (Chapter 10) and about money or pain on the line (Section 31.4). In each case, a constraint is what produces action.

#### Bet on the mind: evolution as problem-solving

In February 2024 Koe restates the argument directly in terms of entropy. "Evolution is the process of solving problems that reverse entropy. Labor work and long work days are a massive problem; people hate it, so social media, the internet and artificial intelligence were born." AI, he says, "will solve the problem of overwork potentially in this lifetime, especially if we solve the problem of aging." His practical conclusion: "You are better off banking on the creative ability of your mind than you are going into a labor work job." He makes an explicit exception: "If you want quick cash then yes, that is actually a very good option right now." For the future, though, and "to earn unlimited because your intelligence is scalable infinitely," he advises choosing work that allows a four-hour workday (Chapter 12) and contributing "your time, energy and focus into the aspects of reality that you want to see flourish."

**Source:** Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md (2024-02-18)

A week later he answers an objection he expects. Society needs construction workers, plumbers and labor workers. Koe agrees, but suggests that "maybe in our lifetime those jobs won't exist." He then asks two questions and answers them separately. Should everyone bank on creative work for meaning and income? No. Should smart people? Yes. With robotics, and factories like Tesla's being over 75% automated (his figure, given without a source), he does not see why robots cannot solve labor. Personal robots doing laundry and cleaning seem to him not far off, which would leave people free to focus on creative work and self-actualization. Will everyone pursue that? No: "some people are going to end up like Wall-E," mainlining Coca-Cola in a floating lounge chair with dopamine channels. His advice is to stop worrying about it. Wise people who flow with nature cannot succumb to that life, because "once you've had that taste of self-actualization and flow and meaningful living, there's no going back." He calls that the awakened point.

He states the principle behind the whole argument in one line: "Success is reserved for the creative; your psyche craves creativity." Creating feels good. By all measures, creating something of your own "lights your brain on fire," and "that is enough of a sign that humans are meant to do this."

**Source:** Zero To $1 Million As A One-Person Business (Working 2-4 Hours A Day).md (2024-02-25)

**Complementary context:** *WALL-E* (Pixar, 2008) depicts a future in which humans live on a spaceship in hover-chairs, consuming screens and drinks while robots do all the work. Koe uses the image as shorthand for automation without self-actualization.

Several distinctions keep this argument from being read as a universal prescription. First, Koe separates the advice by person and horizon: quick cash favors labor now, and the long run favors creative work for those able to pursue it. Second, the claim that "humans are meant to do this" rests on felt experience ("lights your brain on fire"), which is an interpretation rather than evidence. Third, the "Wall-E" outcome is admitted as real for some people. The argument does not promise that automation liberates everyone. It claims that automation rewards those who choose creative work.

#### If a machine can replace your job

By May 2025 the same argument has a sharper edge. Koe addresses the comfortable majority who complain about AI taking jobs: "what's the issue? Why are you complaining?" Average 9-to-5 jobs, he says, suck. He does not mean the 0.1% with nap pods at Google. "We've collectively hated jobs for decades." "Evolution solves problems": one of life's most painful problems, working a job, is being solved, and people are angry about it. In his reading, they are presented with one of the greatest opportunities of a lifetime and fall into "the most cliché trap of comfort and playing victim." They hold on to an old way of life "when all good things aren't permanent." Then he states the diagnostic: "If you work a job that a machine can replace, your life probably lacks novelty and meaning and fulfillment and challenge and complexity and continuous growth and learning." That, he says, is a massive signal to do something new.

**Source:** The Future Of Work (How To Become AI-First).md (2025-05-25)

Koe asserts that this is not opinion but "documented psychological patterns." The transcript cites no study, and the claim should be treated as his interpretation. It is consistent with the flow research he draws on elsewhere (Chapter 10), where novelty, challenge and growth are conditions of optimal experience, but it is not demonstrated in the passage.

In February 2026 the tone changes, and the change should be recorded. Koe distinguishes two types of work. *Mundane busy work* is repetitive and mechanical, like an industrial assembly line, and he does not mind it going away. He says so "in the kindest way possible," which acknowledges that livelihoods are at stake. *Creative work* is "the ability to see reality from your own lens, pull it into your mind, synthesize things together, create something, share it with another person, and then get feedback for your contribution in the form of money" or another form of value. "That's where humans find meaning."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (& The New High-Income Skill Stack).md (2026-02-12)

The 2026 definition of creative work is a compact version of the value chain this chapter builds. It moves from perception (seeing reality through one's own lens), through synthesis (making sense), creation (packaging) and sharing (distribution), to feedback in money (value exchange). It also shows the evolution of Koe's position on automation and employment, which the corpus records across several videos:

| Period | Position on labor and AI | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| 2022–2023 | Humans phase out what they dislike; "I only see good"; labor is "on the horizon of complete removal" | Enthusiastic |
| Early 2024 | "Maybe in our lifetime those jobs won't exist"; smart people should bank on creative work; quick cash still favors labor | Confident, with exceptions |
| 2024 | "Computers will only replace those who deserve to be replaced" (a line recorded elsewhere in the corpus) | Harsh |
| 2025 | A job a machine can replace signals a life lacking meaning; complaint is "playing victim" | Provocative |
| 2025–2026 | AI's impact "probably not going to be as drastic as many people made it out to seem, myself included"; busy work goes away "in the kindest way possible"; livelihoods acknowledged | Moderated, self-correcting |

Two changes are visible. Koe corrects his own estimate of how drastic the change will be. And he moves from blaming the person who gets replaced toward a broader framing, in which AI threatens meaning as well as jobs, with more care for people whose circumstances give them less control. He gives no explicit reason for either change. The underlying thesis does not change: evolution solves the problem of labor, and creative work is where meaning and value remain.

#### "If you are not a builder, you are going to be built over"

Koe's shortest formulation of the consequence comes from October 2022, in the video that introduced his idea of the *value creator*. Humans think and build. We build tools and solutions that make life easier, and "this is never going to stop." So "if you are not a builder then you are going to be built over, or you are going to build for someone else." He says this introduction "lays the framework" for everything that follows.

**Source:** The Rise Of The Value Creator (A Career Path For Polymaths & Self-Improvers).md (2022-10-31)

The sentence turns the entropy argument into a personal choice with three options. You can build, which means reversing entropy through your own systems. You can be built over, which means having your work absorbed by someone else's system, the way the tractor absorbed manual plowing. Or you can build for someone else, contributing your energy to systems you do not own, which is Koe's description of employment (Chapter 2). *Builder* is a common word that Koe uses in a specific sense: a person who always has a project aligned with what they are learning (Chapter 14), and who therefore produces systems rather than only consuming them. Chapter 35 develops the value creator as a role.

#### Spirituality as your part in the whole

The last application of the entropy law is the most surprising one, and Koe develops it in several videos between 2023 and 2024: spirituality. The context is his argument against people who reject money and business on spiritual grounds.

In November 2023 he starts from their own premise. "If the entire goal of spirituality is connection to something larger than yourself, and money in the material world is a way to connect with society as a whole and distribute goods and information that benefits them," then rejecting money contradicts spirituality. Many spiritual people, he says, think that buying a product or service cannot help a specific person. When you peel the belief back, "you know that that's wrong, but the unconscious assumption is just that everything related to money is evil, when everything near you, you watching this right now, is only possible because of money."

From there he goes further. "You are unconsciously allowing the entire universe to tilt in the hands of evil by not becoming a conscious business owner that impacts his customers, employees, readers, economy and the rest to infinity." And then he gives his definition: "Spirituality is understanding your part in the whole of the universe. Spirituality is not disconnecting from that whole to live in the woods and letting the universe decline into chaos thanks to entropy. Spirituality then can be considered and felt when you attempt to reverse entropy by putting effort and energy into the progress and evolution of humanity."

Later in the same video he generalizes about human nature: "To be human is to transcend, expand and create." He also observes that "the world is going mental." As a species, "we are transcending the physical thanks to technology, science and computational power; we are unifying as a species, the unity of consciousness through technology." Then he asks: "Would that have been possible without money? Was there any other way to get to this point of technological advancement without money? Therefore something that is extremely good for humanity is the byproduct of money, and you hate money, and you can't see that as something that is limiting you severely."

**Source:** People Dumber Than You Are Making Millions.md (2023-11-12)

The chain of the argument runs as follows. Spirituality is connection to a larger whole. Money is a means of connection to the social whole. Therefore rejecting money in the name of spirituality is self-contradictory. The positive definition follows: spirituality is felt in the effort to reverse entropy for humanity. The *conscious business owner* is Koe's term for the person who does this through business, impacting customers, employees, readers and the economy, and through them more people "to infinity." The claim that technological unification is "a byproduct of money" is a historical interpretation offered rhetorically. Its force lies in the question it poses to the money-hating spiritual person, not in any detailed historical argument.

In September 2024 the definition gains a second clause. "Purpose does not exist without problems." Purpose lies at "the polar end" of problems, "so by solving a problem you pursue a purpose; you discover your part in something greater than yourself. That's what spirituality is: discovering your part in the whole, transcending that whole to the next, and then the next, until you reach your life's purpose of contribution." This ties money, as a tool for solving problems, to spirituality.

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md (2024-09-29)

The change between the two definitions is a refinement. In 2023 spirituality is *understanding* your part in the whole, felt when you reverse entropy. In 2024 it is *discovering* your part, then *transcending* that whole toward the next, in a sequence that ends in contribution. The 2024 version adds movement through levels, which echoes the developmental maps of Chapter 38 (transcend and include) and the purpose levels of Chapter 9 (survival, status, creativity, contribution). Both versions are compatible: the second contains the first and gives it a direction.

#### The urge to build: "spirituality business"

In February 2024 Koe answers a common position: that past a certain point one does not need to keep making money. People who think this, he says, usually have a missing key in their life. They can improve other things, but "the urge to build is missing, that is the urge to help human evolution and contribute to humanity," broadly, "not just your local circle but on a universal level." "That's called spirituality business." At that stage, he adds, you have built so much leverage and distribution that you can sell whatever you want. This, he says, is why he is building software and plans to write three books by the end of the following year.

**Source:** Zero To $1 Million As A One-Person Business (Working 2-4 Hours A Day).md (2024-02-25)

*Spirituality business* and *urge to build* are coined terms that appear once in the corpus. They name the stage where money stops being the motive for building but remains the means of contribution. Chapter 34 takes up the related question of "enough" in wealth, and this passage gives Koe's answer from the side of meaning. The person who has enough money may still lack the drive to contribute, and that lack, not the money, is the problem.

#### Status, power and the virtue-signaling objection

The last two units of the section, from September 2024, address an objection to everything above: that pursuing status, power and influence is spiritually suspect. Koe's answer has two steps.

First, a principle. Everyone has some degree of status, power or influence, and everyone is trying to get them. "They become bad things when done for unconscious reasons," that is, when you do not understand the big-picture, overarching impact of your pursuit of power and persuasion. He illustrates with the video itself: "This entire video could be completely negative, but if it has a positive impact on your life because of that, does that make it a bad thing?"

Second, an argument about the spiritual strategy of withdrawal. To be perceived as valuable you need power, influence, some form of status. If your strategy for success is being "nice, noble and spiritual," mean people will push you aside and have more impact. And if you let that happen, "you aren't as spiritual as you think you are," because spirituality is being part of something greater than yourself and, in a sense, taking responsibility for that thing and its outcome. Going to the woods to meditate and find God can be good for a time, but then you come back and reintegrate with society. Koe ends with a reversal: "the people who virtue signal that power games are bad are playing their own power game." He calls it selfish, lacking contribution to humanity, and a self-deception. Such people do nothing but preach how others should change, and this distracts them from looking in the mirror.

**Source:** 4 Frameworks To Write Follow-Worthy Content (+ Kortex Template).md (2024-09-08)

The argument connects to the definition of value through the phrase "to be perceived as valuable." Value needs perception (Section 31.2), perception needs attention, and attention, in a crowded world, goes to people with status and influence. A person who refuses those means on principle limits how much entropy they can reverse for others. The phrase "power games" is Koe's own. His claim that critics of power games play their own is a psychological interpretation of their motives, offered polemically. A reader can grant the structural point (that refusing influence is not neutral) without accepting the characterization of every critic.

#### One law, four applications

The section has shown one principle applied in four domains. The table summarizes them.

| Domain | What decays (entropy) | What reverses it | Koe's formulation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evolution and survival | Bodies, things, the universe tend to disorder and death | Problem-solving, efficient systems (the tractor), longevity | "Entropy is the driving force of evolution" |
| The value of a product | Chaos in a person's mind or life (a problem) | A system that achieves a goal by solving a problem | "Value is reversing entropy… that's the value equation" |
| The end of labor work | Human energy spent on repetitive, hated labor | Technology that phases labor out; creative work | "Evolution is the process of solving problems that reverse entropy" |
| Spirituality | The universe "declining into chaos" if one withdraws | Energy invested in humanity's progress; contribution | "Spirituality… can be felt when you attempt to reverse entropy" |

The unification has a practical point that Section 31.3 develops. If value is the reversal of entropy, then the anatomy of any valuable thing should mirror the anatomy of entropy reversal: a problem (disorder), a goal (the ordered state) and a process (the system that bridges them). That is exactly the structure of the "macronutrients of value." Before reaching it, though, the chapter has to deal with the second half of the 2024 definition: something useful is valuable only if it is *seen* as useful.

### 31.2 Value Is Perception

#### "Marketing is perception, value is perception"

The second layer of the definition is stated most directly in August 2023. Koe calls marketing and sales, "paired with writing and speaking," the way "you create value." He defines their role carefully. Marketing and sales are "the psychological kind of cortex or meta structure of what you're going to write or speak so that it is impactful and persuasive and valuable, because marketing is perception, value is perception." Using marketing principles or psychology to frame what you say means catching attention, holding it, and making what you say valuable and understandable. That, he says, "is the most valuable skill."

**Source:** I Had To Learn These High-Income Skills (If I Wanted To Make Money).md (2023-08-16)

The image of a *cortex* or *meta structure* is the key. Marketing is not a separate activity added after the content is made. It is the outer layer that organizes how the content will be received, much as the cortex organizes the brain's processing. The content is the useful substance. The meta structure decides whether that substance reaches anyone. Koe's formula "value is perception" should therefore be read precisely. He is not claiming that substance is irrelevant; Section 31.1 required usefulness, and Section 31.4 requires behavior change. He is claiming that substance which is not perceived produces no value *for the person who fails to perceive it*.

In November 2023 he draws the consequence for anyone who wants something in return. "If value is perception and other people can't perceive what you have to offer as valuable, then you're not going to get the value that you want to receive," which in most cases is money. The structure is the same everywhere, he argues. In a relationship or in business, approaching someone at a bar or in the DMs, "it's all the same thing": you have cultivated value to offer, and you need to persuade the other person to see it so that they give you what you want in return. For online writers, creators and one-person businesses, persuasive communication is in everything. Writing must be persuasive to be shared, and sharing is the value a writer wants from readers in exchange. Landing pages, DMs, a text to a friend, a tweet, an Instagram post, a YouTube video: everything "has to follow a general persuasive structure."

**Source:** The Most Important High-Income Skill To Learn (In The Next 10 Years).md (2023-11-05)

Value appears here as an exchange with two sides, and the currency need not be money. A reader "pays" a writer with a share, a date "pays" with a yes, a customer pays with money. In each case what is received depends on what the other side perceives. This symmetric framing returns in Section 31.4 as "value exchange" and the "vessel of valuable information."

#### Master perception: "dopamine dealers"

The earliest and most provocative version of the idea comes from October 2022, in the same video that asked what value is. If you want to master marketing, Koe says, you must "master perception," because "a better product will always lose to a product that people think is better." Marketers know this well: "they are dopamine dealers." So are content creators, since headlines exist to capture attention. Much of what we see, he says, is misperceived, or polished on the outside to catch attention with something new and novel. His examples are a social media influencer, an article headline and a YouTube title. The polish excites you, or makes you wonder "what do I not have, what can I have that will make my life better." Average people understand the game too. The guy going to the club on Saturday night puts on his best floral shirt, slicks his hair back, and puts on a watch and a perfume marketed to help him get more girls.

**Source:** Why Cheap Dopamine Prevents You From Becoming High Value.md (2022-10-09)

The passage connects value to Chapter 11's account of dopamine as the "molecule of more": the signal of something relevant to a goal, or the promise of something better. Marketers and creators trade in that signal, which is why Koe calls them dealers. The phrase is deliberately unflattering, and it includes himself. The man getting ready for the club shows that the game of perception is not a corporate invention. It is ordinary social behavior, and everyone presents their value so that others can perceive it. The principle "a better product will always lose to a product that people think is better" is a strong rule of thumb, not a law. It describes a tendency in competitive markets, and Koe offers it without data. It is also the most direct expression of the risk that Section 31.4 addresses: perception can be manipulated, and the ethics of persuasion (Chapter 32) exist because of that.

#### Subjective and shifting: perception across time

In March 2024 Koe adds the dimension of time. "The perception of value is subjective; it's dependent on the individual and it shifts when times shift, when the world evolves." His example is the printing press. It was seen as ultra valuable when first invented, but now that it has been "taken over by radio, TV, social media," we no longer see it as that valuable. With the advent of AI, he says, "we may not know what the future of value looks like," but if you understand how to create value you can adapt. He refers the viewer to his earlier "value equation" video (which he says was retitled) for the method.

**Source:** Working Hard Won't Make You Rich (Do This Instead).md (2024-03-31)

In July 2024 he states the practical rule that follows. "Value evolves with time, value is perception, meaning you have to give the right thing to the right person for them to see it as valuable or for them to pay you money." His example is generational: "a boomer will see gold as more valuable than Bitcoin," and will be less persuaded otherwise, unless you get in front of them "with persuasive media where they hang out." He reminds the viewer that creating the value itself was already covered: "solve your own problems and sell the solution, and have a vision and goals so you can actually identify those problems."

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md (2024-07-13)

Two operations are implied, and they correspond to the two layers of value. *Creating* value depends on a vision and goals, because they reveal the problems worth solving (Chapters 7–8 and 28). *Delivering* perceived value depends on matching the thing to the person and reaching them where they are. That second operation has three parts: the right thing, the right person, and the right place ("where they hang out"). The boomer example shows that a correct thing, such as Bitcoin in Koe's implied view, can still fail to be valuable to a specific person until persuasion has done its work.

#### Identity and the extended process of persuasion

The same March 2024 video gives the deepest version of the idea, because it ties perception to identity, the subject of Part II. "If I'm a bodybuilder trying to sell to a Gothic person or a distance runner, the things that I find valuable from the identity that I've forged are probably not going to be seen as valuable to them, unless I recreate their identity through content and writing." That recreation, he says, "is just an extended process of persuasion to get them to change their identity and eventually see the value in my product." He then summarizes on two levels: "value changes based on just the general situation, culture and society we're in, and perception of value changes based on the identity you place whatever it is that is supposed to be valuable in front of."

**Source:** Working Hard Won't Make You Rich (Do This Instead).md (2024-03-31)

The example gathers several threads of the book. Chapter 3 defined identity as a network of goals and beliefs that filters perception. If identity filters perception, it also filters perceived value. The bodybuilder's product (training, nutrition, aesthetics) passes through the bodybuilder's filter as valuable, and through the goth's or the runner's filter as irrelevant or even repellent. The only way to make it valuable to them is to change the filter. Koe calls this an *extended* process because identity changes slowly (Chapter 4). A single ad cannot do it, but months of content and writing can. This is the economic reason behind the long-form, long-term content strategy of Chapters 24–26, and it anticipates the role of awareness levels in Chapter 32.

#### Positioning: speaking to an identity

Koe's term for the practical side of this is *positioning*, an industry word he uses with a specific meaning: presenting an offer in a way fitted to the identity of the customer. In April 2023 he gives a negative example. Suppose you try to make a gamer aware of a health problem. The gamer identifies with being a gamer and drinking Mountain Dew or G Fuel, and you say "you're unhealthy." They will call you an idiot. Koe characterizes gamers as closed-minded "in most cases," while acknowledging some open-minded ones. The lesson: "You have to position the product in a very specific way to speak to that person; you have to understand your customer."

**Source:** Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md (2023-04-09)

The gamer case is a direct application of Chapter 1's account of the closed mind. A belief that has become part of an identity is defended like the body itself (Chapter 3's law of conceptual survival). A direct attack on it triggers defense, not reconsideration. Positioning goes around the defense. It finds a frame from which the product serves something the identity already wants. For the gamer, that might be performance, focus or energy rather than "health." Koe's generalization about gamers is his own impression, and the principle does not depend on it.

A small autobiographical note in February 2023 shows that positioning works on the seller's side too. Koe was at one point a "brand advisor" and at another a "marketing consultant": "they're all kind of the same thing, it's just dependent on who I'm targeting and how I position the actual offer." He used social media as his way of attracting traffic because it is so big that "there isn't really any other choice," and he says he loves working with creators.

**Source:** How To Write Authentic Content (Social Media Growth 101).md (2023-02-05)

The same service under two names illustrates the principle exactly. The substance (the macronutrient) stays fixed while the frame (the perception) changes with the target. The name of an offer is itself a positioning decision, which anticipates the "concept" micronutrient of Section 31.3.

#### Awareness: the mind that can receive

In September 2024 Koe adds a third factor, alongside era and identity: the moment a person is in, defined by their awareness of a problem. "Value is perception; one man's trash is another man's treasure." Some people see his video as unimportant, while others who have watched "till right now" see it as valuable: "that's just how the market works." Many will watch five seconds and say "that's stupid, that's a scam, I'm out." Others "are actually going to be changed by it because their mind is in the place where they can receive it." They are "going through a problem in their life, they are aware of that problem to the point of being able to receive the information and not write it off."

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md (2024-09-29)

The phrase "their mind is in the place where they can receive it" links the perception of value to the *level of mind* (Chapter 4) and to the goal as a lens (Chapter 6). Information becomes relevant only to a mind that has a goal or a problem it serves. A person who is not aware of the problem perceives the solution as noise, or even as a threat. This is why Koe's formula for marketing (Section 31.4) begins with making people aware of a problem. It is also why Chapter 32 treats the five levels of awareness as the first variable of any persuasive message.

#### Buying a worldview

A final unit, from April 2023, explains why the same information can be worth more coming from one person than from another. Purchasing information from individuals, Koe says, is "like buying a book: I get to adopt their worldview." You learn skills from the people who have practiced them most and who hold the underlying philosophy you resonate with, so you learn the skill better. Schools do not do that; their method is "learn this, learn this, learn this." When you learn from Koe, you learn the way he likes to teach, which is usually philosophical with spiritual concepts baked in. That style resonates with certain people much better than with others, "so I can help those people more, and that is what creates value."

**Source:** Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md (2023-04-30)

This is the bridge between "value is perception" and "you are the niche" (Chapter 19). If perception depends on identity, then the seller's identity is part of the product. The same skill taught through a different worldview reaches a different audience, and for that audience it is worth more. Resonance is not an ornament on top of the information. In Koe's account, it is "what creates value." Section 31.5 uses the same logic to answer the objection that "there's already enough information out there."

#### Three layers of perceived value

The section's units can be arranged as three layers that together decide whether a useful thing is perceived as valuable.

| Layer | What varies | Koe's example | Consequence for the creator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Era and culture (macro) | What a society finds valuable at a given time | The printing press; gold vs. Bitcoin for a boomer | Understand how value is created, not only what is valued now, so you can adapt |
| Identity (individual) | The filter of beliefs and goals through which a person looks | The bodybuilder selling to a goth or a runner; the gamer and Mountain Dew | Position the offer for a specific identity; change identities slowly through content |
| Awareness (moment) | Whether the person is aware of the problem the thing solves | Viewers who leave after five seconds vs. viewers who are changed | Raise awareness of the problem before presenting the solution |

A fourth factor runs across all three: the seller. The worldview the seller embodies, and how it resonates with the buyer, change how much the same information is worth. Taken together, the layers explain why Koe can say, without contradiction, that value is something useful *and* that value is perception. Usefulness is necessary, but whether the useful thing produces an exchange depends on these layers. The next section turns this analysis into a method for constructing perceived value deliberately.
