## Chapter 31 — What Value Is

Part X ended with a business that has an audience, a service and a roadmap toward products. All of it rests on a word the book has used dozens of times without examining it: *value*. Creators are told to "provide value," to "become high value," to "deliver value up front." Dan Koe noticed, in 2022, that nobody using the word could say what it meant. This chapter reconstructs the answer he built over the following years. Most of it was assembled between 2023 and 2024, and it was refined until 2026.

The answer comes in layers, and the chapter follows them. Section 31.1 gives the deepest layer: value is the reversal of entropy. Koe applies this one idea to biological and technological evolution, to the price of a product, to the slow disappearance of labor work and to spirituality. Section 31.2 adds the second layer: value only exists when someone perceives it, and perception depends on the era, on the identity of the person looking and on how aware that person is of their problem. Section 31.3 turns the first two layers into method. It covers the "value creation" framework of 2023, the "Legos" of persuasive writing and the 2024 "Value Equation," with its macronutrients and micronutrients of value. Section 31.4 gives the practical test of value: behavior change. A brand, a piece of writing and a product are valuable to the degree that they move people. Section 31.5 applies everything to the product Koe considers the natural starting point: the education product. It sets out his defense of it against the objections he heard over five years, and includes his own story as a buyer of courses.

The chapter depends on earlier material. From Chapter 5 it needs entropy and psychic entropy, because the central definition of value is built on them. From Chapter 4 it needs the *level of mind*, because the perception of value depends on where a person's mind is. From Chapters 6 and 8 it needs the goal as a lens and the triad of goal, path and problem, which reappears here as the structure of every valuable message. From Part X it needs the one-person business and "solve your own problems and sell the solution," because this chapter explains why that formula produces something people pay for. Chapter 32 then takes the second layer (perception) and develops it as persuasion, and Chapter 33 takes the third (the frameworks) and turns it into a product.

### 31.1 Reversing Entropy: One Law Behind Evolution, Value, Work and Spirit

#### The question nobody answered: "you need to become high value"

The chapter's starting point is a confession. In October 2022 Koe opens a video with the kind of post that circulates on self-improvement social media, "hey bro you need to become high value," and admits he has written tweets like it himself. Then he asks what "high value" or "valuable" actually means, and he lists candidate answers without settling on any. Is it acquiring a certain number of marketable skills? Is it how you combine those skills? Is it the result the other person receives, and how that result shapes what you do for them? Is it how you look, so that others perceive you as high value? Is it your personality?

He then extends the question beyond skills. He asks about objects: is a Ferrari more valuable than a Kia Soul to someone who loves their Kia Soul? He asks about people: is an NFL cheerleader more valuable than the person you passed on the road without looking twice? His conclusion is blunt: "I don't think anybody knows the real answer to the question of what is value." The word makes intuitive sense. But the culture demands that people become high-value individuals with high-value skill sets and deliver value to others in order to "make it," and in that culture, Koe argues, one should understand value "to the point of being able to create it." He adds: "I haven't seen many if any people bring value to the word value."

**Source:** Why Cheap Dopamine Prevents You From Becoming High Value.md (2022-10-09)

The examples already contain, in unformed state, the answer he would give later. The Kia Soul owner shows that value depends on who is looking. The cheerleader and the stranger show that it depends on what is noticed. The list of candidates (skills, combination, result, appearance, personality) mixes what a thing *is* with how it is *perceived*. Koe's later framework separates those two dimensions and then reconnects them. In 2022, however, the question is presented as open, and the chapter records it as such. It also records the change of position: the open question of 2022 becomes, by March 2024, a closed and layered definition (developed below). Koe gives no explicit reason for the change. The most plausible reading is that the entropy framework he was building in those years supplied the answer, since the definition he eventually gives is stated in terms of entropy.

#### Value requires development over time: the pearl in the oyster

The same 2022 video contains the first firm statement Koe makes about value, and he calls it "a universal principle": "value requires development over time." His image is a pearl. A pearl forms inside an oyster that is closed off to the world, and only after some time does the oyster open and reveal it. People believe value is "so readily available," Koe says, because of the attention game. Social media shows the tips, the conclusions and the polished results, but not the time spent developing the depth that produced them.

**Source:** Why Cheap Dopamine Prevents You From Becoming High Value.md (2022-10-09)

The principle matters for the rest of the chapter in two ways. First, it explains why the attention economy distorts the perception of value. What is visible is the pearl, so viewers underestimate the closed oyster. The same distortion returns in Section 31.5, where free online content is criticized for showing the high-performing topics and hiding the boring fundamentals. Second, it sets a condition on the person who wants to create value: depth comes first. This links the chapter to the "build to learn" process of Chapter 14 and to the realistic timelines of Chapter 30. When Koe warns buyers of education products that results take "three to five years" (Section 31.5), he is applying the pearl principle to his own customers.

#### Entropy as the driving force of evolution

The answer Koe eventually gives to "what is value" starts from a law that Chapter 5 introduced: entropy. In July 2023 he states it in its simplest form. "Entropy is that everything tends towards disorder": one day you will die, the sun will die out, the universe will die. Anything tangible eventually decomposes, unless humans attempt to reverse entropy. "That's all we're really doing here," he says, "is trying to solve the problems that lead to things falling apart." We try to extend our life on Earth. Even something as small as organizing a bookshelf after it gets dirty is done, in his reading, with the larger survival goal in the back of the mind: "we need to live as long as possible, we need to become immortal, and that's what really everyone is [trying] to do."

**Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Life (In 6-12 Months).md (2023-07-25)

**Complementary context:** In physics, the second law of thermodynamics states that the total entropy of an isolated system does not decrease over time. Living beings and human artifacts maintain local order only by consuming energy and exporting disorder to their surroundings. Koe uses the law as a general metaphor and organizing principle, as Chapter 5 explained. He does not present it as a technical claim about thermodynamics, and the bookshelf example should be read in that spirit: as an interpretation of human motivation, not as a measured fact.

In February 2024 Koe draws the strongest conclusion from the law. "Entropy is the driving force of evolution": since everything tends toward death, and we are aware of that, "out of fear we are pushing towards something related to immortality or youth extension, and problem solving is the way we do that." He goes further. Disorder is not just an obstacle to purpose; it is the condition of purpose. "If things didn't tend toward disorder we would not be in the situation we are today. We wouldn't have any motivation to do anything, we wouldn't have a reason to be here, we wouldn't have a purpose."

**Source:** Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md (2024-02-18)

This inversion is the key move of the section. Entropy usually appears in Koe's work as the enemy: the chaos of an unfocused mind, the decay of a neglected routine (Chapters 5 and 13). Here it becomes the source of meaning. A world without decay would offer no problems, and a world without problems would offer no purpose. Chapter 8 defined purpose as the most urgent problem, and this passage gives that definition its cosmic foundation. Problems exist because things fall apart, and solving them is what living beings do.

#### "Even to remain still we must advance": the unattributed quote and the tractor

Koe supports the idea with a quotation he reads aloud twice: in "The 4-Hour Workday" (January 2023) and again in a March 2024 compilation that reproduces the same passage. The transcript does not attribute it to anyone. "Evolution is forced on us by the fact that systems fall apart with time unless they become more efficient. We can't stop and remain in the same place; even to remain still we must advance." He uses the quote as "another reason why this productivity system is important." Standing still is not a neutral option. A system either becomes more efficient or decays, and that holds for a person, a routine and a business alike. It is the same corollary Chapter 5 drew from the entropy law: there is no such thing as staying the same.

He then adds an argument of his own. Humans have "a natural desire to know more, the curse of knowledge," which he describes as "an endless thirst to learn things that will aid in our survival." With unique minds and knowledge, "we build systems to solve problems that lead to better outcomes in all domains of life": society, culture and personal life. His example is agriculture. Carrying water and plowing fields took enormous physical energy, so humans "used our creativity to create a solution like a tractor and a system to use that energy more efficiently."

**Source:** The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md (2023-01-01); Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md (2024-03-21)

Two clarifications are needed. The first concerns attribution. The quotation is a third party's words, and the corpus never names its author. The argument about the thirst for knowledge and the tractor is Koe's own. The second concerns terminology. Koe uses "the curse of knowledge" in an idiosyncratic sense, and the corpus does not use it consistently. In these passages it names the desire to know more, a neutral or even positive drive. Elsewhere in the same 2024 compilation it names something negative: learning without questioning what one learns, and so absorbing the conventional game. The corpus never resolves whether "curse" is meant pejoratively. A reasonable reading is that the same drive serves both ends. The thirst to learn can condition a person, and it can also equip them to build their own systems.

**Complementary context:** In cognitive psychology and economics, "the curse of knowledge" usually names a bias: once people know something, they find it hard to imagine not knowing it, which makes experts poor at explaining to beginners. Koe's usage is unrelated to that standard meaning.

#### Speculation: the next phase of evolution

In July 2023 Koe pushes the idea toward the future, and he marks it explicitly as speculation. He says he has been "down rabbit holes," and recommends a short story, "The Gentle Seduction," as something that opens the mind to the potential of the future. He is "not sure what the next phase of evolution is," but he is "pretty sure" it will be chemical enhancement, as discussed in the biohacking community, which would bridge humans toward becoming cyborgs and living forever. For now, he says, the goal seems to be prolonging health with longevity practices, to the point of reversing aging. The sentence that ties this to the chapter is: "That's all we're doing with evolution: we're trying to reverse entropy and the problems that come with entropy." He then sets the topic aside "for another time."

**Source:** Focus Is A Superpower (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md (2023-07-11)

**Complementary context:** "The Gentle Seduction" is a science-fiction short story by Marc Stiegler (1989). It follows a woman who gradually adopts transformative technologies over a lifetime. The transcript does not name the author.

The passage is an opinion about the future, adapted from fiction and from a subculture. It is not a thesis Koe develops or defends. It belongs in the chapter because it shows how far he extends the entropy principle. Longevity, biohacking and the tractor are, for him, instances of the same activity: solving the problems that decay creates.

#### The 2024 answer: value is not labor

The pieces come together in a March 2024 video titled "The Value Equation." Koe begins with what value is *not*. The labor theory of value ("I worked one year on this, you owe me $100,000") is "not how value works." Instead, "value works by me giving you something useful, but if you don't see it as useful through persuasion then it's not valuable."

**Source:** The Value Equation How To Become A High-Status Individual.md (2024-03-17)

**Complementary context:** The labor theory of value belongs to classical political economy (Adam Smith, David Ricardo) and to Karl Marx. In its classical forms it holds that the value of a commodity is determined by the labor required to produce it. Koe does not engage with that technical tradition. He uses the name for an everyday attitude: the belief that effort entitles one to payment. Chapter 34 shows the same target in his critique of the employee mindset (payment by the hour). This book reports his use of the term and does not correct his reading of the economic theory.

The definition has two parts, and they become the two layers of the rest of the chapter. Something must be *useful*, which is a matter of substance. And it must be *seen as useful*, which is a matter of perception, achieved "through persuasion." Koe names them immediately. The *macronutrients of value* are the perspective, meaning how the value is framed and constructed. The *micronutrients of value* shape how the value is perceived. Section 31.3 reconstructs both. The dietary metaphor fits the definition: macronutrients are the bulk of a meal, and micronutrients are small additions without which the meal does not nourish.

#### Value as structured communication, sense-making and a signal of meaning

Koe then states what the framework lets a person do. It lets you change your life, help others for money, write, speak and think clearly, and create content, landing pages or marketing that sells. It lets you navigate relationships in a mutual way, and understand and move past emotions. The breadth of the list follows from the definition he gives next: "Value in this case is structured communication or sense making to the mind." Value is how a mind makes sense of the world, packaged and given to someone else. It is also a framework for creating solutions to problems, and he connects it to his picture of the good life: solve one problem, then the next, contributing to evolution as you go. He calls it the foundation of human behavior, business and communication.

The passage ends with a string of equivalences: "Value is truth, value is story, value is progress, value is transformation, value is reversing entropy, value is a signal of meaning in your psyche." He presents these terms as "synonymous or heavily related."

**Source:** The Value Equation How To Become A High-Status Individual.md (2024-03-17)

The list is better read as a set of angles on one object than as a set of strict synonyms. Koe does not argue that truth and story are identical. Each term ties the definition to a part of the book. *Truth* and *sense-making* tie it to the thinking of Chapter 17. *Story* ties it to the mind as a story machine (Chapter 23). *Progress* and *transformation* tie it to behavior change (Section 31.4). *Reversing entropy* ties it to Chapter 5. *A signal of meaning in your psyche* ties it to the subjective experience Koe calls "the click," treated in Section 31.4. A reader can test a claim of value against any of these angles. Does it make sense of something? Does it move someone from one state to a better one? Does it reduce disorder in someone's mind or life?

#### The value equation: reversing entropy by creating systems

The video closes with the formula that gives it its title: "Value is reversing entropy. Entropy is the decline into chaos. Entropy is reversed by creating systems to achieve goals by solving problems. That's the value equation."

**Source:** The Value Equation How To Become A High-Status Individual.md (2024-03-17)

The formula is a chain with four links: chaos is reversed through *systems*, which serve *goals*, which are reached by solving *problems*. Each link connects to earlier chapters. Systems connect to Chapter 12 (systematizing) and to the tractor example above. Goals connect to Chapters 6 and 8, where the goal is a lens that turns information into relevance. Problems connect to Chapter 8's definition of purpose. The formula therefore unifies the personal system of the book (anti-vision, vision, goals, projects, focused work) with the economic system (offers, products, marketing). Reversing entropy in one's own life and creating value for others are the same operation, applied to different targets.

Koe attaches a lifestyle to the formula, which he calls the *lifestyle of the 1%*. All high-level individuals, he says, reverse entropy daily. They pursue goals, solve problems, hypothesize, test, and create solutions to distribute. This demands constant learning, building and distributing value, and he calls it "literally the one thing that will set you apart." The "1%" here is a rhetorical marker of rarity, not a measured statistic.

**Complementary context:** Alex Hormozi, whom the architecture of this book lists among Koe's references on offers, popularized a different "value equation" in *$100M Offers* (2021): perceived value rises with the dream outcome and the perceived likelihood of achievement, and falls with time delay and effort. The units gathered for this chapter do not connect Koe's formula to Hormozi's. The two share a name and an interest in perceived value, but Koe's equation is a definition in terms of entropy, systems, goals and problems, not a ratio. Some of Hormozi's factors resemble Koe's micronutrients (proof raises perceived likelihood; risk reversal lowers perceived effort), but the corpus does not draw that parallel.

#### Evolution phases out labor work

The entropy definition has a consequence that Koe develops at length, and it explains part of his view of work and AI. If evolution means solving the problems that cause decay, and repetitive labor is one of the problems people hate most, then evolution will keep eliminating labor. The idea appears first in June 2022, in a plainer form: "Humans are lazy; they tend to phase out what they don't like doing." This is why, Koe argues, the people who make the most money or see the most success are creative people, not people in manual low-skill labor. He says the pattern shows across human evolution. When people got tired of plowing fields by hand, a creative person innovated and made a tractor, "expanding their mind until the imaginary idea of a tractor became an actual tractor," pulling together all available resources. Over time, things keep getting phased out.

**Source:** How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md (2022-06-25)

The tractor appears in both lines of argument, the 2023 productivity argument and the 2022 labor argument, and it does the same work in each. It is the model of a system that reverses entropy by spending less energy for the same result, and the model of a creative act that makes a whole class of labor unnecessary.

In the same video Koe gives a present-day example. He saw a tweet complaining about automated touch-screen ordering at McDonald's and other fast-food chains, which phase out cashiers because they save the company money. Many people see this as bad. But people who believe in themselves, or know they have higher potential and can learn new skills through self-education ("the endless ocean of resources online"), spending a few hours a day, are not worried, or are worried only a little at first. The lesson he draws: "consistently improve yourself and acquire high value skills and become a student of life." Doing so opens room for creativity in yourself, and your creative solutions open more opportunity and creativity for others. The creator economy is his example.

He then cites a source and adopts it. He has been studying Naval Ravikant and finds his philosophy "very aligned with mine." Naval's tweet reads: "If the work doesn't require creativity, delegate it, automate it, or leave it." Koe's interpretation: take personal responsibility for your future and increase your skill set, so that you are not phased out by "human nature's continuous creation of a better future."

**Source:** How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md (2022-06-25)

**Complementary context:** Naval Ravikant is an entrepreneur and investor (co-founder of AngelList) whose aphorisms on wealth, leverage and specific knowledge, collected in a 2018 tweetstorm titled "How to Get Rich (without getting lucky)," are among the most adapted sources in Koe's corpus (see Chapters 20, 25 and 34).

#### "A gradual erosion of human labor"

In April 2023 Koe gives the idea a name borrowed from a third party. He reads a tweet from Zero HP Lovecraft, an account he finds interesting but does not follow. The tweet says that the real AI singularity, if it happens, will not look like a science-fiction doomsday. The real, and probably inevitable, form is "a gradual erosion of human labor."

**Source:** Self Improvers Are Creating Their Own Careers (The New Economy).md (2023-04-16)

**Complementary context:** Zero HP Lovecraft is the pseudonym of an online writer of speculative fiction and commentary, known mainly on Twitter/X. The phrase is his. The use Koe makes of it, as a description of the evolutionary process he has been describing, is Koe's.

In the same video Koe states his own position, and in 2023 it is enthusiastic: he "only see[s] good" in automation and AI. "A decline in labor work means an incline in creative and knowledge work for people that choose to continue working." He considers universal basic income (UBI) and is skeptical of the assumption behind it, that people with free time will naturally self-actualize: "either they'll have more time to self-actualize or they'll have more time to destroy their lives." People who want control can use the moment. Between the two futures he prefers "the AI route" to UBI, "because it forces people to get creative if they want to survive; it forces people to be human."

**Source:** Self Improvers Are Creating Their Own Careers (The New Economy).md (2023-04-16)

The phrase "it forces people to be human" assumes a definition of the human that the next units make explicit: to be human is to create. The preference for pressure over provision mirrors Koe's ideas about tactical stress (Chapter 10) and about money or pain on the line (Section 31.4). In each case, a constraint is what produces action.

#### Bet on the mind: evolution as problem-solving

In February 2024 Koe restates the argument directly in terms of entropy. "Evolution is the process of solving problems that reverse entropy. Labor work and long work days are a massive problem; people hate it, so social media, the internet and artificial intelligence were born." AI, he says, "will solve the problem of overwork potentially in this lifetime, especially if we solve the problem of aging." His practical conclusion: "You are better off banking on the creative ability of your mind than you are going into a labor work job." He makes an explicit exception: "If you want quick cash then yes, that is actually a very good option right now." For the future, though, and "to earn unlimited because your intelligence is scalable infinitely," he advises choosing work that allows a four-hour workday (Chapter 12) and contributing "your time, energy and focus into the aspects of reality that you want to see flourish."

**Source:** Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md (2024-02-18)

A week later he answers an objection he expects. Society needs construction workers, plumbers and labor workers. Koe agrees, but suggests that "maybe in our lifetime those jobs won't exist." He then asks two questions and answers them separately. Should everyone bank on creative work for meaning and income? No. Should smart people? Yes. With robotics, and factories like Tesla's being over 75% automated (his figure, given without a source), he does not see why robots cannot solve labor. Personal robots doing laundry and cleaning seem to him not far off, which would leave people free to focus on creative work and self-actualization. Will everyone pursue that? No: "some people are going to end up like Wall-E," mainlining Coca-Cola in a floating lounge chair with dopamine channels. His advice is to stop worrying about it. Wise people who flow with nature cannot succumb to that life, because "once you've had that taste of self-actualization and flow and meaningful living, there's no going back." He calls that the awakened point.

He states the principle behind the whole argument in one line: "Success is reserved for the creative; your psyche craves creativity." Creating feels good. By all measures, creating something of your own "lights your brain on fire," and "that is enough of a sign that humans are meant to do this."

**Source:** Zero To $1 Million As A One-Person Business (Working 2-4 Hours A Day).md (2024-02-25)

**Complementary context:** *WALL-E* (Pixar, 2008) depicts a future in which humans live on a spaceship in hover-chairs, consuming screens and drinks while robots do all the work. Koe uses the image as shorthand for automation without self-actualization.

Several distinctions keep this argument from being read as a universal prescription. First, Koe separates the advice by person and horizon: quick cash favors labor now, and the long run favors creative work for those able to pursue it. Second, the claim that "humans are meant to do this" rests on felt experience ("lights your brain on fire"), which is an interpretation rather than evidence. Third, the "Wall-E" outcome is admitted as real for some people. The argument does not promise that automation liberates everyone. It claims that automation rewards those who choose creative work.

#### If a machine can replace your job

By May 2025 the same argument has a sharper edge. Koe addresses the comfortable majority who complain about AI taking jobs: "what's the issue? Why are you complaining?" Average 9-to-5 jobs, he says, suck. He does not mean the 0.1% with nap pods at Google. "We've collectively hated jobs for decades." "Evolution solves problems": one of life's most painful problems, working a job, is being solved, and people are angry about it. In his reading, they are presented with one of the greatest opportunities of a lifetime and fall into "the most cliché trap of comfort and playing victim." They hold on to an old way of life "when all good things aren't permanent." Then he states the diagnostic: "If you work a job that a machine can replace, your life probably lacks novelty and meaning and fulfillment and challenge and complexity and continuous growth and learning." That, he says, is a massive signal to do something new.

**Source:** The Future Of Work (How To Become AI-First).md (2025-05-25)

Koe asserts that this is not opinion but "documented psychological patterns." The transcript cites no study, and the claim should be treated as his interpretation. It is consistent with the flow research he draws on elsewhere (Chapter 10), where novelty, challenge and growth are conditions of optimal experience, but it is not demonstrated in the passage.

In February 2026 the tone changes, and the change should be recorded. Koe distinguishes two types of work. *Mundane busy work* is repetitive and mechanical, like an industrial assembly line, and he does not mind it going away. He says so "in the kindest way possible," which acknowledges that livelihoods are at stake. *Creative work* is "the ability to see reality from your own lens, pull it into your mind, synthesize things together, create something, share it with another person, and then get feedback for your contribution in the form of money" or another form of value. "That's where humans find meaning."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (& The New High-Income Skill Stack).md (2026-02-12)

The 2026 definition of creative work is a compact version of the value chain this chapter builds. It moves from perception (seeing reality through one's own lens), through synthesis (making sense), creation (packaging) and sharing (distribution), to feedback in money (value exchange). It also shows the evolution of Koe's position on automation and employment, which the corpus records across several videos:

| Period | Position on labor and AI | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| 2022–2023 | Humans phase out what they dislike; "I only see good"; labor is "on the horizon of complete removal" | Enthusiastic |
| Early 2024 | "Maybe in our lifetime those jobs won't exist"; smart people should bank on creative work; quick cash still favors labor | Confident, with exceptions |
| 2024 | "Computers will only replace those who deserve to be replaced" (a line recorded elsewhere in the corpus) | Harsh |
| 2025 | A job a machine can replace signals a life lacking meaning; complaint is "playing victim" | Provocative |
| 2025–2026 | AI's impact "probably not going to be as drastic as many people made it out to seem, myself included"; busy work goes away "in the kindest way possible"; livelihoods acknowledged | Moderated, self-correcting |

Two changes are visible. Koe corrects his own estimate of how drastic the change will be. And he moves from blaming the person who gets replaced toward a broader framing, in which AI threatens meaning as well as jobs, with more care for people whose circumstances give them less control. He gives no explicit reason for either change. The underlying thesis does not change: evolution solves the problem of labor, and creative work is where meaning and value remain.

#### "If you are not a builder, you are going to be built over"

Koe's shortest formulation of the consequence comes from October 2022, in the video that introduced his idea of the *value creator*. Humans think and build. We build tools and solutions that make life easier, and "this is never going to stop." So "if you are not a builder then you are going to be built over, or you are going to build for someone else." He says this introduction "lays the framework" for everything that follows.

**Source:** The Rise Of The Value Creator (A Career Path For Polymaths & Self-Improvers).md (2022-10-31)

The sentence turns the entropy argument into a personal choice with three options. You can build, which means reversing entropy through your own systems. You can be built over, which means having your work absorbed by someone else's system, the way the tractor absorbed manual plowing. Or you can build for someone else, contributing your energy to systems you do not own, which is Koe's description of employment (Chapter 2). *Builder* is a common word that Koe uses in a specific sense: a person who always has a project aligned with what they are learning (Chapter 14), and who therefore produces systems rather than only consuming them. Chapter 35 develops the value creator as a role.

#### Spirituality as your part in the whole

The last application of the entropy law is the most surprising one, and Koe develops it in several videos between 2023 and 2024: spirituality. The context is his argument against people who reject money and business on spiritual grounds.

In November 2023 he starts from their own premise. "If the entire goal of spirituality is connection to something larger than yourself, and money in the material world is a way to connect with society as a whole and distribute goods and information that benefits them," then rejecting money contradicts spirituality. Many spiritual people, he says, think that buying a product or service cannot help a specific person. When you peel the belief back, "you know that that's wrong, but the unconscious assumption is just that everything related to money is evil, when everything near you, you watching this right now, is only possible because of money."

From there he goes further. "You are unconsciously allowing the entire universe to tilt in the hands of evil by not becoming a conscious business owner that impacts his customers, employees, readers, economy and the rest to infinity." And then he gives his definition: "Spirituality is understanding your part in the whole of the universe. Spirituality is not disconnecting from that whole to live in the woods and letting the universe decline into chaos thanks to entropy. Spirituality then can be considered and felt when you attempt to reverse entropy by putting effort and energy into the progress and evolution of humanity."

Later in the same video he generalizes about human nature: "To be human is to transcend, expand and create." He also observes that "the world is going mental." As a species, "we are transcending the physical thanks to technology, science and computational power; we are unifying as a species, the unity of consciousness through technology." Then he asks: "Would that have been possible without money? Was there any other way to get to this point of technological advancement without money? Therefore something that is extremely good for humanity is the byproduct of money, and you hate money, and you can't see that as something that is limiting you severely."

**Source:** People Dumber Than You Are Making Millions.md (2023-11-12)

The chain of the argument runs as follows. Spirituality is connection to a larger whole. Money is a means of connection to the social whole. Therefore rejecting money in the name of spirituality is self-contradictory. The positive definition follows: spirituality is felt in the effort to reverse entropy for humanity. The *conscious business owner* is Koe's term for the person who does this through business, impacting customers, employees, readers and the economy, and through them more people "to infinity." The claim that technological unification is "a byproduct of money" is a historical interpretation offered rhetorically. Its force lies in the question it poses to the money-hating spiritual person, not in any detailed historical argument.

In September 2024 the definition gains a second clause. "Purpose does not exist without problems." Purpose lies at "the polar end" of problems, "so by solving a problem you pursue a purpose; you discover your part in something greater than yourself. That's what spirituality is: discovering your part in the whole, transcending that whole to the next, and then the next, until you reach your life's purpose of contribution." This ties money, as a tool for solving problems, to spirituality.

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md (2024-09-29)

The change between the two definitions is a refinement. In 2023 spirituality is *understanding* your part in the whole, felt when you reverse entropy. In 2024 it is *discovering* your part, then *transcending* that whole toward the next, in a sequence that ends in contribution. The 2024 version adds movement through levels, which echoes the developmental maps of Chapter 38 (transcend and include) and the purpose levels of Chapter 9 (survival, status, creativity, contribution). Both versions are compatible: the second contains the first and gives it a direction.

#### The urge to build: "spirituality business"

In February 2024 Koe answers a common position: that past a certain point one does not need to keep making money. People who think this, he says, usually have a missing key in their life. They can improve other things, but "the urge to build is missing, that is the urge to help human evolution and contribute to humanity," broadly, "not just your local circle but on a universal level." "That's called spirituality business." At that stage, he adds, you have built so much leverage and distribution that you can sell whatever you want. This, he says, is why he is building software and plans to write three books by the end of the following year.

**Source:** Zero To $1 Million As A One-Person Business (Working 2-4 Hours A Day).md (2024-02-25)

*Spirituality business* and *urge to build* are coined terms that appear once in the corpus. They name the stage where money stops being the motive for building but remains the means of contribution. Chapter 34 takes up the related question of "enough" in wealth, and this passage gives Koe's answer from the side of meaning. The person who has enough money may still lack the drive to contribute, and that lack, not the money, is the problem.

#### Status, power and the virtue-signaling objection

The last two units of the section, from September 2024, address an objection to everything above: that pursuing status, power and influence is spiritually suspect. Koe's answer has two steps.

First, a principle. Everyone has some degree of status, power or influence, and everyone is trying to get them. "They become bad things when done for unconscious reasons," that is, when you do not understand the big-picture, overarching impact of your pursuit of power and persuasion. He illustrates with the video itself: "This entire video could be completely negative, but if it has a positive impact on your life because of that, does that make it a bad thing?"

Second, an argument about the spiritual strategy of withdrawal. To be perceived as valuable you need power, influence, some form of status. If your strategy for success is being "nice, noble and spiritual," mean people will push you aside and have more impact. And if you let that happen, "you aren't as spiritual as you think you are," because spirituality is being part of something greater than yourself and, in a sense, taking responsibility for that thing and its outcome. Going to the woods to meditate and find God can be good for a time, but then you come back and reintegrate with society. Koe ends with a reversal: "the people who virtue signal that power games are bad are playing their own power game." He calls it selfish, lacking contribution to humanity, and a self-deception. Such people do nothing but preach how others should change, and this distracts them from looking in the mirror.

**Source:** 4 Frameworks To Write Follow-Worthy Content (+ Kortex Template).md (2024-09-08)

The argument connects to the definition of value through the phrase "to be perceived as valuable." Value needs perception (Section 31.2), perception needs attention, and attention, in a crowded world, goes to people with status and influence. A person who refuses those means on principle limits how much entropy they can reverse for others. The phrase "power games" is Koe's own. His claim that critics of power games play their own is a psychological interpretation of their motives, offered polemically. A reader can grant the structural point (that refusing influence is not neutral) without accepting the characterization of every critic.

#### One law, four applications

The section has shown one principle applied in four domains. The table summarizes them.

| Domain | What decays (entropy) | What reverses it | Koe's formulation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evolution and survival | Bodies, things, the universe tend to disorder and death | Problem-solving, efficient systems (the tractor), longevity | "Entropy is the driving force of evolution" |
| The value of a product | Chaos in a person's mind or life (a problem) | A system that achieves a goal by solving a problem | "Value is reversing entropy… that's the value equation" |
| The end of labor work | Human energy spent on repetitive, hated labor | Technology that phases labor out; creative work | "Evolution is the process of solving problems that reverse entropy" |
| Spirituality | The universe "declining into chaos" if one withdraws | Energy invested in humanity's progress; contribution | "Spirituality… can be felt when you attempt to reverse entropy" |

The unification has a practical point that Section 31.3 develops. If value is the reversal of entropy, then the anatomy of any valuable thing should mirror the anatomy of entropy reversal: a problem (disorder), a goal (the ordered state) and a process (the system that bridges them). That is exactly the structure of the "macronutrients of value." Before reaching it, though, the chapter has to deal with the second half of the 2024 definition: something useful is valuable only if it is *seen* as useful.

### 31.2 Value Is Perception

#### "Marketing is perception, value is perception"

The second layer of the definition is stated most directly in August 2023. Koe calls marketing and sales, "paired with writing and speaking," the way "you create value." He defines their role carefully. Marketing and sales are "the psychological kind of cortex or meta structure of what you're going to write or speak so that it is impactful and persuasive and valuable, because marketing is perception, value is perception." Using marketing principles or psychology to frame what you say means catching attention, holding it, and making what you say valuable and understandable. That, he says, "is the most valuable skill."

**Source:** I Had To Learn These High-Income Skills (If I Wanted To Make Money).md (2023-08-16)

The image of a *cortex* or *meta structure* is the key. Marketing is not a separate activity added after the content is made. It is the outer layer that organizes how the content will be received, much as the cortex organizes the brain's processing. The content is the useful substance. The meta structure decides whether that substance reaches anyone. Koe's formula "value is perception" should therefore be read precisely. He is not claiming that substance is irrelevant; Section 31.1 required usefulness, and Section 31.4 requires behavior change. He is claiming that substance which is not perceived produces no value *for the person who fails to perceive it*.

In November 2023 he draws the consequence for anyone who wants something in return. "If value is perception and other people can't perceive what you have to offer as valuable, then you're not going to get the value that you want to receive," which in most cases is money. The structure is the same everywhere, he argues. In a relationship or in business, approaching someone at a bar or in the DMs, "it's all the same thing": you have cultivated value to offer, and you need to persuade the other person to see it so that they give you what you want in return. For online writers, creators and one-person businesses, persuasive communication is in everything. Writing must be persuasive to be shared, and sharing is the value a writer wants from readers in exchange. Landing pages, DMs, a text to a friend, a tweet, an Instagram post, a YouTube video: everything "has to follow a general persuasive structure."

**Source:** The Most Important High-Income Skill To Learn (In The Next 10 Years).md (2023-11-05)

Value appears here as an exchange with two sides, and the currency need not be money. A reader "pays" a writer with a share, a date "pays" with a yes, a customer pays with money. In each case what is received depends on what the other side perceives. This symmetric framing returns in Section 31.4 as "value exchange" and the "vessel of valuable information."

#### Master perception: "dopamine dealers"

The earliest and most provocative version of the idea comes from October 2022, in the same video that asked what value is. If you want to master marketing, Koe says, you must "master perception," because "a better product will always lose to a product that people think is better." Marketers know this well: "they are dopamine dealers." So are content creators, since headlines exist to capture attention. Much of what we see, he says, is misperceived, or polished on the outside to catch attention with something new and novel. His examples are a social media influencer, an article headline and a YouTube title. The polish excites you, or makes you wonder "what do I not have, what can I have that will make my life better." Average people understand the game too. The guy going to the club on Saturday night puts on his best floral shirt, slicks his hair back, and puts on a watch and a perfume marketed to help him get more girls.

**Source:** Why Cheap Dopamine Prevents You From Becoming High Value.md (2022-10-09)

The passage connects value to Chapter 11's account of dopamine as the "molecule of more": the signal of something relevant to a goal, or the promise of something better. Marketers and creators trade in that signal, which is why Koe calls them dealers. The phrase is deliberately unflattering, and it includes himself. The man getting ready for the club shows that the game of perception is not a corporate invention. It is ordinary social behavior, and everyone presents their value so that others can perceive it. The principle "a better product will always lose to a product that people think is better" is a strong rule of thumb, not a law. It describes a tendency in competitive markets, and Koe offers it without data. It is also the most direct expression of the risk that Section 31.4 addresses: perception can be manipulated, and the ethics of persuasion (Chapter 32) exist because of that.

#### Subjective and shifting: perception across time

In March 2024 Koe adds the dimension of time. "The perception of value is subjective; it's dependent on the individual and it shifts when times shift, when the world evolves." His example is the printing press. It was seen as ultra valuable when first invented, but now that it has been "taken over by radio, TV, social media," we no longer see it as that valuable. With the advent of AI, he says, "we may not know what the future of value looks like," but if you understand how to create value you can adapt. He refers the viewer to his earlier "value equation" video (which he says was retitled) for the method.

**Source:** Working Hard Won't Make You Rich (Do This Instead).md (2024-03-31)

In July 2024 he states the practical rule that follows. "Value evolves with time, value is perception, meaning you have to give the right thing to the right person for them to see it as valuable or for them to pay you money." His example is generational: "a boomer will see gold as more valuable than Bitcoin," and will be less persuaded otherwise, unless you get in front of them "with persuasive media where they hang out." He reminds the viewer that creating the value itself was already covered: "solve your own problems and sell the solution, and have a vision and goals so you can actually identify those problems."

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md (2024-07-13)

Two operations are implied, and they correspond to the two layers of value. *Creating* value depends on a vision and goals, because they reveal the problems worth solving (Chapters 7–8 and 28). *Delivering* perceived value depends on matching the thing to the person and reaching them where they are. That second operation has three parts: the right thing, the right person, and the right place ("where they hang out"). The boomer example shows that a correct thing, such as Bitcoin in Koe's implied view, can still fail to be valuable to a specific person until persuasion has done its work.

#### Identity and the extended process of persuasion

The same March 2024 video gives the deepest version of the idea, because it ties perception to identity, the subject of Part II. "If I'm a bodybuilder trying to sell to a Gothic person or a distance runner, the things that I find valuable from the identity that I've forged are probably not going to be seen as valuable to them, unless I recreate their identity through content and writing." That recreation, he says, "is just an extended process of persuasion to get them to change their identity and eventually see the value in my product." He then summarizes on two levels: "value changes based on just the general situation, culture and society we're in, and perception of value changes based on the identity you place whatever it is that is supposed to be valuable in front of."

**Source:** Working Hard Won't Make You Rich (Do This Instead).md (2024-03-31)

The example gathers several threads of the book. Chapter 3 defined identity as a network of goals and beliefs that filters perception. If identity filters perception, it also filters perceived value. The bodybuilder's product (training, nutrition, aesthetics) passes through the bodybuilder's filter as valuable, and through the goth's or the runner's filter as irrelevant or even repellent. The only way to make it valuable to them is to change the filter. Koe calls this an *extended* process because identity changes slowly (Chapter 4). A single ad cannot do it, but months of content and writing can. This is the economic reason behind the long-form, long-term content strategy of Chapters 24–26, and it anticipates the role of awareness levels in Chapter 32.

#### Positioning: speaking to an identity

Koe's term for the practical side of this is *positioning*, an industry word he uses with a specific meaning: presenting an offer in a way fitted to the identity of the customer. In April 2023 he gives a negative example. Suppose you try to make a gamer aware of a health problem. The gamer identifies with being a gamer and drinking Mountain Dew or G Fuel, and you say "you're unhealthy." They will call you an idiot. Koe characterizes gamers as closed-minded "in most cases," while acknowledging some open-minded ones. The lesson: "You have to position the product in a very specific way to speak to that person; you have to understand your customer."

**Source:** Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md (2023-04-09)

The gamer case is a direct application of Chapter 1's account of the closed mind. A belief that has become part of an identity is defended like the body itself (Chapter 3's law of conceptual survival). A direct attack on it triggers defense, not reconsideration. Positioning goes around the defense. It finds a frame from which the product serves something the identity already wants. For the gamer, that might be performance, focus or energy rather than "health." Koe's generalization about gamers is his own impression, and the principle does not depend on it.

A small autobiographical note in February 2023 shows that positioning works on the seller's side too. Koe was at one point a "brand advisor" and at another a "marketing consultant": "they're all kind of the same thing, it's just dependent on who I'm targeting and how I position the actual offer." He used social media as his way of attracting traffic because it is so big that "there isn't really any other choice," and he says he loves working with creators.

**Source:** How To Write Authentic Content (Social Media Growth 101).md (2023-02-05)

The same service under two names illustrates the principle exactly. The substance (the macronutrient) stays fixed while the frame (the perception) changes with the target. The name of an offer is itself a positioning decision, which anticipates the "concept" micronutrient of Section 31.3.

#### Awareness: the mind that can receive

In September 2024 Koe adds a third factor, alongside era and identity: the moment a person is in, defined by their awareness of a problem. "Value is perception; one man's trash is another man's treasure." Some people see his video as unimportant, while others who have watched "till right now" see it as valuable: "that's just how the market works." Many will watch five seconds and say "that's stupid, that's a scam, I'm out." Others "are actually going to be changed by it because their mind is in the place where they can receive it." They are "going through a problem in their life, they are aware of that problem to the point of being able to receive the information and not write it off."

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md (2024-09-29)

The phrase "their mind is in the place where they can receive it" links the perception of value to the *level of mind* (Chapter 4) and to the goal as a lens (Chapter 6). Information becomes relevant only to a mind that has a goal or a problem it serves. A person who is not aware of the problem perceives the solution as noise, or even as a threat. This is why Koe's formula for marketing (Section 31.4) begins with making people aware of a problem. It is also why Chapter 32 treats the five levels of awareness as the first variable of any persuasive message.

#### Buying a worldview

A final unit, from April 2023, explains why the same information can be worth more coming from one person than from another. Purchasing information from individuals, Koe says, is "like buying a book: I get to adopt their worldview." You learn skills from the people who have practiced them most and who hold the underlying philosophy you resonate with, so you learn the skill better. Schools do not do that; their method is "learn this, learn this, learn this." When you learn from Koe, you learn the way he likes to teach, which is usually philosophical with spiritual concepts baked in. That style resonates with certain people much better than with others, "so I can help those people more, and that is what creates value."

**Source:** Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md (2023-04-30)

This is the bridge between "value is perception" and "you are the niche" (Chapter 19). If perception depends on identity, then the seller's identity is part of the product. The same skill taught through a different worldview reaches a different audience, and for that audience it is worth more. Resonance is not an ornament on top of the information. In Koe's account, it is "what creates value." Section 31.5 uses the same logic to answer the objection that "there's already enough information out there."

#### Three layers of perceived value

The section's units can be arranged as three layers that together decide whether a useful thing is perceived as valuable.

| Layer | What varies | Koe's example | Consequence for the creator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Era and culture (macro) | What a society finds valuable at a given time | The printing press; gold vs. Bitcoin for a boomer | Understand how value is created, not only what is valued now, so you can adapt |
| Identity (individual) | The filter of beliefs and goals through which a person looks | The bodybuilder selling to a goth or a runner; the gamer and Mountain Dew | Position the offer for a specific identity; change identities slowly through content |
| Awareness (moment) | Whether the person is aware of the problem the thing solves | Viewers who leave after five seconds vs. viewers who are changed | Raise awareness of the problem before presenting the solution |

A fourth factor runs across all three: the seller. The worldview the seller embodies, and how it resonates with the buyer, change how much the same information is worth. Taken together, the layers explain why Koe can say, without contradiction, that value is something useful *and* that value is perception. Usefulness is necessary, but whether the useful thing produces an exchange depends on these layers. The next section turns this analysis into a method for constructing perceived value deliberately.

### 31.3 The Creation of Value

#### Value creation: the skill behind 95% of the results

If value has a substance (usefulness) and a perception, then creating value is a skill that can be learned. In April 2023 Koe names it. Alongside all the skills he has discussed, "there is one skill that is responsible for 95 percent of the results of my business success." It contributed most to his brand, sales, content and revenue growth over three to four years: "I call this skill value creation." His motto for it is: "do what you love, but study psychology, marketing and sales so you don't become a starving artist." The eight-step framework he presents in that video is, he says, what brought his business past $1M in revenue ("closing in on 2 million") in three years.

**Source:** Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md (2023-04-09)

The 95% figure is Koe's self-assessment, not a measurement, and it works as emphasis. The motto is more precise. *Starving artist* is a common phrase that Koe uses repeatedly and in a specific sense: the person who does what they love without understanding how value is perceived, and therefore cannot sustain the work. The cure is not to abandon what one loves but to stack the skills that make it perceivable. This connects to the skill stack of Chapter 35 and to the 2026 definition of creative work in Section 31.1, which ends in "feedback… in the form of money."

#### Universal patterns and the three drivers of behavior

Koe grounds value creation in a short metaphysical argument, and he flags it as such ("yes, I'm getting spiritual here"). "Every money making skill that people tell you to learn is linked with universal patterns and human psychology." Universal patterns are what everything is built from. "The essence of being is how knowledge was constructed: knowledge is second order being, and spirit is first order, or in other words experience." We experience something before we label it. Then we turn the experience into knowledge and share it, and everyone operates and communicates through that knowledge, forming a social fabric and a civilization. Skills that are not linked to human psychology and universal patterns do not make money. His example is Photoshop art, which "isn't inherently persuasive" or does not communicate the value of something people desire. In that case one must stack skills: "stack value creation onto the skills you want to monetize."

He then names the patterns. At the base of all human behavior, "including people pulling out their credit cards to pay you," there are three drives. The first is *survival*: the desire to replicate information in your genes and your consciousness, physically and mentally. The second is *evolution*: "the desire to outpace destruction through creation." The third is *transformation*: the desire to achieve a higher quality of experience. He treats the three as synonymous for the rest of the video. Storytelling, games, music and architecture, indeed anything created, mimic these patterns, because everything we create is "of being from reality." That is why the patterns can be observed and dissected in any creation.

**Source:** Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md (2023-04-09)

The three drives restate Section 31.1 in psychological terms. "Outpace destruction through creation" is the reversal of entropy described as a desire. Survival here includes the survival of information in consciousness, which is Chapter 3's law of conceptual survival. Transformation is the subject of Section 31.4. The distinction between *first-order being* (experience) and *second-order being* (knowledge) explains why Koe insists that value comes from lived experience. Knowledge that was never experienced is a copy of a copy, and it lacks the specificity that makes a solution perceivable as unique. This is a philosophical framework Koe proposes, not an empirical theory, and he presents it as his own interpretation.

#### The eight steps: "marketing Legos" for turning dirt into gold

The core of the 2023 video is a framework of eight steps. Koe asks the viewer to treat them "as marketing firepower or marketing Legos": pieces to use in landing pages, sales pages, opt-in pages, website content and a brand bio. The reason is that one must capture attention, hold it, and deliver value on the attention captured. The steps are "steps from turning dirt into gold because marketing is perception." In his words, it does not matter how good the product is; it matters how important and valuable it is perceived to be. The eight steps illustrate *perceived value*. They assume the reader is learning to create an offer, improve an offer, or promote an existing product.

**Source:** Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md (2023-04-09)

The phrase "it doesn't matter how good your product is" should not be read as the whole theory. It is the 2023 emphasis of a framework that, in 2024, explicitly requires usefulness *and* perception (Section 31.1). In 2023 the video's object is perception, and it pushes that side to the limit.

The eight steps, in the order Koe gives them, are as follows.

| Step | Name | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Level of awareness | Identify how aware the target is of the problem and its solutions (five levels, treated in Chapter 32) |
| 2 | Positioning through the eight human desires | Attack the problem from a specific angle rooted in a desire "impossible to ignore" |
| 3 | Big problem | Identify a big problem from the chosen level and angle, and agitate it |
| 4 | Unique mechanism | Present the solution as one's own system, built from solving one's own problem, with a compelling name |
| 5 | Bullet spray benefits | List as many compelling benefits as possible; one may be the reason to buy |
| 6 | Proof | Show that the result is possible: testimonials, or personal experience and others' data |
| 7 | Big idea | Summarize the value in one sentence |
| 8 | Risk reversal and end result | State the desirable outcome and reduce the risk of trying |

Steps 1 and 4 are developed elsewhere in the book: the levels of awareness in Chapter 32, and the unique mechanism as personal system in Chapters 27 and 33. The remaining steps are reconstructed here.

#### Step 2: the eight human desires

Koe says much of this step comes from Digital Economics, his masterclass on productizing yourself. He wants to go deeper than the *Eternal markets* of Chapter 19 (health, wealth, relationships). There are, he says, "eight human desires that are impossible to ignore," and all content, copy, sales and landing pages must be framed from a problem that resides in one of them: "you have to find a problem and then attack it from a specific angle; this is positioning, or creating an angle." The eight are: survival; life enjoyment; freedom from fear; sexual companionship; comfort and clarity; perceived status; safety of community; and social acceptance. Koe does not attribute the list to any source.

He gives two examples. A make-money-online product can be framed from the status angle ("I live this fancy lifestyle and I have this rich car") or from the life-enjoyment angle, which he calls "a bit less scammy" ("here's how the quality of your life is going to increase from learning this skill"). Then he proposes an exercise with his own course, 2 Hour Writer. It could be framed through survival: you need high-value skills to survive and make an income in the modern world. It could even be framed through sexual companionship: build a brand, and from social authority DM whoever you want. Of that second option he says it is "not the smartest way to write your copy, but you could."

**Source:** Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md (2023-04-09)

**Complementary context:** Lists of core human desires are common in the direct-response copywriting tradition. Drew Eric Whitman's *Cashvertising* (2008), for example, lists "Life-Force 8" desires that overlap closely with Koe's list. The corpus does not name any source, so the resemblance is noted here without attributing a borrowing.

Positioning in Section 31.2 meant fitting an offer to an identity. Here it gets a concrete mechanism: choosing which desire the problem will be framed through. The same product changes perceived value depending on the angle. Koe's remark that one angle is "less scammy" than another shows that the choice is also ethical, a point Chapter 32 develops. In 2026 Koe condenses the eight desires into "three tensions" (survival, identity, progress), operated by five psychological levers. He says the reason is to make the framework actionable, and the newer version contains the older one. Chapter 32 presents that version.

#### Step 3: the big problem, and why agitation matters

From the awareness level and the chosen angle, one identifies "a big problem" to start the writing, speaking, offers, content, products and promotions. Koe's example combines level 1 (unaware) with the perceived-status angle. The big problem becomes not having a six-figure income. Then one agitates it: show how it affects who they can date and where they can live, possibly whether they can pay bills comfortably (which crosses into the survival desire), and "how the world naturally lacks a level of respect for them." He adds, candidly: "I don't know if this is true… we're painting a picture here."

**Source:** Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md (2023-04-09)

The admission "I don't know if this is true" is worth noting. In agitation, Koe treats the picture as a persuasive construction rather than a factual claim. That raises the question of honesty in persuasion, which Section 31.4 (the education brand's responsibility) and Chapter 32 (the ethics of persuasion) address. Section 31.4 also returns to agitation under the name "amplifying the problem," which Koe later calls "probably the most important part" of any persuasive piece.

#### Step 5: bullet spray benefits

With the big problem and the unique mechanism in place, the task is to help people see why the product applies to them and how it benefits their lives. Koe's favorite way is bullet points: "bullet spray them with it." His analogy is the list tweet, such as "five steps to a better life: go on a walk…" A reader may not agree with the first item or resonate with the second, but the third makes them like and retweet. Benefits work the same way. List as many compelling benefits as you can, because "just one of those may be the reason that they buy."

**Source:** Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md (2023-04-09)

The logic follows from Section 31.2. Because perception varies by identity and moment, a single benefit will miss most readers. A spray of benefits multiplies the chances that one of them meets a particular reader's filter. The list is a way of addressing several identities at once.

#### Step 6: proof when you have no testimonials

Social proof and testimonials matter, Koe says, but many people lack them at first. Once people buy, getting testimonials should be "your full-time job." People are afraid to sell without them. But you have your personal experience, and you have the experience of others in the form of statistics and anecdotes you can research online. That is why he starts 90% of his videos or newsletters with a personal experience that illustrates the problem, followed by his story of how he overcame it. "Everyone's first product can just be something that they created for themselves": talk about your experience, the problem and how you overcame it, and add testimonials to the landing page later. "You may make less sales at the beginning and that's fine; it's all a progression."

**Source:** Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md (2023-04-09)

The sentence "everyone's first product can just be something that they created for themselves" connects proof to "solve your own problems and sell the solution" (Chapter 28). The founder's own transformation is the first proof. It is weaker than a hundred testimonials, but it is honest and available on day one.

#### Step 7: the big idea

The *big idea* is "a one sentence summary of everything else that we just listed… that illustrates the value of what you're providing." The most popular quotes, the content you like most, and the parts of the video that excited you are, Koe says, big ideas. His examples: "Bitcoin is the digital currency of the future," "Uber is the modern taxi service," "ChatGPT is [changing] everything." The big idea can overlap with the big problem or with the name of the unique mechanism, or it can be one of them. Big ideas are found by hunting for them and making them your own. He refers to his video on how to stop forgetting what you read, and laments that it got "abysmal views."

**Source:** Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md (2023-04-09)

**Complementary context:** The "big idea" is a classic concept of twentieth-century advertising, associated with figures such as David Ogilvy and the direct-response tradition. Koe uses it without naming a source.

The big idea compresses the whole value proposition into something that can travel. It anticipates the 2024 "concept" micronutrient (below) and the idea hunting of Chapter 22.

#### Step 8: risk reversal and the end result

The final step brings together the problem (positioned to a level), the mechanism and the benefits, and asks two questions: what is the clear, desirable outcome, and what is on the line if the buyer does not reach it? A compelling risk reversal helps "close the most deals at the beginning of your journey." Most people use a money-back guarantee, but Koe suggests playing creatively between the desirable end result and the risk reversal. His example is a dating product: "if you don't land five dates in 60 days, we refund your money."

The guarantee puts pressure on the seller to deliver, especially as a beginner. It does not matter that you have not achieved those results for others before, he says; that is why you refund. Trying your hardest teaches you what is possible, and iterations improve the product. Then he adds *caveats*: refund only if the buyer executes and proves every action, for example by getting the number and sending screenshots of the texts. If they execute, it is almost impossible to fail. He calls it "a win-win": "the pressure is put on you to deliver, and the pressure is put on them to actually act on what they purchase," "because most people don't do that."

**Source:** Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md (2023-04-09)

The guarantee is designed to change behavior on both sides. It is risk reversal for the buyer and a forcing function for the seller. The caveats show that Koe already knew in 2023 that results depend on the buyer's execution. Section 31.4 shows how that knowledge later becomes a warning that no knowledge product can guarantee results.

#### Applying the pieces to content: long form, sales and short form

Koe closes the 2023 framework by applying it to social media. A long-form piece of content can follow this format: illustrate a problem, agitate it, give context, raise the reader's awareness level (show how the problem affects their life), give actionable steps (which makes them solution aware), weave in bullet-spray benefits to encourage implementation, and use the big idea as the headline or title. His example is "the one person business," which is both his unique mechanism and his big idea, and the subject of his most popular videos. For engagement, he says, target levels 1–3: "it's usually the problem-solution, that's content." For sales, go from level 2 to level 5. Address a more specific person who is aware of their problem, illustrate why it affects their life and why they should change, then introduce the product and an incentive. Short-form content is limited to one to three pieces at a time. The big idea alone can be a tweet, or the problem plus the big idea, or the problem plus the unique mechanism, or the steps.

**Source:** Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md (2023-04-09)

The application shows why Koe calls the steps "Legos." They are not a sequence that must always run complete. They are interchangeable pieces that can be combined at different scales: a tweet uses one or two, a newsletter uses most, and a landing page uses all of them.

#### The nine Legos of persuasive writing and speaking

Four months later, in August 2023, Koe summarizes the same principles in a slightly different list, which he calls "Legos for your writing and speaking" so that you can do both persuasively. He presents it as a summary of the principles of marketing, sales, writing and speaking, and he refers to the "Value creation" video for more detail.

**Source:** I Had To Learn These High-Income Skills (If I Wanted To Make Money).md (2023-08-16)

| # | Nine Legos (August 2023) | Correspondence with the eight steps (April 2023) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A desired outcome | Part of step 8 (end result) |
| 2 | A burning problem standing in the way | Step 3 (big problem) |
| 3 | A proven path to that outcome, unique to your experience | Step 4 (unique mechanism), now with "proven" |
| 4 | The level of awareness of the person you target | Step 1 |
| 5 | An angle based on "the eight human desires" (not enumerated here) | Step 2 |
| 6 | A list of benefits and how solving the problem affects their life | Step 5 |
| 7 | Proof, from yourself or previous customers, that you can solve it | Step 6 |
| 8 | A big idea that catches attention "in the sea of internet noise" | Step 7 |
| 9 | A risk reversal or guarantee "that tips them over the edge" | Step 8 |

The comparison shows that the nine Legos are the same framework reorganized, not a new one. Two things change. The *desired outcome* becomes a separate piece and moves to the front, so the list now opens with the goal rather than the awareness level. And the mechanism becomes a *proven path*, which ties it to experience. Several of these elements (desired outcome, proof, big idea, risk reversal) are terms from the copywriting tradition. Koe adapts them without naming a specific author, and he presents the selection and arrangement as his own.

#### The Value Equation of 2024: macronutrients of value

The March 2024 video "The Value Equation" rebuilds the framework on the definition of value given in Section 31.1. The *macronutrients of value* are three, and Koe defines each one carefully.

The first is the *problem*: "a limitation or challenge that creates pain when unsolved." The second is the *goal*: "an impactful end result that allows the recipient to evolve beyond the problem." The third is the *process*: "a creative system that breeds knowledge, skill and awareness to bridge the gap between problem and solution."

He then shows that the triad structures everything. To improve your life, identify a problem, direct your mind toward a goal, and experiment with solutions until you create a path: "this is called creativity." To make money, help others identify a problem, direct their attention to a goal, and give them clarity to act on a better future. For a better relationship, both people realize the problem, share a goal, and both put effort into solving it. "A problem, goal and process provide the structure for anything you do": writing, marketing, behaving, building a product, telling a story. He links it to the "purpose, path, priority" chapter of his book *The Art of Focus*.

**Source:** The Value Equation How To Become A High-Status Individual.md (2024-03-17)

The triad is the goal–path–problem principle of Chapter 8 seen from the side of value. Its three definitions map exactly onto the value equation. The problem is entropy experienced as pain. The goal is the ordered state that "allows the recipient to evolve." The process is the "creative system" that reverses entropy. This is why Koe calls the triad the *macronutrients*: it is the substance of value, the part that makes something useful. Its definitions also bring together the self and the other. The same structure that improves one's own life ("this is called creativity") is the structure one sells to others.

Koe immediately shows how to use it. A landing page follows this exact structure. Start with the problem, to frame the reader's perspective. Hint at a goal, meaning what they will achieve by purchasing. Then present the process: what the product is, how it affects their life, and how they implement it. The same applies to anything one writes, whether a course, written content or a tweet. Write one tweet about a problem, another about a goal, and another about a process or step-by-step plan: "boom, now you have three tweets, a newsletter, landing page, everything." "This is a universal framework of value."

**Source:** The Value Equation How To Become A High-Status Individual.md (2024-03-17)

He then works through an example with his own skill set. The problem: I hate working long hours. The goal: work four hours a day. The process: start a business, learn the required skills, focus on high-leverage tasks. In the process, he says, get very specific and detailed to give clarity. Each element can be turned into different content ideas.

**Source:** The Value Equation How To Become A High-Status Individual.md (2024-03-17)

The worked example is the 4-Hour Workday of Chapter 12, recast as an offer. The problem and the goal are Koe's own, and the process is the system he built to solve them. It shows "solve your own problems and sell the solution" in the language of the value equation. The problem he solved for himself becomes the problem he names for his reader, and the system becomes the product.

#### Micronutrients of value: shaping perception

On top of the "constructed perspective" of the macronutrients, the *micronutrients of value* shape how the value is perceived. Koe lists six and defines each.

| Micronutrient | Koe's definition | Example from the same video |
|---|---|---|
| Concept | Packaging and naming your value to capture attention and stick in people's heads | "The 4-Hour Workday" |
| Proof | Giving belief it's possible through personal or peer results | Client results; opening newsletters with a personal experience of the problem |
| Risk reversal | Reducing the perceived uncertainty, friction and difficulty of getting results | "You only need 10 minutes each day" |
| Pain points | Amplifying the problem to show how it affects life beyond the surface | No time for family, for other goals, no meaningful work |
| Benefits | Increasing desire to reach the solution, making motivation sustainable if repeated | Choose when, what and with whom you work |
| Awareness | How many people suffer from the problem, can benefit, and are ready to receive the clarity to act | (Developed in Chapter 32) |

**Source:** The Value Equation How To Become A High-Status Individual.md (2024-03-17)

Each micronutrient comes with a method, which Koe illustrates in turn.

*Concept.* Naming a concept brings a novel perspective to a good idea, preferably a life-changing one. His examples are the Eisenhower Matrix, "1% better every day," and his own "one person business," "4-Hour workday" and "the value equation." Names like these work well for headlines, video titles and hooks. His most quotable point is about their origin: "concepts are just made up." The Eisenhower Matrix was made up, even though it is popular, so "make more things up and test them."

**Complementary context:** The Eisenhower Matrix is a time-management grid (urgent/important) named after US President Dwight D. Eisenhower and popularized by Stephen Covey's *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People* (1989). "1% better every day" is associated with James Clear's *Atomic Habits* (2018).

The concept micronutrient is the theoretical basis of Koe's habit of coining terms, a habit visible throughout this book (anti-vision, Koe's Law, the value equation itself). Chapter 18 explained why naming one's own concepts produces originality. Here the same act appears as a tool of perceived value. A good idea without a name does not stick, and a named idea can be remembered, repeated and attributed.

*Proof.* For a product, proof can be a client result: "I've helped John Smith prioritize tasks to work less and increase revenue" (John Smith is a placeholder). In his newsletters, Koe usually starts with a personal experience that illustrates the problem. That opening covers the problem (a macronutrient) and proof through personal experience (a micronutrient) at once: "I'm knocking out two birds with one stone." Use client stories, statistics, research or logic to show what is possible, so that people believe in and care about your value.

*Risk reversal.* His example is "you only need 10 minutes each day to start," because "people need clarity more than they need motivation." The aim is to reduce the perceived risk of change and the friction of starting. Marketing uses money-back guarantees. In writing, Koe says, he attempts "to make things so stupidly clear that people don't have a choice but to act."

*Pain points.* His examples are having no time for family, no time to pursue other goals, and no meaningful work to focus on. On his landing pages he usually starts with a bullet list of pain points (he points to his writing course page). The list checks fit: does the reader care about this, and want to fix it? If so, they stick around. It also opens a *curiosity loop*, like the beginning of a story, and makes people desire a solution, because people normally "cloud over their problems" and ignore them until they reach a life they hate. Koe urges mapping the macro- and micronutrients for anything one creates: a newsletter, a YouTube video, "even a love song to a potential partner."

*Benefits.* His example list is: choose when you work, what you work on, and who you work with. Most people, he says, should write benefit-based posts at first, to establish authority when they start distributing value on social media, which he calls "the new Society" and "the Town Square of Commerce." His example post: you started a business because you want to choose when, what and with whom you work; entrepreneurship "isn't about working long hours, it's about taking control of your time, money and enjoyment." The structure is a hook, then benefits, then a lesson or the goal. The post therefore contains benefits, a concept (entrepreneurship) and a goal. He scheduled it on Twitter so viewers could check it, and notes that it is the kind of post Cortex (his software) helps write.

**Source:** The Value Equation How To Become A High-Status Individual.md (2024-03-17)

Three observations tie the micronutrients together. First, several of them recombine pieces of the 2023 framework: proof is step 6, risk reversal is step 8, benefits are step 5, pain points are the agitation of step 3, awareness is step 1, and concept extends the big idea of step 7. Second, the line "people need clarity more than they need motivation" repeats a thesis of Chapter 5. Psychic entropy is solved by clarity, not by motivation, and Koe now uses it as a principle of offer design. A clear first step reduces the disorder that stops action. Third, the love-song example shows the scope Koe claims for the framework. If value is structured sense-making, then the same structure applies to a sales page and to a courtship, which is what the November 2023 passage about bars and DMs (Section 31.2) said in other words.

#### Proof that compounds: the testimonial flywheel and appreciating assets

Two units extend the proof micronutrient beyond the first sale. One comes from a guest, and the other is Koe's own.

In a December 2021 interview, Justin Welsh describes his system. "Whenever people pull the trigger on it, I put them into a funnel where I ask for video testimonials two weeks later." With 2,500 students, he has "hundreds of video testimonials that are really, really powerful," and they "all get automated to my landing page, so my conversion rate continues to increase over time." He describes the loop: "People buy, people video promote, I post about it, people go to the site, conversion increases, they buy, they video promote, and around and around and around it goes." He insists: "There's no more complicated system than just that specific sort of flywheel."

**Source:** Justin Welsh Shows You How To Start & Grow A One-Person Business.md (2021-12-20)

**Complementary context:** Justin Welsh is a former SaaS sales executive who built a one-person business teaching solopreneurship, mainly through LinkedIn, Twitter and courses. He is a recurring guest and case in Koe's corpus (see Section 31.5 and Chapter 30).

The *testimonial flywheel* is Welsh's system, not Koe's. It is the answer to Koe's own instruction that getting testimonials should be "your full-time job" once people buy: automate the request so that each sale produces proof, and each piece of proof produces sales.

In March 2023 Koe adds a twist of his own, which he names: "testimonials from creators are appreciating assets." At the start, he says, it is even better to help people for free in the creator economy, because you are networking with others who distribute value online. He and Joey met by helping each other on a free call, and each has a testimonial from the other. Koe had about 500 followers then; now he has "upwards of like 2 million." Joey's testimonial from Koe is now far more authoritative, worth tens of thousands of dollars more, because people know and trust Koe and will buy immediately. So, he advises, don't only try to land big fish and maximize money: it is a progression, "stepping stone, stepping stone, stepping stone."

**Source:** The Best Online Business To Make $1 Million In 3-5 Years.md (2023-03-19)

The term *appreciating asset* is borrowed from finance and applied to proof. A testimonial's perceived value depends on the perceived value of the person giving it, so a testimonial from someone who later grows gains value over time. The idea fits the "value is perception" layer exactly. The words of the testimonial do not change, but the perception of its author does. It also connects to the networking of Chapter 26, where helping peers without immediate return is described as building relationships that compound.

### 31.4 Transformation and Behavior Change

#### "Impact causes movement": the major click

Sections 31.1–31.3 defined value and the way it is constructed. This section gives the test Koe applies to it: does it move people? In July 2024, speaking about writing, he calls the idea "the major click." "What you're trying to do with writing is create an argument as to why people should go towards a specific goal, and how to get there, because that's what you're doing at any given moment. If you're not pushing people along, then your writing doesn't really have impact, because impact causes movement." He gives his own case: "If I'm trying to convince people to live the good life, I need to give them the tools and resources and mindset and just ideas to allow them to go and achieve that." And he adds that "this is more than just business… you're improving yourself along the way."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md (2024-07-28)

The definition of writing as "an argument as to why people should go towards a specific goal, and how to get there" is the value equation in rhetorical form. The goal is the ordered state, the "how" is the process, and the "why" is the problem that makes movement necessary. *Impact causes movement* is Koe's shorthand: a piece with no effect on anyone's behavior has, by this standard, created no value, however true or elegant it is.

#### Behavior change: persuade, not force or deceive

A month later Koe states the principle in its ethical form. "Impactful writing is about behavior change. Your job is to write arguments that persuade, not force or deceive, people to make better decisions." That, he says, is how you contribute to humanity, because humans' edge "is to change the flow of evolution." You are either digging yourself and those around you into a hole, or improving: raising your consciousness and passing lessons down to people similar to you, since people learn best from someone in a similar personality or position. "You're either raising consciousness or you're decreasing consciousness, and it's a ripple effect of those around you, and in the digital world that is amplified."

**Source:** This Simple Reading Habit Will Change Your Life (I Promise).md (2024-08-04)

*Behavior change* is an ordinary phrase that Koe uses with a defined sense: the purpose of impactful writing, achieved by persuasion and excluding force and deception. The three verbs mark the boundary of ethical persuasion that Chapter 32 develops. Persuasion offers reasons and frames that a free person can accept or refuse. Force removes the choice. Deception corrupts it. The phrase "change the flow of evolution" connects back to Section 31.1. If evolution is the reversal of entropy, then changing people's decisions for the better is a direct contribution to it, and changing them for the worse adds to the disorder. The ripple effect explains why the stakes grow online, where one person's influence is multiplied.

#### Good writing is energy transfer

A short statement from May 2023 adds a stylistic corollary: "Good writing is about energy transfer, not being professional." Koe jokes, "we earned English degrees here," and insists that "everyone can write, everyone can write with impact, you don't need to be super grammatically correct."

**Source:** I Made $800,000 In One Year Writing 2 Hours A Day.md (2023-05-05)

If value is measured by movement, then the quality that matters in writing is whatever produces movement, and Koe calls that *energy*. Polish, correctness and professional register are secondary. *Energy transfer* is one of his coined terms. Elsewhere in the corpus it names the excitement of what one learned while pursuing one's goals, passed on to the reader (Chapter 24). The two senses agree: the writer moves the reader by transmitting the energy of their own discovery.

#### The message: value exchange and the vessel of valuable information

In January 2024, in the summary of his book *The Art of Focus*, Koe states the economic side of the same idea. "It is impossible to achieve your version of success without value exchange." You must become "a vessel of valuable information" and exchange it for your desired value in relationships, business and life. If you want money, you exchange your form of value with someone who has money; "it isn't just going to appear out of thin air." A valuable message communicates in a way that is relatable, understandable and actionable. That requires understanding human nature and psychology, which, he says, is best learned through marketing and sales, because they contain practical frameworks for psychology together with real-world practice. "Value is determined by perception, and perception is influenced by an individual's worldview." The message must therefore be crafted for a group's or an individual's level of mind, identity and conditioning. Certain words capture, hold and deliver value on attention. Either you get people to focus on you, or their focus gravitates to something more distracting or more valuable.

**Source:** The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md (2024-01-18)

The passage gathers the first three sections into three requirements for a valuable message. It must be *relatable*, matched to identity and worldview (Section 31.2). It must be *understandable*, structured as sense-making (Section 31.1). It must be *actionable*, producing behavior change (this section). The *vessel* image recurs in Koe's work. The person, like the business in Chapter 9, is a container through which something valuable passes to others. The closing remark about attention ties value to the competition described in Chapter 25: a message that fails to capture, hold and deliver loses attention to something else.

#### The click: how value is felt

What does value feel like from the receiving side? In March 2024 Koe describes it. Each of us has many small, unconnected ideas in our heads. Reading a sentence that "just clicks," that is "exactly what I needed," happens when you are exposed to the right idea from the right perspective, perceived the right way. When many pieces of the value framework are already in place and you find the last one, that click is value: "this cascade of insight starts going off, that's value." The same thing happens when a product is put in front of you and you buy it instantly because it is exactly what you need. Pay attention to these moments, he advises, so that you can replicate them in life and business.

**Source:** The Value Equation How To Become A High-Status Individual.md (2024-03-17)

*The click*, or *cascade of insight*, is the subjective side of the definition "value is a signal of meaning in your psyche" (Section 31.1). It also explains why the three layers of perception matter. The click happens only when the idea is right *for that mind at that moment*. The same sentence read a year earlier, by a mind lacking the other pieces, would not have clicked. Koe's advice to notice one's own clicks turns personal experience into research. Each time something clicks for you, you have a record of what made a message valuable, and you can reproduce it for others. Elsewhere in the corpus, "the click" also names the moment a creator understands that growth is a skill rather than luck (Chapter 26). The two uses share the image of a sudden fit, but they refer to different things.

#### "Spiritually impregnating": teaching as replication

In April 2023 Koe describes the deepest form of this transfer with a deliberately strange metaphor. He hopes he is "replicating myself into you," "spiritually impregnating you, if you will, to do something better with your life." This, he says, is "exactly what parents do" as they raise, teach and educate their children: they put pieces of themselves, their mental form, into their children. So do teachers, and every other person who comes into contact and communicates with others. From this he argues that "education is what creates better selves in this world," and that it goes on to affect the collective well-being of society and civilization.

**Source:** The Micro Education Business Model (How To Monetize Your Knowledge).md (2023-04-23)

The metaphor joins two ideas from earlier chapters. One is the law of conceptual survival (Chapter 3): ideas reproduce, and identities are built from them. The other is the definition of survival given in Section 31.3, "the desire to replicate information in your genes and consciousness." Teaching is replication at the level of consciousness, as parenting is at the level of genes. Koe means it as a description of responsibility as well as influence. A person who places pieces of their mind in others is shaping their future selves, which is why the same section ends with a warning about ethics.

#### Brand is transformation

The units on brand apply the behavior-change test to reputation. The latest and clearest statement is from June 2026: "Your brand is the transformation. Remember that. That's your guiding light." Why do you admire someone? "Because they have changed your life in some fundamental way. Their content or product led to behavior change. They first change your mind, and then they change your actions, and then the results that came from those actions, you associated with that person. You gave them credit for it." His examples: James Clear convinced millions to adopt tiny habits that made big changes. Jordan Peterson, "in his prime," convinced millions of young men to take responsibility and pursue meaning. Alan Watts helped people stop taking life so seriously. Naval Ravikant taught the power of digital leverage. If you heard an idea from Naval and noticed its outcome in your life, you would attribute the outcome to Naval.

**Source:** If You Have Multiple Interests, Start A One-Person Business.md (2026-06-13)

**Complementary context:** James Clear is the author of *Atomic Habits* (2018). Jordan Peterson is a Canadian clinical psychologist and author of *12 Rules for Life* (2018). Alan Watts (1915–1973) was a British writer who popularized Zen and Eastern philosophy in the West. All three are recurring references in Koe's corpus (Appendix B).

The sequence Koe describes has three steps: mind, then action, then result, which the person then attributes to the source. That sequence explains authority. Authority is not claimed by the creator. It is granted by the audience, in proportion to the results the audience attributes to them. This is why the brand, defined in Chapter 27 as a digital character, is in the end the transformation it causes.

In August 2024 Koe draws the strategic conclusion. "Behavior change is the driver of authority and recognition. When you write your quote-unquote book with time, on social media, in emails and in free downloads, you build a brand that blows past 99% of people." Creating your *niche of one* (Chapter 19), he recalls, means outlining the book and then writing it in newsletters, threads and social posts "over 6 to 12 months, until people genuinely understand your brand." The corpus gives this timeline inconsistently. In some videos it is 6–12 months, in others 3–6 months, with no stated reason. One possible reading is that the shorter range refers to first recognition and the longer one to the point where people "genuinely understand" the brand.

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md (2024-08-25)

In the same video he adds a principle of memory: "People remember those who first taught them the knowledge that is useful to them. Be that person for more people." His example: *The Power of Now* was recommended to him by Matt Ogus, and whenever he reads it, he thinks of Matt Ogus. If Koe was the first to introduce someone to the "one person business" and make it click, they will remember him, which gives him the authority "even if the other person is talking about the one person business."

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md (2024-08-25)

**Complementary context:** Matt Ogus is a fitness YouTuber who, according to Koe's account in other videos, introduced him to the gym and recommended Eckhart Tolle's *The Power of Now*, the book behind the awareness episode of Chapter 2.

The principle of the first teacher explains why the timing of value matters as much as its content. Being first to make an idea click for a person earns a lasting association, even if others teach the same idea later. It is another reason why perception, more than authorship, determines who receives credit.

#### Planting an idea is like buying stock

The most developed metaphor for this compounding comes from a March 2023 conversation with Dickie Bush. Bush recalls a point Koe made at dinner: "your impact is less about someone engaging with your content but more… when you plant an idea in someone's head and that goes on to influence a lot of their decisions, it's almost like you are buying stock… or a venture capitalist… some kind of investment." When the recipients go on to do great things with the idea, "their gratitude to you gets more and more valuable." The affinity "has compounded because of the success that they've seen via his ideas; they feel like they owe him even more." Bush gives examples. Ship 30 alumni with hundreds of thousands of followers and dollars trace their start to Ship 30 ("that equity has been compounding"). James Clear's success keeps growing because his ideas changed people's lives. The implication for creating, Bush concludes, is a question: "How can I put something in someone's head that they're going to be able to use and reference and act on and see success with, such that it compounds favorably for them and for me?"

**Source:** Dan Koe & Dickie Bush On One-Person Businesses, Creative Workflows, and Lifestyle Design.md (2023-03-16)

**Complementary context:** Dickie Bush is a former finance professional and co-founder of Ship 30 for 30, a cohort program that teaches online writing.

The idea is Koe's. Bush articulates and develops it in the conversation, and the terms *affinity* and *equity* come from his phrasing. The metaphor completes the account of brand as transformation by adding time. An idea planted today is an asset whose value grows with the success of the person who received it, just as a testimonial from a growing creator appreciates (Section 31.3). Both describe value that compounds after the exchange.

#### The education brand: raising consciousness, with responsibility

In August 2024 Koe states what all of this implies about the creator's job. Beyond goals, problems and paths, other things in the psyche influence how people perceive and act: "prior experiences, firm and loose beliefs and skill levels across all domains of life," all of which can be conscious or unconscious, known or unknown. "Your entire job as an education brand is to raise the consciousness of your audience." By *education brand* he means "a brand that gives actual value. A creator that teaches their audience and improves their life, not an influencer that posts half-naked pictures, flexes their lifestyle, or posts self-deprecating memes."

A few minutes later he adds a warning. "Since you have to persuade and change people's minds, you have to understand that this is a big responsibility. This is taking on a lot of responsibility and emotional labor for your life. That can be very fulfilling, and it can also turn very bad if you are not developed or ethical enough to be doing this type of work."

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md (2024-08-25)

The two statements belong together. Raising consciousness requires changing minds, and changing minds is power. Power used by someone who is not developed or ethical can lower consciousness instead, which is the "decreasing consciousness" side of the ripple effect described above. Koe's term *emotional labor* names the personal cost of carrying that responsibility. This is one of the few places where he presents the creator's role as a burden as well as an opportunity. It links to the level of mind (Chapter 4): the developmental level of the teacher limits the quality of the change they produce in others.

#### Content for impact, not income

In April 2023 Koe gives the same priority in the form of a practical rule. "Stop thinking of content as a means to income." He offers reflective questions. Will your ideas be mentioned in a top-10 podcast episode? In the introduction of a best-selling book? In viral videos, or in tight-knit newsletters? Aim "for impact, and the income will come." To achieve this, "you must develop yourself, you must develop your mind, your cognitive ability."

**Source:** The Micro Education Business Model (How To Monetize Your Knowledge).md (2023-04-23)

The rule follows from the chain the section has built. Income comes from value exchange, value exchange depends on perceived value, perceived value comes from the transformation that is attributed, and transformation depends on the quality of the ideas, which depends on the creator's development. Aiming directly at income skips the steps that produce it. The rule also repeats the pearl principle (Section 31.1): the depth has to be developed first.

#### "Feed the hungry": teach those who want to learn

A brief unit from June 2023 marks the boundary of persuasion from the other side. Koe cites "feed the hungry" from scripture and adds his own completion: "and those that are full will eventually grow hungry." He acknowledges the literal interpretation but reads the line symbolically: teach people who want to be taught, and do not force it on others.

**Source:** Get Mad At Where You Are In Life (A Deep Explanation).md (2023-06-11)

**Complementary context:** The injunction to feed the hungry appears in several places in the Bible, for example Isaiah 58:7 and Matthew 25:35. The clause "those that are full will eventually grow hungry" is Koe's addition, as far as the transcript shows.

Read alongside the awareness layer of Section 31.2, the line is practical advice. The "hungry" are the people whose minds are ready to receive. Forcing value on the "full" produces rejection, as the gamer example showed. But fullness is temporary, and someone who is not ready today may become ready when their problems change. The addition keeps the door open without justifying pressure.

#### Selling a transformation: marketing and sales defined

The remaining units of the section turn the behavior-change test into marketing formulas. Koe defines the two skills in January 2023. *Marketing* is "creating a message that is attention grabbing, relevant and valuable for a specific person," from that person's perspective. *Sales* is "a process for making people aware of their problems and presenting a solution to those problems." Note, he says, that sales does not target only people who are already aware; it is a process. Sales can be synonymous with storytelling, "because the human mind makes sense of the world via stories." A story strings things together from the past to now to the future, and shows how they interact with your situation. Sales, marketing and story are all built around transformations. People want a desirable outcome in the future, want to avoid where they were in the past, and perhaps want out of their current position: they want to improve.

**Source:** The $1 Million Dollar Skill Stack (Learn In This Order).md (2023-01-08)

In November 2023 he gives a shorter definition in which perception and transformation meet. Writers, creators and one-person businesses want people to perceive what they distribute as important, so "you have to understand the problems and goals that are on their mind so you can frame what it is that you're giving to them… so that it does actually register in their awareness." "This is marketing. Sales as a whole is just understanding the goals and problems of your target audience and providing clarity in between, or a solution, a product, a system, a how-to video, whatever it is. You help people transform."

**Source:** The Daily Routine That Changed My Life (4 Habits Most People Ignore).md (2023-11-19)

"Clarity in between" is the most compact formulation in the corpus. The goal and the problem belong to the audience. What the seller provides is the clarity that bridges them, which is the process macronutrient of Section 31.3. The phrase "register in their awareness" is the perception layer. The definition of sales as storytelling connects the chapter to Chapter 23, where story is the mind's native structure.

#### The transformation formulas

Across 2023 and 2024 Koe gives the transformation several shapes. Each is presented as complete in itself, and each restates the same structure.

In March 2023 he gives a "universal principle" for coaching, consulting and tutoring offers. It has three components. The *goal* is where your customer wants to be, or what you have achieved. The *path* is your unique way of getting them there. The *problem* is what they struggle with now, where you were or where they are, and how you bridge the gap. "That's all that marketing is": "you're selling a transformation that either you have achieved yourself, you have helped others achieve, or you want to start helping others achieve." Freelancers, he notes, gain experience by working with clients first. They do not even have results with the skill when they start selling, so you do not need results to start. His example: look up a planner on Amazon, create your own, put it on Amazon and start making money. Marketing, sales and traffic (for example a personal brand) are details that come after.

**Source:** The Best Online Business To Make $1 Million In 3-5 Years.md (2023-03-19)

In November 2023 he gives the version he says is "all you need to know in order to actually make money," after marketing his own products or services "for almost 6 years." A story or transformation has three parts: a *burning problem*, a *desirable outcome* and a *clear solution*. "This is a perspective; you're giving people a new mind, you're changing people's minds in order to earn with your mind. That's your entire marketing campaign: goal, problem, path to get there." Test each element until you "strike gold with the right positioning," and then you are set for a while. Write about each component nonstop in short content, to test and validate it. Or, if you have already written a lot, look in your content for a high-performing goal, problem and system, and create a product from it: "it will sell." These three components are used whenever you ask someone to buy, whether in emails, on a landing page, in an Instagram story or in a YouTube promotion.

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md (2023-11-26)

In December 2023 he ties the structure to the anti-vision of Chapter 7. On a landing page, start by writing the reader's anti-vision in detail, then the vision they could achieve, and last present your solution, system, steps or unique mechanism as the way to fill the gap. "That's it. That's how you write a landing page, that's how you write a newsletter, that's how you create a YouTube video: anti-vision, vision, solution." He also advises emulating other landing pages to understand headlines and content structure, as training wheels for the first one.

**Source:** How Intelligent Creators Will Make Money In 2024 (The Build Teach Earn Method).md (2023-12-03)

In March 2024 he shows why this structure is more than a sales trick. "We identified problems with an anti-vision, we created solutions with a vision. When we actually solve the problems and move towards the vision, we're improving ourselves, we've created a marketing strategy of transformation, and we've created a product, which is the system that you used to reach those goals through education, skill acquisition and action. So you're killing three birds with one stone. This is self-improvement, other improvement."

**Source:** If Your Life Is Spiraling Out Of Control, Here's The Solution To Feeling Lost.md (2024-03-10)

In the same month he gives a five-step sales story. "First you make them aware of a problem; second you make them realize how that problem will impact their life if it goes unsolved; third you introduce a solution, your product, that can solve that problem; fourth you show them proof that change is possible with testimonials or personal experience; and fifth you give them the clarity to act and continue on a new path." That, he says, is "how you write a landing page, run a sales call, direct message people, or get people to read your newsletter, like I did here." He points out that he has just used the structure to promote his newsletter. His summary: "the sales process is not inherently sleazy; it is guiding people through a story."

**Source:** Working Hard Won't Make You Rich (Do This Instead).md (2024-03-31)

The variants line up as follows.

| Date | Formula | Problem element | Goal element | Path element | Extra elements |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023-03 | Goal, path, problem | Problem | Goal | Path (your unique way) | — |
| 2023-11 | Burning problem, desirable outcome, clear solution | Burning problem | Desirable outcome | Clear solution | Testing until the positioning "strikes gold" |
| 2023-12 | Anti-vision, vision, solution | Anti-vision | Vision | Solution / unique mechanism | — |

The table makes visible what Koe implies but never puts in one place. The formulas are one structure under five names. The 2023-12 version shows that the same three-part structure organizes self-improvement (Chapter 7), and the 2024-03 "three birds" passage states the consequence. A person who works through their own anti-vision and vision has, as a by-product, a marketing strategy and a product. This is the theoretical core of "solve your own problems and sell the solution" (Chapter 28). It also explains Koe's remark that you do not need results to start. A transformation you "want to start helping others achieve" still has the full structure, so it can be sold honestly as an aspiration and a path, provided the claims stay within what you have actually done.

#### Amplifying the problem

Among all the pieces, Koe singles out one as "probably the most important." In September 2024, after describing phone addiction, he stops to comment on what he has just done. "Notice how I'm not only illustrating the problem but I'm amplifying it, and that's important; that's probably the most important part of your content, your emails, your landing pages, your DMs." Its function is to show how the problem is actually a problem in the reader's life. "You can state the problem, you can be like, 'oh, you're addicted to your phone.' I could say that right now; is it going to make you stop?" It will not. But he can bring up how the phone drains energy, how it affects the way you move in the world, your ability to focus and your ability to build the future you want, and how it leads to "sitting there and rotting away if it goes unchecked." "The deeper I go, and if I hit the right button," the more people want to buy, provided you have a solution with proof that works. They could spend 10–40 hours searching to piece it together. But if you offer something worth paying for, and they do not have a bad relationship with money, they buy for convenience: "it's okay to spend money, I spend money every day, so do you."

**Source:** The Fastest Way To Build A One-Person Business (Beginner Guide).md (2024-09-15)

Amplification works because of the awareness layer. A stated problem is information the mind can dismiss. An amplified problem connects to the reader's goals (energy, focus, the future they want), and only problems connected to goals register as problems (Chapter 6: without a goal there is no problem). Amplification is the "pain points" micronutrient and the "agitate" of step 3, now given priority over everything else. Koe attaches two conditions: the solution must work and have proof. Amplifying a problem one cannot solve would be manipulation by his own standard ("persuade, not force or deceive"). The closing point about convenience anticipates the economics of Section 31.5.

#### A product is a system for behavior change

The last part of the section applies the behavior-change test to the product itself. In January 2024 Koe repeats what he teaches in Digital Economics: "a good product is a system for behavior change. Value equals behavior change." If people change what they are doing toward a positive outcome, that is value, and that is what content and products try to do.

**Source:** The 7 Best Internet Business Models (From Zero Experience To Advanced).md (2024-01-21)

In September 2023 he had given the same idea a market and a method. "The best products spark positive behavior change," and this is reflected in the Eternal markets: health, wealth, relationships and happiness. The method: "Write to yourself, build for yourself and sell to yourself." Create a product or service from the knowledge and skills you acquired on your journey, something you would have wanted or that would truly benefit your life now. Creators sell everything: courses, software, cotton pants (out of fear of chemicals in polyester pants), blue-light glasses, planners, coaching, kitchenware, shoes. The key question is: "How are you going to make people healthier, happier, wiser or wealthier with the knowledge you gain by pursuing your goals and figuring it out for yourself?"

**Source:** Life Is A Video Game (You Can't Escape The Matrix).md (2023-09-05)

In February 2025 he compresses the roles of brand, content and product into one line: "For brand, tell a story. For content, make a map. For product, enforce a habit." The transcript reads "and force a habit," and "enforce" is the likely reading. He does not develop the line further in that video.

**Source:** A Full Guide To Making Your First Profitable Product (Beginners, Take Notes).md (2025-02-10)

The heuristic can be read through this chapter. The brand tells the story of transformation (brand is transformation). Content maps the path from problem to goal (the macronutrients). The product enforces the habit, the repeated behavior that turns knowledge into change. A product that only informs, without structuring behavior, does not meet Koe's definition of a good product.

#### The psychology of purchasing: money or pain on the line

Why should buying a product produce more change than consuming the same information for free? In January 2023 Koe gives a psychological answer. There is "a lot of psychology involved in purchasing a course": putting something on the line forces you to sit down with the boring strategies and implement them. Without money or pain on the line, there is no reason to implement. The alternative is looking for entertainment online, "hoping that you can find the perfect balance of entertainment and value so that you can just endlessly consume content while doing nothing about your life." He insists that nobody is immune, himself included: "don't act like you're immune to this, nobody is immune to this, I'm not immune to this." He catches himself clicking mindlessly on his phone and watching for a minute before asking himself what he is doing.

**Source:** The Most Profitable Niche Is You (Create Your Niche Of One).md (2023-01-29)

*Money or pain on the line* is the purchase seen as a forcing function, the same mechanism as the tactical stress of Chapter 10 and the deadlines of Chapter 12. It is part of the product's value: the price itself contributes to the behavior change. Koe's admission that he is not immune keeps the point from becoming contempt for buyers. The pull of endless consumption (Chapter 11) is universal, and commitment devices exist because of it. Section 31.5 shows the same mechanism in Koe's own story, where a wasted $3,500 did what fifty courses had not.

#### The limits: tools, not truth

Koe's later statements put clear limits on what a product can do. They also mark a change from 2023.

In October 2024: "We're not creating products to completely solve the problems in people's lives, because that's not on us, that's on the person that has the problem." Whether to solve the problem is up to them. "We're simply giving things to people to experiment with to make that process more efficient over time," gradually increasing "the efficiency that comes from evolution" and contributing toward it.

**Source:** The Death Of The Personal Brand (& The Future Of Creative Work).md (2024-10-06)

In September 2025: "Create with intention. Create to serve. Understand that ideas, content, beliefs, and products are tools, not truth. Create more tools. If people interpret them as truth, they will learn their lesson at some point." And the best time to start is now, "because there's no other time to start aside from now."

**Source:** The Death Of Social Media (& The Future Of Content Creation).md (2025-09-07)

In March 2026, about his own teaching: "I can't guarantee that this is going to 100% work. It's the same thing with courses… giving out knowledge doesn't guarantee a result like a product or service does. And even products or services… don't even guarantee results. You get a skin care product and it may work for you… But if you don't address the underlying root cause of what's causing you not to get results, regardless of the skin care product or the knowledge that you have, then you're probably not going to succeed." "Some people just don't have the identity"; they "haven't done the inner work to overcome their fear of uncertainty or failure." "There's so many different moving variables, and one little piece of knowledge isn't going to save you. This has to be a lifelong journey of wanting it."

**Source:** How To Build A $1M One-Person Business Faster With AI.md (2026-03-15)

These statements should be set against the guarantee of step 8 (Section 31.3), because together they show a change of emphasis. In April 2023 Koe recommended a result guarantee ("five dates in 60 days or we refund your money") as the way to close the most deals at the beginning. Even then it was conditional on the buyer proving every action. From 2024 to 2026 he increasingly stresses that results lie outside the product's control. The problem belongs to the person who has it. Products are tools to experiment with, not truths. And no knowledge guarantees a result without the *inner work* on the *root cause*, which in Koe's account is identity and the fear of uncertainty or failure. He gives no explicit reason for the shift, beyond the observation that the client's execution lies outside the product's control. The two positions are compatible, since the 2023 guarantee already conditioned the refund on execution, but the emphasis moves from promise to limit.

The 2026 statement also connects the end of this chapter to its beginning. If a product is a system for behavior change, and behavior is governed by identity (Chapters 3–4), then the deepest products must reach identity. Information alone, the "one little piece of knowledge," does not reach it. That is the problem the next section takes up. Education products are, in Koe's view, the most valuable products because education precedes behavior. They are also the most criticized, because information alone so often fails to change anything.

### 31.5 Education Products

#### Why information is "the greatest product one can sell"

Koe's case for education products follows directly from the chapter's definitions. He states it most rigorously in September 2023, as an answer to the main objection to information products: "it's not real, it's not tangible, it's just words on a screen." His reply is a chain of premises. If the root of your existence and quality of life is your mind; and if "your mind is an information processing machine"; and if the information you consume heavily influences where you end up in life and in the lives you affect; then "information products are the greatest product one can sell." Anyone who says otherwise "has not thought it through or experienced the business model." Such a person is "conditioned by their favorite guru's world view and can't escape it."

**Source:** Micro Education Businesses Are The Future & How To Start With Zero Dollars.md (2023-09-17)

The argument is valid if its premises hold, and each premise comes from earlier in the book. The mind as an information-processing system comes from Chapter 5 ("everything is information"). The idea that information consumed shapes identity comes from Chapters 1 and 11 (the information environment and the choice of consumption). The idea that identity shapes outcomes comes from Part II. The conclusion depends on reading "the greatest" as "the one with the most leverage over a person's life," not as "the one that always works." Section 31.4 has already shown that Koe himself rejects the second reading.

He repeats the claim over the years. In September 2024: packaged knowledge "is readily accessible and it's valuable; people actually want it, people actually benefit from it; it changes people's lives a lot more than some consumer product that you have in your bathroom," "much more than a fancy bar of soap." People like to complain about courses or education, "when your mind and how you operate within reality, in the entirety of your life, is determined by your education." So "contributing in that fashion with digital education products is a very meaningful route," and "also a very profitable route, because it is valuable, and the economy favors the profitable."

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md (2024-09-29)

In February 2025 he defines the product. An *education product* comes in the form of courses, communities or cohorts, often accompanied by templates, worksheets or other things that add value and "allow people to actually change their behavior." These, he says, are the products "that line Alex Hormozi's portfolio, because he knows where those are going to go in the future. He's taking a bet on them." They are the info products people "love to paint in a bad light," as if education were not the most important aspect of one's development, or the thing that prepares one for a future schools cannot keep up with. "A good education product can change someone's life ten times more than a physical product or software could, any day."

**Source:** A Full Guide To Making Your First Profitable Product (Beginners, Take Notes).md (2025-02-10)

In September 2025 he gives the strongest formulation, inside a description of what a developed ("level three") creator outputs: short content that helps rather than hurts, long content that educates, inspires and pushes toward better, and a product that "attempts to solve the root cause," such as a book, a tool or a program. "There's a lot of hate around courses, but they are arguably the most life-changing types of products, because education is the precursor to behavior," and behavior changed at scale "ripples throughout civilization." Physical products and most software, he says, do not. The hate exists because anyone can become a level-one creator and anyone can create a course, "because the bar is so low." People imitate and repeat the stigma, which has become a meme. For a developed person, education does not have to be free, unless you have "a terrible relationship with money." Selling it is "one of the greatest goods you can do."

**Source:** The Death Of Social Media (& The Future Of Content Creation).md (2025-09-07)

*Education is the precursor to behavior* is the bridge between this section and the last. If value is behavior change, and education precedes behavior, then education products sit at the root of the value chain. In Koe's eyes this explains both their power and their bad reputation. Their power comes from acting on the mind, which governs everything else. Their reputation suffers because the low barrier to entry fills the market with poor examples. The "ten times" comparison and the bar of soap are rhetorical, not measured. Koe's reference to Alex Hormozi's portfolio is a claim about another person's investments that the corpus does not document further.

**Complementary context:** Alex Hormozi is an American entrepreneur and author (*$100M Offers*, *$100M Leads*) whose holding company, Acquisition.com, invests in businesses including education companies. He is one of Koe's references on offers and direct response.

#### Education businesses as the most meaningful and results-oriented models

The 2023 units add two claims about education *businesses*, and a social argument about them.

In April 2023, before presenting a new path, Koe says you have to "shed some limiting beliefs." People talk badly about education businesses (info products, courses, YouTube content, information) because they imagine "a bunch of dudes… in the corner circle-jerking each other, scamming people and manipulating." Information is intangible, and that makes it easy to suspect. But people who have bought courses understand how life-changing they can be. Even a $50 course that was not that good still delivered information and kept its promise, so labeling it a scam is unjustified. Talk badly about bad courses, he says, but "it's 50 bucks." Meanwhile most college courses are "complete dog [__]," and nobody complains about spending $2,000 on one, "because it's conditioned into their head." He says everyone should have an education business, because the information in each person's head is unique: "you have a 100,000 product stuck in your head." His summary: "education businesses are the most meaningful business models for beginners."

**Source:** The Micro Education Business Model (How To Monetize Your Knowledge).md (2023-04-23)

The figure in "a $100,000 product in your head" changes over the years. In 2025 Koe speaks of "$1 million trapped in your head," and adds that the product alone is not enough without marketing and launches. The image is the same; only the number grows. Chapter 33 treats it as the starting point of product creation.

In the same video he calls education businesses "the most results-oriented business models out there." People complain about paying $50 for a course, then spend $15,000 to $75,000 on a watch and consider it more valuable than "the information that you hold in your mind that influences every single action you take in this world, and the success you have henceforth from it." He adds that "you will only find new, specific and relevant information from courses in the creator economy; that's just how it is." Most people, he says, are simply impatient. They buy a $50 course, see a promise ("I made six figures / seven figures doing this"), and expect instant results. He says he tries to preface everything with "it's going to take like three to five years."

**Source:** The Micro Education Business Model (How To Monetize Your Knowledge).md (2023-04-23)

In November 2023 he gives the social version of the argument. "People criticize new creator education businesses as quote-unquote grifts and scams for solving the only true problems that will raise the baseline consciousness of humanity." "People who sell education, courses and coaching in the health, wealth, relationships and happiness domains of life are attacking the root of positive behavior change." He points to his video "You have $100,000 worth of knowledge stuck in your head," which is about learning in a decentralized way and distributing information.

**Source:** People Dumber Than You Are Making Millions.md (2023-11-12)

Three ideas in these units deserve separate attention. The first is the comparison with college. Koe uses it to expose a double standard: the same people who distrust a $50 course accept a $2,000 college course without question, because the institution has conditioned them (Chapter 2). The second is the "three to five years" warning. It is the pearl principle (Section 31.1) applied to customers, and it shows that Koe locates the cause of many "scam" accusations in the buyer's impatience rather than in the product. The third is the phrase *baseline consciousness of humanity*. It places education businesses in the Eternal markets (Chapter 19) and gives them the civilizational role that Section 31.4 assigned to the education brand. Koe's claim that new, specific information is found "only" in creator-economy courses is an overstatement, but it points to something real. Information that is specific, current and tested by a practitioner often appears there before it reaches institutions.

#### Deep knowledge: "a zip file for the mind"

What makes educational information valuable rather than redundant? Koe answers with two ideas: deep knowledge and condensation.

In the January 2024 summary of *The Art of Focus* he reads from the book: "Curiosity is the path to doing the things you want to do; curiosity is what makes you you, and that is a necessity heading into the future of automated work. If you learn the same thing as everyone else, to be trained into the select few jobs that are left, you can be replaced by almost anyone. The solution is deep knowledge." He thinks of deep knowledge "as a zip file for the mind," and defines it as "information that gradually raises the level of mind of the student; it helps them solve a sequence of problems toward a meaningful hierarchy of goals." Through the acquisition and distribution of deep knowledge, one can create one's own career, "because the future demands that we earn with our mind, not our time." He advises solving your own problems, from the superficial to the metaphysical, to increase the value you offer.

**Source:** The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md (2024-01-18)

The definition of *deep knowledge* draws together several of the book's concepts: the level of mind (Chapter 4), the sequence of problems (Chapter 8) and the hierarchy of goals (Chapter 8). Deep knowledge is not a fact but a structured progression that changes the learner's level. The "zip file" image explains its economic value. Compression means that what took the author years can be unpacked by the student in much less time.

In May 2022 Koe had made the same point with numbers. Humans, he says, can process 126 bits of information per second. Suppose reading and understanding something takes you "60 million bits of information to process." If you package it from a new perspective, simplified but still holding meaning and value (condensed, say, to "10 million bits"), and make it make more sense, then "you're helping other people learn that same thing much, much, much faster than you did, because you have condensed that information." They develop faster because of you, and their quality of life improves: "that is extremely valuable."

**Source:** Stop Trying To Be Unique... Do This Instead.md (2022-05-28)

**Complementary context:** The figure of roughly 120–126 bits per second as the limit of conscious attention comes from Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's discussion in *Flow* (1990), which Koe draws on elsewhere (Chapters 6 and 10). The 60-million and 10-million figures are illustrative, as the corpus notes, and have no source.

Condensation is value creation in the sense of Section 31.1. The learner's confusion is a form of entropy, and the creator reverses it by organizing the information. This is why, in Koe's view, re-teaching what already exists is not redundant. A clearer package of the same knowledge is a real gain for whoever receives it. The aggregation argument below generalizes the point.

#### Learning experiences: selling a second version of your mind

By January 2026 Koe's idea of the education product has changed form. "The future of education in my eyes is learning experiences rather than static courses." You are not only selling information; "you're practically selling a second version of your mind." It is like selling coaching without being present. You pass everything you know to the AI and let people learn at their own speed. He addresses an objection: if schools implement AI, are they competition? No. Schools teach what people need to operate within society. You teach something very few people know and that schools would not think to teach. "You are placing a price tag on your own curiosity and self-improvement." In learning experiences, people interact, get feedback and do the thing with guidance. That is the main differentiator and value proposition, because learning by doing is the best way to learn. People escape "tutorial hell" (Chapter 14) and, by going through the experience, come away with a final product.

**Source:** How I'd build a one-person business (if I started over in 2026).md (2026-01-03)

This is a real change in Koe's position, and it belongs here. The corpus shows a shift between 2023 and 2026. Until 2025, information products are "the greatest product one can sell," and media-based products are "the education of the future." In the same January 2026 video, though, Koe says info products have reached "the final stage of market sophistication," that "static courses don't cut it," and, at different moments of the video, that info products are "dying" and that they are "not dead." His later statements in 2026 settle on a middle position: information products are abundant and will not die, but "small-scale software will be the new info product." The next level is an AI implementation tool built on a course's knowledge base. His reasons are market saturation, the fact that most buyers never finish courses, AI's ability to generate information, and the lower barrier to building software. The underlying thesis does not change: education precedes behavior, and education as a function is too foundational to be commoditized. What changes is the vehicle, from the static course or PDF to the interactive learning experience. Chapter 33 develops the consequences for product design ("systems are the new product").

#### What people pay for: aggregation, speed and convenience

The most common objection to selling education is "it's already free online." Koe's answer is one of the most stable ideas in the corpus. It appears in October 2022 and is reproduced almost word for word in a February 2024 compilation. Only a specific type of person, he says, raises the objection: people not in the game of business, not "in the arena," who do not have the money to invest in such products. "What people pay for is aggregation, speed and convenience," and that is everywhere. Why buy food that is terrible for your health instead of eating good food at home? Convenience. Why spend 20 hours filtering fragmented YouTube videos instead of taking a step-by-step course to an end result?

**Source:** If You Are High Value, Start A One-Person Business.md (2022-10-02); The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md (2024-02-06)

The idea's origin is worth tracing, because it arrives through a guest. In a December 2021 interview, Koe raises a problem he has long struggled with. Creators are told to "give value up front" in free content and on the landing page, but not to give "so much away that makes people want to try to do it on their own." Justin Welsh answers with a point he says he and a big Twitter creator (unnamed) agreed on over coffee in Nashville: "I can take everything in my product today that is in the paid product, and… it has all been shared for free somewhere." "People don't pay me for some new secret, even though they believe they are… What they're really paying for is aggregation. They're paying for everything in one spot. They're paying to go from point A to point B in one hour, not to have to sift through my content for the last two years and spend a month trying to put it all together." Hence his rule: "I always give freely." His LinkedIn-to-Twitter siphoning system, for example, is described for free on his website, while the product assembles it "in a way that is extremely digestible and affordable."

**Source:** Justin Welsh Shows You How To Start & Grow A One-Person Business.md (2021-12-20)

The same interview contains a tension that the corpus records. Earlier in the conversation, Welsh credits Eddie Shlainer, who runs verygoodcopy.com, with an idea Welsh calls "something about curtain language": "you use this language in your copy to infer that there is something magical behind the curtain, and the audience needs to pay to pull that curtain back and see what's behind it." Welsh says the idea "has always stuck with me" and that he builds his promotion sequence around it. His memory of the exact term is hedged ("something about").

**Source:** Justin Welsh Shows You How To Start & Grow A One-Person Business.md (2021-12-20)

The two ideas seem to contradict each other. The copy promises magic behind a curtain, while the seller admits that everything behind it is already free. Welsh's own statements resolve the tension, and both can hold because they describe different things. *Curtain language* describes the copy: the framing that creates curiosity and desire. *Aggregation* describes the product: what the buyer actually receives and pays for, which is organization and speed. The lesson for this chapter is that perceived value (the curtain) and delivered value (the aggregation) are separate layers, as Sections 31.1 and 31.2 argued. The ethical risk Chapter 32 discusses lies in the gap between them. A curtain that hides nothing better than aggregation is a frame. A curtain that hides nothing at all is a deception. *Curtain language* and the *testimonial flywheel* are Welsh's (the first adapted from Shlainer). *Aggregation, speed and convenience* is the formula Koe adopted and repeats in his own words. The corpus credits both Koe and Welsh with it.

In September 2023 Koe extends the argument with a provocation. "Free education only works for the one percent that have been conditioned to be motivated, driven and ambitious." Even they still see the value in aggregated information "and will probably buy out of convenience." To the question "why isn't this information available for free?" he answers: "It is; you just aren't ambitious enough to go and find it and do it for yourself." People pay for convenience almost every day: "if you order something off of Amazon rather than going to the store to get it, then you have no business talking about free versus paid content."

**Source:** Micro Education Businesses Are The Future & How To Start With Zero Dollars.md (2023-09-17)

In February 2026 he refines the idea of why people pay, borrowing a distinction from the investor Chris Paik (transcribed as "Pake"). People pay for speed and efficiency when they want to *escape* an experience: DMV wait times, a fast-food worker getting the order wrong, company bureaucracy. People pay a premium for experiences they want to *savor*: a five-star restaurant, live theater, handcrafted goods. Paik's quotation concerns the division of labor between silicon and carbon and ends with "AI handles the friction and humans handle the narrative," for example abolishing baristas while canonizing chefs.

**Source:** The Future Of Work (& The New High-Income Skill Stack).md (2026-02-12)

**Complementary context:** Chris Paik is a venture capitalist (Pace Capital) who writes essays on technology and markets. The "escape vs. savor" framing is his, and Koe adapts it as an answer to what machines and humans will each be paid for.

The 2026 refinement splits the old formula in two. Aggregation, speed and convenience describe the *escape* side: the buyer wants the learning process to be shorter and less painful. The *savor* side is new, and it connects with Koe's later thesis that the human value of education lies in the narrative, the perspective and the relationship (Chapter 36). A learning experience that people want to savor is valued for who delivers it, not only for how fast it is. This links back to "buying a worldview" (Section 31.2).

#### Answering the objections: "there's already enough information," "it's a scam"

Koe answers the objections to info products systematically, mostly in 2024.

*"There's already enough information out there."* In July 2024 he calls this simply false: "if you know what education is, or if you have self-awareness on how you become educated, you know that is just not true; you understand that people learn best from people that they actually relate to." Everyone can be "a teacher to those under them, who then grow and evolve; it's a natural hierarchy," because "certain people learn best from certain people, usually people with very similar personalities." He uses himself as an example: "some people aren't going to learn this information that I'm teaching good from me… because of my talking and teaching style. People say I'm robotic and emotionless, and sure I am, but I love listening to, like, stoic philosophers, and that's what gets through to me, or harsh advice. Some people don't jive with that… so they need to learn it from someone else who relates to them better." Hence: "if you are yourself and you can be yourself, then you can productize and monetize yourself."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md (2024-07-28)

*Natural hierarchy* is one of Koe's coined terms, and here it means a hierarchy of teachers in which each person teaches those just behind them and similar to them. It is the opposite of the dominator hierarchy criticized in Chapter 2. The answer applies the identity layer of perceived value (Section 31.2) to the supply of information. Information is not interchangeable, because its value depends on who delivers it to whom. The same answer underlies "beginner hell" (Chapter 26), where beginners are told to serve people one or two steps behind them.

*"Everyone's selling an information product; it seems like a scam."* In the same video Koe gives four answers. First, he has already answered it: people learn differently from different people. Second: "Every single business on this Earth should have some form of digital product or education product to help align them with what their brand mission and values are, to give people some form of meaning, since they've lost meaning in the other ideologies… like religion and even the school system or government, because that's all being shattered." "People need more decentralized meaning and purpose in their lives, given by individuals who actually pursue their own goals, think freely, and can pass that along with the education that they teach without being dogmatic or ideological about it." Third, the objection "shows that you don't understand business 101; you don't understand sell what's already selling. These things are profitable for a reason, and the economy favors the profitable." Fourth: "You close your mind instantly and label it a scam, and it's no wonder why you stay the same for the rest of your life, because you don't learn, which is the prerequisite to improving anything."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md (2024-07-28)

In February 2024 he gives the same objection a more analytical diagnosis: it is a lack of perspective. "You have no experience in the industry," you judge it from outside, you do not understand its inner workings, and "you are filling in the blanks with the experience that you currently have, none of which is related to the actual information product market."

**Source:** Zero To $1 Million As A One-Person Business (Working 2-4 Hours A Day).md (2024-02-25)

A month later he dismisses a related complaint, that "everyone's just selling digital products." His reply: "Welcome to the real world. Now it's not the digital world anymore; it's not just an idea that's waiting to be tested against reality, it's already been tested." He adds a remark about speed: "We aren't in 2020 anymore, and with the pace of evolution those four years from 2020 to '24 is like a hundred years in the past." "Times are changing very quick, and if you can't adapt you die."

**Source:** Working Hard Won't Make You Rich (Do This Instead).md (2024-03-31)

The "hundred years" comparison is hyperbole. The underlying point is that a business model which has survived contact with the market is no longer speculation, and complaining about its popularity does not change that. "If you can't adapt you die" is the economic form of the entropy corollary in Section 31.1: "even to remain still we must advance."

The answers mix several kinds of argument, and it is worth separating them. The natural hierarchy is an argument about how learning works. *Decentralized meaning* (a term that appears once in the corpus) is a cultural thesis: old sources of meaning are breaking, and individuals who teach from their own pursuit can partly replace them. Chapter 36 develops the idea as the creator economy acting as a decentralized school. "Sell what's already selling" and "the economy favors the profitable" are market arguments. Profitability shows that people are willing to pay, though by itself it does not show that every product is good. The last two answers, the closed mind and the lack of perspective, are diagnoses of the objector, drawn from Chapters 1 and 17. They explain why someone might hold the objection, but they do not refute it on its merits. Koe presents them together, and the reader can weigh them separately.

#### Books, schools and pyramids

Three more objections receive pointed answers.

*Courses versus books.* In February 2025: "As long as people buy books, they will buy courses." People criticize courses and then sell a book, not understanding that "it's the exact same thing. The courses are just organized in a way that is easier to retain. They're arguably more valuable than a book." Koe has written a book and understands that it "feels cool," "but think straight, think clearly." He tells critics to stop repeating what the "anti-gurus" put in their heads. Conditioning your mind to see only half the picture is "as stupid as you think courses are." "You don't whine about there being 129,864,880 books in the world, most of them bad, because anyone can type on a keyboard."

**Source:** A Full Guide To Making Your First Profitable Product (Beginners, Take Notes).md (2025-02-10)

**Complementary context:** The figure of 129,864,880 books comes from a 2010 Google Books estimate of the number of distinct books published in modern history. Koe quotes it without a source.

*The creator economy as a school.* In July 2024, in a rant he tells viewers they can skip: "you complain about certain skills not being taught in schools, but now that they're taught in public for free you just complain that they're a scam. Please make it make sense." Making money, relationships and "all the stuff that actually make life living, or that allow you to take control of your own future," are now taught "in the new school system called the creator economy." They are taught by "the only people that can teach them": people who make money, or have good relationships, or "do all of these sovereign things." If professors are not sovereign, he asks, why would you want them to teach you? "If they had those skills they wouldn't be a teacher or professor. Some of them would, out of passion, but you get what I'm saying."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md (2024-07-28)

*"It's a pyramid scheme."* In June 2023 Koe answers the label applied to people who pass on teachings through courses ("selling a course on how to sell a course"). Critics apply it because they have not done or experienced it themselves. They take one little piece out of the whole story or picture, "like taking a pixel from an image and expecting it to make sense as a pixel." They label it so that they do not have to actually do it, even though it will be a necessary part of life.

**Source:** The One Person Business Model 2.0 (Turn Yourself Into A Business).md (2023-06-25)

These answers share a structure. Each points to an inconsistency in the critic's standard. Books are accepted but courses rejected. Schools are criticized for not teaching life skills, yet people who teach those skills are criticized too. A system is judged by a fragment of it. The "anti-gurus" are Koe's name for critics whose dismissal of courses is itself an unexamined, repeated belief. His claim that people with real skills "wouldn't be a teacher or professor" is a generalization he softens himself ("some of them would, out of passion"). The pixel metaphor connects to the *zoom out* of Chapter 6: a part judged without the whole is misperceived.

#### The education of the future: the Justin Welsh route

In February 2025 Koe offers a positive model. After a few products, he says, one can "take the Justin Welsh route and chill out with a multi-million dollar info product business." He means not make-money-online courses but "something more sophisticated around your interests." Media-based products are not get-rich-quick scams, and one should not think that of Skillshare and Udemy either: "they're the education of the future. Creators are the decentralized educators." Welsh, he recounts, got burned out as an executive at a tech startup. He turned his skills and expertise into consulting, and then into media products: courses, though they could also be cohorts, an ebook or a book. Now he has time for his family, the products "sell on their own," there is no fulfillment work, and he works two to four hours a day, plus writing on social media to attract an audience.

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md (2025-02-23)

The Welsh route is the roadmap of Chapter 30 in a single biography: a skill used in employment, then service (consulting), then product. *Decentralized educators* is Koe's name for creators who teach the paths they themselves opened. Chapter 36 develops the idea as the creator economy acting as a new school system. The case also shows why Koe prefers education products for a one-person business. They require no fulfillment, and so they fit within the four-hour workday (Chapter 12).

#### Three kinds of information consumer, and the epoch of information

In February 2024 Koe distinguishes three groups of people by how they use online information. The first group, most people, use it to distract themselves through argument, mainstream news and "flavor of the day drama." The second realizes this is a trap leading the wrong way and becomes curious about the opposite direction, looking for good information to build their life with. The third is trapped in a negative cycle of consuming content that tells them new information, such as courses, is bad. Courses get a bad rap, he says, but many people who take them know there are incredible ones that genuinely change lives. He asks people with negative views about courses, coaching or info products to watch with an open mind, or not to watch at all. It makes no sense, he says, to "write off an epoch of existence, the age of information, just because you had one bad experience" with a sleazy marketer or a "snake oil salesman." He mentions Liver King as an example, though "he's not selling courses."

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md (2024-02-06)

**Complementary context:** "Liver King" (Brian Johnson) is a fitness influencer who promoted an "ancestral" lifestyle of raw organ meat and admitted in 2022, after denying it, that he used anabolic steroids. The scandal made him a byword for deceptive online influencers.

*The distracted* and *the curious* are Koe's names for the first two groups; the third has no short name. The classification places the objection to courses inside the information environment of Chapter 1. Rejecting all courses can itself be a conditioned belief, acquired from content that profits from the rejection. The phrase "an epoch of existence" ties the argument to Chapter 2's account of the shift from the industrial age to the information age. Refusing to buy information, in an economy built on it, is in Koe's eyes like refusing to use tools in an age of tools.

#### The buyer's side: why free content fails, and what buying does

So far the section has argued for education products from the seller's side. Koe's own experience as a buyer tells a more mixed story, and the corpus records it without resolving it.

In January 2023 he explains why learning from free content never gave him results. First, the information is "so scattered." Second, social media creators are playing "the game of catching attention," and he includes himself: "I know this well, this is what I do." In order to grow, and not be "stuck at zero subscribers posting into the void," creators deliver value as educational or actionable advice or as entertainment, usually "short, sweet and just focused on high performing topics." Most of the topics inside the agency course he took, he says, if they had been posted online as separate videos "without people showing intent by actually purchasing a course… nobody would talk about it." They are boring, and nobody wants to sit down and listen to them.

**Source:** The Most Profitable Niche Is You (Create Your Niche Of One).md (2023-01-29)

This is the pearl principle again (Section 31.1), seen from the market's side. The attention game selects for the visible pearl and filters out the slow, boring fundamentals that produce it. A paid product, with a buyer who has shown intent, is the format in which the fundamentals can be delivered. It is also the counterpart of "the boring fundamentals" of Chapter 12: most of the result comes from repeating basics that never trend.

But purchase alone is not the answer either. In October 2023 Koe tells a warning story: "I used to be a course hoarder… I think I purchased like 50 software development courses when I was learning programming, and I would just hoard them, and I'd watch them, and I'd build projects along with them, but I didn't really learn anything." They built knowledge "to an extent, but they never really led to any tangible results, especially in terms of income, which is the main reason we're all really learning things."

In the same video he tells the opposite story. When he decided to go all in on social media, "I invested 3,500 in a coaching program." He describes two camps. One says "these people are charging five to ten thousand dollars for coaching, that's insane," because, in his words, "one, you probably don't have the money; two, you haven't experienced good education before; three, you don't see the potential, you don't believe, you don't have the mindset, you don't have the awareness… of opportunity." The second camp enjoys learning, sees the potential, believes it can be part of it, and invests. His result: "I only showed up to one coaching call, and I'm not mad about it, I'm very grateful." "The only thing that kicked me into action was wasting that money, 3,500 bucks down the drain. I went to the coaching call, I realized, oh, I already know all of this stuff, why am I not doing anything about it, and I started doing it. But if I didn't spend that money I wouldn't have done it."

**Source:** A Dangerous (But Effective) Strategy To Get Out Of A Rut.md (2023-10-02)

In December 2023 he names the most common failure among buyers. Most people either buy courses and do nothing, or cannot stay consistent with writing content. Others in the space tell him that "the biggest problem with my clients is that they just don't write content." "That's literally all you have to do to stand out."

**Source:** The Future Of Work Is Play (How To Create A Digital Career).md (2023-12-31)

The three stories should be read together, because each qualifies the others. The course hoarder shows that buying information without a project produces knowledge but not results. That is the "tutorial hell" of Chapter 14, where knowledge must not outrun execution. The coaching story shows that buying can produce action, but in Koe's case the trigger was not the content (he "already knew all of this stuff"). It was the stress of the sunk cost. That is the "money or pain on the line" mechanism of Section 31.4, working through regret rather than commitment. The complaint that clients "don't write content" shows the same gap from the seller's side. Taken together, the stories suggest the condition Koe's later statements make explicit: a purchase helps or not depending on whether it produces action. The corpus documents both effects without choosing between them. In 2026 Koe says "I love courses. I take them all the time," while insisting that learning is a process of output. The tension does not disappear. It becomes a rule: buy education in order to act, not in order to know.

#### Courses or books?

A related piece of advice dates from April 2022. Most of Koe's business knowledge came "through trial and error and specific knowledge via courses. So in terms of business, I recommend courses over books," unless you want a foundational understanding of influence, persuasion or specific tactics. For self-improvement, spirituality and philosophy, "definitely recommend books."

**Source:** 7 Books That Will Change Your Life In 2022.md (2022-04-23)

The heuristic separates two kinds of knowledge. Business knowledge is specific, changes quickly and is learned by doing, so courses (organized, current, practitioner-made) and trial and error serve it best. Philosophical knowledge is general and slow-changing, and it reshapes worldview, so books serve it best. Koe's views on reading changed over time, as Chapter 16 documents. At one point he calls reading to learn a form of "entertainment" when there is no project, while keeping reading as a way to expand one's worldview. The 2022 heuristic is an early form of that later distinction between reading "to learn how to do" and reading "to see the world." The exception for influence and persuasion is consistent with this chapter: the principles of perceived value are foundational and stable, so a book serves them well.

#### The author's case: the six-figure agency course

The section ends with Koe's own first purchase of business education. He tells the story in detail in January 2023 and summarizes it in other videos. It illustrates both the value of education and its limits.

Before buying, he did not see the value in courses. "I didn't trust courses, I didn't trust the people selling them, I knew that all of the information could be found online," in YouTube videos or cheaper courses like those on Udemy, "but that never worked": he saw no results from learning that way. He found "a dude that had a good reputation," Billy Willson (spelled "Billy Wilson" in one transcript). He paid what the transcript renders as "9.99 or 9.97" (the exact figure is ambiguous, though it was a large sum for him) for a "six-figure agency course." He calls it a great course that he learned a lot from, but it was "a huge chunk out of my finances."

**Source:** The Most Profitable Niche Is You (Create Your Niche Of One).md (2023-01-29)

The circumstances explain the stakes. He was "fed up" with multiple business attempts and with learning online and from books. He wanted nothing more to do with the space, but he gave himself "one last try." He researched the latest and greatest business models that could take him to six figures, a good thing to aim for at the start, he says, though "you soon realize that that is just the beginning." "This one had to work, or else I go back to coding or getting a web design job." He was about to drop out of school, was deciding whether to do his homework, and had a part-time job. Once he hit the first resistance (reaching out cold, emailing, getting on calls), it all made sense, but with so much pressure it all fell to pieces. He did not get results, and he ended up taking a job, because it made the most sense at the time. His full story, he says, is "a jumble of multiple business failures, and then eventually getting a job, and then eventually making one work."

**Source:** The Most Profitable Niche Is You (Create Your Niche Of One).md (2023-01-29)

In his June 2022 catalog of failed businesses, the episode is failure number four. Searching "how to make six figures online," he found Willson's course, which he calls really good. It taught lead generation, advertising and copywriting. He sent "maybe 10–50 cold emails," closed no clients, gave up and moved on.

**Source:** 7 Lessons From 7 Failed Online Businesses (And Making 7 Figures).md (2022-06-18)

His diagnosis names three things that, on top of the pressure in his life, destroyed his chance with the Facebook ads agency. First, he "didn't give a flying [__] about running a Facebook ads agency"; he did not want to actually work on the business. Second, cold emailing local businesses was time-consuming and stressful, and none of them had the money to pay him. He concedes he could have targeted richer clients. Third, "I was never confident in my niche, and that led to an immense amount of friction and shiny object syndrome." The journey, he says, taught him a lot that he would use in future business models. It also revealed a problem that would repeat "time and time again": whenever he started a business, or had something going well, he would come back to the question "what is my niche."

**Source:** The Most Profitable Niche Is You (Create Your Niche Of One).md (2023-01-29)

**Complementary context:** Billy Willson is an entrepreneur known for selling courses on starting a social media marketing agency. The corpus spells his name both "Willson" and "Wilson."

The case belongs in a chapter on value for three reasons. First, it shows that a good education product (Koe never retracts his praise of the course) can still fail to produce results. The failure came from the buyer's situation, not the information: no interest in the business, an unsuitable market, an unclear niche. This is the 2026 point about root causes (Section 31.4) seen in Koe's own past. The course could not supply the identity and the confidence the business required. Second, it shows the "value is perception" layer at work on Koe as a buyer. Distrust of courses had kept him learning from scattered free content. A seller's reputation, and his own desperation, made him perceive the course as worth a large share of his finances. Third, it is the origin of the theme of Chapter 19. The repeated question "what is my niche," and the friction it produced, are what Koe later answered with "you are the niche." The corpus records two readings of his own *shiny object syndrome*. Here it is part of the cause of failure. Elsewhere he defends it as experimentation that stacked skills, and later says it has been "trying to tell you" that specialization slows development. Both readings appear in his work without resolution; Chapter 20 discusses them. A further tension appears in his later lessons. The 10–50 emails he sent are the kind of small sample he would criticize as too small to judge a business by, so the failure may say as much about the size of the test as about the model.

The chapter closes where Koe's career as a seller of education began, with a disappointed buyer. The theory of value he later built (substance and perception, behavior change, education as the precursor to behavior, results that depend on the buyer's identity) can be read in part as his answer to what happened to him. Chapter 32 takes the perception layer and develops it into a full account of persuasion: its ethics, the levels of awareness, market sophistication and the three tensions.
