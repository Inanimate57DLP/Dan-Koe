## Chapter 28 — Business as a Life's Work

Part IX ended with a person who has a brand: a public character, a vision, a body of content and the trust that accumulates around it. Part X asks what that person does for a living. The answer Dan Koe gives is not a separate topic attached to the rest of his system; it is the system itself, seen from the side of the market. Chapter 9 already introduced the claim in its largest form: the life's work is what you choose to do with your life, and in the present era its most natural *vessel* (Koe's word for the container that carries a purpose) is a business built on the internet. This chapter examines that business directly. It asks why Koe treats business as a discipline of personal development rather than its opposite, what philosophy joins the two, what he means by "entrepreneur," why he thinks the one-person business is the default form of work for the coming decades, what the minimum definition of a business is, and what parts every one-person business is made of.

The chapter depends on three earlier blocks. From Part IV it needs the goal, the problem and the project: Koe's business philosophy is the goal–path–problem structure of Chapter 8 pointed outward, and his "product" is the project of Chapter 9 offered to others. From Part VII it needs "you are the niche" (Chapter 19): if the customer is your former self, the business is you, made public. From Part IX it needs the audience and the brand (Chapters 25–27): the business, in its minimal definition, is an audience plus a product, and the previous three chapters were about the first half.

The chapter proceeds in six steps. Section 28.1 sets out the thesis Koe compresses as "personal growth equals business growth" and the reasons he gives for treating a business as a *forcing function* for every area of life. Section 28.2 presents the philosophy he calls "solve your own problems and sell the solution," with the processes he built around it and its qualifications. Section 28.3 widens the definition of entrepreneurship until it covers everyone, and records how Koe's position moved from a career choice to a state of mind; it also gathers his warnings against startup ideas. Section 28.4 explains the one-person business as a model, its successive definitions, the career paths he drew for it and the "seven billion companies" thesis about the future of work. Section 28.5 reduces business to its minimum (traffic and an offer, people and a product) and collects the versions of the core sequence Koe repeats from 2022 to 2026. Section 28.6 closes with the pillars (brand, content, product, and sometimes promotion or marketing), the many formulations they received, the observation that the solo founder is every department at once, and the levers that decide how much the business earns.

### 28.1 Personal Growth Equals Business Growth

#### The thesis and its origin

The phrase Koe repeats most often about the relation between self and business is a symmetry: "personal growth equals business growth." He explains it in November 2023 by way of creativity. "Creativity is dependent on the expansion of your mind, and this is why I always say that personal growth equals business growth." Personal growth, in this formulation, means expanding the mind so that the self can expand; business growth depends on creativity; creativity depends on the expanded mind. The equation is therefore a causal chain, not a slogan: a business can only grow as far as the mind running it can see.

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

He illustrates the chain with his own beginning. Through education and by exposing himself to the unknown, he "registered new opportunities to solve my problem." The problem was money and a life he did not want; the obstacle was that "I wasn't going to be able to solve the problem that I had from the same mind that created it." He had to explore business opportunities he would not previously have understood, or even *registered* as opportunities. Because he had not seen it as possible to start a business that required money or a skill set he did not have, he started with the one route his mind could see at that moment: freelancing.

The verb "registered" is technical in Koe's vocabulary. It comes from the model of perception built in Chapters 3 and 6: the mind filters information through its goals and identity, so an opportunity that does not fit the current mental model is not rejected but simply not seen. The business consequence is that a person cannot choose a business model they cannot perceive. Expanding the mind is therefore the first business task, because it enlarges the set of options that can be registered at all. Section 28.4 returns to the same mechanism when Koe explains why he presents the one-person business as a starting point: most people do not consider it because their environment has not made it visible.

The sentence about solving a problem "from the same mind that created it" has a history worth noting. In 2023 Koe used it, as here, in the first person and without attribution; in other passages of the same year he paraphrased it as "no problem can be solved from the same level of consciousness that created it," announced that he would reformulate it as *level of mind* (the concept Chapter 4 defined), and in October 2023 attributed it to Einstein without reservation. From late 2024 he writes "Einstein supposedly said." The idea is the same throughout; what changes is the attribution, from none to firm to doubtful, and the vocabulary, from "consciousness" to "mind" to "development."

**Complementary context:** The line is popularly attributed to Albert Einstein, but no reliable source for it has been found; the author's late hedge ("supposedly") matches its status.

#### A new product is a new identity

A month later Koe gives the thesis its most compact identity form. "Creating a new product isn't just creating a new product, it's creating a new identity with the skill set and mindset you must acquire to build the product, sell the product and improve the product. Business growth is a vessel for personal growth."

**Source:** How Intelligent Creators Will Make Money In 2024 (The Build Teach Earn Method).md

The sentence connects business to the theory of identity change in Chapter 4. There, every durable change in behavior was a change in identity, and identities were networks of goals and the skills, beliefs and habits that serve them. A product is a goal of that kind: to build it, sell it and improve it, a person must acquire skills and a mindset they did not have, and those additions do not stay in the business. They reorganize the person. Read this way, the equation runs in both directions. Personal growth makes business growth possible (the first formulation), and business growth forces personal growth (the second). The business is a vessel in the precise sense of Chapter 9: it carries the development of the person who builds it.

#### Business instead of meditation

Koe is explicit that this is his own method and not the only one. In October 2022 he says that "business and writing and content creation is my method, is my tool, as opposed to like meditation, for self-actualizing and self-transcending." Writing to yourself, he adds, is "a great way of just personal growth in general."

**Source:** The Rise Of The Value Creator (A Career Path For Polymaths & Self-Improvers).md

Two terms in the sentence come from developmental psychology. *Self-actualization* is the realization of one's potential (in another passage Koe defines it as "to fulfill the desire to become everything one can be"), and *self-transcendence* is the movement beyond the concerns of the individual self toward something larger. The claim is not that business replaces meditation for everyone, but that it can play the same role: a practice through which a person both develops and is drawn beyond themselves. Chapter 9 (Section 9.4) collected the stronger version of the idea, business as "arguably the most spiritual endeavor you can take," and Chapter 40 discusses meditation itself. Here the point is narrower: in Koe's own life, the business is the practice.

**Complementary context:** Abraham Maslow placed self-actualization at the top of his original hierarchy of needs and, in his late work, added self-transcendence above it. Koe uses both words in roughly that sense without discussing Maslow's model in these passages.

#### The business as a forcing function

In March 2023, in a long conversation with Dickie Bush, Koe adopts a term from his guest. "I just registered what that actually means," he says of Bush's phrase *forcing function*, and applies it: "the one-person business model is a forcing function for improving every area of your life." That, he adds, is why he pushes the model so hard at that moment.

**Source:** Dan Koe & Dickie Bush On One-Person Businesses, Creative Workflows, and Lifestyle Design.md

A forcing function, in Bush's use, is a project whose unavoidable by-product is a set of new skills: you commit to something that cannot be done without them, and acquiring them stops being optional. The term belongs to Bush; the application to the one-person business, and the claim that it reaches every area of life, is Koe's. The phrase recurs later in the corpus in other applications (teaching in public as a forcing function for understanding; investing money you cannot yet afford as pressure to grow), which suggests that Koe found in it a name for something he already believed: that the right commitment does the work that willpower cannot.

#### Six things a business demands

The fullest explanation of why business forces development comes from an August 2023 video. Koe first reduces the activity to its core: business, entrepreneurship or being a creator "is just providing value and receiving the value you want as money." He then lists the reasons that activity builds confidence, each framed as a demand the business makes on the person.

**Source:** You Aren't Successful Because You Care Too Much (How To Stop).md

*The first demand is to evolve or die.* "Most people die at 25": they get the job, the spouse, the house and the car, exactly what society wants, someone "docile and of little threat to their system," and from then on they are trying to survive. Koe immediately makes clear that the death is not literal. "Death is symbolic and all understanding is metaphorical": if life is growth and growth requires the new, then death is the death of new ideas, new potentials, new projects and new practices. Life is "ever flowing and evolving," and obstructing that flow by settling for a subpar life is the death he means. Entrepreneurship, he argues, ensures that you do not die in this sense in a changing world, with one condition attached: "if you persist until success." The argument draws on Chapter 5's claim that there is no staying the same under entropy, and on the critique of the default path in Chapter 2. A job does not stop the world from changing; it only stops the person from changing with it.

**Complementary context:** The image echoes a saying usually attributed to Benjamin Franklin ("many people die at twenty-five and aren't buried until they are seventy-five"), which Sahil Bloom quotes in another part of the corpus. In this passage Koe states it without attribution.

*The second demand is expertise.* Subpar understanding yields subpar results, and understanding differs from knowledge (the distinction Chapter 17 develops as horizontal knowing versus vertical understanding). Here Koe gives it a spiritual geometry: "understanding is the upward motion of expanding consciousness; meditation is the downward motion of expanding consciousness." Most mystics and spiritual teachers, he says, forget the balance. "It's not creativity or productivity, monk or warrior, peace or progress, being or doing; it's both." "The duality collapses into one." The practical consequence is a warning: stack theory without practice and you fail; you need daily education *and* execution. The passage is a metaphor, not a model of consciousness offered as fact, and it anticipates the balance between peace and progress that Chapter 39 treats at length. Its function here is to defend business against the charge of being unspiritual: the effort to understand a market, a skill or a customer is, in this image, the same expansion of consciousness that meditation produces, approached from the other direction.

*The third demand is results*, for yourself and for customers. "If you don't get results you aren't valuable." Koe reads the absence of results as information, not as a verdict: it is "a sign to improve, not quit." True value requires development over time, and entrepreneurship ensures that you develop into what he calls "a valuable asset."

*The fourth demand is that you suck first*, so you can improve. It is foolish, he says, to think you won't be bad at almost everything at first, and the expectation of competence from the start is what keeps most people from acting on their dreams. Once you are past the first hump, you "snowball into a season of pure progress." The "first hump" is the same one Chapter 8 described when it explained how passion is created by investing energy until a goal pays you back; Chapter 29 turns the principle into the "permission to suck" that justifies launching an imperfect first product.

*The fifth demand is new habits of work, rest and play.* "You cannot reach level 2 with the character that got you to level one." The video-game image (Chapter 10) makes the point that the person who succeeded at one level has habits adapted to that level, and that the next level requires a different character. Growth requires change, and change is uncomfortable, "which is why most settle early."

*The sixth demand is a skill set based on psychology.* Business is communicating your value to others and receiving something in return, so it forces you to understand your own mind and therefore the minds of others. "Marketing, sales, writing and speaking are the backbone of business success." (In the same passage he announces that he will redo his "million-dollar skill stack" video, the subject of Chapter 35.)

| Demand | What the business requires | What it develops in the person |
|---|---|---|
| Evolve or die | Continuous adaptation in a changing world | Escape from the symbolic "death at 25" of the settled life |
| Expertise | Understanding, not mere knowledge | Theory joined to daily execution ("it's both") |
| Results | Outcomes for yourself and customers | Development into a "valuable asset"; failure read as feedback |
| Suck first | Tolerating early incompetence | A "season of pure progress" after the first hump |
| New habits | A different character for each level | New patterns of work, rest and play |
| Psychology skill set | Communicating value to other minds | Self-understanding and understanding of others |

Later in the same video Koe adds the mechanism behind the confidence. Building a product builds confidence because you will suck at first, as with anything, and you get *direct feedback* on what can be improved: "people can be brutal, but most are nice." Business seems foreign to many people, but that is no reason to see it as frightening. The feedback also cuts the other way. "If you want to actually make money you need to actually provide value," and "if you aren't continuing to grow your audience or money, maybe start thinking about how valuable you really are." The sentence is harsh, and it is meant to be. It turns income and audience growth into a measuring instrument for the value one is creating. It also contains the limit of the instrument: it measures *perceived* value in a market, which Chapter 31 will show is not the same as value in itself, and it measures it only for someone who is actually putting work in front of people.

#### What choosing business does to a life

In a May 2023 video on living on autopilot, Koe gives the same argument from the side of values. Someone who values building a business values providing value to others. To do that they must increase their skill and develop themselves, and they must have "a mind that is attractive and able to actually spark positive behavior change through business." Choices aligned with that value then produce three effects: more energy (higher output), less financial stress (more money), and a deeper satisfaction that comes from the intellectual development that writing or content creation brings.

**Source:** Most People Are NPCs (Don't Waste Your Life On Autopilot).md

The example belongs to an argument about how values organize choices (the video's subject is the NPC who lives without chosen values, a figure introduced in Chapter 1). Its relevance here is that it states the forcing-function thesis as a chain of consequences rather than a list of demands: value business, and you are compelled to value your own development, because only a developed mind can produce the behavior change a business sells.

#### Self-improvement as the seed of business

The last element of the thesis connects the domains of self-improvement to the domains of business. In April 2023 Koe puts it as a symmetry. "Self-improvement is about solving your own problems in those domains; business is about solving other people's problems within those domains. So you bring the two together." The domains are mind, body and business (elsewhere, health, wealth, relationships and happiness, the Eternal markets of Chapter 19). The prescription follows: set a purposeful goal to improve yourself, self-educate, acquire skills for mind, body and business, let curiosity guide which methods you test, and you will have the experience necessary to help others and "profit off of living with purpose."

**Source:** Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md

He then answers an objection in advance. The word "project" does not sound as if it applies to every domain of life, "but hear me out." In health, "you only make tangible progress when you turn it into a measurable personal project." You go to the gym, write down your routine, document every lift. If you do not see progress, you research and find the gaps in your knowledge; perhaps nutrition is holding you back, so you start tracking calories. Now your physique progresses but your energy does not, so perhaps you "fall down an esoteric rabbit hole of how beef liver is the most nutrient-dense food on the planet," experiment with it and see whether you get results. At the end you have "an interesting, unique way of getting results in health that other people would benefit from that are like you."

The example is a miniature of the whole business philosophy. A domain becomes a *measurable personal project* (Koe's term): it gets a goal, a record and a feedback loop. The feedback reveals a gap; the gap leads to research; the research leads to an experiment, sometimes an unusual one; the experiment yields a personal method. The method is unique not because it was designed to be different but because it is the residue of one person's sequence of problems. And the people who would benefit from it are, specifically, "people like you," which is the argument of Chapter 19 in one phrase. The beef liver is deliberately idiosyncratic; it shows that the "unique solution" Koe values is often a strange detail that only someone who followed the problem all the way down would find.

#### Two cautions: self-awareness and the starving artist

Two short principles complete the bridge between self-improvement and business, one about where to look and one about what else to learn.

The first is that "self-awareness is the greatest business hack." In September 2024 Koe tells beginners to pay attention to their own behavior on social media and as customers. When you buy something, why did you buy it? "Okay, you just extracted a lesson; now implement it into your business." When you read a post and follow its author, did the bio change your decision? "Maybe it locked it in, but most of the time no." The principle was presented in Chapter 19 as a way of finding one's niche; here it shows the same idea at work on marketing. If the customer is your former self, your own reactions as a consumer are the closest thing you have to customer research.

**Source:** The Fastest Way To Build A One-Person Business (Beginner Guide).md

The second is that passion is not enough. A May 2024 video opens with its thesis: "do what you love, but study psychology, human behavior and the internet so you don't become a starving artist." The *starving artist* is a common phrase that Koe uses with a specific meaning across the corpus: the creator who does what they love but refuses to learn how value is perceived and distributed, and therefore cannot sustain the work. Psychology and human behavior stand for persuasion; the internet stands for distribution. The rest of that video argues that school and jobs do not teach monetization and lays out the eight steps of what Koe calls the "$1 million Creator"; Section 28.6 uses its last step, the levers.

**Source:** How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md

#### Never get trapped in a stage

Koe closed his December 2022 "One Person Business Roadmap" with what he called the main lesson of the video, and reused the passage almost word for word in a long 2024 compilation: "never stop learning, never stop building, never stop evolving. Don't get trapped in one of the stages, evolve. Distraction and comfort are the only things that can get in your way. Self-actualize, self-monetize and self-transcend."

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The "stages" are the stages of the roadmap itself (service, product, and later forms of the business, which Chapter 30 reconstructs), but the warning applies to the whole thesis of this section. If business growth follows personal growth, then a business that stops at a stage is a person who stopped developing, and the two enemies Koe names are not external. Distraction is the failure of attention described in Part III and Chapter 11; comfort is the settling that the "evolve or die" argument diagnosed. The closing triad compresses the section into three verbs. *Self-actualize* is the personal development; *self-monetize* is the business that carries it (Koe's term *self-monetization* is defined in Section 28.6); *self-transcend* is the movement beyond oneself toward contribution, which Chapter 9 called the highest level of purpose. The order matters: in Koe's sequence, monetization sits between actualization and transcendence, as the bridge that lets a developing person sustain the work through which they reach beyond themselves.

### 28.2 Solve Your Own Problems and Sell the Solution

#### Where the philosophy was born

Section 28.1 described the business as the vehicle of a person's development. This section describes the principle that makes the vehicle earn money. Koe gives it a fixed name, *solve your own problems and sell the solution*, and in February 2024 he tells the story of its birth. People make millions solving fitness, productivity, career, money, relationship and lifestyle problems, because every person has them, and those problems prevent the average individual from doing the only thing they want, which is to enjoy life. "What better and more profitable problem is there to solve than the one you experience every day?" That question, he says, "is where my philosophy of solve your own problems and sell the solution was born." The applications are direct: results in fitness, sell a fitness program; results in focus, sell a productivity course; results with a skill, sell a tutorial. He adds a provocation: if you have not solved any problem in your life (which he doubts), you are not qualified; "get out of your mom's basement and start doing something with your life."

**Source:** Zero To $1 Million As A One-Person Business (Working 2-4 Hours A Day).md

The argument has the structure of the Eternal markets from Chapter 19. The most profitable problems are the most universal ones, and the most universal ones are the ones you already live with. The insult at the end is not gratuitous within the logic of the system: the qualification for this kind of business is not a credential but a solved problem, and the remedy for having none is to start solving one.

#### The two kinds of problem-solving collapse into one

The core formulation appears in nearly identical words in at least five videos of 2023 and 2024, and it is worth seeing how each adds a piece. The common sentence is "personal development is about solving your own problems; business is about solving other people's problems."

In April 2023 Koe draws the product from it. "You create a product that would have helped your former self, or would have helped you get to where you are faster." His example is the planner: "if I need mental clarity, I'm going to create a planner or a journal and then I'm just going to sell that to others." The term *former self* (the person you were before you solved the problem) is the name Chapter 19 gave to the customer of this model.

**Source:** Your 20s Are Meant To Build (How To Not Waste Them).md

Three days later he adds the medium. "The internet collapses both into one and allows you to earn from solving your own problems and living with purpose." The prescription is to develop yourself, document the solutions through an education business, and help develop others; and its horizon is large: "by treating your life as your life's work you go on to change the course of humanity for the better."

**Source:** The Micro Education Business Model (How To Monetize Your Knowledge).md

In a video of the same month he states the method and its sequence. Collapse the two into one: solve your own problems, document the process online, and ask for money in return by creating a product, a system or an implementation; "it all works itself out. You are the niche." The pattern of problems is the Eternal markets (health, wealth, relationships, "happiness optional"), and he suggests an order: fix your health first (get fit), then your money problems, then your relationships, then your mindset and mental health. After that comes reflection ("what did I do?"), the conversion of the experience into step-by-step advice organized by an overarching theory or philosophy, and packaging in the right *modality*: a course, an email course, an e-book, a physical product. He closes the video with a three-part formula and a claim about the markets themselves: "solve your own problems, document the solution, get paid," because the Eternal markets contain "burning evergreen problems" that people will face "until the end of time." "The future is bright for the people that take responsibility for their lives."

**Source:** Self Improvers Are Creating Their Own Careers (The New Economy).md

In *The Art of Focus* (2024), the book whose chapter 13 is titled "The Vessel," the formulation acquires its most quotable opening: "Everyone is an entrepreneur, some just choose to get paid for the problems they solve in their personal life. All profitable products start as a project, whether the intention to sell is there or not." To generate a creative, independent income "to free yourself from society's chains," you need to sell a valuable product, and a personal project that solves a burning problem all beings encounter is the best starting point. Koe tells readers to stop overcomplicating making money and "trying to solve creative problems that don't exist." Acquiring skills to solve your own problems is "an automatic filter for value": a problem you actually had is, by definition, a real one. Then you turn the solution into a product.

**Source:** The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md

The cumulative structure is clear when the versions are laid side by side. The symmetry (my problems, their problems) defines business as the outward continuation of self-improvement. The *former self* gives the customer. The internet gives the medium that makes the collapse possible at scale. The sequence of domains (health, money, relationships, mindset) gives an order of operations. The documentation and the "overarching theory" give the step that turns an experience into something teachable. And the "automatic filter for value" explains why the method is safer than inventing a product: the problem is guaranteed to exist because you lived it. The order of domains reflects the argument of Chapter 9 (Section 9.1) that shallow problems must be solved before deeper ones; Koe does not present it as a rule, only as the sequence he suggests.

#### Same goals, different paths: your path is your product

The philosophy depends on a premise about human beings that Koe states in September 2023. "The best route ... is to solve your own problems in life and sell the solution, because everyone has the same goals and problems; they're just viewing them from different lenses, from different identities." Everyone wants financial freedom, health, vitality, love: the benefits of improving health, wealth and relationships. But one person tries budgeting, another a side hustle, another an e-commerce store, another a social media brand; "what you do to achieve the goals will be completely different from someone else, so that path is your product." "Your job is to attract people like you by talking about your journey, then package up your path to achieving the goal and sell it."

**Source:** How To Reprogram Your Mind & Build Your Intelligence.md

The premise has two halves, and both matter. The shared goals make the market large (this is the Eternal-markets argument). The different paths make each seller unique, and they also make the match between seller and buyer selective: someone who chose budgeting will not be persuaded by a path built on e-commerce, but someone with "the same identity and similar perception" will find that what you sell "resonates." *Your path is your product* (his phrase) is therefore both a theory of differentiation and a theory of fit, and it explains how the model avoids the saturation Chapter 27 argued does not exist.

Earlier in the same video Koe states the mechanism in its simplest form and attaches a caution to it. He presents "the one-person business, or turning yourself into the business": if he attracts like-minded people and creates a solution to a problem in his own life, such as a planner, he can sell it, because it helped him; if like-minded people follow him because of the information he shares online "through my character and virtual reality, which is a personal brand on social media," that is how he earns an income. "Pretty simple." But: "I don't want it to be an ideology, but that's what it is; just don't adopt it as law."

**Source:** How To Reprogram Your Mind & Build Your Intelligence.md

The caution is consistent with the rest of the video, which warns against identifying with static ideologies (Chapter 1, Section 1.7, and Chapter 17 on creating one's own philosophy). Koe is aware that his model has become a doctrine for some of his audience, and he asks them to hold it as a principle to test rather than a creed to defend.

#### The entire philosophy: life as a video game across the Eternal markets

In July 2024 Koe places the principle inside the game metaphor of Chapter 10 and calls the result "the entire philosophy I'm putting across." Problems are preferably found in the Eternal markets: health, wealth, relationships and happiness. (The transcript reads "internal markets," almost certainly a mistranscription of "eternal.") "You're treating your life like a video game; you're increasing those domains of your life ... by setting goals for each of them under an overarching vision, to identify problems and then solve those problems so that you are valuable." Different people solve the same domain through different interests: "one person can solve their wealth problems with financing, the other can solve it with business." So "everyone creates these unique solutions that they can then pass down and make more efficient so people can self-actualize faster; that's the entire philosophy."

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md

The passage joins three structures from earlier chapters. The *overarching vision* (Chapter 7) sits above goals for each domain (Chapter 8's goal hierarchy); the goals generate problems (Chapter 8's goal–path–problem: without a goal there is no problem); solving the problems makes you valuable; and the solutions, being personal, are unique and can be passed down. The final clause gives the philosophy its social purpose. The point of passing a solution down is that the next person "can self-actualize faster." In this view a product is accumulated trial and error that one person has done so another does not have to repeat it, the same idea Chapter 9 expressed through the image of building a house well enough to pass it on.

#### Profit off your purpose: Koe's guiding light

The April 2023 video that gave the philosophy its title also gives it a procedure, which Koe calls the process for profiting "off of your purpose." It has six steps: (1) identify a problem in your life; (2) set a goal to reveal your present purpose; (3) experiment with different solutions; (4) find what works and create your own; (5) talk about your journey in public; (6) offer your solution with a price tag on it. "Repeating this process has been my guiding light in life."

**Source:** Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md

Each step takes a concept from an earlier chapter. Step one uses the redefinition of purpose as the most urgent problem (Chapter 8). Step two introduces *present purpose* (Koe's term): the purpose that a goal reveals for now, set against a concrete problem, as opposed to a lifelong mission one is supposed to discover. Steps three and four are the self-experimentation of Chapter 15. Step five is teaching and building in public (Chapter 14). Step six is the business. The procedure is the six steps of personal development with a seventh activity, publication, inserted before the last, and a price tag added at the end.

The same video explains where the "profitable interests" that feed the process come from, and Koe's answer is that "we start with life direction." You need a vision, goals and priority tasks you can execute every day (he points to an earlier video, "Society is a pyramid scheme," and to a free "power planner"). "This is how you gain experience in life by moving towards things": direction gives you the experience, knowledge and skill, and the direction to acquire both, which you can then pass down to those on the same path. The order is significant. A person does not begin by looking for something to sell; they begin by having somewhere to go, and the sellable experience is a by-product of moving toward it.

He then generalizes the health example quoted in Section 28.1. "When you treat every area of your life as a purpose-oriented project, you are required to self-experiment, and through self-experimentation you can create a unique set of experiences and lessons and skills and expertise that you can pass down to others." A *purpose-oriented project* is his name for any domain of life given a goal and run as an experiment. And the benefits that come from living this way are the marketing: "The benefits you reap from living with purpose are your marketing firepower to help people live better lives. That is the underlying philosophy. [What] you forge through self-experimentation is how you generate a creative income from your pursuits."

The phrase *marketing firepower* has a specific sense here (the corpus also uses it, elsewhere, for the persuasive pieces of a landing page). In this passage it means that the results of a purposeful life are themselves the argument for buying the product: a better body, a calmer mind or a working business are visible proofs, and the person who has them does not need to invent claims. This anticipates the more developed idea, below, that "your marketing is the intrinsic philosophy that you form with your reasons."

#### A duty, not only a business

In July 2023 Koe gives the same process an ethical and spiritual reading. "As you learn and acquire skills, it is your duty to build real-world projects that get results for other people, and that is how you both create a profitable product in business and fulfill your spiritual duty of feeling as if you are part of something greater than yourself by helping other people at scale."

**Source:** Niche Down Is Terrible Advice (For Smart People).md

The word "duty" is unusual in a corpus that mostly speaks of choice and freedom, and it marks a step in the argument. Learning that is never applied to anyone else stays private; Koe treats it as incomplete. The real-world project that helps others is where the private development of 28.1 becomes the contribution of Chapter 9's highest level of purpose, and the same act produces the profit. The claim is that the commercial and the spiritual are not two motives competing for the same act, but two descriptions of it.

#### Merging art and business

Koe's 2025 version of the idea borrows from Naval Ravikant. The last step of a February 2025 process is to "merge art and business," which he introduces with Naval's line: "To build product, make something people want. To create art, make something you want. The best do both." Koe comments that it is sad that people cannot accept that business, entrepreneurship or being a creator can be very fulfilling, "because happiness is a two-sided coin: the creativity required to make progress and contributing to something greater than yourself," that is, becoming valuable and sharing that value with the internet. "You merge art and business when you make something you want and share it with people who have similar interests, because they will probably want it too." His prescription: "write content for yourself, treat the internet as your public journal, put effort into getting that spread to more people, build a product you would buy, use and benefit from."

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

The quotation is Naval's; the reading of it is Koe's. Naval's sentence opposes two creative motives (what others want, what you want) and praises those who satisfy both. Koe dissolves the opposition with the premise of this section: if you are the niche, what you want and what people like you want are the same thing, so the artist's motive and the businessman's motive converge. The definition of happiness as "a two-sided coin" (creativity plus contribution) is Koe's own, and Chapter 39 develops its fuller version (happiness as resistance overcome plus connection).

#### Offline it's growth, online it's business

The most compact statement of the whole philosophy is a six-step sequence Koe gave in April 2023 and repeated in a 2024 compilation. "Set a goal, find a problem, learn how to solve it, document how you solved it, distill it into a replicable process, give it to others that want to be helped. Do it offline and it's called growth. Do it online and it's called business. That's it."

**Source:** Your 20s Are Meant To Build (How To Not Waste Them).md; Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md

Three days later he gave a five-step version without the first step: find a problem, learn how to solve it, document how you solved it, distill it into a replicable process, give it to others who want to be helped. In that version the closing line reads "do it offline and it's called improvement; do it online and it's called business."

**Source:** The Micro Education Business Model (How To Monetize Your Knowledge).md

The difference between the versions is small but not trivial. Without "set a goal," the process starts from a problem already present; with it, the problem is generated by the goal, which is the stricter logic of Chapter 8 (a problem only exists relative to a goal). The two closing lines, "growth" and "improvement," mean the same thing. What the sentence asserts in either form is an identity: the activity that makes up a business is the same activity that makes up personal development, and the only variable is whether it is made public. The 2024 compilation ends the passage with a line about the twenties that ties it back to Chapter 5: you realize your potential "by becoming aware of the rut that you are digging yourself in just by the default state of humanity"; "consciousness is chaotic unless we order it"; "don't waste your 20s: build your body, build your mind, build your business."

The phrase *replicable process* (sometimes *replicable system*) is the hinge of the sequence. Documenting how you solved something produces a story; distilling it produces a process that someone else can follow. Section 28.6 will show that Koe uses exactly this difference to separate content from product: "the difference between content and product is convenience, clarity and a replicable system that gets results."

#### If you have not yet achieved a meaningful goal

The sequence presupposes that you have already solved something. For those who have not, Koe gives a longer procedure in November 2023. (1) Set a goal in one domain of your life. (2) Drown yourself in knowledge and education around it. (3) Document that knowledge as content, because "teaching is how you learn and identify what you don't know so that you can learn efficiently; this is crucial." (4) Experiment with different techniques: training programs, diets, ways to make money. Then identify the fundamentals, truths and principles that run across the techniques. (5) Achieve the goal by creating your own way of getting there. Finally, distribute it as a digital product that sells while you sleep, "with obscenely high profit margins." From there you have the resources (a readership and an income) to build whatever other businesses you want.

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

The numbering in the transcript is irregular (Koe says "fourth" and "fifth" for what are really the fifth and sixth moves), but the logic is clear. The procedure is the build-to-learn method of Chapter 14 turned into a business plan: documentation is not a later stage but part of the learning, because teaching exposes the gaps in what you know; experimentation precedes principle; and the principles extracted from many techniques are what let you create "your own way." The step that turns a learner into a seller is the same step that turns a follower of techniques into someone with a method.

#### Experimentation: you cannot put your name on someone else's planner

Koe insists that the philosophy works only through experimentation. "The entirety of my success has been built on solving my own problems and selling the solution, and you can only do that through experimentation." You cannot take someone else's planner, put your name on it and sell it; you have to try things out to make it your own. A fitness creator has to experiment with the different diets and training programs and create one: "take the best and create something new."

**Source:** Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md

His own planner is the example he returns to most. In June 2023: when you solve your own problems through self-education, skill acquisition and building a business, you have a solution to pass down, and those solutions do double duty. They are how you gain a readership on social media and build leverage, and they are how you create a product to sell. "When I fixed my productivity problems through self-education and practice, I created a planner that I could sell. It's that simple." The same solution is content and product; the readership grows from sharing it, and the product is its packaged form.

**Source:** Get Mad At Where You Are In Life (A Deep Explanation).md

In August 2023 he turns the planner into a full scenario and shows how much the self-experiment teaches beyond the product itself. Suppose he wants to sell a planner. He experiments on himself first, "because you need experience and results; most businesses fail because they're trying to sell to people that have a problem they've never experienced." He buys four or five of the best-selling planners on Amazon, buys productivity books and courses, drowns himself in podcasts, accounts and videos on productivity, then structures his days, maps his work and applies what he learned. By then he has an idea for improving the planner or doing something new. But he has also, without intending to, exposed himself to the whole business. He has listened to high-performing content he can recreate to promote the planner and build a following without ads. He has bought planners, books and courses from people who make a living from them, so he can reverse-engineer their marketing and web pages. And he has results, so he has direct experience of the problem, the desired outcome and the system, "which composes my marketing strategy." "It's all right in front of you, but you aren't paying attention."

**Source:** I Had To Learn These High-Income Skills (If I Wanted To Make Money).md

The scenario shows why Koe treats the self-experiment as the core of the method rather than a preliminary. The same weeks of research produce three things at once: the product (a better method), the content strategy (the topics that already perform), and the marketing (the problem, outcome and mechanism, known from the inside, plus the competitors' persuasion studied as a customer). The "it's all right in front of you" line connects to the self-awareness principle of 28.1: the consumer's eyes, turned into a creator's eyes, are the research department.

A June 2024 version answers a common objection: do you need experience selling planners, or even experience in productivity, to sell a planner? Koe's answer is that the people who sell Coca-Cola created the product; they did not first have experience selling it. You create, sell, test; it will suck at first; you iterate: "that's what product development is," starting "with something that sucks" and turning it "into something better based off of audience feedback." His planner procedure in this version: buy a few planners and use them to see which methods give the best results; take the best parts of each to create a new method; test and iterate on yourself until you have solved the problem in your own life; manufacture, sell and distribute to the audience you built with content; get results for customers and keep iterating until the product brings in the revenue you want. The digital versions are a printable planner, a Notion template (which he has sold) or an e-book or course that walks through the productivity system.

**Source:** Change Your Life In 365 Hours (The New Rich Focus On These Tasks Daily).md

There is an apparent tension between this insistence on originality and other advice in the corpus that tells beginners to "sell what's already selling" and not to be clever with a first product (Chapter 26). Koe's reconciliation works by layers: what can be copied is the market, the marketing and "the why behind why it works" (headlines, problems, offer structure); what must be created is the system, the method that makes the product one's own. The planner scenarios show both layers at once: he buys the best-selling planners (the market is validated) and he builds a new method from their best parts and his own testing (the system is his).

#### When you do not know what to sell

For those who have profitable interests but no product idea, Koe offers a heuristic. "If you don't know what to sell, you sell something that you would have wanted on your journey or something that you want but can't find right now." The first half points backward to the former self; the second points to the present gap in the market as experienced by you.

**Source:** Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md

He illustrates the second half with a tweet by Ryan Ayala, whose podcast he was about to appear on: "Ralph Lauren couldn't find clothes he loved, so he decided to make and sell them himself, assuming that if he wanted them other people would too." Koe calls it "a very important business lesson." A clothing brand (its name is unclear in the transcript) quoted the tweet to explain why it had made a short-inseam cotton short: "couldn't find it, so I made it." Koe's comment: "I'm the same exact way, but I've done it with digital products." His own cases, mentioned in the same video, are the planner and the Modern Mastery community, which he designed as the community he would have wanted.

The Ralph Lauren story is a third party's (Ayala's tweet about a designer), and Koe uses it as an illustration, not as evidence. Its logic is the same as the "you are the niche" argument: the assumption that what you want, others like you will want too. It also states that assumption's risk honestly in the word "assuming." The heuristic works when the seller's taste is shared by enough people, and the test of that is the market.

#### Qualified by a solved problem: the education business

The philosophy implies a particular kind of business. In February 2024, and again in July 2024 in almost identical words, Koe says: "if you've solved the problem in your life, you're qualified to start a business; not just any business, an education business, as one person, with zero startup cost, with the knowledge you already have in your head, regardless of experience level."

**Source:** Zero To $1 Million As A One-Person Business (Working 2-4 Hours A Day).md; The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md

The *education business* (Koe's term, sometimes the *micro education business model*) is the business of selling what you learned while solving a problem: a course, a guide, a system, a coaching program. Its advantages, as listed, are structural: one person can run it, it costs almost nothing to start, and its raw material is knowledge already in the founder's head. The phrase "regardless of experience level" depends on the argument of Chapter 26 that you serve the people one or two steps behind you; the qualification is relative, not absolute. Chapter 31 defends education as a product against its common objections.

The negative form of the same principle explains, for Koe, why most businesses fail. Creators "are self-sufficient in the products that they create to solve the real problems of humanity, problems that they've experienced. That's the problem with most businesses: they try to solve a problem that they haven't experienced, and that's why the businesses fail."

**Source:** How Intelligent Creators Will Make Money In 2024 (The Build Teach Earn Method).md

The claim is presented as a principle, not supported with data, and it should be read as Koe's diagnosis rather than an established finding about business failure. Its internal logic is the "automatic filter for value": a lived problem is guaranteed to be real, an imagined one is not.

In September 2023 he extends the argument into ethics. People will tell you to "start a real business," but what they often mean, he says, is "I am fabricating problems that don't benefit humanity; don't start a business around real problems that will raise the collective consciousness." People with a poor relationship with money think marketing and sales are evil; they do not understand that the exchange of goods is human nature and has always existed. "Money is neutral until you put it in the hands of good or evil," and consistent progress toward conscious goals ensures that you lean toward the good. "When you solve your own problems and sell the solution, you can guarantee that those problems exist on the cosmic scale and rest well knowing that you are making a difference."

**Source:** Life Is A Video Game (You Can't Escape The Matrix).md

The phrase "on the cosmic scale" is hyperbole, but its function is precise: a problem you lived is a problem that exists in reality, not one manufactured by a marketer. The defense of money as neutral is developed in Chapter 34 (money programming), and the ethics of persuasion in Chapter 32.

#### First, master your survival

A March 2023 process adds a precondition. Its first step is to "master your survival," and Koe explains why it comes first: "you can't sustain authenticity when you need something from someone else for survival." Being self-reliant, able to generate an independent income, with a self-confidence that does not depend on others, is what lets you act authentically. He adds two qualifications. Mastery is a process, so you do not wait until you have mastered survival before starting; you would probably never register it as done, because it never ends. And every individual has to self-actualize in order to contribute to humanity in the best way they can, where self-actualization is "to fulfill the desire to become everything one can be, to actualize your potential."

**Source:** The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md

He admits a limit to his advice. Self-actualization can be achieved through "the perfect career path," but he cannot help with that; he chose business and self-improvement because otherwise you will never have full control over your days or how you make money. His recommendation is to solve the *biological problems* in your life (health, finances, socialization) and to discover your interests through improvement. That takes time, but it does not prevent earning an independent income now, through what he calls the *minimum viable offer*: learn a skill and start selling it (Chapter 29 develops it).

The precondition reverses a common assumption about authenticity. One might think that authenticity comes first and money follows. Koe argues the opposite order: a person who depends on someone else for survival will bend their speech and work to that dependency, so independence is the condition of authenticity, not its reward. The connection to self-reliance (Chapter 35) is direct.

#### The product as a holistic solution

The fourth step of the same March 2023 process explains what the product of this philosophy looks like. It is a *holistic solution*, built in three sub-steps.

**Source:** The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md

First, identify a desirable goal: a big goal you are trying to achieve, have achieved or achieved long ago. (Are you fit? Did you acquire a skill?) Koe warns against limiting yourself to the belief that "only this thing makes money": if you know Photoshop, sell a Photoshop course; if you know how to make a website, create a product called "how to create a website" and improve it from there. It does not have to be perfect. "Until you actually get a product out, you have nothing to improve, you have nothing to iterate on," and the lessons of life and business pass you by because you have nowhere to apply them.

Second, identify a starting point: where you were, why you did not like it. Paint the lifestyle you were living against the one you live now: what was painful, what is beneficial, what is the why.

Third, outline a unique path, structured like a book. Point A is the problem, which opens a *curiosity loop* (the open question that makes people want to know more, a concept of Chapter 23) and hooks people; point B is the goal achieved and the lifestyle created; the chapters in between are what people want to know. This is where the holistic solution comes in: "you decide the synthesis."

The three sub-steps (goal, starting point, path) reproduce the goal–path–problem structure of Chapter 8 in the form of an offer, and they anticipate the story structure of Chapter 23: a product is a transformation narrated from A to B. "You decide the synthesis" connects to the synthesizer of Chapter 18: the product's uniqueness lies in the way one person connected the pieces.

#### Your marketing is your intrinsic philosophy

In May 2023, after listing his reasons for walking, Koe turns the list into a business lesson. "Start a business as I always do, by solving your own problems and selling the solution that you pick up along the way, and your marketing is the intrinsic philosophy that you form with your reasons." He could "100%" sell a walking program, "30 days of walks," as a digital product or a book, with the reasons he had just listed as the copy of the landing page; he guarantees that what he said was persuasive and that some viewers will start walking. "That's the most meaningful style of business model anyways": "you're changing people's lives by changing your own."

**Source:** Most People Won't Change (How To Recreate Yourself).md

The example makes concrete the earlier claim that the benefits of a purposeful life are "marketing firepower." The persuasion was not written for a sale; it was the set of reasons that made Koe change his own behavior. The *intrinsic philosophy* (his phrase) is the reasoning a person built for themselves, and if it convinced them, it is likely to convince people like them. The last sentence of the passage is a compact definition of the whole model.

#### Shallow problems first

A 2022 passage connects the philosophy to Chapter 9's argument that one cannot skip levels of purpose. Lack of self-esteem, Koe says, is often a health problem: perhaps you have no muscle mass or do not like how you look. That is a surface-level problem. Through the lens of finding purpose, "you have to solve those shallow external problems first and then bust through to your inner core" to reach meaning and purpose, because we have covered that core with comparisons to other people, and the comparisons create the shallow problems. You cannot ignore them; you have to solve them, because they are what most people struggle with, and "that's how you can help them, once you actually solve them for yourself." His example: low self-esteem, traced to a lack of muscle, leads you to learn the gym, find a unique take or your own system for getting in shape, and then teach it.

**Source:** If You Are High Value, Start A One-Person Business.md

Chapter 9 (Section 9.1) used the same passage to establish the developmental order; its business corollary is that shallow problems are not a lesser market. They are the largest market, precisely because almost everyone is stuck at them, and solving them for oneself is both the first step toward depth and the first product one can honestly sell.

#### Naval's seven billion companies, and package the method

In June 2023 Koe gives the philosophy a third party's frame. He quotes Naval Ravikant: there are almost seven billion people on the planet; someday he hopes there will be almost seven billion companies. Koe's *one-person business philosophy*, built on that line, holds that the best businesses change lives and produce transformations. To make a winning product, stop trying to solve imaginary problems: "solve your own problems, change your own life, package up the method, slap a price tag on it." You are less likely to fail when you solve real, meaningful problems. Most people entering business, he adds, lack an understanding of marketing, sales, psychology or evolution.

**Source:** The One Person Business Model 2.0 (Turn Yourself Into A Business).md

The Naval quotation is examined as a thesis about the future of work in Section 28.4; here Koe uses it as a premise for a product philosophy. If every person can be a company, the company each person is best placed to build is the one that sells the method they used on their own life.

#### Start as one person

The last element is an argument about scale. In *The Art of Focus* Koe writes: "How to start a billion-dollar company? Instead, start as one person. If you can't turn your interests into income, you don't have the skill to start a business that requires 10 times the resources." An individual can attract an audience and put a valuable product in front of it. Most people complicate modern business: they are told "you need to niche down and target an audience that you probably don't care to work with," and so "modern business advice is a surefire way to end up in another line of work that you hate."

**Source:** The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md

The argument is a skills argument, not an argument against ambition. A large company needs, at a minimum, the ability to attract attention and convert it into revenue; a person who cannot do that alone, with their own interests as material, will not do it with ten times the resources. The criticism of niching down repeats Chapter 19's case against choosing a market one does not care about; its new element is the outcome it predicts. A business built around strangers' problems recreates the job the founder was trying to leave.

### 28.3 Entrepreneurship Is for Everyone

#### Two dirty words

If business is the outward side of personal development, then the word "entrepreneur" should apply to almost anyone who develops. Koe knows that this is not how the word sounds to most people, and in February 2025 he begins from that resistance. "Business and entrepreneurship have become dirty words." We have "a pretty immature definition of them": when people hear them, self-doubt floods in. "That requires a lot of money to start." "I'm not talented enough." "Charging people money, that sounds like a scam." "I don't know how to create an LLC or do my taxes, so I'm never going to learn." "Sounds boring." He is not, he says, talking about entrepreneurship "as a role or a title or something that's reserved for talented people," but about "what it actually is, which is a state of mind, a state of consciousness."

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

The list of objections is useful because each corresponds to a part of the system that answers it. Money to start: the one-person business costs almost nothing (Section 28.4). Talent: everything is a skill (Chapter 15). Scam: money is neutral and persuasion is as ethical as its user (Section 28.2 and Chapter 32). LLCs and taxes: a distraction before the first customers (below in this section). Boring: the business as the vessel of a life's work and a game (Chapters 9 and 10). The redefinition as a "state of mind" is the move that makes the rest of the section possible.

#### Everyone is an entrepreneur

Koe's broadest claim is that everyone already is an entrepreneur. In March 2023, as part of what he calls "my philosophy": "everyone is an entrepreneur; some just decide to get paid for the value they have to provide," linked to the idea that entrepreneurship is modern-day survival.

**Source:** The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md

In April 2023 he expands it, referring to an earlier "entire 10-minute rant" on the same subject. "Entrepreneurship is for everyone. You are an entrepreneur right now. You have value to provide and you provide it to some people. All you need to do is put that value on the internet or in front of people with a price tag on it and boom, you're an entrepreneur. Strip your mind of the limiting beliefs of what you think entrepreneurship is and just start contributing to humanity and getting paid for doing so. That's entrepreneurship: by helping other people." The same passage reappears as the third step of a "future-proofing" sequence ("build your business") in a 2024 compilation.

**Source:** Your 20s Are Meant To Build (How To Not Waste Them).md; Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md

The argument has a simple structure. Value is already being provided (to friends, colleagues, family, an employer). What separates that from entrepreneurship is two operations: putting the value where more people can see it, and attaching a price. Neither requires a new identity in the dramatic sense; both require removing a belief about what entrepreneurship is. The *Art of Focus* version quoted in Section 28.2 ("some just choose to get paid for the problems they solve in their personal life") specifies which value: the solutions to one's own problems.

#### A state of mind, not a title

Over 2024 and 2025 Koe's definition shifts from what entrepreneurs do to what kind of mind they have. In July 2024: "Entrepreneurship is a mindset, not a title, a mindset dedicated to solving an infinite set of creative problems, a way to hedge against getting trapped in a repetitive routine that someone else assigned to you. It is an infinite game, not a finite one." Your work evolves; it does not stagnate unless you allow it, "but even then at least it's your choice." "If you stay in a 9-to-5 for too long, you become a monkey in a cubicle."

**Source:** Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md

The definition borrows the distinction between finite and infinite games (Chapter 10, Section 10.7), and the *monkey in a cubicle* is the image of the hunter psyche trapped in a repetitive environment from Chapter 2. Entrepreneurship, so defined, is a protection against two things: the assigned routine and the stagnation that follows it.

In February 2025, quoting his forthcoming short book *Purpose and Profit*, he takes the definition one step further. "Entrepreneurship is not a role or a title, it's a state of mind." You can be an employee with the mind of an entrepreneur (high agency, solving problems), and that is who employers want to hire and who will make up the top 1%. So even if you work for someone else, "the only logical option still is to be an entrepreneur."

**Source:** The creator economy is dying thanks to AI.md

This is a real change of position, and the corpus records it. In December 2023, in "The Future Of Work Is Play," Koe set the employee mindset against the traits of the entrepreneur and advised leaving employment early to avoid further conditioning: employee and entrepreneur were opposed trajectories. From late 2024 the opposition softens ("you can still be an employee that has high agency"), and by 2025 the two words have become states of mind rather than careers; in September 2025 he says "stop thinking of employee and entrepreneur as titles, think of them as states of mind." Koe does not present the change as a correction; he presents it as a redefinition of words that had become dirty. Both positions can be held together if one distinguishes the condition from the path: the entrepreneurial state of mind is a condition available to anyone, including employees; the business of one's own remains, for Koe, the route to full control over time and income.

#### Sahil Bloom: being enterprising inside a 9-to-5

The softer version owes something to a guest. In a January 2025 conversation, Sahil Bloom adds a layer to Koe's belief that everyone should be an entrepreneur. "You can try entrepreneurship while working in a stable job. Entrepreneurship is fundamentally about creating something. It's about being enterprising. It's about experimenting. It's about creating value when there was no expectation of you to do that. It's about taking on new things, being curious, identifying problems ... fundamentally, entrepreneurship is about identifying problems, creating solutions and then scaling those solutions. You can do that in your 9-to-5 job. You don't have to burn the boats." Entrepreneurship, in his view, "can be executed in a lot of different formats": quitting to start something entirely your own, or building "scalable solutions to problems that you find" within a company. He agrees with Koe that the process is "really important ... for engaging your mind."

**Source:** The Time Billionaire (A Concept That Will Change Your Life) With Sahil Bloom.md

Bloom then gives a concrete exercise for someone at a large company, "getting your annual inflation pay raise for doing your job description," who wants to be more entrepreneurial. "Every day for a week, keep a blank notepad where you identify problems that you see in the company": problems of coworkers, bosses, customers. "Start brainstorming some solutions to those and bring them to the table and actually go and execute on them." His claim: "I guarantee you will start seeing an acceleration in your trajectory within that company, because the number of people who are willing to take the initiative to be entrepreneurial in a normal day-to-day job [is] effectively zero; you will stand out. You will be top 10% ... you'll start making a whole lot more money."

The definition and the exercise are Bloom's (*enterprising* is his word), and the guarantee is his opinion, offered from experience rather than data. Their place in Koe's system is clear. Bloom's definition (identify problems, create solutions, scale them) is Koe's goal–path–problem loop described from the side of an organization, and the notepad exercise is a small *measurable personal project* run inside a job. What Bloom adds is the explicit permission not to quit, which Koe's 2023 rhetoric had withheld.

#### Justin Welsh and the word "entrepreneur"

The stigma of the word affects even successful solo founders. In a December 2021 conversation, Justin Welsh tells Koe that for "the last 2 years, 2 and 1/2 years" he was "really, really afraid or uncomfortable to call myself an entrepreneur," because to him entrepreneurs were people who start SaaS companies and raise "a hundred million dollars in capital." At dinner with the former CEO of his first startup (a unicorn), he described what he did, and the CEO answered: "You're an entrepreneur. That's a business. That's a business that scales. You can scale that business year over year." Two or three weeks before the recording he had started calling himself an entrepreneur instead of a "personal brand" or a "one-man business." Koe relates: there is a stigma that an entrepreneur builds a huge company and is "out of touch," and another that the word is "kind of cheesy."

**Source:** Justin Welsh Shows You How To Start & Grow A One-Person Business.md

The story is Welsh's, and it is useful as evidence of how strong the startup image of entrepreneurship is: a person running a profitable, growing business still did not feel entitled to the word until someone from the startup world granted it. Koe's later redefinitions can be read as an attempt to remove the need for that permission.

#### Creator as a way of life

The same broadening applies to the word "creator." In July 2024 Koe says: "I don't think the creator economy can be reduced to just people who create content for the sake of a job; I see it as a way of life, being a creator." Reducing "creator" to "some myopic job title" is close-minded. He then describes what creators do in five steps. (1) "They're identifying problems in their life, because they have agency and they don't need permission from a boss to go and solve a problem." (2) "They research how to solve it through the internet." (3) "They synthesize that information." (4) "They create their own system for getting results." (5) "They pass that down to someone behind them so that person can get results faster, and humanity can evolve just by that process alone."

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md

This is about "solving human problems, meaningful problems," and Koe gives it an argument for durability: "AI can't go into your head yet and fix your mental health issues or your growth needs, your self-actualization needs." And it is the reason for his niche thesis: "This is why I say you are the niche, because you are simply passing down what worked for you to someone else who has a very similar personality as you by attracting them to your vision and goals."

The five steps are the "solve your own problems" philosophy described as a character rather than a procedure. The first step defines the creator by *agency* (the capacity to act on a problem without permission, which Chapter 35 develops); the middle steps are the research, synthesis and system-building of Part VI; the last is transmission. The claim about AI is dated ("yet") and limited to the kind of problem it names; Chapter 36 examines what the author thinks AI does and does not replace.

#### Entrepreneurship as modern hunting

Later in the same video Koe gives entrepreneurship its evolutionary frame. "Entrepreneurship is how you hunt for your survival in the modern world. Why? Because true entrepreneurship requires you to become future-proof." The process, "simple on the surface but complex in depth, as all meaningful things are," has two moves. "You create value through creativity, experimentation and experience: you improve yourself." "You distribute value through attention, media and persuasion, so you improve humanity." "When you are in control of the vision, AI and technology become the tool, not the master." And "the beautiful thing about that simple process is that it never ends."

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md

The two moves organize the rest of the book. *Create value* names what Parts IV through VI teach (direction, focus, learning, thinking, creativity) and what Chapter 31 defines. *Distribute value* names what Parts VIII and IX teach (writing, audience, brand) and what Chapter 32 calls persuasion. The hunting image comes from Chapter 2 (the hunter psyche in a cubicle) and is completed here: in an economy where survival is obtained through markets, building something of value and bringing it to people is the contemporary form of the hunt. "Future-proof" anticipates Chapter 36: a person in control of the vision directs the tools rather than being replaced by them.

#### Create your own career

A November 2022 lesson, which Koe introduces as difficult and requiring experience, explains why his own work does not fit any existing job description. Asked how to start an online business with $0, most people would say freelancing or an agency. But Koe does not know how to describe what he does: in an elevator he would say "I'm a writer, I just blog"; he writes tweets on self-improvement, makes videos on business and spirituality, posts on Instagram; he dislikes "influencer," and "creator" does not get it across. His business model cannot be turned into a career for everyone or a college curriculum one could be trained in. You create your own career, he says, "by following what works, by doing what works, but in your own way, without getting in your own way," iterating and blowing past societal expectations until you have a holistic view that outsiders do not understand. (His example of the outsider's confusion: "so you're a YouTuber, but you make a hundred thousand dollars a month?") Tangibly: if there is a problem in your life, such as not liking eight-hour days, create solutions for every problem until you live your ideal lifestyle.

**Source:** 11 Lessons From Growing A 7-Figure One Person Business.md

The lesson is the opposite of the assigned career of Chapter 2. A career that can be described in a curriculum is a career someone else designed; the career Koe recommends is assembled from solutions to one's own problems, and it is illegible from the outside precisely because no one else had that sequence of problems.

In December 2023 he draws the same idea as a diagram of "the main career path of the future." (1) You. (2) Pursue your curiosities. (3) Go the content route, the code route, or both. (4) Become an employee or an entrepreneur straight away, or an employee to gain skills that let you become an entrepreneur, using the employee income to fuel the entrepreneurial work. (5) Once you have a product (which, as an employee, you are also selling) (6) find distribution: "you are the distribution with code and content," which "equals really as much money as you want." Underneath runs a chain: curiosity, learning, obsession, "a life you never thought possible." The slogan of the video: "we have the chance to create a career, not be assigned one." The transcript does not allow the layout of the graphic to be reconstructed exactly; the sequence above is the order in which Koe narrates it.

**Source:** The Future Of Work Is Play (How To Create A Digital Career).md

The diagram is notable for including employment as a legitimate stage, a year before the "state of mind" redefinition. The employee route appears as a way to fund and train the entrepreneurial one, which partly qualifies the sharper opposition between the two in the same video.

For the generalist of Chapter 20, the first step has a specific form. In May 2024 Koe advises: "do it all," writer, designer, marketer, filmmaker, bodybuilder, runner, whatever curiosity desires, since the internet lets an individual learn almost anything fast and get results from diverse interests. Step one is to choose a career path that does not box you into one skill or interest: become an entrepreneur. "But that alone doesn't cut it": you can still get trapped in a single niche where you can no longer find meaning in your work.

**Source:** The Age Of The Generalist (How To Earn A Living Doing What You Enjoy).md

The qualification is important. Entrepreneurship is necessary for the generalist but not sufficient, because a business can become its own box. The rest of Koe's answer is the "you are the niche" model: a business built around a person can change as the person's interests change, while a business built around a topic cannot.

#### Build a solution instead of accepting a constraint

A principle that runs through Koe's 2022 one-person business video is that constraints are problems to be solved, not facts to be accepted. Rather than accept "you can only work eight hours," build the system or solution that allows the outcome you want. He reapplies it at once to a common objection: "if you want to start a business without having to show your body, then build a [expletive] business without having to show your body." His examples are working fewer hours through systems and faceless writing accounts. "Stop convincing yourself you can't do something because you're not doing it like everyone else."

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself).md

The principle is the business version of Chapter 8's use of constraints: a constraint narrows the search for a path and therefore makes the problem solvable. Its specific target is the habit of disqualifying oneself by comparison ("everyone shows their face, I can't, so I can't do this"). The objection is converted into a design requirement.

#### Against startup ideas

The broad definition of entrepreneurship comes with a warning about the narrow image it replaces. In February 2024 Koe says that when people think of starting a business they think of startup culture: a crazy idea, such as a food app that suggests dinner ideas, without realizing that there are only so many restaurants nearby, all of them findable on Yelp. Such ideas often come "after a night of drinking with your friends" ("this is a million-dollar business") and go nowhere, because the idea is stupid and the person lacks perspective on what a business is and how to make an income from their own interests, skills, expertise and the problems they have already solved. People glorify "insane, beautiful, pretty ideas" online and get conditioned, often by internet content, into thinking they need one. Special cases exist, where someone executes a great idea and "the universe conspires in their favor," but they are very rare and lucky; "it's not wise to gamble on your success; it's wise to invest smartly in your future with skill acquisition."

**Source:** Zero To $1 Million As A One-Person Business (Working 2-4 Hours A Day).md

In July 2024 he gives the epistemic reason. Most people who try to build a startup or have "some crazy business idea after a night of drinking with their friends" think their idea will change the world, "but they're usually delusional, because they don't have any prior business experience." If you had the idea with zero experience, your mind is limited in the ideas it can have ("you haven't built anything that leads to better ideas"), so "I can almost guarantee that someone has already tried and failed with it." By contrast, "there's a reason that people, and specifically creators, are making millions solving fitness, productivity, career, money, relationships and lifestyle problems: because every person has them."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md

The second passage applies the mechanism of Section 28.1 in reverse. If creativity depends on the expansion of the mind, and the mind expands by building, then a person who has built nothing has a small space of possible ideas, and the idea that seems revolutionary inside that space is probably one that many others have already had. Good ideas are a product of experience, not a substitute for it. The contrast with the universal problems is the same filter as before: lived problems are real, imagined revolutions usually are not. The guarantee is rhetorical and should be read as a strong opinion.

A 2023 metaphor gives the warning a moral scope. Stop trying to "land on Saturn" and build a billion-dollar company when that is not a real, tangible problem that makes lives better. "Why are we trying to land on Saturn when we can't even live on Earth?" Most of the population still suffers from *low-consciousness problems* (Koe's term for the basic problems of health, money and relationships), and the individuals who form the collective are holding back "the collective ego" from enlightenment. His recommendation is to start with a micro education business model: solve real problems in your own life and distribute the solutions. Start by understanding the Eternal markets; solving your problems there pushes you toward self-actualization, creates a *unique map* (your story, which you can pass down) and earns income by helping others heal and achieve their goals. Then, once everyone is at a higher state of consciousness, people can do what they love and solve the deeper problems they are passionate about, "like landing on Saturn at that point."

**Source:** The One Person Business Model 2.0 (Turn Yourself Into A Business).md

The metaphor is not against ambition; its last sentence keeps Saturn as a later destination. It is an argument about order, at two scales. For the individual, solve the basic problems first (Chapter 9's levels); for the collective, raise the base before the peaks. The vocabulary of "collective ego" and "enlightenment" belongs to the developmental frame of Part XIV, and the claim that it is the individuals who hold the collective back is Koe's interpretation, not an empirical finding.

#### Building without customers, and the wrong priorities

Two warnings concern what beginners do instead of selling. In March 2026: "Too many people try to build businesses nowadays and they don't actually try to get customers. They're like, 'I'm going to build this software, I'm going to build this thing,' motivated, but then when it comes time to actually do the business stuff, they're gone."

**Source:** How To Build A $1M One-Person Business Faster With AI.md

In June 2024 he describes the same failure as a lack of prioritization. Step one is to write daily content on social media; the biggest problem is focusing on the wrong things. (He refers to his video on 365 hours, which explains how to use one hour a day on the highest-leverage task, since most people have one hour, not eight to twelve.) People build a website when they should find customers, build a product when they should attract an audience, file for an LLC and design a logo when they should do almost anything else. "This lack of prioritization screams that you have no idea what you're doing." He concedes that LLCs and taxes are confusing, but an LLC does not help you make money; building a product or service and getting people to buy it through content and an audience does. His threshold: "once you make 50,000, think about starting an LLC."

**Source:** Ideas Are The New Oil (How More People Get Rich In The Digital Age).md

The two warnings belong together because they describe the same avoidance. Building software, designing a logo or filing paperwork feels like progress and protects the founder from the uncomfortable part of business, which is exposure to people who might say no. The priority ladder of Chapter 12 (the most challenging task first) applies here: the highest-leverage task is the one that brings customers, and it is usually the one being avoided. The $50,000 threshold is a rule of thumb from Koe's experience, not legal or tax advice, and the rules for business entities vary by country.
