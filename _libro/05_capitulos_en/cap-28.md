## Chapter 28 — Business as a Life's Work

Part IX ended with a person who has a brand: a public character, a vision, a body of content and the trust that accumulates around it. Part X asks what that person does for a living. The answer Dan Koe gives is not a separate topic attached to the rest of his system; it is the system itself, seen from the side of the market. Chapter 9 already introduced the claim in its largest form: the life's work is what you choose to do with your life, and in the present era its most natural *vessel* (Koe's word for the container that carries a purpose) is a business built on the internet. This chapter examines that business directly. It asks why Koe treats business as a discipline of personal development rather than its opposite, what philosophy joins the two, what he means by "entrepreneur," why he thinks the one-person business is the default form of work for the coming decades, what the minimum definition of a business is, and what parts every one-person business is made of.

The chapter depends on three earlier blocks. From Part IV it needs the goal, the problem and the project: Koe's business philosophy is the goal–path–problem structure of Chapter 8 pointed outward, and his "product" is the project of Chapter 9 offered to others. From Part VII it needs "you are the niche" (Chapter 19): if the customer is your former self, the business is you, made public. From Part IX it needs the audience and the brand (Chapters 25–27): the business, in its minimal definition, is an audience plus a product, and the previous three chapters were about the first half.

The chapter proceeds in six steps. Section 28.1 sets out the thesis Koe compresses as "personal growth equals business growth" and the reasons he gives for treating a business as a *forcing function* for every area of life. Section 28.2 presents the philosophy he calls "solve your own problems and sell the solution," with the processes he built around it and its qualifications. Section 28.3 widens the definition of entrepreneurship until it covers everyone, and records how Koe's position moved from a career choice to a state of mind; it also gathers his warnings against startup ideas. Section 28.4 explains the one-person business as a model, its successive definitions, the career paths he drew for it and the "seven billion companies" thesis about the future of work. Section 28.5 reduces business to its minimum (traffic and an offer, people and a product) and collects the versions of the core sequence Koe repeats from 2022 to 2026. Section 28.6 closes with the pillars (brand, content, product, and sometimes promotion or marketing), the many formulations they received, the observation that the solo founder is every department at once, and the levers that decide how much the business earns.

### 28.1 Personal Growth Equals Business Growth

#### The thesis and its origin

The phrase Koe repeats most often about the relation between self and business is a symmetry: "personal growth equals business growth." He explains it in November 2023 by way of creativity. "Creativity is dependent on the expansion of your mind, and this is why I always say that personal growth equals business growth." Personal growth, in this formulation, means expanding the mind so that the self can expand; business growth depends on creativity; creativity depends on the expanded mind. The equation is therefore a causal chain, not a slogan: a business can only grow as far as the mind running it can see.

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

He illustrates the chain with his own beginning. Through education and by exposing himself to the unknown, he "registered new opportunities to solve my problem." The problem was money and a life he did not want; the obstacle was that "I wasn't going to be able to solve the problem that I had from the same mind that created it." He had to explore business opportunities he would not previously have understood, or even *registered* as opportunities. Because he had not seen it as possible to start a business that required money or a skill set he did not have, he started with the one route his mind could see at that moment: freelancing.

The verb "registered" is technical in Koe's vocabulary. It comes from the model of perception built in Chapters 3 and 6: the mind filters information through its goals and identity, so an opportunity that does not fit the current mental model is not rejected but simply not seen. The business consequence is that a person cannot choose a business model they cannot perceive. Expanding the mind is therefore the first business task, because it enlarges the set of options that can be registered at all. Section 28.4 returns to the same mechanism when Koe explains why he presents the one-person business as a starting point: most people do not consider it because their environment has not made it visible.

The sentence about solving a problem "from the same mind that created it" has a history worth noting. In 2023 Koe used it, as here, in the first person and without attribution; in other passages of the same year he paraphrased it as "no problem can be solved from the same level of consciousness that created it," announced that he would reformulate it as *level of mind* (the concept Chapter 4 defined), and in October 2023 attributed it to Einstein without reservation. From late 2024 he writes "Einstein supposedly said." The idea is the same throughout; what changes is the attribution, from none to firm to doubtful, and the vocabulary, from "consciousness" to "mind" to "development."

**Complementary context:** The line is popularly attributed to Albert Einstein, but no reliable source for it has been found; the author's late hedge ("supposedly") matches its status.

#### A new product is a new identity

A month later Koe gives the thesis its most compact identity form. "Creating a new product isn't just creating a new product, it's creating a new identity with the skill set and mindset you must acquire to build the product, sell the product and improve the product. Business growth is a vessel for personal growth."

**Source:** How Intelligent Creators Will Make Money In 2024 (The Build Teach Earn Method).md

The sentence connects business to the theory of identity change in Chapter 4. There, every durable change in behavior was a change in identity, and identities were networks of goals and the skills, beliefs and habits that serve them. A product is a goal of that kind: to build it, sell it and improve it, a person must acquire skills and a mindset they did not have, and those additions do not stay in the business. They reorganize the person. Read this way, the equation runs in both directions. Personal growth makes business growth possible (the first formulation), and business growth forces personal growth (the second). The business is a vessel in the precise sense of Chapter 9: it carries the development of the person who builds it.

#### Business instead of meditation

Koe is explicit that this is his own method and not the only one. In October 2022 he says that "business and writing and content creation is my method, is my tool, as opposed to like meditation, for self-actualizing and self-transcending." Writing to yourself, he adds, is "a great way of just personal growth in general."

**Source:** The Rise Of The Value Creator (A Career Path For Polymaths & Self-Improvers).md

Two terms in the sentence come from developmental psychology. *Self-actualization* is the realization of one's potential (in another passage Koe defines it as "to fulfill the desire to become everything one can be"), and *self-transcendence* is the movement beyond the concerns of the individual self toward something larger. The claim is not that business replaces meditation for everyone, but that it can play the same role: a practice through which a person both develops and is drawn beyond themselves. Chapter 9 (Section 9.4) collected the stronger version of the idea, business as "arguably the most spiritual endeavor you can take," and Chapter 40 discusses meditation itself. Here the point is narrower: in Koe's own life, the business is the practice.

**Complementary context:** Abraham Maslow placed self-actualization at the top of his original hierarchy of needs and, in his late work, added self-transcendence above it. Koe uses both words in roughly that sense without discussing Maslow's model in these passages.

#### The business as a forcing function

In March 2023, in a long conversation with Dickie Bush, Koe adopts a term from his guest. "I just registered what that actually means," he says of Bush's phrase *forcing function*, and applies it: "the one-person business model is a forcing function for improving every area of your life." That, he adds, is why he pushes the model so hard at that moment.

**Source:** Dan Koe & Dickie Bush On One-Person Businesses, Creative Workflows, and Lifestyle Design.md

A forcing function, in Bush's use, is a project whose unavoidable by-product is a set of new skills: you commit to something that cannot be done without them, and acquiring them stops being optional. The term belongs to Bush; the application to the one-person business, and the claim that it reaches every area of life, is Koe's. The phrase recurs later in the corpus in other applications (teaching in public as a forcing function for understanding; investing money you cannot yet afford as pressure to grow), which suggests that Koe found in it a name for something he already believed: that the right commitment does the work that willpower cannot.

#### Six things a business demands

The fullest explanation of why business forces development comes from an August 2023 video. Koe first reduces the activity to its core: business, entrepreneurship or being a creator "is just providing value and receiving the value you want as money." He then lists the reasons that activity builds confidence, each framed as a demand the business makes on the person.

**Source:** You Aren't Successful Because You Care Too Much (How To Stop).md

*The first demand is to evolve or die.* "Most people die at 25": they get the job, the spouse, the house and the car, exactly what society wants, someone "docile and of little threat to their system," and from then on they are trying to survive. Koe immediately makes clear that the death is not literal. "Death is symbolic and all understanding is metaphorical": if life is growth and growth requires the new, then death is the death of new ideas, new potentials, new projects and new practices. Life is "ever flowing and evolving," and obstructing that flow by settling for a subpar life is the death he means. Entrepreneurship, he argues, ensures that you do not die in this sense in a changing world, with one condition attached: "if you persist until success." The argument draws on Chapter 5's claim that there is no staying the same under entropy, and on the critique of the default path in Chapter 2. A job does not stop the world from changing; it only stops the person from changing with it.

**Complementary context:** The image echoes a saying usually attributed to Benjamin Franklin ("many people die at twenty-five and aren't buried until they are seventy-five"), which Sahil Bloom quotes in another part of the corpus. In this passage Koe states it without attribution.

*The second demand is expertise.* Subpar understanding yields subpar results, and understanding differs from knowledge (the distinction Chapter 17 develops as horizontal knowing versus vertical understanding). Here Koe gives it a spiritual geometry: "understanding is the upward motion of expanding consciousness; meditation is the downward motion of expanding consciousness." Most mystics and spiritual teachers, he says, forget the balance. "It's not creativity or productivity, monk or warrior, peace or progress, being or doing; it's both." "The duality collapses into one." The practical consequence is a warning: stack theory without practice and you fail; you need daily education *and* execution. The passage is a metaphor, not a model of consciousness offered as fact, and it anticipates the balance between peace and progress that Chapter 39 treats at length. Its function here is to defend business against the charge of being unspiritual: the effort to understand a market, a skill or a customer is, in this image, the same expansion of consciousness that meditation produces, approached from the other direction.

*The third demand is results*, for yourself and for customers. "If you don't get results you aren't valuable." Koe reads the absence of results as information, not as a verdict: it is "a sign to improve, not quit." True value requires development over time, and entrepreneurship ensures that you develop into what he calls "a valuable asset."

*The fourth demand is that you suck first*, so you can improve. It is foolish, he says, to think you won't be bad at almost everything at first, and the expectation of competence from the start is what keeps most people from acting on their dreams. Once you are past the first hump, you "snowball into a season of pure progress." The "first hump" is the same one Chapter 8 described when it explained how passion is created by investing energy until a goal pays you back; Chapter 29 turns the principle into the "permission to suck" that justifies launching an imperfect first product.

*The fifth demand is new habits of work, rest and play.* "You cannot reach level 2 with the character that got you to level one." The video-game image (Chapter 10) makes the point that the person who succeeded at one level has habits adapted to that level, and that the next level requires a different character. Growth requires change, and change is uncomfortable, "which is why most settle early."

*The sixth demand is a skill set based on psychology.* Business is communicating your value to others and receiving something in return, so it forces you to understand your own mind and therefore the minds of others. "Marketing, sales, writing and speaking are the backbone of business success." (In the same passage he announces that he will redo his "million-dollar skill stack" video, the subject of Chapter 35.)

| Demand | What the business requires | What it develops in the person |
|---|---|---|
| Evolve or die | Continuous adaptation in a changing world | Escape from the symbolic "death at 25" of the settled life |
| Expertise | Understanding, not mere knowledge | Theory joined to daily execution ("it's both") |
| Results | Outcomes for yourself and customers | Development into a "valuable asset"; failure read as feedback |
| Suck first | Tolerating early incompetence | A "season of pure progress" after the first hump |
| New habits | A different character for each level | New patterns of work, rest and play |
| Psychology skill set | Communicating value to other minds | Self-understanding and understanding of others |

Later in the same video Koe adds the mechanism behind the confidence. Building a product builds confidence because you will suck at first, as with anything, and you get *direct feedback* on what can be improved: "people can be brutal, but most are nice." Business seems foreign to many people, but that is no reason to see it as frightening. The feedback also cuts the other way. "If you want to actually make money you need to actually provide value," and "if you aren't continuing to grow your audience or money, maybe start thinking about how valuable you really are." The sentence is harsh, and it is meant to be. It turns income and audience growth into a measuring instrument for the value one is creating. It also contains the limit of the instrument: it measures *perceived* value in a market, which Chapter 31 will show is not the same as value in itself, and it measures it only for someone who is actually putting work in front of people.

#### What choosing business does to a life

In a May 2023 video on living on autopilot, Koe gives the same argument from the side of values. Someone who values building a business values providing value to others. To do that they must increase their skill and develop themselves, and they must have "a mind that is attractive and able to actually spark positive behavior change through business." Choices aligned with that value then produce three effects: more energy (higher output), less financial stress (more money), and a deeper satisfaction that comes from the intellectual development that writing or content creation brings.

**Source:** Most People Are NPCs (Don't Waste Your Life On Autopilot).md

The example belongs to an argument about how values organize choices (the video's subject is the NPC who lives without chosen values, a figure introduced in Chapter 1). Its relevance here is that it states the forcing-function thesis as a chain of consequences rather than a list of demands: value business, and you are compelled to value your own development, because only a developed mind can produce the behavior change a business sells.

#### Self-improvement as the seed of business

The last element of the thesis connects the domains of self-improvement to the domains of business. In April 2023 Koe puts it as a symmetry. "Self-improvement is about solving your own problems in those domains; business is about solving other people's problems within those domains. So you bring the two together." The domains are mind, body and business (elsewhere, health, wealth, relationships and happiness, the Eternal markets of Chapter 19). The prescription follows: set a purposeful goal to improve yourself, self-educate, acquire skills for mind, body and business, let curiosity guide which methods you test, and you will have the experience necessary to help others and "profit off of living with purpose."

**Source:** Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md

He then answers an objection in advance. The word "project" does not sound as if it applies to every domain of life, "but hear me out." In health, "you only make tangible progress when you turn it into a measurable personal project." You go to the gym, write down your routine, document every lift. If you do not see progress, you research and find the gaps in your knowledge; perhaps nutrition is holding you back, so you start tracking calories. Now your physique progresses but your energy does not, so perhaps you "fall down an esoteric rabbit hole of how beef liver is the most nutrient-dense food on the planet," experiment with it and see whether you get results. At the end you have "an interesting, unique way of getting results in health that other people would benefit from that are like you."

The example is a miniature of the whole business philosophy. A domain becomes a *measurable personal project* (Koe's term): it gets a goal, a record and a feedback loop. The feedback reveals a gap; the gap leads to research; the research leads to an experiment, sometimes an unusual one; the experiment yields a personal method. The method is unique not because it was designed to be different but because it is the residue of one person's sequence of problems. And the people who would benefit from it are, specifically, "people like you," which is the argument of Chapter 19 in one phrase. The beef liver is deliberately idiosyncratic; it shows that the "unique solution" Koe values is often a strange detail that only someone who followed the problem all the way down would find.

#### Two cautions: self-awareness and the starving artist

Two short principles complete the bridge between self-improvement and business, one about where to look and one about what else to learn.

The first is that "self-awareness is the greatest business hack." In September 2024 Koe tells beginners to pay attention to their own behavior on social media and as customers. When you buy something, why did you buy it? "Okay, you just extracted a lesson; now implement it into your business." When you read a post and follow its author, did the bio change your decision? "Maybe it locked it in, but most of the time no." The principle was presented in Chapter 19 as a way of finding one's niche; here it shows the same idea at work on marketing. If the customer is your former self, your own reactions as a consumer are the closest thing you have to customer research.

**Source:** The Fastest Way To Build A One-Person Business (Beginner Guide).md

The second is that passion is not enough. A May 2024 video opens with its thesis: "do what you love, but study psychology, human behavior and the internet so you don't become a starving artist." The *starving artist* is a common phrase that Koe uses with a specific meaning across the corpus: the creator who does what they love but refuses to learn how value is perceived and distributed, and therefore cannot sustain the work. Psychology and human behavior stand for persuasion; the internet stands for distribution. The rest of that video argues that school and jobs do not teach monetization and lays out the eight steps of what Koe calls the "$1 million Creator"; Section 28.6 uses its last step, the levers.

**Source:** How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md

#### Never get trapped in a stage

Koe closed his December 2022 "One Person Business Roadmap" with what he called the main lesson of the video, and reused the passage almost word for word in a long 2024 compilation: "never stop learning, never stop building, never stop evolving. Don't get trapped in one of the stages, evolve. Distraction and comfort are the only things that can get in your way. Self-actualize, self-monetize and self-transcend."

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The "stages" are the stages of the roadmap itself (service, product, and later forms of the business, which Chapter 30 reconstructs), but the warning applies to the whole thesis of this section. If business growth follows personal growth, then a business that stops at a stage is a person who stopped developing, and the two enemies Koe names are not external. Distraction is the failure of attention described in Part III and Chapter 11; comfort is the settling that the "evolve or die" argument diagnosed. The closing triad compresses the section into three verbs. *Self-actualize* is the personal development; *self-monetize* is the business that carries it (Koe's term *self-monetization* is defined in Section 28.6); *self-transcend* is the movement beyond oneself toward contribution, which Chapter 9 called the highest level of purpose. The order matters: in Koe's sequence, monetization sits between actualization and transcendence, as the bridge that lets a developing person sustain the work through which they reach beyond themselves.

### 28.2 Solve Your Own Problems and Sell the Solution

#### Where the philosophy was born

Section 28.1 described the business as the vehicle of a person's development. This section describes the principle that makes the vehicle earn money. Koe gives it a fixed name, *solve your own problems and sell the solution*, and in February 2024 he tells the story of its birth. People make millions solving fitness, productivity, career, money, relationship and lifestyle problems, because every person has them, and those problems prevent the average individual from doing the only thing they want, which is to enjoy life. "What better and more profitable problem is there to solve than the one you experience every day?" That question, he says, "is where my philosophy of solve your own problems and sell the solution was born." The applications are direct: results in fitness, sell a fitness program; results in focus, sell a productivity course; results with a skill, sell a tutorial. He adds a provocation: if you have not solved any problem in your life (which he doubts), you are not qualified; "get out of your mom's basement and start doing something with your life."

**Source:** Zero To $1 Million As A One-Person Business (Working 2-4 Hours A Day).md

The argument has the structure of the Eternal markets from Chapter 19. The most profitable problems are the most universal ones, and the most universal ones are the ones you already live with. The insult at the end is not gratuitous within the logic of the system: the qualification for this kind of business is not a credential but a solved problem, and the remedy for having none is to start solving one.

#### The two kinds of problem-solving collapse into one

The core formulation appears in nearly identical words in at least five videos of 2023 and 2024, and it is worth seeing how each adds a piece. The common sentence is "personal development is about solving your own problems; business is about solving other people's problems."

In April 2023 Koe draws the product from it. "You create a product that would have helped your former self, or would have helped you get to where you are faster." His example is the planner: "if I need mental clarity, I'm going to create a planner or a journal and then I'm just going to sell that to others." The term *former self* (the person you were before you solved the problem) is the name Chapter 19 gave to the customer of this model.

**Source:** Your 20s Are Meant To Build (How To Not Waste Them).md

Three days later he adds the medium. "The internet collapses both into one and allows you to earn from solving your own problems and living with purpose." The prescription is to develop yourself, document the solutions through an education business, and help develop others; and its horizon is large: "by treating your life as your life's work you go on to change the course of humanity for the better."

**Source:** The Micro Education Business Model (How To Monetize Your Knowledge).md

In a video of the same month he states the method and its sequence. Collapse the two into one: solve your own problems, document the process online, and ask for money in return by creating a product, a system or an implementation; "it all works itself out. You are the niche." The pattern of problems is the Eternal markets (health, wealth, relationships, "happiness optional"), and he suggests an order: fix your health first (get fit), then your money problems, then your relationships, then your mindset and mental health. After that comes reflection ("what did I do?"), the conversion of the experience into step-by-step advice organized by an overarching theory or philosophy, and packaging in the right *modality*: a course, an email course, an e-book, a physical product. He closes the video with a three-part formula and a claim about the markets themselves: "solve your own problems, document the solution, get paid," because the Eternal markets contain "burning evergreen problems" that people will face "until the end of time." "The future is bright for the people that take responsibility for their lives."

**Source:** Self Improvers Are Creating Their Own Careers (The New Economy).md

In *The Art of Focus* (2024), the book whose chapter 13 is titled "The Vessel," the formulation acquires its most quotable opening: "Everyone is an entrepreneur, some just choose to get paid for the problems they solve in their personal life. All profitable products start as a project, whether the intention to sell is there or not." To generate a creative, independent income "to free yourself from society's chains," you need to sell a valuable product, and a personal project that solves a burning problem all beings encounter is the best starting point. Koe tells readers to stop overcomplicating making money and "trying to solve creative problems that don't exist." Acquiring skills to solve your own problems is "an automatic filter for value": a problem you actually had is, by definition, a real one. Then you turn the solution into a product.

**Source:** The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md

The cumulative structure is clear when the versions are laid side by side. The symmetry (my problems, their problems) defines business as the outward continuation of self-improvement. The *former self* gives the customer. The internet gives the medium that makes the collapse possible at scale. The sequence of domains (health, money, relationships, mindset) gives an order of operations. The documentation and the "overarching theory" give the step that turns an experience into something teachable. And the "automatic filter for value" explains why the method is safer than inventing a product: the problem is guaranteed to exist because you lived it. The order of domains reflects the argument of Chapter 9 (Section 9.1) that shallow problems must be solved before deeper ones; Koe does not present it as a rule, only as the sequence he suggests.

#### Same goals, different paths: your path is your product

The philosophy depends on a premise about human beings that Koe states in September 2023. "The best route ... is to solve your own problems in life and sell the solution, because everyone has the same goals and problems; they're just viewing them from different lenses, from different identities." Everyone wants financial freedom, health, vitality, love: the benefits of improving health, wealth and relationships. But one person tries budgeting, another a side hustle, another an e-commerce store, another a social media brand; "what you do to achieve the goals will be completely different from someone else, so that path is your product." "Your job is to attract people like you by talking about your journey, then package up your path to achieving the goal and sell it."

**Source:** How To Reprogram Your Mind & Build Your Intelligence.md

The premise has two halves, and both matter. The shared goals make the market large (this is the Eternal-markets argument). The different paths make each seller unique, and they also make the match between seller and buyer selective: someone who chose budgeting will not be persuaded by a path built on e-commerce, but someone with "the same identity and similar perception" will find that what you sell "resonates." *Your path is your product* (his phrase) is therefore both a theory of differentiation and a theory of fit, and it explains how the model avoids the saturation Chapter 27 argued does not exist.

Earlier in the same video Koe states the mechanism in its simplest form and attaches a caution to it. He presents "the one-person business, or turning yourself into the business": if he attracts like-minded people and creates a solution to a problem in his own life, such as a planner, he can sell it, because it helped him; if like-minded people follow him because of the information he shares online "through my character and virtual reality, which is a personal brand on social media," that is how he earns an income. "Pretty simple." But: "I don't want it to be an ideology, but that's what it is; just don't adopt it as law."

**Source:** How To Reprogram Your Mind & Build Your Intelligence.md

The caution is consistent with the rest of the video, which warns against identifying with static ideologies (Chapter 1, Section 1.7, and Chapter 17 on creating one's own philosophy). Koe is aware that his model has become a doctrine for some of his audience, and he asks them to hold it as a principle to test rather than a creed to defend.

#### The entire philosophy: life as a video game across the Eternal markets

In July 2024 Koe places the principle inside the game metaphor of Chapter 10 and calls the result "the entire philosophy I'm putting across." Problems are preferably found in the Eternal markets: health, wealth, relationships and happiness. (The transcript reads "internal markets," almost certainly a mistranscription of "eternal.") "You're treating your life like a video game; you're increasing those domains of your life ... by setting goals for each of them under an overarching vision, to identify problems and then solve those problems so that you are valuable." Different people solve the same domain through different interests: "one person can solve their wealth problems with financing, the other can solve it with business." So "everyone creates these unique solutions that they can then pass down and make more efficient so people can self-actualize faster; that's the entire philosophy."

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md

The passage joins three structures from earlier chapters. The *overarching vision* (Chapter 7) sits above goals for each domain (Chapter 8's goal hierarchy); the goals generate problems (Chapter 8's goal–path–problem: without a goal there is no problem); solving the problems makes you valuable; and the solutions, being personal, are unique and can be passed down. The final clause gives the philosophy its social purpose. The point of passing a solution down is that the next person "can self-actualize faster." In this view a product is accumulated trial and error that one person has done so another does not have to repeat it, the same idea Chapter 9 expressed through the image of building a house well enough to pass it on.

#### Profit off your purpose: Koe's guiding light

The April 2023 video that gave the philosophy its title also gives it a procedure, which Koe calls the process for profiting "off of your purpose." It has six steps: (1) identify a problem in your life; (2) set a goal to reveal your present purpose; (3) experiment with different solutions; (4) find what works and create your own; (5) talk about your journey in public; (6) offer your solution with a price tag on it. "Repeating this process has been my guiding light in life."

**Source:** Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md

Each step takes a concept from an earlier chapter. Step one uses the redefinition of purpose as the most urgent problem (Chapter 8). Step two introduces *present purpose* (Koe's term): the purpose that a goal reveals for now, set against a concrete problem, as opposed to a lifelong mission one is supposed to discover. Steps three and four are the self-experimentation of Chapter 15. Step five is teaching and building in public (Chapter 14). Step six is the business. The procedure is the six steps of personal development with a seventh activity, publication, inserted before the last, and a price tag added at the end.

The same video explains where the "profitable interests" that feed the process come from, and Koe's answer is that "we start with life direction." You need a vision, goals and priority tasks you can execute every day (he points to an earlier video, "Society is a pyramid scheme," and to a free "power planner"). "This is how you gain experience in life by moving towards things": direction gives you the experience, knowledge and skill, and the direction to acquire both, which you can then pass down to those on the same path. The order is significant. A person does not begin by looking for something to sell; they begin by having somewhere to go, and the sellable experience is a by-product of moving toward it.

He then generalizes the health example quoted in Section 28.1. "When you treat every area of your life as a purpose-oriented project, you are required to self-experiment, and through self-experimentation you can create a unique set of experiences and lessons and skills and expertise that you can pass down to others." A *purpose-oriented project* is his name for any domain of life given a goal and run as an experiment. And the benefits that come from living this way are the marketing: "The benefits you reap from living with purpose are your marketing firepower to help people live better lives. That is the underlying philosophy. [What] you forge through self-experimentation is how you generate a creative income from your pursuits."

The phrase *marketing firepower* has a specific sense here (the corpus also uses it, elsewhere, for the persuasive pieces of a landing page). In this passage it means that the results of a purposeful life are themselves the argument for buying the product: a better body, a calmer mind or a working business are visible proofs, and the person who has them does not need to invent claims. This anticipates the more developed idea, below, that "your marketing is the intrinsic philosophy that you form with your reasons."

#### A duty, not only a business

In July 2023 Koe gives the same process an ethical and spiritual reading. "As you learn and acquire skills, it is your duty to build real-world projects that get results for other people, and that is how you both create a profitable product in business and fulfill your spiritual duty of feeling as if you are part of something greater than yourself by helping other people at scale."

**Source:** Niche Down Is Terrible Advice (For Smart People).md

The word "duty" is unusual in a corpus that mostly speaks of choice and freedom, and it marks a step in the argument. Learning that is never applied to anyone else stays private; Koe treats it as incomplete. The real-world project that helps others is where the private development of 28.1 becomes the contribution of Chapter 9's highest level of purpose, and the same act produces the profit. The claim is that the commercial and the spiritual are not two motives competing for the same act, but two descriptions of it.

#### Merging art and business

Koe's 2025 version of the idea borrows from Naval Ravikant. The last step of a February 2025 process is to "merge art and business," which he introduces with Naval's line: "To build product, make something people want. To create art, make something you want. The best do both." Koe comments that it is sad that people cannot accept that business, entrepreneurship or being a creator can be very fulfilling, "because happiness is a two-sided coin: the creativity required to make progress and contributing to something greater than yourself," that is, becoming valuable and sharing that value with the internet. "You merge art and business when you make something you want and share it with people who have similar interests, because they will probably want it too." His prescription: "write content for yourself, treat the internet as your public journal, put effort into getting that spread to more people, build a product you would buy, use and benefit from."

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

The quotation is Naval's; the reading of it is Koe's. Naval's sentence opposes two creative motives (what others want, what you want) and praises those who satisfy both. Koe dissolves the opposition with the premise of this section: if you are the niche, what you want and what people like you want are the same thing, so the artist's motive and the businessman's motive converge. The definition of happiness as "a two-sided coin" (creativity plus contribution) is Koe's own, and Chapter 39 develops its fuller version (happiness as resistance overcome plus connection).

#### Offline it's growth, online it's business

The most compact statement of the whole philosophy is a six-step sequence Koe gave in April 2023 and repeated in a 2024 compilation. "Set a goal, find a problem, learn how to solve it, document how you solved it, distill it into a replicable process, give it to others that want to be helped. Do it offline and it's called growth. Do it online and it's called business. That's it."

**Source:** Your 20s Are Meant To Build (How To Not Waste Them).md; Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md

Three days later he gave a five-step version without the first step: find a problem, learn how to solve it, document how you solved it, distill it into a replicable process, give it to others who want to be helped. In that version the closing line reads "do it offline and it's called improvement; do it online and it's called business."

**Source:** The Micro Education Business Model (How To Monetize Your Knowledge).md

The difference between the versions is small but not trivial. Without "set a goal," the process starts from a problem already present; with it, the problem is generated by the goal, which is the stricter logic of Chapter 8 (a problem only exists relative to a goal). The two closing lines, "growth" and "improvement," mean the same thing. What the sentence asserts in either form is an identity: the activity that makes up a business is the same activity that makes up personal development, and the only variable is whether it is made public. The 2024 compilation ends the passage with a line about the twenties that ties it back to Chapter 5: you realize your potential "by becoming aware of the rut that you are digging yourself in just by the default state of humanity"; "consciousness is chaotic unless we order it"; "don't waste your 20s: build your body, build your mind, build your business."

The phrase *replicable process* (sometimes *replicable system*) is the hinge of the sequence. Documenting how you solved something produces a story; distilling it produces a process that someone else can follow. Section 28.6 will show that Koe uses exactly this difference to separate content from product: "the difference between content and product is convenience, clarity and a replicable system that gets results."

#### If you have not yet achieved a meaningful goal

The sequence presupposes that you have already solved something. For those who have not, Koe gives a longer procedure in November 2023. (1) Set a goal in one domain of your life. (2) Drown yourself in knowledge and education around it. (3) Document that knowledge as content, because "teaching is how you learn and identify what you don't know so that you can learn efficiently; this is crucial." (4) Experiment with different techniques: training programs, diets, ways to make money. Then identify the fundamentals, truths and principles that run across the techniques. (5) Achieve the goal by creating your own way of getting there. Finally, distribute it as a digital product that sells while you sleep, "with obscenely high profit margins." From there you have the resources (a readership and an income) to build whatever other businesses you want.

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

The numbering in the transcript is irregular (Koe says "fourth" and "fifth" for what are really the fifth and sixth moves), but the logic is clear. The procedure is the build-to-learn method of Chapter 14 turned into a business plan: documentation is not a later stage but part of the learning, because teaching exposes the gaps in what you know; experimentation precedes principle; and the principles extracted from many techniques are what let you create "your own way." The step that turns a learner into a seller is the same step that turns a follower of techniques into someone with a method.

#### Experimentation: you cannot put your name on someone else's planner

Koe insists that the philosophy works only through experimentation. "The entirety of my success has been built on solving my own problems and selling the solution, and you can only do that through experimentation." You cannot take someone else's planner, put your name on it and sell it; you have to try things out to make it your own. A fitness creator has to experiment with the different diets and training programs and create one: "take the best and create something new."

**Source:** Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md

His own planner is the example he returns to most. In June 2023: when you solve your own problems through self-education, skill acquisition and building a business, you have a solution to pass down, and those solutions do double duty. They are how you gain a readership on social media and build leverage, and they are how you create a product to sell. "When I fixed my productivity problems through self-education and practice, I created a planner that I could sell. It's that simple." The same solution is content and product; the readership grows from sharing it, and the product is its packaged form.

**Source:** Get Mad At Where You Are In Life (A Deep Explanation).md

In August 2023 he turns the planner into a full scenario and shows how much the self-experiment teaches beyond the product itself. Suppose he wants to sell a planner. He experiments on himself first, "because you need experience and results; most businesses fail because they're trying to sell to people that have a problem they've never experienced." He buys four or five of the best-selling planners on Amazon, buys productivity books and courses, drowns himself in podcasts, accounts and videos on productivity, then structures his days, maps his work and applies what he learned. By then he has an idea for improving the planner or doing something new. But he has also, without intending to, exposed himself to the whole business. He has listened to high-performing content he can recreate to promote the planner and build a following without ads. He has bought planners, books and courses from people who make a living from them, so he can reverse-engineer their marketing and web pages. And he has results, so he has direct experience of the problem, the desired outcome and the system, "which composes my marketing strategy." "It's all right in front of you, but you aren't paying attention."

**Source:** I Had To Learn These High-Income Skills (If I Wanted To Make Money).md

The scenario shows why Koe treats the self-experiment as the core of the method rather than a preliminary. The same weeks of research produce three things at once: the product (a better method), the content strategy (the topics that already perform), and the marketing (the problem, outcome and mechanism, known from the inside, plus the competitors' persuasion studied as a customer). The "it's all right in front of you" line connects to the self-awareness principle of 28.1: the consumer's eyes, turned into a creator's eyes, are the research department.

A June 2024 version answers a common objection: do you need experience selling planners, or even experience in productivity, to sell a planner? Koe's answer is that the people who sell Coca-Cola created the product; they did not first have experience selling it. You create, sell, test; it will suck at first; you iterate: "that's what product development is," starting "with something that sucks" and turning it "into something better based off of audience feedback." His planner procedure in this version: buy a few planners and use them to see which methods give the best results; take the best parts of each to create a new method; test and iterate on yourself until you have solved the problem in your own life; manufacture, sell and distribute to the audience you built with content; get results for customers and keep iterating until the product brings in the revenue you want. The digital versions are a printable planner, a Notion template (which he has sold) or an e-book or course that walks through the productivity system.

**Source:** Change Your Life In 365 Hours (The New Rich Focus On These Tasks Daily).md

There is an apparent tension between this insistence on originality and other advice in the corpus that tells beginners to "sell what's already selling" and not to be clever with a first product (Chapter 26). Koe's reconciliation works by layers: what can be copied is the market, the marketing and "the why behind why it works" (headlines, problems, offer structure); what must be created is the system, the method that makes the product one's own. The planner scenarios show both layers at once: he buys the best-selling planners (the market is validated) and he builds a new method from their best parts and his own testing (the system is his).

#### When you do not know what to sell

For those who have profitable interests but no product idea, Koe offers a heuristic. "If you don't know what to sell, you sell something that you would have wanted on your journey or something that you want but can't find right now." The first half points backward to the former self; the second points to the present gap in the market as experienced by you.

**Source:** Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md

He illustrates the second half with a tweet by Ryan Ayala, whose podcast he was about to appear on: "Ralph Lauren couldn't find clothes he loved, so he decided to make and sell them himself, assuming that if he wanted them other people would too." Koe calls it "a very important business lesson." A clothing brand (its name is unclear in the transcript) quoted the tweet to explain why it had made a short-inseam cotton short: "couldn't find it, so I made it." Koe's comment: "I'm the same exact way, but I've done it with digital products." His own cases, mentioned in the same video, are the planner and the Modern Mastery community, which he designed as the community he would have wanted.

The Ralph Lauren story is a third party's (Ayala's tweet about a designer), and Koe uses it as an illustration, not as evidence. Its logic is the same as the "you are the niche" argument: the assumption that what you want, others like you will want too. It also states that assumption's risk honestly in the word "assuming." The heuristic works when the seller's taste is shared by enough people, and the test of that is the market.

#### Qualified by a solved problem: the education business

The philosophy implies a particular kind of business. In February 2024, and again in July 2024 in almost identical words, Koe says: "if you've solved the problem in your life, you're qualified to start a business; not just any business, an education business, as one person, with zero startup cost, with the knowledge you already have in your head, regardless of experience level."

**Source:** Zero To $1 Million As A One-Person Business (Working 2-4 Hours A Day).md; The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md

The *education business* (Koe's term, sometimes the *micro education business model*) is the business of selling what you learned while solving a problem: a course, a guide, a system, a coaching program. Its advantages, as listed, are structural: one person can run it, it costs almost nothing to start, and its raw material is knowledge already in the founder's head. The phrase "regardless of experience level" depends on the argument of Chapter 26 that you serve the people one or two steps behind you; the qualification is relative, not absolute. Chapter 31 defends education as a product against its common objections.

The negative form of the same principle explains, for Koe, why most businesses fail. Creators "are self-sufficient in the products that they create to solve the real problems of humanity, problems that they've experienced. That's the problem with most businesses: they try to solve a problem that they haven't experienced, and that's why the businesses fail."

**Source:** How Intelligent Creators Will Make Money In 2024 (The Build Teach Earn Method).md

The claim is presented as a principle, not supported with data, and it should be read as Koe's diagnosis rather than an established finding about business failure. Its internal logic is the "automatic filter for value": a lived problem is guaranteed to be real, an imagined one is not.

In September 2023 he extends the argument into ethics. People will tell you to "start a real business," but what they often mean, he says, is "I am fabricating problems that don't benefit humanity; don't start a business around real problems that will raise the collective consciousness." People with a poor relationship with money think marketing and sales are evil; they do not understand that the exchange of goods is human nature and has always existed. "Money is neutral until you put it in the hands of good or evil," and consistent progress toward conscious goals ensures that you lean toward the good. "When you solve your own problems and sell the solution, you can guarantee that those problems exist on the cosmic scale and rest well knowing that you are making a difference."

**Source:** Life Is A Video Game (You Can't Escape The Matrix).md

The phrase "on the cosmic scale" is hyperbole, but its function is precise: a problem you lived is a problem that exists in reality, not one manufactured by a marketer. The defense of money as neutral is developed in Chapter 34 (money programming), and the ethics of persuasion in Chapter 32.

#### First, master your survival

A March 2023 process adds a precondition. Its first step is to "master your survival," and Koe explains why it comes first: "you can't sustain authenticity when you need something from someone else for survival." Being self-reliant, able to generate an independent income, with a self-confidence that does not depend on others, is what lets you act authentically. He adds two qualifications. Mastery is a process, so you do not wait until you have mastered survival before starting; you would probably never register it as done, because it never ends. And every individual has to self-actualize in order to contribute to humanity in the best way they can, where self-actualization is "to fulfill the desire to become everything one can be, to actualize your potential."

**Source:** The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md

He admits a limit to his advice. Self-actualization can be achieved through "the perfect career path," but he cannot help with that; he chose business and self-improvement because otherwise you will never have full control over your days or how you make money. His recommendation is to solve the *biological problems* in your life (health, finances, socialization) and to discover your interests through improvement. That takes time, but it does not prevent earning an independent income now, through what he calls the *minimum viable offer*: learn a skill and start selling it (Chapter 29 develops it).

The precondition reverses a common assumption about authenticity. One might think that authenticity comes first and money follows. Koe argues the opposite order: a person who depends on someone else for survival will bend their speech and work to that dependency, so independence is the condition of authenticity, not its reward. The connection to self-reliance (Chapter 35) is direct.

#### The product as a holistic solution

The fourth step of the same March 2023 process explains what the product of this philosophy looks like. It is a *holistic solution*, built in three sub-steps.

**Source:** The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md

First, identify a desirable goal: a big goal you are trying to achieve, have achieved or achieved long ago. (Are you fit? Did you acquire a skill?) Koe warns against limiting yourself to the belief that "only this thing makes money": if you know Photoshop, sell a Photoshop course; if you know how to make a website, create a product called "how to create a website" and improve it from there. It does not have to be perfect. "Until you actually get a product out, you have nothing to improve, you have nothing to iterate on," and the lessons of life and business pass you by because you have nowhere to apply them.

Second, identify a starting point: where you were, why you did not like it. Paint the lifestyle you were living against the one you live now: what was painful, what is beneficial, what is the why.

Third, outline a unique path, structured like a book. Point A is the problem, which opens a *curiosity loop* (the open question that makes people want to know more, a concept of Chapter 23) and hooks people; point B is the goal achieved and the lifestyle created; the chapters in between are what people want to know. This is where the holistic solution comes in: "you decide the synthesis."

The three sub-steps (goal, starting point, path) reproduce the goal–path–problem structure of Chapter 8 in the form of an offer, and they anticipate the story structure of Chapter 23: a product is a transformation narrated from A to B. "You decide the synthesis" connects to the synthesizer of Chapter 18: the product's uniqueness lies in the way one person connected the pieces.

#### Your marketing is your intrinsic philosophy

In May 2023, after listing his reasons for walking, Koe turns the list into a business lesson. "Start a business as I always do, by solving your own problems and selling the solution that you pick up along the way, and your marketing is the intrinsic philosophy that you form with your reasons." He could "100%" sell a walking program, "30 days of walks," as a digital product or a book, with the reasons he had just listed as the copy of the landing page; he guarantees that what he said was persuasive and that some viewers will start walking. "That's the most meaningful style of business model anyways": "you're changing people's lives by changing your own."

**Source:** Most People Won't Change (How To Recreate Yourself).md

The example makes concrete the earlier claim that the benefits of a purposeful life are "marketing firepower." The persuasion was not written for a sale; it was the set of reasons that made Koe change his own behavior. The *intrinsic philosophy* (his phrase) is the reasoning a person built for themselves, and if it convinced them, it is likely to convince people like them. The last sentence of the passage is a compact definition of the whole model.

#### Shallow problems first

A 2022 passage connects the philosophy to Chapter 9's argument that one cannot skip levels of purpose. Lack of self-esteem, Koe says, is often a health problem: perhaps you have no muscle mass or do not like how you look. That is a surface-level problem. Through the lens of finding purpose, "you have to solve those shallow external problems first and then bust through to your inner core" to reach meaning and purpose, because we have covered that core with comparisons to other people, and the comparisons create the shallow problems. You cannot ignore them; you have to solve them, because they are what most people struggle with, and "that's how you can help them, once you actually solve them for yourself." His example: low self-esteem, traced to a lack of muscle, leads you to learn the gym, find a unique take or your own system for getting in shape, and then teach it.

**Source:** If You Are High Value, Start A One-Person Business.md

Chapter 9 (Section 9.1) used the same passage to establish the developmental order; its business corollary is that shallow problems are not a lesser market. They are the largest market, precisely because almost everyone is stuck at them, and solving them for oneself is both the first step toward depth and the first product one can honestly sell.

#### Naval's seven billion companies, and package the method

In June 2023 Koe gives the philosophy a third party's frame. He quotes Naval Ravikant: there are almost seven billion people on the planet; someday he hopes there will be almost seven billion companies. Koe's *one-person business philosophy*, built on that line, holds that the best businesses change lives and produce transformations. To make a winning product, stop trying to solve imaginary problems: "solve your own problems, change your own life, package up the method, slap a price tag on it." You are less likely to fail when you solve real, meaningful problems. Most people entering business, he adds, lack an understanding of marketing, sales, psychology or evolution.

**Source:** The One Person Business Model 2.0 (Turn Yourself Into A Business).md

The Naval quotation is examined as a thesis about the future of work in Section 28.4; here Koe uses it as a premise for a product philosophy. If every person can be a company, the company each person is best placed to build is the one that sells the method they used on their own life.

#### Start as one person

The last element is an argument about scale. In *The Art of Focus* Koe writes: "How to start a billion-dollar company? Instead, start as one person. If you can't turn your interests into income, you don't have the skill to start a business that requires 10 times the resources." An individual can attract an audience and put a valuable product in front of it. Most people complicate modern business: they are told "you need to niche down and target an audience that you probably don't care to work with," and so "modern business advice is a surefire way to end up in another line of work that you hate."

**Source:** The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md

The argument is a skills argument, not an argument against ambition. A large company needs, at a minimum, the ability to attract attention and convert it into revenue; a person who cannot do that alone, with their own interests as material, will not do it with ten times the resources. The criticism of niching down repeats Chapter 19's case against choosing a market one does not care about; its new element is the outcome it predicts. A business built around strangers' problems recreates the job the founder was trying to leave.

### 28.3 Entrepreneurship Is for Everyone

#### Two dirty words

If business is the outward side of personal development, then the word "entrepreneur" should apply to almost anyone who develops. Koe knows that this is not how the word sounds to most people, and in February 2025 he begins from that resistance. "Business and entrepreneurship have become dirty words." We have "a pretty immature definition of them": when people hear them, self-doubt floods in. "That requires a lot of money to start." "I'm not talented enough." "Charging people money, that sounds like a scam." "I don't know how to create an LLC or do my taxes, so I'm never going to learn." "Sounds boring." He is not, he says, talking about entrepreneurship "as a role or a title or something that's reserved for talented people," but about "what it actually is, which is a state of mind, a state of consciousness."

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

The list of objections is useful because each corresponds to a part of the system that answers it. Money to start: the one-person business costs almost nothing (Section 28.4). Talent: everything is a skill (Chapter 15). Scam: money is neutral and persuasion is as ethical as its user (Section 28.2 and Chapter 32). LLCs and taxes: a distraction before the first customers (below in this section). Boring: the business as the vessel of a life's work and a game (Chapters 9 and 10). The redefinition as a "state of mind" is the move that makes the rest of the section possible.

#### Everyone is an entrepreneur

Koe's broadest claim is that everyone already is an entrepreneur. In March 2023, as part of what he calls "my philosophy": "everyone is an entrepreneur; some just decide to get paid for the value they have to provide," linked to the idea that entrepreneurship is modern-day survival.

**Source:** The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md

In April 2023 he expands it, referring to an earlier "entire 10-minute rant" on the same subject. "Entrepreneurship is for everyone. You are an entrepreneur right now. You have value to provide and you provide it to some people. All you need to do is put that value on the internet or in front of people with a price tag on it and boom, you're an entrepreneur. Strip your mind of the limiting beliefs of what you think entrepreneurship is and just start contributing to humanity and getting paid for doing so. That's entrepreneurship: by helping other people." The same passage reappears as the third step of a "future-proofing" sequence ("build your business") in a 2024 compilation.

**Source:** Your 20s Are Meant To Build (How To Not Waste Them).md; Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md

The argument has a simple structure. Value is already being provided (to friends, colleagues, family, an employer). What separates that from entrepreneurship is two operations: putting the value where more people can see it, and attaching a price. Neither requires a new identity in the dramatic sense; both require removing a belief about what entrepreneurship is. The *Art of Focus* version quoted in Section 28.2 ("some just choose to get paid for the problems they solve in their personal life") specifies which value: the solutions to one's own problems.

#### A state of mind, not a title

Over 2024 and 2025 Koe's definition shifts from what entrepreneurs do to what kind of mind they have. In July 2024: "Entrepreneurship is a mindset, not a title, a mindset dedicated to solving an infinite set of creative problems, a way to hedge against getting trapped in a repetitive routine that someone else assigned to you. It is an infinite game, not a finite one." Your work evolves; it does not stagnate unless you allow it, "but even then at least it's your choice." "If you stay in a 9-to-5 for too long, you become a monkey in a cubicle."

**Source:** Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md

The definition borrows the distinction between finite and infinite games (Chapter 10, Section 10.7), and the *monkey in a cubicle* is the image of the hunter psyche trapped in a repetitive environment from Chapter 2. Entrepreneurship, so defined, is a protection against two things: the assigned routine and the stagnation that follows it.

In February 2025, quoting his forthcoming short book *Purpose and Profit*, he takes the definition one step further. "Entrepreneurship is not a role or a title, it's a state of mind." You can be an employee with the mind of an entrepreneur (high agency, solving problems), and that is who employers want to hire and who will make up the top 1%. So even if you work for someone else, "the only logical option still is to be an entrepreneur."

**Source:** The creator economy is dying thanks to AI.md

This is a real change of position, and the corpus records it. In December 2023, in "The Future Of Work Is Play," Koe set the employee mindset against the traits of the entrepreneur and advised leaving employment early to avoid further conditioning: employee and entrepreneur were opposed trajectories. From late 2024 the opposition softens ("you can still be an employee that has high agency"), and by 2025 the two words have become states of mind rather than careers; in September 2025 he says "stop thinking of employee and entrepreneur as titles, think of them as states of mind." Koe does not present the change as a correction; he presents it as a redefinition of words that had become dirty. Both positions can be held together if one distinguishes the condition from the path: the entrepreneurial state of mind is a condition available to anyone, including employees; the business of one's own remains, for Koe, the route to full control over time and income.

#### Sahil Bloom: being enterprising inside a 9-to-5

The softer version owes something to a guest. In a January 2025 conversation, Sahil Bloom adds a layer to Koe's belief that everyone should be an entrepreneur. "You can try entrepreneurship while working in a stable job. Entrepreneurship is fundamentally about creating something. It's about being enterprising. It's about experimenting. It's about creating value when there was no expectation of you to do that. It's about taking on new things, being curious, identifying problems ... fundamentally, entrepreneurship is about identifying problems, creating solutions and then scaling those solutions. You can do that in your 9-to-5 job. You don't have to burn the boats." Entrepreneurship, in his view, "can be executed in a lot of different formats": quitting to start something entirely your own, or building "scalable solutions to problems that you find" within a company. He agrees with Koe that the process is "really important ... for engaging your mind."

**Source:** The Time Billionaire (A Concept That Will Change Your Life) With Sahil Bloom.md

Bloom then gives a concrete exercise for someone at a large company, "getting your annual inflation pay raise for doing your job description," who wants to be more entrepreneurial. "Every day for a week, keep a blank notepad where you identify problems that you see in the company": problems of coworkers, bosses, customers. "Start brainstorming some solutions to those and bring them to the table and actually go and execute on them." His claim: "I guarantee you will start seeing an acceleration in your trajectory within that company, because the number of people who are willing to take the initiative to be entrepreneurial in a normal day-to-day job [is] effectively zero; you will stand out. You will be top 10% ... you'll start making a whole lot more money."

The definition and the exercise are Bloom's (*enterprising* is his word), and the guarantee is his opinion, offered from experience rather than data. Their place in Koe's system is clear. Bloom's definition (identify problems, create solutions, scale them) is Koe's goal–path–problem loop described from the side of an organization, and the notepad exercise is a small *measurable personal project* run inside a job. What Bloom adds is the explicit permission not to quit, which Koe's 2023 rhetoric had withheld.

#### Justin Welsh and the word "entrepreneur"

The stigma of the word affects even successful solo founders. In a December 2021 conversation, Justin Welsh tells Koe that for "the last 2 years, 2 and 1/2 years" he was "really, really afraid or uncomfortable to call myself an entrepreneur," because to him entrepreneurs were people who start SaaS companies and raise "a hundred million dollars in capital." At dinner with the former CEO of his first startup (a unicorn), he described what he did, and the CEO answered: "You're an entrepreneur. That's a business. That's a business that scales. You can scale that business year over year." Two or three weeks before the recording he had started calling himself an entrepreneur instead of a "personal brand" or a "one-man business." Koe relates: there is a stigma that an entrepreneur builds a huge company and is "out of touch," and another that the word is "kind of cheesy."

**Source:** Justin Welsh Shows You How To Start & Grow A One-Person Business.md

The story is Welsh's, and it is useful as evidence of how strong the startup image of entrepreneurship is: a person running a profitable, growing business still did not feel entitled to the word until someone from the startup world granted it. Koe's later redefinitions can be read as an attempt to remove the need for that permission.

#### Creator as a way of life

The same broadening applies to the word "creator." In July 2024 Koe says: "I don't think the creator economy can be reduced to just people who create content for the sake of a job; I see it as a way of life, being a creator." Reducing "creator" to "some myopic job title" is close-minded. He then describes what creators do in five steps. (1) "They're identifying problems in their life, because they have agency and they don't need permission from a boss to go and solve a problem." (2) "They research how to solve it through the internet." (3) "They synthesize that information." (4) "They create their own system for getting results." (5) "They pass that down to someone behind them so that person can get results faster, and humanity can evolve just by that process alone."

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md

This is about "solving human problems, meaningful problems," and Koe gives it an argument for durability: "AI can't go into your head yet and fix your mental health issues or your growth needs, your self-actualization needs." And it is the reason for his niche thesis: "This is why I say you are the niche, because you are simply passing down what worked for you to someone else who has a very similar personality as you by attracting them to your vision and goals."

The five steps are the "solve your own problems" philosophy described as a character rather than a procedure. The first step defines the creator by *agency* (the capacity to act on a problem without permission, which Chapter 35 develops); the middle steps are the research, synthesis and system-building of Part VI; the last is transmission. The claim about AI is dated ("yet") and limited to the kind of problem it names; Chapter 36 examines what the author thinks AI does and does not replace.

#### Entrepreneurship as modern hunting

Later in the same video Koe gives entrepreneurship its evolutionary frame. "Entrepreneurship is how you hunt for your survival in the modern world. Why? Because true entrepreneurship requires you to become future-proof." The process, "simple on the surface but complex in depth, as all meaningful things are," has two moves. "You create value through creativity, experimentation and experience: you improve yourself." "You distribute value through attention, media and persuasion, so you improve humanity." "When you are in control of the vision, AI and technology become the tool, not the master." And "the beautiful thing about that simple process is that it never ends."

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md

The two moves organize the rest of the book. *Create value* names what Parts IV through VI teach (direction, focus, learning, thinking, creativity) and what Chapter 31 defines. *Distribute value* names what Parts VIII and IX teach (writing, audience, brand) and what Chapter 32 calls persuasion. The hunting image comes from Chapter 2 (the hunter psyche in a cubicle) and is completed here: in an economy where survival is obtained through markets, building something of value and bringing it to people is the contemporary form of the hunt. "Future-proof" anticipates Chapter 36: a person in control of the vision directs the tools rather than being replaced by them.

#### Create your own career

A November 2022 lesson, which Koe introduces as difficult and requiring experience, explains why his own work does not fit any existing job description. Asked how to start an online business with $0, most people would say freelancing or an agency. But Koe does not know how to describe what he does: in an elevator he would say "I'm a writer, I just blog"; he writes tweets on self-improvement, makes videos on business and spirituality, posts on Instagram; he dislikes "influencer," and "creator" does not get it across. His business model cannot be turned into a career for everyone or a college curriculum one could be trained in. You create your own career, he says, "by following what works, by doing what works, but in your own way, without getting in your own way," iterating and blowing past societal expectations until you have a holistic view that outsiders do not understand. (His example of the outsider's confusion: "so you're a YouTuber, but you make a hundred thousand dollars a month?") Tangibly: if there is a problem in your life, such as not liking eight-hour days, create solutions for every problem until you live your ideal lifestyle.

**Source:** 11 Lessons From Growing A 7-Figure One Person Business.md

The lesson is the opposite of the assigned career of Chapter 2. A career that can be described in a curriculum is a career someone else designed; the career Koe recommends is assembled from solutions to one's own problems, and it is illegible from the outside precisely because no one else had that sequence of problems.

In December 2023 he draws the same idea as a diagram of "the main career path of the future." (1) You. (2) Pursue your curiosities. (3) Go the content route, the code route, or both. (4) Become an employee or an entrepreneur straight away, or an employee to gain skills that let you become an entrepreneur, using the employee income to fuel the entrepreneurial work. (5) Once you have a product (which, as an employee, you are also selling) (6) find distribution: "you are the distribution with code and content," which "equals really as much money as you want." Underneath runs a chain: curiosity, learning, obsession, "a life you never thought possible." The slogan of the video: "we have the chance to create a career, not be assigned one." The transcript does not allow the layout of the graphic to be reconstructed exactly; the sequence above is the order in which Koe narrates it.

**Source:** The Future Of Work Is Play (How To Create A Digital Career).md

The diagram is notable for including employment as a legitimate stage, a year before the "state of mind" redefinition. The employee route appears as a way to fund and train the entrepreneurial one, which partly qualifies the sharper opposition between the two in the same video.

For the generalist of Chapter 20, the first step has a specific form. In May 2024 Koe advises: "do it all," writer, designer, marketer, filmmaker, bodybuilder, runner, whatever curiosity desires, since the internet lets an individual learn almost anything fast and get results from diverse interests. Step one is to choose a career path that does not box you into one skill or interest: become an entrepreneur. "But that alone doesn't cut it": you can still get trapped in a single niche where you can no longer find meaning in your work.

**Source:** The Age Of The Generalist (How To Earn A Living Doing What You Enjoy).md

The qualification is important. Entrepreneurship is necessary for the generalist but not sufficient, because a business can become its own box. The rest of Koe's answer is the "you are the niche" model: a business built around a person can change as the person's interests change, while a business built around a topic cannot.

#### Build a solution instead of accepting a constraint

A principle that runs through Koe's 2022 one-person business video is that constraints are problems to be solved, not facts to be accepted. Rather than accept "you can only work eight hours," build the system or solution that allows the outcome you want. He reapplies it at once to a common objection: "if you want to start a business without having to show your body, then build a [expletive] business without having to show your body." His examples are working fewer hours through systems and faceless writing accounts. "Stop convincing yourself you can't do something because you're not doing it like everyone else."

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself).md

The principle is the business version of Chapter 8's use of constraints: a constraint narrows the search for a path and therefore makes the problem solvable. Its specific target is the habit of disqualifying oneself by comparison ("everyone shows their face, I can't, so I can't do this"). The objection is converted into a design requirement.

#### Against startup ideas

The broad definition of entrepreneurship comes with a warning about the narrow image it replaces. In February 2024 Koe says that when people think of starting a business they think of startup culture: a crazy idea, such as a food app that suggests dinner ideas, without realizing that there are only so many restaurants nearby, all of them findable on Yelp. Such ideas often come "after a night of drinking with your friends" ("this is a million-dollar business") and go nowhere, because the idea is stupid and the person lacks perspective on what a business is and how to make an income from their own interests, skills, expertise and the problems they have already solved. People glorify "insane, beautiful, pretty ideas" online and get conditioned, often by internet content, into thinking they need one. Special cases exist, where someone executes a great idea and "the universe conspires in their favor," but they are very rare and lucky; "it's not wise to gamble on your success; it's wise to invest smartly in your future with skill acquisition."

**Source:** Zero To $1 Million As A One-Person Business (Working 2-4 Hours A Day).md

In July 2024 he gives the epistemic reason. Most people who try to build a startup or have "some crazy business idea after a night of drinking with their friends" think their idea will change the world, "but they're usually delusional, because they don't have any prior business experience." If you had the idea with zero experience, your mind is limited in the ideas it can have ("you haven't built anything that leads to better ideas"), so "I can almost guarantee that someone has already tried and failed with it." By contrast, "there's a reason that people, and specifically creators, are making millions solving fitness, productivity, career, money, relationships and lifestyle problems: because every person has them."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md

The second passage applies the mechanism of Section 28.1 in reverse. If creativity depends on the expansion of the mind, and the mind expands by building, then a person who has built nothing has a small space of possible ideas, and the idea that seems revolutionary inside that space is probably one that many others have already had. Good ideas are a product of experience, not a substitute for it. The contrast with the universal problems is the same filter as before: lived problems are real, imagined revolutions usually are not. The guarantee is rhetorical and should be read as a strong opinion.

A 2023 metaphor gives the warning a moral scope. Stop trying to "land on Saturn" and build a billion-dollar company when that is not a real, tangible problem that makes lives better. "Why are we trying to land on Saturn when we can't even live on Earth?" Most of the population still suffers from *low-consciousness problems* (Koe's term for the basic problems of health, money and relationships), and the individuals who form the collective are holding back "the collective ego" from enlightenment. His recommendation is to start with a micro education business model: solve real problems in your own life and distribute the solutions. Start by understanding the Eternal markets; solving your problems there pushes you toward self-actualization, creates a *unique map* (your story, which you can pass down) and earns income by helping others heal and achieve their goals. Then, once everyone is at a higher state of consciousness, people can do what they love and solve the deeper problems they are passionate about, "like landing on Saturn at that point."

**Source:** The One Person Business Model 2.0 (Turn Yourself Into A Business).md

The metaphor is not against ambition; its last sentence keeps Saturn as a later destination. It is an argument about order, at two scales. For the individual, solve the basic problems first (Chapter 9's levels); for the collective, raise the base before the peaks. The vocabulary of "collective ego" and "enlightenment" belongs to the developmental frame of Part XIV, and the claim that it is the individuals who hold the collective back is Koe's interpretation, not an empirical finding.

#### Building without customers, and the wrong priorities

Two warnings concern what beginners do instead of selling. In March 2026: "Too many people try to build businesses nowadays and they don't actually try to get customers. They're like, 'I'm going to build this software, I'm going to build this thing,' motivated, but then when it comes time to actually do the business stuff, they're gone."

**Source:** How To Build A $1M One-Person Business Faster With AI.md

In June 2024 he describes the same failure as a lack of prioritization. Step one is to write daily content on social media; the biggest problem is focusing on the wrong things. (He refers to his video on 365 hours, which explains how to use one hour a day on the highest-leverage task, since most people have one hour, not eight to twelve.) People build a website when they should find customers, build a product when they should attract an audience, file for an LLC and design a logo when they should do almost anything else. "This lack of prioritization screams that you have no idea what you're doing." He concedes that LLCs and taxes are confusing, but an LLC does not help you make money; building a product or service and getting people to buy it through content and an audience does. His threshold: "once you make 50,000, think about starting an LLC."

**Source:** Ideas Are The New Oil (How More People Get Rich In The Digital Age).md

The two warnings belong together because they describe the same avoidance. Building software, designing a logo or filing paperwork feels like progress and protects the founder from the uncomfortable part of business, which is exposure to people who might say no. The priority ladder of Chapter 12 (the most challenging task first) applies here: the highest-leverage task is the one that brings customers, and it is usually the one being avoided. The $50,000 threshold is a rule of thumb from Koe's experience, not legal or tax advice, and the rules for business entities vary by country.

### 28.4 The One-Person Business as the Future of Work

#### What a one-person business is

The *one-person business* is the name Koe gives to the model that carries everything described so far. It is a common phrase in online business circles; Koe gives it a specific content and changes that content over time. The earliest full description in the corpus comes from October 2022, and it is reproduced almost word for word in a February 2024 compilation. What has always attracted him to the model, rather than to a business with employees, is a list: "a simple website as a storefront, a digital product stack with high profit margins, 4-hour workdays and potential $1 million years," and control over what you work on and whom you work with. It is for those who value self-reliance, time freedom, location freedom, sovereignty and autonomy: doing what you want, with enough money to do it, while impacting the world and having a purpose.

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

He then describes its mechanics in two layers. The first is social media, used to build an audience and a community and to attract like-minded people, which gives "leverage for the future." He contrasts it with freelancing: a freelancer sends cold DMs all day, while someone with an audience can sell different offers whenever they want, "as long as they're helpful and people buy willingly." Social media is the traffic. The second layer is *no-code tools*: software such as website builders and social media schedulers that hosts the products, the content and the newsletter. These tools perform specialized tasks that people used to be employed to do (his example is the social media manager, whose work he expects to be outsourced to cheap software that handles repetitive tasks), and so they let *solopreneurs* have every aspect of the business managed while they oversee the software and add their mind and creative ability.

A July 2024 definition keeps the same structure in three components. "The one-person business is for those who value self-reliance, time and location freedom." (1) Social media "for building leverage, attracting like minds and building a name for ourselves from nothing." (2) "No-code tools and software for digital real estate, product hosting and email lists that can't be taken away like social media." (3) A "lifestyle designed to create a work schedule that best suits the individual, usually 2 to 4 hours of work a day to start, sometimes more if you get into the flow."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md

| Layer | Function | Examples in the corpus |
|---|---|---|
| Social media | Traffic, audience, leverage; "building a name from nothing" | Writing on X or LinkedIn; the audience as an alternative to cold DMs |
| No-code tools | Operations and owned assets ("digital real estate") | Website builders, schedulers, product hosting, email lists |
| Lifestyle design | A schedule fitted to the person | Two to four hours a day at the start; more in flow |
| Digital product stack | Revenue with high margins | Courses, templates, guides sold from a simple storefront |

The phrase "email lists that can't be taken away like social media" introduces a distinction Chapter 24 developed (the newsletter as owned audience): social media is rented attention, the list is owned. *Digital real estate* is Koe's recurring term for such assets (profiles, newsletters, products), treated as property that compounds. And the two-to-four-hour schedule ties the model to the 4-Hour Workday of Chapter 12: the business is designed around a lifestyle, not the reverse.

#### From "one person" to "turning yourself into a business"

The literal name became a problem as Koe's business grew. In June 2023, in a video titled as the model's "2.0," he says that the one-person business has many interpretations and that "it's just a concept ... just made up." Now that he has a small team, it is strange to call himself a one-person business, so he redefines it: *turning yourself into a business*. You may hire a virtual assistant, freelancers or contractors (he has no employees, only a few contractors for odd jobs), and he "gives permission," while admitting he is not the gatekeeper. The concept, he says, is "a novel way of grabbing attention and saying: become high value and put yourself out there until enough people know who you are, what you do and why you do it," and opportunities compound from there. That is all you really need to succeed.

**Source:** The One Person Business Model 2.0 (Turn Yourself Into A Business).md

This is a documented change of position. The 2022 definition was literal (one person, no employees, the alternative to having a team). The 2023 definition is attitudinal: the business is built on a person, whatever help that person hires. Koe's stated reason is simply that having a team made the old name awkward. The two definitions can be read as describing different moments of the same business, the start and the maturity, and the original definition does in fact reappear in 2024 videos aimed at beginners. Chapter 37 follows the business further, to the point where Koe built software companies with real teams and examined what breaks when the model is scaled.

The admission that the concept is "just made up" is worth noticing. Koe does not claim that the one-person business is a new economic category; he calls it a "novel way of grabbing attention" for an old instruction (become valuable and make yourself known). The name is packaging, and he says so.

#### Justin Welsh: personal, authentic, and not "no-code"

Two remarks from the December 2021 conversation with Justin Welsh, one of the people most associated with the term, help fix its meaning. Koe offers his own view while setting up a question about promotion: "the one-man business, it's much more clearly personal. It's much more authentic. And with that comes different ways of selling things sometimes, because as a business it's like you have to stick to the brand message; you have to stick to this certain way of saying things." The one-person business has more freedom of voice in what it sells and how.

**Source:** Justin Welsh Shows You How To Start & Grow A One-Person Business.md

Later Koe asks whether Welsh treats "one-man business" and "no-code business" as synonyms, since he uses both in his bios. Welsh answers: "I don't think so. I think that no-code businesses can have lots of employees, and I think a one-man business can be ... someone coding something ... building a piece of software." The difference in his bios is only a matter of the lingo each platform prefers.

**Source:** Justin Welsh Shows You How To Start & Grow A One-Person Business.md

The two remarks mark the model's boundaries. Koe's distinguishes it from the corporate brand: a person can speak in their own voice, change their mind and sell in ways a company's brand guidelines would not allow (Chapter 27 developed the personal brand on the same ground). Welsh's distinguishes it from a technical category: what defines the one-person business is the number of people at its center, not whether it uses code. The no-code tools of the 2022 description are a convenience, not a definition.

#### Why the one-person business: the best route for most people

Koe argues repeatedly that the model is the best option for nearly everyone who wants to start a business, and the reasons accumulate over three years.

In October 2022 he says it is the best route for "95 plus percent" of people who want to start a business, and gives as the first reason that it follows a *human progression pattern*: you evolve with time, and the business evolves with you. In the same month, in the promotion of his "Digital Economics" cohort, he repeats that it is "the best option for 95 percent of people out there that want to start a business; it's low risk, it's low cost," and that "you need to understand marketing, content, brand and product."

**Source:** If You Are High Value, Start A One-Person Business.md; How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md

In November 2022 he adds a reason that concerns the person rather than the economics. He is "so big on" the one-person business, the creator business model, because "it's self-reliance based; it forces you to be original if you want to thrive in the market," and because it sets you up for the future by building *digital leverage* and spreading your ideas across a nation, which nobody could do before the internet.

**Source:** Why You Can't Be Yourself (The Power Of Being Alone).md

In December 2024 he gives the beginner's argument, with an unusual admission at the start: he talks about the one-person business partly because it does well on YouTube, but it is also the most logical option for beginners. Most people dream of a startup, software, an apparel brand, a pre-workout or a supplement, none of which is beginner-friendly: they need capital for manufacturing, product and development, and people get discouraged because they cannot do it yet (dropshipping has some workarounds). The one-person business needs no money, only the skills, interests or expertise you already have and an internet connection. It is "skill, interest and expertise agnostic; it works for all of them." And while building it you learn skills on the go, stacking high-value skills you can carry into another business model, and increase your income and cash flow until you can invest in software, apparel, e-commerce or whatever else you want.

**Source:** Build A One-Person Business As A Normal Person (From $0 To $10K).md

| Reason | First stated | What it claims |
|---|---|---|
| Human progression pattern | 2022-10 | The business evolves as the person evolves |
| Low risk, low cost | 2022-10 | Almost no capital at stake |
| Self-reliance forces originality | 2022-11 | Without a company behind you, you must be distinct to thrive |
| Digital leverage | 2022-11 | Ideas spread at a scale impossible before the internet |
| Skill, interest and expertise agnostic | 2024-12 | Works with whatever the person already has |
| A school for later businesses | 2024-12 | Builds skills and cash flow for capital-heavy ventures |

The "95 percent" figure is an opinion expressed as a number, not a measured proportion. The admission that the topic "does well on YouTube" is an instance of the transparency Koe sometimes shows about his own incentives; it does not refute the argument, but it is a reason to weigh it as the view of someone who sells education about this model.

#### Full circle: the ancestral entrepreneur at global scale

Koe also gives the model a historical frame. In April 2023: "No, you don't need to start a billion-dollar company. The internet has leveled the playing field for skill and knowledge acquisition," and for "just being able to build a product and put it in front of someone." "Evolution has led to this point of us coming full circle to how our ancestors were entrepreneurs": "you just all work in your little community, and it's like a little utopia," "and now it's much larger scale and global." The components follow: "a specific set of skills that you learn, the ones that you want to learn"; traffic "on social media by putting yourself in public as a personal brand"; and a product that would have helped your former self, the planner of Section 28.2. "You don't need a one-billion-dollar company; you need a one-person business." The passage reappears in a 2024 compilation with the former-self product and the line on personal development and business attached.

**Source:** Your 20s Are Meant To Build (How To Not Waste Them).md; Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md

*Full circle* is a common phrase that Koe uses here in a specific sense: the internet returns work to the form it had before industrial employment, in which individuals within communities traded what they made, but now the community is global. The image is idealized ("a little utopia"), and Koe offers it as a frame rather than as economic history. Its function in the argument is to make the one-person business look less like a novelty and more like a return to normal, with the industrial job as the historical anomaly (the thesis of Chapter 2, Section 2.6).

#### A starting point that removes limiting beliefs

Koe gives two reasons for treating the one-person business as a first business rather than a final one. The first, from November 2024, is practical. He is building a software startup (transcribed as "CeX"; elsewhere in the corpus it appears as Cortex), and his audience makes it easier at every step: he already has potential customers; if he wanted investors (he says he is not seeking them) he thinks he could find them easily; and hiring is easier because the audience refers great people. "Starting any kind of business, I believe, should come after starting a one-person business and just learning all the skills that you need to learn, especially when you don't have any money or cash flow yet." You do not need a large audience; his grew over "5 to 6 years" of doing it full-time.

**Source:** The Cheap Dopamine Epidemic Stop Ruining Your Life.md

The second, from January 2025, is perceptual. Koe explains how a project is "fabricated" from a problem and a vision: if you struggle with money, you might build a project aligned with, for example, a vision of a successful business, with the constraint of working four hours a day so you have time for family and relationships, which narrows the project drastically. But without the right environment and beliefs, you will not notice the one-person business as an option at all; you will believe that business requires a brick-and-mortar store, startup capital for software, or many skills and leadership experience. Someone up to date with technology and business would know that freelance and creator work exist and that anyone can post and have their work spread to potential customers. "That's why I present the one-person business as a starting point": "it removes most of the limiting beliefs they have" and gets people learning skills and building projects that can evolve into something else.

**Source:** The Fastest Way To Ruin Your Life (Avoid This While You're Young).md

The second reason returns to the mechanism of Section 28.1: what is not registered cannot be chosen. The one-person business is offered as the model that the largest number of people can *see* as possible from where they are, and its function is partly pedagogical: once people start, they learn skills and build projects, and their minds expand enough to register other options. Chapter 37 shows Koe following this path himself, from one-person business to software startup.

#### Career paths: four diagrams of the same route

Between 2023 and 2024 Koe draws the trajectory of the one-person business several times, with different emphases. They are best read together.

The *modern career path* (August 2023) was shown on screen. At the top is you: your topics, interests and curiosities. It branches into code (websites, membership software, funnels, automation, AI, whether no-code or programmed) and content (writing and speaking, involving social media, video, email, design and branding). Both branch into marketing and sales, "which are psychology, mechanics, attention and awareness," and marketing and sales pull in an audience. To the audience you sell the offer, "and that's where you make money." That is one offer; beyond it lie funnels, upsells, downsells, offer stacks and cross-promotion, and once you make some money, "you already know what to do next."

**Source:** I Had To Learn These High-Income Skills (If I Wanted To Make Money).md

The *self-reliant career path* (April 2023) leads to independent income generation. Koe presented it as a graphic that the transcript does not allow to be fully reconstructed; the sequence can be assembled from his recap. (1) *Profitable interests*: life direction plus burning problems in health, wealth and relationships, with curiosity choosing the methods. (2) A *project*: self-experimentation toward a goal. (3) A *product*: what you would have wanted on your journey. (4) A *personal distribution center*: traffic, through a personal brand. "We had profitable interest, we turned it into a project that would be turned into a product, and now we need a personal distribution center." The personal brand is "the meta project that houses all of the projects that turn into products" (the phrase Chapter 27 discussed).

**Source:** Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md

The *value creator business model* (July 2023) adds the long horizon. Create social media content and build distribution under your name as a person; then build an education product or service your past self could have used (a course, coaching, a tutoring offer, or a tool: if he struggled with productivity and is now productive, a planner; at the time he was making "Power Planner 2.0" as a keepsake for *The Art of Focus* book launch). When you reach your target income (from $100,000 a year up to $3–5 million a year "with a lot of time"), build whatever else you are passionate about. He is careful about what this does not say: not "don't start the company of your dreams," but "build leverage and actually start making some form of good money first," invest in the bigger thing, and use your personal brand to fuel it.

**Source:** The Value Creator (A New Internet Career Path For Intelligent People).md

The *one-person business path* (July 2024) is the most complete as a sequence. (1) Start a personal brand as the foundation. (2) Solve your own problems in the real world: health, wealth and relationships. (3) Distribute your findings, opinions and beliefs through writing. (4) Start with a freelance or coaching service and get results. (5) Sell a physical or digital product that requires less of your time once your audience grows. (6) Make "a hefty income in 2 to 4 years" with persistence and iteration. (7) Expand your vision and build whatever you want: software, "spaceships." You do not need a billion-dollar company and should not think about one until you have the cash flow to afford the team. The core: attract an audience by passing down lessons from your progress toward self-generated goals ("that's how you become unique, that's how you become the niche"), then monetize by building a product that solves a problem in your life and selling the solution. And the qualification: if you are improving yourself, you are qualified to start a business, since most people are not improving and need "that better option from a person that they resonate with"; the best education is relating to the teacher. It costs $0 to start thanks to technology and social media.

**Source:** Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md

| Diagram | Date | Starting point | Middle | End |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Self-reliant career path | 2023-04 | Profitable interests | Project → product | Personal distribution center (brand as meta project) |
| Value creator business model | 2023-07 | Content and distribution under your name | Education product for the past self | Target income, then build what you are passionate about |
| Modern career path | 2023-08 | You (topics, interests, curiosities) | Code and content → marketing and sales | Audience → offer → offer stacks |
| One-person business path | 2024-07 | Personal brand | Own problems → writing → service → product | Hefty income in 2–4 years → expand (software, "spaceships") |

The diagrams differ in what they put first (interests, distribution, the person, the brand), but they share a skeleton: a person with interests and problems, a stage where those become something valuable (a project, a system, a skill), a stage of distribution (content, brand, audience), a stage of monetization (offer, product, service), and an open end in which the cash flow and leverage built by the one-person business fund something larger. The 2024 path inserts the service before the product, a sequence that Chapter 29 explains and Chapter 30 turns into a roadmap. The time horizons vary across the corpus (a year to get the hang of it, two to four years for a hefty income, five for $1 million, five to six for Koe's own audience); they are milestones of different things rather than contradictory forecasts, and Chapter 30 gathers them.

#### Choose a career that allows a 4-hour workday

In February 2024 Koe compresses the career advice into a single guideline about leverage, which he defines as "doing the right things": "I'm going to save you a lot of pain: choose a career that allows for a 4-hour workday. Start a one-person business, become a value creator, use the internet to do what you love, learn the skills that allow for this."

**Source:** Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md

The guideline appears in a video about productivity, after a guideline about time blocks, and its placement is the argument. Time management can only improve how a person uses the hours a career demands; it cannot change how many hours that is. The choice of career is the higher-leverage decision, and the 4-Hour Workday of Chapter 12 is only possible inside a career built for it.

#### Seven billion companies

The thesis that the one-person business is the future of work rests, in Koe's telling, on a line by Naval Ravikant that he quotes from 2022 to 2026: "There are almost 7 billion people on this planet; someday I hope there will be almost 7 billion companies." His relation to the line changes over time, and the changes are worth following.

In May 2022 he calls Naval "a huge inspiration for this video" and describes his own reaction: at first he did not understand the line ("not everyone can have a business"), but a year or two after first hearing it he came to believe it can and probably will happen soon, "so might as well just try to take advantage now."

**Source:** If You Want To Secure Your Future… Follow This New Career Path.md

In December 2023 he says the line has "lived in my head rent free" and reads it as the *decentralization of work*: technology lets individuals create their own careers and self-sufficient communities, and he sees this unfolding in the creator economy, with someone selling clothing, others selling knowledge or planners, others delivering home-cooked meals.

**Source:** The Future Of Work Is Play (How To Create A Digital Career).md

In June 2024 he gives it a more idealistic reading. In the creator economy everyone can talk about and do what they want; nature filters people into interests they enjoy, which turn out to be complementary to everyone else's; that is how synergy or unification is created online, by people leaning into their nature, pursuing curiosity and distributing value and goods while AI removes the problems humans do not want to solve. He admits that it is "kind of a pipe dream," but a good vision to hold, because "you don't predict the future, you create it." He also notes that there are caveats.

**Source:** Change Your Life In 365 Hours (The New Rich Focus On These Tasks Daily).md

In July 2024 the line becomes a thesis with a definition attached: the future of work belongs to one-person businesses. "A business is a legal structure that allows you to, one, build what you want; two, solve valuable problems; and three, profit from both." He says this because he is "tired of people thinking that business or entrepreneurship is reserved for people with startup capital or just a certain personality or set of traits." It can be started "with $0 to your name, so there's not really a risk, and if you start and fail, at least you come out with a few extra skills that benefit your future."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md

The definition is the most useful sentence in this sequence, because it shows what "business" means in Koe's vocabulary: a common word given a specific sense. It is not defined by employees, capital, offices or revenue thresholds, but by three functions: building, solving valuable problems, and profiting from both. Under this definition, a person who writes a newsletter and sells a guide is running a business, and the legal structure is merely the wrapper.

In October 2024 he steps back from the strong form. The line is "just speculation for the future, but it seems to be the way things are going." You can have a company or business while working a job. Even in a possible world with universal basic income, "you're still going to want to work, because it's a natural part of life," and then the only option will be to finally pursue your passions.

**Source:** The Death Of The Personal Brand (& The Future Of Creative Work).md

In February 2026 he revisits it in the context of AI. With AI you can learn and build anything, so "every person has the chance to become their own enterprise." Will everyone? "Of course not." He is offering a potential opportunity to those who want it.

**Source:** The Future Of Work (& The New High-Income Skill Stack).md

| Date | Koe's reading of Naval's line | Status he gives it |
|---|---|---|
| 2022-05 | At first not understood; later believed | "Can and probably will happen soon" |
| 2023-12 | Decentralization of work in the creator economy | Unfolding now |
| 2024-06 | Synergy of people leaning into their nature | "Kind of a pipe dream" but a vision to create |
| 2024-07 | The future of work belongs to one-person businesses | Thesis, with a definition of business |
| 2024-10 | Companies alongside jobs; work even under UBI | "Just speculation" |
| 2026-02 | With AI, everyone can be their own enterprise | An opportunity; "of course not" everyone will |

The sequence shows a thesis that grew more confident and then more modest. Two things stay constant: the line is Naval's and Koe always says so, and Koe never claims that everyone will in fact run a company; even the strongest version is about what is possible for an individual. Chapter 20 (Section 20.4) showed the same line in its "future of work is play" form, and Chapter 36 develops the AI context and Koe's later, more detailed analysis of universal basic income.

**Complementary context:** Naval Ravikant is an investor and the co-founder of AngelList, known for a widely shared 2018 Twitter thread, "How to Get Rich (without getting lucky)," whose ideas on specific knowledge, leverage and accountability recur throughout Koe's corpus. The "seven billion companies" line belongs to the same body of aphorisms.

#### The world is going personal

The thesis has a second, historical layer. In May 2022 Koe says that we are losing trust in formal education and corporations and want human connection; everything is going decentralized (his example: YouTube teaches modern skills much faster than colleges). The arc he describes runs in three stages: there used to be individuals within communities; then corporations "came to take this all for myself"; and now it is going back to individuals and communities. "The world is going personal." He calls it inevitable and says he is part of it.

**Source:** If You Want To Secure Your Future… Follow This New Career Path.md

In September 2023 he attributes a related line to Naval ("eventually everybody will be in the creator economy") and adds his own interpretation: the world is shifting from corporate dominance to individual power thanks to technology, a shift reflected in nature by "the principle of division and unity: everything divides and reunites over time," which also "wraps in entropy," the fact that nothing is permanent.

**Source:** Life Is A Video Game (You Can't Escape The Matrix).md

The "principle of division and unity" is Koe's adaptation of a broadly non-dual idea (things separate and reunite) to the economy: the corporation was a unity, and its division into individuals is part of a cycle rather than a final state. The appeal to entropy (Chapter 5) supports the same conclusion from another angle: no arrangement of work is permanent. Both are interpretive frames, offered to make the trend feel natural, not as evidence that it is occurring.

#### Will employment disappear?

Koe's view of employment within this thesis has moved, and the corpus records the movement. In 2023 he said that "we are on the horizon of complete removal" of labor work. In June 2024, answering an objection about megacorporations, he takes a more moderate position: he does not see employment going away, but he sees work decentralizing further. Corporations have executives, leaders and employees post on social media and become creators, to add authority to the company; creators who grow larger will have other creators under them as contractors; corporations hire more contractors. Work is going "more remote, more contract-based" as AI and technology make this more resourceful for companies and individuals. "Idea workers thrive in the digital age": digital real estate can be built at zero cost, digital products can be built and distributed at low marginal cost, and digital communication reaches anyone online. The *Digital Society* ("also called the creator economy at times") is where new fortunes are made.

**Source:** Ideas Are The New Oil (How More People Get Rich In The Digital Age).md

In February 2025 the position sharpens again, in a different direction. "The jobs of the future will be reserved for the elite, the top 1%, the NBA of jobs. The entry level is going extinct, because replaceable work is replaceable." So "the main option ... for those with high ambition and multiple interests is to create your own path, and the only way to do that is by becoming an entrepreneur, no matter how long it takes for your dreams to gain traction." Since you will explore your interests anyway, "you might as well try to make it all work in some entrepreneurial endeavor as your vessel for your multiple interests," in a way that lets you sustain and control your lifestyle.

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

The three positions are not simply contradictory. The first concerns a long horizon of labor in general; the second concerns how corporations are adapting in the near term; the third concerns the entry level specifically, under pressure from AI, and the "NBA of jobs" image (a small number of elite positions, as in professional basketball) describes what remains. What changes is the emphasis: from disappearance, to decentralization, to elitization. Koe gives a reason only for the last (AI and automation turn entry-level work into senior work). The consequence he draws is constant across all three: for someone ambitious with many interests, a business of one's own is the vessel. Chapter 36 (Section 36.3) examines the claim that replicable work is being replaced.

#### The consume-and-save mindset

The last piece of the thesis is a diagnosis of why people do not see the opportunity. In June 2024 Koe says that most people do not know what to do to generate new wealth, "but you do know, because you're looking at it right now" (the digital space where the viewer is watching the video), yet the *consume and save mindset* limits how you perceive "the gold rush that is happening right in front of you." "All new fortunes are being built in the digital space," and digital real estate "will be referenced 20 years from now as the method this generation used to generate wealth."

**Source:** Ideas Are The New Oil (How More People Get Rich In The Digital Age).md

The consume-and-save mindset is the financial program of the default path (Chapters 2 and 34): work for a salary, spend some, save the rest. It is a filter in the sense of Chapter 3, and like every filter it makes some things invisible: from inside it, the digital space is a place to consume, not a place to build. The forecast about "20 years from now" is Koe's prediction, stated with confidence; its role here is the same as the "full circle" image, to make the opportunity feel historical rather than speculative.

### 28.5 Audience Plus Product

#### The oldest structure of business

The previous sections described why to build a business and what kind. This section asks what a business minimally *is*. Koe's answer is the same in dozens of formulations between 2022 and 2026: a business is people plus a product, traffic plus an offer, an audience plus a way to monetize it. The formulations differ in vocabulary and in the details they add, and those differences carry most of what Koe teaches about how the two halves interact.

In June 2023, presenting the third "pillar" of the 4-Hour Workday (the compounding nature of readership growth), he states the definition: "business online and offline requires people and a product, or traffic and an offer." People come from a few sources: ads; geographic location (the foot traffic of a physical small business); manual outreach (cold calls, cold emails); or creating content to grow a readership in "the new digital society," the creator economy. Most businesses, he says, are still locked in the old paradigm of having to focus every day on getting customers, and they neglect social media "as a necessary part of life and as the foundation of modern business." Every business will have to be on social media, and "readership growth compounds"; people still do not realize this.

**Source:** The 4 Hour Workday (How Creatives Work Less & Earn More).md

In August 2024 he denies that any of this is new. He speaks "under the lens of building an audience, because this is a new traffic mechanism for businesses," but "building an audience is just how you build a business. It's always been how you build a business, with media and advertising" (radio, television, now social media). Most attention is now on social media, which is "such a blessing for individuals who understand that they can just write content and build an audience and build a business that way." What sparked his idea that "you are the niche," he says, was seeing that "many people are operating from 20 to 30-year-old business mindsets and trying to apply that to the modern social media age." As one person you can build an audience and a hyper-profitable business without boxing yourself into a specific niche.

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

The two passages make the same point from two directions. The definition is ancient: every business that ever existed needed customers and something to sell them. What is new is the *traffic mechanism*, the channel through which people are brought to the product, and the error Koe diagnoses is using old mechanisms (location, cold outreach, paid placement alone) or old mindsets (a narrow niche chosen from a market analysis) in an environment where attention has moved to platforms an individual can access for free.

He adds a piece of external evidence in September 2024. On business social media or in an occasional episode of *Shark Tank*, "the first thing that comes out of their mouth is build an audience." He had just finished *Rework* by Jason Fried and David Heinemeier Hansson of 37signals ("pretty cool people on Twitter ... I like them a lot"). "The world is changing; people are finally getting on board with the fact that an audience is a crucial asset for any kind of business you build." He does not say what *Rework* argues beyond this.

**Source:** The Fastest Way To Build A One-Person Business (Beginner Guide).md

**Complementary context:** *Rework* (2010) is a short book of business essays by the founders of 37signals, the company behind Basecamp; one of its chapters, "Build an audience," argues that companies should cultivate a following by sharing what they know rather than buying attention. *Shark Tank* is the American television series in which entrepreneurs pitch investors. Koe cites both as signs of consensus, not as sources of his argument.

#### Why the audience is not optional

Koe gives three reasons why an audience is a necessity rather than an add-on, each from a different angle.

The first concerns the end of employment as a source of customers. In October 2024: the internet is how you attract an audience to your life's work, and an audience, large or small ("small being enough to make more than enough money to be fulfilled"), "is not optional," because you are no longer working for a company that attracts the audience for you while you play the specific, replaceable role you were hired for. In a job, someone else does the marketing; in your own business, nobody does it unless you do.

**Source:** The Death Of The Personal Brand (& The Future Of Creative Work).md

The second concerns leverage. In June 2024: the first piece of a business is people who want to pay you (customers, also known as an audience), and the reason to build an audience rather than simply find clients is that "you want leverage," not "a second 9-to-5 with client work" (the freelancing or agency model). You want to create what you want, control your day and make sales while you sleep, and the most accessible way to do that is an audience on social media, where the attention is: "new media, decentralized media."

**Source:** Ideas Are The New Oil (How More People Get Rich In The Digital Age).md

The third concerns testing. The audience "doesn't have to come from social media"; it can come from paid ads, podcast sponsorships and other channels. But social media is "the free and readily accessible route" that should form the foundation of the rest, "because you can test content on social media before you decide to blow money on ads with it."

**Source:** The Death Of The Personal Brand (& The Future Of Creative Work).md

The third reason marks a refinement in Koe's view of paid advertising. In 2021 and 2022 he and Justin Welsh treated ads as not worth splitting focus over, and Koe described building an audience as a way to avoid "throwing money into a furnace" by testing ideas with ads. From 2024 he presents ads as a later stage: steroids after "natural bodybuilding," something a scaling software company eventually needs, and something organic posts make profitable by validating the angles first. The 2024 sentence above sits at that point: ads are not rejected, they are sequenced after free validation. The two positions apply to different situations: a beginner without capital, and a business with validated angles and money to spend.

#### Principles, not business models

If every business is audience plus product, then choosing a "business model" is a secondary question. In August 2024 Koe says so directly. You do not need a fancy business model. Stop thinking about starting an agency or an e-commerce store, and start thinking about business principles: "you need an audience and a way to monetize that audience; that's it." You get an audience, you have a product, you give the product to the audience: that is what an e-commerce store, an agency, a personal brand and "literally any business on this Earth" is. Technology created a new type of media; media is how you build, attract or target an audience; social media is where most attention is. You need attention to make money, or to attract an audience that buys. A startup without users makes no money.

**Source:** Minimalist Productivity Work Less, Do More.md

In February 2025 he expands the principle into three parts. "A better route aside from pursuing those models is to zoom out a layer and to view the principles that make all of those models work, because once you understand those, you can create success in whatever you want to do, and you don't have to put a label on it." To do your own thing you need (1) a valuable product or service, (2) a way to attract people who care, and (3) the skill to make people care. "Thanks to the internet, all of these are accessible to those with an internet connection."

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

The 2025 version is not a different formula but a more precise one. The first two parts are the familiar product and audience; the third, the skill to make people care, names what connects them. Attracting people who *could* care is distribution; making them *actually* care is persuasion, the subject of Chapter 32. Earlier formulations contained it implicitly; this one makes it a separate requirement. The phrase *zoom out a layer* is Koe's name for the move from models to principles, and it applies the thesis of Chapter 15 (principles over tactics) to business: a person who understands why all models work does not need to pick one.

In March 2023, he defines from the opposite side what counts as a business at all. The best business model of 2023 is "an actual business model": not a side hustle, not online surveys paying five cents per survey, not investing your weekly allowance in crypto. A business has to generate revenue; you need cash flow. And "a business has the ability to scale, pivot and position itself in the market regardless of algorithm changes and trends."

**Source:** The Best Online Business To Make $1 Million In 3-5 Years.md

The *actual business model* (his phrase) is a definition by contrast. What separates a business from a hustle is not size but three capacities: generating cash flow, scaling, and surviving changes outside its control. The last criterion explains why Koe insists on owned products and owned audiences: a business that depends on one platform's algorithm or one advertiser's payments cannot pivot or position itself when those change.

#### Code and content: the back end and the front end

In August 2023 Koe gives the principle a technological form. "Code is the back end of the internet; content is the front end. Combine both and you have people and a product. Send people to product and you have profit." You can learn to code, which is very valuable, but unless it is your passion you are better off learning content and building with what coders have already built (website builders, social platforms) to distribute it. Content requires self-understanding: you must capture attention, hold attention and deliver value to that attention.

**Source:** You Aren't Successful Because You Care Too Much (How To Stop).md

The formula maps the two halves of business onto the two layers of the internet. Code hosts the product; content attracts the people. Koe's position on whether one needs to learn to code oscillates across the corpus: "you don't need to learn to code" (2023); after Balaji Srinivasan, learning both code and content "is not optional" (late 2023); "content route, code route, or both" (the December 2023 diagram above); and, from 2026, "media matters more than code," since AI has made code accessible while the value of content remains subjective. The August 2023 version is the moderate one: learn content unless coding is your passion, and use the tools coders have built. The consistent element is the priority of media for beginners, which Chapter 30 (Section 30.3) develops.

#### The two levers of the New Rich

In June 2024 Koe presents audience and product as the "levers of the new rich," the two priorities for anyone seeking independence. The first is the audience: building one removes your dependency on your employer, the government and "any centralized entity controlling most aspects of your life." He addresses the obvious objection, that social media is itself centralized. His answer: there is no real solution yet for building an audience outside it except an email list, which is why a newsletter matters; it transfers your audience off the platform so you cannot be deplatformed. The second is the product: building one so that people give you money in exchange for something that benefits their lives, which is "the only way to take control of your income and stop relying on anyone else to make you money." He maps the two levers to his own courses, one on writing and one on monetization.

**Source:** Change Your Life In 365 Hours (The New Rich Focus On These Tasks Daily).md

The pair is framed here in terms of sovereignty rather than revenue. The audience removes one dependency (on whoever controls your access to people), and the product removes another (on whoever controls your income). The honest concession about centralization is typical of Koe's treatment of platforms (Chapter 25 recorded his shifting judgment of social media): the newsletter is not a complete answer, but it is the best available.

#### Money requires people and a product

In August 2023 the product appears as step three of a sequence whose first two steps are writing (to grow an audience) and speaking (to nurture it). If you are not growing an audience, Koe says, zoom out and realize "it's a skill issue": you need a big-picture understanding of social media to get eyes on good content. "Money requires people and a product" (products include services); it is the only way to make money. You can try to live off platform ad revenue, but "you will be a slave to the algorithm." Creating your own product or service takes control of your income, and then "social media as a skill becomes a faucet that you can turn on whenever you want to make more money." He refers viewers to his videos on the one-person business model and its 2.0 version, the minimum viable offer, and "how to create a product."

**Source:** You Aren't Successful Because You Care Too Much (How To Stop).md

The *faucet* image explains why the product half is not optional for a creator who already has an audience. An audience without a product earns only what the platform decides to pay, on the platform's terms. An audience with a product earns what the creator decides to sell, whenever the creator chooses to promote it. *Skill issue* (Koe's recurring phrase) reframes a stalled audience as a problem of competence that can be solved, not as bad luck; Section 28.6 returns to it.

#### It's all traffic and offers

The earliest and most fully developed version of the principle comes from a November 2022 video, "11 Lessons From Growing A 7-Figure One Person Business," whose first lesson is "it's all traffic and offers." Business is a game of creating a good product or service and putting it in front of the right people. Without traffic (people viewing it, social shares, growth, an audience, ads, any media that brings people to your product), nobody buys. With a lot of traffic but no way to monetize it, you will not make money either; his example is an Instagram theme page with a million uninterested followers and no marketing or sales knowledge. Marketing, promotion strategy and funnels are tactics serving the principle of traffic and offers. "Principles are a must and come first"; studying funnels does not matter if you do not have traffic and a good offer. You need to understand the principles, and the place where you will apply them (social media), so well that you could do it with zero experience.

**Source:** 11 Lessons From Growing A 7-Figure One Person Business.md

In March 2023 he restates it as a universal principle: a business has an offer and a traffic source; "offer equals value and traffic equals people." If you have no offer and do not put it in front of people, you will not sell; if you have an audience but no offer, you will not make money. And the same principle applies beyond business. In relationships, if you are a *valuable offer* and you put yourself among desirable people (not people you do not care to form relationships with), you match the two; if you go into a bar scene full of people but you are not a valuable offer, "you won't sell or convert." In the same passage he adds that "we need traffic before we need an offer."

**Source:** The Best Online Business To Make $1 Million In 3-5 Years.md

A few weeks later he develops the dating parallel into an order of operations. The fundamentals of starting a business are traffic and offers, and the same holds for dating: if you lack a product or service, or the value to create one, or the education in what goes into one, then in dating terms you need to cultivate your value. That is why dating and relationships usually come last, after solving health and other problems. Once you have an offer, a product, a service, or are "a valuable being," you need people: traffic to send to it. Aside from creating an offer, the business actions that move the lever are building distribution of some form; social media is a good way, so the fundamental actions include replying to larger accounts, "the basic social media advice that no one listens to but is talked about in every single course."

**Source:** Your Future Will Be Determined By 3 Things.md

The relationship analogy is a metaphor, but in Koe's hands it does real explanatory work. It shows that "offer" means value in general, not a commercial package; that the two halves are independent (a valuable person in an empty room and an unremarkable person in a crowded bar both fail); and that the order of work follows from it: develop the value first, then find the people. That order seems to contradict the March 2023 sentence "we need traffic before we need an offer," and the corpus records the tension. In the same video Koe recommends starting with a tutoring service sold through direct outreach before having an audience, "not banking on the audience"; elsewhere he advises monetizing from the first follower, while yet another passage places becoming an authority before the first digital product. He never resolves the contradiction explicitly. The most coherent reading, which his own practice supports, separates the principle from the tactic: in principle both halves are necessary and traffic is the scarcer asset for a beginner, but tactically one can start with a small offer and direct outreach, building the audience while selling. The subsection on monetizing from the start, below, presents Koe's arguments for the second half of that reading.

#### The affiliate thread: traffic and offers as arithmetic

To show how far the principle can go with zero experience, the November 2022 video includes a hypothetical. An affiliate earns a commission for selling someone else's product: the WordPress or Elementor affiliate programs pay about 30%; a digital course can usually pay 50%. Suppose you start a Twitter account, write a valuable, attention-grabbing thread on web design fundamentals, and put a 50% affiliate link to a web design course at the end. You then pay someone $25 to $50 to retweet it; social media marketers and agencies do this constantly, because they pay for other people's traffic, and "an audience is traffic." (On Instagram you could pay for a story share, with your profile as the offer.) Pay $100 for four retweets from accounts with 100,000 followers each: a potential 400,000 people, plus second-order retweets, say a million impressions. Assume 30% read to the bottom (300,000 people), some fraction clicks, about 1,000 reach the landing page, and 5% convert. That is 50 buyers of a $99 course at a 50% commission, about $2,500. Spend $100, make $2,500: $2,400 of profit "from doing absolutely nothing but understanding traffic and offers."

**Source:** 11 Lessons From Growing A 7-Figure One Person Business.md

The numbers are illustrative, and Koe admits in the passage that he is not sure of real conversion rates. A few minutes later he adds the essential qualification: you cannot write one thread and expect $2,400; the result requires *systemized promotion*, done repeatedly. The example's value is structural rather than predictive. It separates the two variables completely (the offer belongs to someone else; the traffic is bought from someone else) and shows that the person in the middle earns money only by understanding how to connect them.

#### Product and people: the two moving pieces

In March 2024 Koe states the principle in terms of value. Even a person with every trait he has just listed would find that "your skill set is [worthless] if nobody knows or cares about it." (The transcript says "invaluable," which in context clearly means "valueless.") There are two problems: "if nobody knows what you create, it isn't valuable"; "if nobody cares about your creation, it isn't valuable." In business "you need a product and a group of people that care about the product enough to buy it." You can put the product in front of people through written content, paid ads, newsletter sponsorships, podcast appearances and so on, but "if the people can't see why the product benefits their lives, they're not going to buy it." And you can believe your creation is "the most valuable thing on the market," but if you do not put it in front of people nobody buys, "because nobody's there." So "there's two moving pieces to the puzzle: it's product and people," and lacking either means no money.

**Source:** Working Hard Won't Make You Rich (Do This Instead).md

The dating analogy returns in the same video, with an added element. You can be "the most developed individual in the world," but if you sit in your room all day you will not meet a partner (product without people). If you are "a smelly, trashy person" and go where there are many people, they will not want to meet you, "simply because you haven't developed yourself" (people without product). And even a very developed person will be perceived by people of other identities as someone they do not want to talk to. The conclusion: "you have to be both the right place, the right time and the right person."

**Source:** Working Hard Won't Make You Rich (Do This Instead).md

The added element is perception. Value is not only something you have but something others perceive through their own identity (the thesis Chapter 31 develops as "value is perception"). Being the right person is not enough if you are in front of the wrong people; the right place and time are part of the offer.

The origin of this line of thought is a third party. In June 2022 Koe cites JK Molina: "if your offer sucks, then it doesn't matter how much traffic you send to it." Koe extends it with the same dating image (if you suck as a person and put yourself where there are many people, you will not get dates) and adds a point about luck: if the offer is very good and you send a lot of traffic to it, you open yourself to more luck. You need people to increase your chances of luck. "When in doubt, build more leverage": get your name in front of more people, network, build an audience, develop your skill set.

**Source:** 7 Lessons From 7 Failed Online Businesses (And Making 7 Figures).md

The sentence about the offer is Molina's; the dating extension and the reading of traffic as exposure to luck are Koe's. The luck point is easy to miss and important: traffic does not guarantee sales, but it increases the number of chances for a good offer to meet the right person, which is why Koe treats audience-building as a probabilistic investment rather than a transaction.

The lesson just before it in the same video makes the numbers point explicit. People trying to land clients send five to ten DMs, make no money, call the method nonsense and quit, and Koe admits he did the same. "It's a numbers game." You can step up your marketing and sales knowledge to be more effective, but it is about getting in front of the right people: you have your offer and you have traffic. Shoot your shot, then shoot again.

**Source:** 7 Lessons From 7 Failed Online Businesses (And Making 7 Figures).md

In January 2024 he shows that the numbers required are smaller than people imagine. If you sell two $150 digital products a day, you make six figures a year in revenue. That requires about 80 people a day reaching a landing page that converts at 2.5%. With an X or Twitter thread promoting the product at the bottom, you would need around 10,000 impressions. Even with 1,000 followers, if a friend with 10,000 followers shares a persuasive thread, Koe says he "can almost guarantee" two sales a day. And even without the sales, you are building an audience that may buy later.

**Source:** The 7 Best Internet Business Models (From Zero Experience To Advanced).md

The arithmetic is approximately consistent: two sales of $150 a day is $300 a day, roughly $110,000 a year; 2.5% of 80 visitors is two buyers. The conversion rate and impression estimates are Koe's working assumptions, not reported data, and the "almost guarantee" is his confidence rather than a measured outcome. The point of the calculation is the scale it reveals: a six-figure business does not require a mass audience, only a reliable connection between a modest flow of people and a good offer.

#### Something to sell and someone to buy

In August 2025 Koe applies his habit of breaking things down to money itself. "In order to make money, what do you need? Something to sell and someone to buy it." Start with the purpose of the activity and work backward. If you have no idea what to sell, search for specific knowledge on how to come up with a profitable product idea (he links his own guide), then build the product. Many creators and businesses he has worked with had a product idea and, a year later, "I didn't even start building it." "You don't have the chance to make any money if you do not have a product or service to sell." If you have a product but no buyers, research how to generate traffic: a personal brand, social media content, SEO, cold outreach, Facebook ads. "All of them work. Some are better than others only in the context of your specific situation": with no money, Facebook ads may be unwise; for a beginner, probably social media and a personal brand, where you spend nothing and success depends entirely on skill.

**Source:** How To Master Any Skill Fast (Give Yourself 2 Weeks).md

The passage applies the goal-first method of Chapter 8 to money: define what the goal requires, find which half is missing, and research that half specifically. Its pluralism about traffic sources is worth noting. Koe recommends social media for beginners not because other channels fail but because, for someone with skill and no capital, it is the channel whose cost is paid in skill rather than money.

In September 2023 he gives the same structure as three *applications* to study, which he distinguishes from skills: "the three applications, not necessarily skills, that we need to study are social media, offer creation and traffic generation." These are the channels through which skills are learned and directed toward building something of your own: "you must build a brand, offer, and practice driving traffic between the two; that's how you make money." When you learn skills, you "filter the information through what you are building and what you are applying them to." (The skills themselves are the subject of his "one million dollar skill stack" videos, which Chapter 35 covers.)

**Source:** How To Reprogram Your Mind & Build Your Intelligence.md

The distinction between applications and skills is the goal-as-lens principle of Chapter 6 applied to learning for business. Copywriting, design or video editing are skills; building a brand, an offer and the traffic between them is the project that tells you which skills to learn and how deeply.

In August 2023 he names the product-plus-people structure *evergreen vessels*, the second layer after the "evergreen skills" (communicating value and getting what you want). Most people fail to make money because they do not understand how business works: you need a good product, which takes a host of skills to make, and you need people sent to it. And the evergreen skills operate at every stage. People purchase because (1) you led them to the product with writing, speaking, marketing and sales; (2) when they saw the product, what they saw was writing, speaking, marketing and sales; and (3) once they are inside, sparking *positive behavior change* so that they actually use it is also writing, speaking, marketing and sales.

**Source:** I Had To Learn These High-Income Skills (If I Wanted To Make Money).md

The third stage is the one people forget. Persuasion does not end at the sale; a product that changes nothing in the buyer's behavior has failed, and getting someone to implement what they bought is a communication problem of the same kind as getting them to buy. Chapter 31 makes positive behavior change the definition of value.

#### Build an audience, monetize an audience: when to start selling

The minimal definition raises a practical question: should the audience come before any selling, or should one sell from the start? Koe's answer, in most of the corpus, is to sell from the start, and he gives several reasons.

In July 2024, presenting the third pillar of a business (the offer), he says: brand attracts, content gets followers, offer monetizes. "That's what business is, if you strip it down to exactly what it is: build an audience, monetize an audience. Doesn't matter if it's your creator [audience] or Facebook ads; that's an audience." Then: "No, you don't need to start waiting in order to start monetizing ... it's just a gatekeeper mindset. You don't need to wait; you need something to iterate on." If you have no product or service, sell something "no matter how bad," even a curation: "if you're selling 50 of my favorite quotes for $10 in an e-book, do that, so at least you can improve on it." "Your products will suck; every single first iteration of anything will suck until you improve it, because you can only spot problems when there is actually a problem in real life; otherwise you're just solving fake problems that don't exist."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md

The *gatekeeper mindset* (Koe's term) is the belief that you need some amount of experience or followers before you are permitted to charge. Its antidote rests on an epistemic argument already present in Section 28.2: real problems only become visible in contact with reality. A product that does not exist cannot fail in instructive ways; a bad product in the market generates exactly the information needed to make it better. The *fake problems* a person solves while waiting are the business equivalent of tutorial hell (Chapter 14).

In December 2023 he puts the same position in terms of authority. Two routes are commonly recommended: do not monetize at the start, or monetize at the start. Koe is in the second camp, monetizing on social media from the start without followers, because you need a way to build authority. Those who say "build something off social media first, then come on and monetize" do not realize that social media "is a perfectly viable way to build authority and wealth in today's world. It's not that you need experience before you come on social media; it's that you build experience on social media." It did not use to be this way, but social media is "arguably the most viable way to start a business nowadays."

**Source:** How Intelligent Creators Will Make Money In 2024 (The Build Teach Earn Method).md

A February 2024 passage gives the reason in terms of data. The crucial next step after creating value is to turn it into internet content and post it, "because you need data" to know whether your ideas are truly valuable or perceived as valuable. "You need to put your value on the public market." You can write like Alan Watts or a philosopher, but if you are not growing or getting engagement, and therefore not making sales, it is not sustainable as a full-time pursuit. You need a balance of internal and external: "play the game, the external game, from a place of 'I want to give value.'"

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The *public market* is Koe's term for the place where value is tested by others' responses. The Alan Watts example (Watts is one of Koe's favorite thinkers, used here as a stylistic ideal that does not by itself produce a living) contrasts the inner game of creating with the outer game of being perceived. Neither is sufficient: inner value without an outer test cannot be known to be valuable; outer success without inner value is not what Koe recommends. The phrase "from a place of I want to give value" is the condition that keeps the outer game from becoming manipulation.

Finally, in December 2023 he gives a heuristic for what to sell first. "Start small. Don't try to create some insane offer that you can charge $10,000 to $20,000 for." (The figure is garbled in the transcript; the sense is a very high-ticket offer.) Figure out what you have become best at and do not overcomplicate it: if you got results with threads or long-form posts, build a product or service around that; if you are great at profile and content design, around that; if your productivity system gives you off-the-charts focus, around that. "If you've done it and it's helped your business, that means it can help someone else who struggles in that area." The main obstacle will be marketing and sales, creating the perception of the offer. And selling is how you learn marketing: it is not that marketing comes first; you put something out ("not terrible in terms of quality," but likely to get few buyers at first because of its marketing) and fill the gaps in your marketing message until sales come. You get better at social media the same way, by experimenting with techniques (thread writing, post structures, mastermind groups) until you get results. "Sell the technique, not the skill."

**Source:** How Intelligent Creators Will Make Money In 2024 (The Build Teach Earn Method).md

*Sell the technique, not the skill* is a precise piece of advice. A skill (writing, design, productivity) is broad and hard to package; a technique (a thread structure, a profile design method, a planning system) is a specific way of getting a specific result, and it can be taught, named and sold. It anticipates the unique mechanism of Chapter 27 and the "systems are the new product" thesis of Chapter 33.

The four passages above are consistent with each other and with the rest of Koe's practice (he launched his first product with about 500 followers). They are in tension with another passage, outside this chapter's material, which places "become an authority" before launching a digital product, and with the "traffic before offer" sentence discussed above. The reconciliation the corpus supports is that authority is built *while* selling something small: the first product is a learning instrument, and a later, data-driven product is built on what the first one revealed. Chapter 29 develops the minimum viable offer and the permission to launch something imperfect.

#### The core sequence, in its versions

Koe summarizes his model many times as a short sequence, and the versions are close enough to be read as one sequence with variations. "Business and entrepreneurship have always been the same: build an audience, monetize the audience," he says in July 2024, and the recap that follows is the most complete: "you make money whenever you want by creating a product or service based on a problem you solved for yourself, building an audience where the attention is right now, the internet, monetizing the audience by offering your product or service in a persuasive way." The steps: (1) "achieve a self-generated goal by creating a system"; (2) "focus on health, wealth, relationships and happiness"; (3) "turn that system into a product or service that can help someone with your same problem," because "if you don't have a product, you don't have a business"; (4) "build an audience by writing to yourself, so you can attract people who share your same vision and thus same problems, so they see your system as valuable and purchase it." What to write: "your opinions, beliefs and mindset; teach your interests and skills; share your favorite ideas; show people how you solve your problems; share your vision so others can resonate." (5) "Then iterate, iterate, iterate. Don't get trapped in client work. Don't get trapped with one product. Keep solving problems as they arise in your life and business. Never stop growing and evolving. You'll figure out what to do next."

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md

The other versions vary in what they emphasize.

In March 2024 the emphasis is on time. "Nobody is going to give you the time; you must take it." Start with one hour every morning "before the distractions wake up." Solve a problem in your life that births a profitable solution; build an audience by distributing value with writing and content; turn your solution into a product and iterate until you make as much as you want. Do not rely on a paycheck for survival: "transcend your survival through entrepreneurship and remove the earning limit from your life."

**Source:** The Value Equation How To Become A High-Status Individual.md

The hour before distractions is the one-hour start of Chapter 12; *remove the earning limit* names the difference between a salary, which is capped by an employer, and a business, which is capped only by the levers of Section 28.6.

In August 2024 the emphasis is on channels. To use what he calls the meta business model: (1) study social media; (2) write content on platforms such as X or LinkedIn to build a quality audience (these are only examples); (3) start a newsletter to keep and nurture the audience with longer, trust-building writing, and optionally repurpose it to every other platform. (The video itself started as a newsletter; "if you want to start on YouTube, writing is how you start"; if you show up on camera not knowing what to say, it is because you did not write it first.) (4) As the audience grows, launch digital products and services (courses, templates, freelance work, coaching) that build cash flow without spending your life savings, because they are free to create. (5) Promote your product or service to your audience in a systemized fashion. He mentions that Cortex, his software company, was built without venture capital thanks to the audience and the cash flow of this kind of business.

**Source:** Minimalist Productivity Work Less, Do More.md

In September 2024 the emphasis is on psychology and persistence, in a recap Koe calls "how to make as much money as you want." (1) "Understand your own mind so you can market to someone that is similar to you." (2) "Turn the skills, knowledge and interests that benefit your life into an education product." (3) "Distribute that product to an audience that you build with content and writing." (4) "Create a weekly system to ensure that your audience sees your product." (5) "The last, final and most important step is to iterate and persist." The education product is "for beginners," "anyone can start that," "you can create an education product out of anything." "That's all you need to make as much money as you want, and that is not a lie."

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md

In October 2024 the emphasis is on the life's work. Asked how one pursues one's life's work on the internet, Koe gives four requirements: (1) a *positive aim* for your life (improving, developing, gaining value), a phrase the corpus elsewhere traces to Jordan Peterson; (2) a way to attract an audience to that value; (3) a project turned into a product, a way to actually make money; (4) experimentation, iteration and persistence. He recaps the chain: "you have a positive direction, you're making progress, you're becoming valuable, you have an audience that you're attracting, you have a product, so you have a way to actually make money."

**Source:** The Death Of The Personal Brand (& The Future Of Creative Work).md

In March 2024 the emphasis is on the obsession that Chapter 10 described. After going deep, "we have to build up." The fourth step is *experiment upward*: "testing persistence and iteration in building something of value with the depth that you learn ... you get the value and then you distribute it so you can profit from it." The assumption is that once you have found your obsession, you want to make it a sustainable part of life, so you must earn a living from it. "In today's world that means": building a project to help others solve their problems; teaching in public to attract people with those problems; selling your project so you can continue pursuing your obsession; improving the project "as soon as you learn what can only be learned through feedback"; and evolving to a new project "when you've reached the level that can only be reached through business." "There are many ways to do this; that's why you have to experiment."

**Source:** If Your Life Is Spiraling Out Of Control, Here's The Solution To Feeling Lost.md

Teaching in public, which this version requires, sits in apparent tension with other advice in the corpus not to announce one's goals because announcing them gives the dopamine of having achieved them. Koe's own distinction resolves it: telling people your goals for approval is one thing; showing people how what you are learning can benefit them is another.

In June 2026 the emphasis shifts to interests and to AI, in what Koe calls the *learner's business*, for people without capital who do not want to fundraise and want something creative and meaningful. (1) Study your interests. "They are inherently valuable because you value them, meaning someone else can value them too. And many already value them. And no, people can't just ask AI, because they often don't know what to ask." (2) Learn how to package them, "because you aren't Marcus Aurelius. Writing fortune-cookie tweets isn't going to get you anywhere. You must understand attention mechanics and engagement psychology. And now that you know what to ask AI, research that." (3) Weave in your unique voice, "because your audience doesn't care if you have new ideas. They want your opinion, your point of view on an idea that works." (4) Turn yourself into the business: "Your brand, content and product all stem from your interests [and] the transformation they can provide."

**Source:** If You Have Multiple Interests, Start A One-Person Business.md

The learner's business marks a change of emphasis, not of structure. The 2022–2024 versions start from a problem you solved; the 2026 version starts from interests you study, and it answers the objection that AI makes studied knowledge worthless: people need someone to tell them what to ask. The Marcus Aurelius line is a precise criticism. A famous philosopher's short sayings are valuable because of who said them; an unknown writer's aphorisms (the "fortune-cookie tweets") have no such backing, so the unknown writer must earn attention through structure and engagement (Chapter 23).

Finally, a 2022 self-promotion shows the same sequence as a product. Koe describes the method of his program *Digital Economics* as "productizing yourself" through systems, with a Notion "Command Center" of plug-and-play templates: create your brand, write content, systemize it, create a product, create a service, make it profitable from the start with zero experience, and promote and automate the promotion for consistent sales. In short: grow your brand with content, lead people into your product or service, then automate or systemize it down to two to four hours a day so you can continue to grow and monetize.

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md

What changes is where Koe places the starting point, which tracks the evolution of his thought from problem-solving toward interests and from self-improvement toward learning.

### 28.6 The Pillars and the Levers

#### From two halves to several pillars

Section 28.5 reduced a business to two halves. When Koe describes how a one-person business is actually built, he divides it further, into *pillars*: brand, content, product, and in many versions a fourth, variously called promotion, promotions, marketing, monetization or offer. The pillars are not a different theory from "audience plus product"; brand and content are the two parts of the audience side (who attracts people, and what attracts them), product is the offer, and the fourth pillar is the act of connecting them. What makes the pillars worth a section is that Koe formulated them many times, with different metaphors, and that the metaphors carry different lessons. Chapter 19 (Section 19.2) presented one early version (goals become the brand, problems the content, systems the product, benefits the marketing) as the architecture of "you are the niche." This section gathers the others.

#### The macronutrients of business

In November 2023 Koe gives the pillars a nutritional analogy. In nutrition there are four macronutrients: fat, carbohydrates, protein and alcohol. "In business there are four pillars that will determine your success, the macronutrients of business, which is brand, content, product, promotions." He then breaks each down in terms of the self: brand is your character, content is your mindset and skill set, product is how you achieve a meaningful goal, and promotions are why you achieve it.

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

The analogy has a second level. In nutrition, a plethora of micronutrients are vital for bodily function; in business, "there are skills, interests, experience, knowledge, creativity and personality that determine the potency of your efforts." "Your job is to fuel your business with the proper Creative Nutrition to succeed." The *micronutrients* (in this sense; the corpus also uses the word for the complementary elements of value in Chapter 31) are the person's raw material: the same four pillars built by two different people will have different potency because what feeds them differs.

The transcript lists the four macronutrients and the four pillars in the same order but does not map them one to one; the only explicit correspondence is that content is the carbohydrates. It appears in a third metaphor, the *business keto diet*. Just as a ketogenic diet removes carbohydrates, the business keto diet removes content and relies only on ads (the direct response marketing route). Koe advises against it. It is "clearly a very viable way of getting customers," but the benefits of content make an ads-only approach unreasonable, "especially for beginners without money to invest in making mistakes to learn advertising." A daily writing habit gives four things the keto business lacks: (1) "you can validate high-performing ideas as content to use in your ads or promotions"; (2) you keep the audience you acquire; (3) you are not always burning money to acquire new customers; and (4) you create customers over time by writing content for all *levels of awareness* (the stages of a prospect's awareness of their problem and its solution, from Eugene Schwartz, which Chapter 32 explains).

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

The keto metaphor is useful because it states the trade-off fairly. Ads are not rejected; they are a viable diet for someone who can afford the mistakes of learning them. Content is the cheaper and more durable energy source for someone who cannot, and, as Section 28.5 showed, it also generates the data that later makes ads profitable.

In the same video Koe maps the four pillars onto the person under the name *mental monetization*: "let's turn yourself into the business, or understand mental monetization: brand is your character; content is your mindset and skill set; product is how you achieve a meaningful goal; promotions are why you achieve the meaningful goal." The term (which the corpus also uses, in other years, for related ideas and as a candidate product name) designates this specific move: reading every part of a business as a part of the self.

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

#### What, how and why

The formulation Koe repeats most often comes from December 2022 and reappears, with additions, in the February 2024 compilation. "Brand is what you do, product is how you do it, marketing is why you do it. Do something that improves your quality of life, teach others how you did it, and show them why they should care."

**Source:** You Have A $100,000 Product In Your Head (One Person Business Series).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

He develops each term. The brand has a goal toward which it leads people; your brand is what you do every day to move toward that goal. The product is how you do it: the system, method or process you used to get better results for yourself, or something you built to do what you do better; it comes from experience and experimentation. Marketing is the why, and if you built the product for yourself, "the marketing is laid out for you," because you could market it to yourself; the why "plays itself out for someone similar to you." The video's graphic puts at the top the knowledge and experience (the valuable information) acquired through the journey of self-actualization, with a precise qualification: "it isn't once you self-actualize, it's along the way," and documenting it as you go. That information is broken down into brand, product and marketing (what, how, why), which together equal a one-person business, or *self-monetization*.

The 2022 video then defines the term. "Self-monetization is just a way of framing your knowledge and experiences online in the form of brand, product, content, with consistent effort to put your name out there and build your own public resume." Koe clarifies that he is not talking about B2B info products but about productizing yourself: treating yourself as the niche, writing to yourself, building for yourself, and monetizing that way. Traditional marketers dislike it, he says, but his results, and the established players who ask him how he does it, show that it need not be as complicated as copywriters, marketers and salespeople make it. He says he is making ten times more than when he followed copywriting and sales hacks, while still using that skill set authentically, in social posts and sales pages, to speak to a target customer he knows well (himself).

**Source:** You Have A $100,000 Product In Your Head (One Person Business Series).md

The what/how/why version has a specific advantage: it explains why marketing becomes easy in this model. If the product is how you solved your own problem, the reasons to buy it are the reasons you had to build it, and you already know them. *Self-monetization* is the name for the whole operation (the third verb of Section 28.1's closing triad), and the *public resume* (the brand as visible proof of competence, discussed in Chapter 27) is its by-product. The claim to earn "ten times more" is Koe's own comparison of his results before and after, not a general finding. The relation to traditional direct response is one of adaptation, not rejection: the persuasive skill set is kept and used "authentically," on a customer the writer knows from the inside.

#### Story, school and map; attract, nurture and implement

Two three-part formulations give the pillars different jobs.

In March 2023: "Your brand is your story" (where you are now, regardless of experience); "your content is your school" (what helped you get there, passed down); "your product is the map" (a holistic system that helps people get to where you are with less trial and error). The internet gives you the power to self-educate faster than ever; take it upon yourself to improve your life, pursue your curiosities and share your discoveries.

**Source:** The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md

In August 2024: "brand is to attract, content is to nurture, product is to implement." Social media attracts; newsletters, emails or free downloads educate; the product or service helps people implement.

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

The first is narrative: it describes the pillars as a story, an education and a route. The second is functional: it describes what each pillar does to a prospect as they move through the business. The second is the one that best matches the funnel of Chapter 24 (short form attracts, long form nurtures, the product converts and delivers).

#### The 2024–2026 formulations: philosophy, goals, transformation, offer

In January 2024 Koe gives the shortest version: "your philosophy is your brand, the way you live is your product." His philosophy is that people should build their own thing, become sovereign and do what they want; his product is the writing system that allows people to do that. The way he lives (wake up, write for two hours with breaks for creativity, handle other business tasks) is "pretty enticing." He has results and a unique system that produced them, and the system came from trial and error toward his end goal, the philosophy of doing his own thing and becoming free.

**Source:** The 7 Best Internet Business Models (From Zero Experience To Advanced).md

In January 2026, describing how he would build a one-person business if he started over, he returns to what he calls the classic model. For decades, info products and coaching "reigned supreme": a creator who did not want to depend on sponsorships and ad revenue built an info product or coached, and as "one singular person" (the caveat that matters) those were the only options. One person could be their own marketing, content, sales and product department, "and I guess HR department." You write content to get traffic without needing a physical business in the right location; you do something interesting or learn a passion until you are somewhat of an expert, then share it in an educational or entertaining way. "Your brand is you. Your content are little pieces of your mind. And your products are processes that solved a problem in your life and led to something better." Many people overcomplicate that.

**Source:** How I'd build a one-person business (if I started over in 2026).md

Later the same month he announces a reduction. "The three pillars, which used to be four pillars, of a one-person business are brand, content and product." They differ from those of a traditional business: brand is your goals (what do you want out of life, and what are you helping people achieve?); content, the media through which you attract people to the brand, is knowledge (what are you learning along the way, and what is useful for them to know?); product is process (how did you reach a desirable goal, and how can you help them reach it faster?).

**Source:** If you have multiple interests, do not waste the next 2-3 years.md

In March 2026 the third pillar is called the offer. Following his template gives "the three pillars of a modern, successful one-person business," or, if it is not immediately successful ("which it probably won't be, don't expect that"), "a starting point," so that "it's not a guessing game anymore." (1) Brand: "who you are, what you help people achieve, and why people should care about both." (2) Content: "your ideas, opinions and teachings that attract people to your brand." (3) Offer: "your product, service and compelling landing page you can send people to from your content." In the recap, the brand "illustrates your values and attracts the right kind of people," and content "slowly builds trust over time and allows you to reach more people." "Simply put, you have people whose trust grows in you over time, and most businesses could only wish for that."

**Source:** How To Build A $1M One-Person Business Faster With AI.md

In June 2026 he compresses the three once more: "Your brand is the transformation, your content is the map, and your product is the tool."

**Source:** If You Have Multiple Interests, Start A One-Person Business.md

And in November 2023, between the macronutrients and these later versions, he states what all of them describe: "this business model that I always talk about" is just a personal brand with a product, "you and a product that you would personally buy," treating yourself as the niche and "using your content as traffic to that product," making a meaningful income that way. "It's that simple."

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

The changes are of two kinds. The first is the count: four pillars from 2022 to 2024, with the fourth named marketing, promotions, monetization or offer depending on the video, and three in 2026. Koe says that the pillars "used to be four" without saying which was dropped; since the 2026 offer pillar includes the landing page, the most plausible reading is that promotion was absorbed into the offer, but the source does not state this. The second is the metaphors, which sometimes collide: "map" belongs to the product in March 2023 and to the content in 2026; content is a "school" in one version and a "map" in another. Koe gives no reason for these shifts and presents the later versions as updates. They do not contradict each other in substance. All of them describe the same architecture (a person whose goals and character attract, whose knowledge nurtures, and whose method is sold) seen through a different lens; only the metaphors clash.

#### The product pillar: how you achieved a meaningful goal

Of the pillars, Koe develops the product most fully in this part of the corpus, through a definition, two sets of questions and a worked example.

The definition comes from November 2023: "product is how you achieved a meaningful goal." When you are the niche, people follow you because they want the lifestyle you have, or the mindset or skill set that made it possible; they want to do, speak or look like you. His analogy is walking down the street, seeing someone in a well-fitting all-black shirt, finding the brand and buying it: "I kind of become a piece of that person, or I integrate that piece to create who I am." You are already educating your followers with content, so what does the product add? "The difference between content and product is convenience, clarity and a replicable system that gets results."

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

The definition answers a question that worries many creators: why would anyone pay for what is available free in the content? Koe's answer is that the content and the product contain the same knowledge in different forms. Content is scattered, partial and self-directed; the product is convenient (organized in one place), clear (sequenced and explained) and replicable (a system that can be followed to a result). The shirt analogy adds a motive: people buy products to integrate a piece of someone they admire into their own identity, which connects the product to the identity theory of Chapter 3.

He then gives four questions to outline a product, each with his own answer and a hypothetical one. (1) What is the most meaningful goal you have achieved? His: "I earn a living writing about my interests." Hypothetical: "I got a six-pack without doing cardio," or "I unlocked extreme focus to build my side business." (2) What steps do people need to take to get there? His: write, build a readership, persuade and sell. Hypothetical: minimalist training and intermittent fasting. (3) What do people need to learn to take those steps? His: social media, copywriting, content. Hypothetical: the principles of training and nutrition. (4) What can people practice and implement in their day to get results? His: "the 2 hour content ecosystem" (his product, the subject of Chapter 24). Hypothetical: "the executive training system for busy people." "Boom, that is the outline of your product." He admits the examples are "kind of mediocre; you can do a lot better," and when he repeats the list later in the video he gives three questions, merging the learning question into the steps.

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

The four questions reproduce, in order, the structure of a transformation: an outcome (the meaningful goal), a path (the steps), the knowledge the path requires, and the daily practice that makes it real. Their answers become the product's promise, its modules, its lessons and its system. The fourth question is the one that turns information into a product in the sense of the definition above: a named, repeatable practice ("the 2 hour content ecosystem") is what makes the knowledge replicable.

For those who do not yet know what product they could make, a December 2022 video offers three questions and a bonus. (1) What is the highlight of your day (writing, yoga, the gym)? What is seamless for you to do and learn about? Or what do you do for work, something you are very good at and could teach? Look especially for something you have done that you do not consider impressive, perhaps because you are so immersed in a community where everyone has done it that it seems normal. (2) What do you do better than your peers or most people? Test it by how you justify doing it (his example, developed elsewhere in the corpus, is the bodybuilder Chris Bumstead, whose dedication shows in how he explains his choices). (3) Look at whom you follow and what they sell. If you follow people you learn from, some of them are selling something; deconstruct their landing pages and emails, reverse-engineer them and emulate (if you like self-improvement and online business, deconstruct Koe's; if you like fitness, ask what lets fitness creators do it full-time). The bonus, for those who have not started: be decisive. "Pick a path that slightly interests you and just start," which gets you to knowing much faster than procrastinating, and keep learning and building according to what you learn.

**Source:** You Have A $100,000 Product In Your Head (One Person Business Series).md

The first question contains a useful observation about perception: the skills a person takes for granted are often invisible to them precisely because their environment normalizes them, so the search for a product begins by questioning what one considers ordinary. The third question is the consumer's-eyes-to-creator's-eyes move of Section 28.2. The bonus repeats a principle that runs through Chapter 7: you will never know exactly what you want before you begin, and exposure reveals it.

The January 2026 version of the planner example closes the product pillar and adds the economics. If you love productivity, study it, talk about it online, iterate (your content will not be good at first), improve until you grow, and use the traffic to feed a product. Which product? What did you buy that helped you? A planner? Order the top five planners on Amazon, break them down, use them, deconstruct what you do and do not like, and create your own, not necessarily a physical one: a Notion template, a printable planner, or a course combined with them. To make $50,000, $100,000 or $500,000 a year, it is "really just those two levers": content, which produces traffic, and how good the product is. Your value is not set by an employer; it is practically uncapped, depending on how many people you reach, which forces you to drop the *employment mindset*. "This is never going to go away, but the vessel by which you do it will continue to change": in the future it may not be a printed planner at all.

**Source:** How I'd build a one-person business (if I started over in 2026).md

The last sentence distinguishes the principle from its container. The principle (solve a problem for yourself, attract people like you, sell them the solution) is presented as permanent; the vessel (a paper planner, a Notion template, a course, whatever comes next) changes with technology. Its practical consequence is that a creator should not become attached to a format.

#### You are every department

The pillars describe what a one-person business contains; a further observation concerns who does the work. Koe learned it in a year at a web design agency, which he calls "a blessing" because it showed him exactly what had been missing in his earlier freelancing businesses. The agency had marketing, sales and operations departments, plus the creative, who was him: "marketing and sales got the customers, operations did whatever they do, and gave them to me." His realization: "for my freelance one-person business, I have to become all of those things. I have to be the media company, I have to be the marketing department, I have to be the sales department, I have to be the operations department." Technology now allows one person to do that: social media for marketing and sales, to attract clients; software such as Kartra, ClickFunnels, CRMs and project management tools to manage the rest. "You need technical know-how and you need the creative skill, and match the two together." The realization led him to learn copywriting, sales and direct response marketing, skills "people tell you to learn but nobody actually learns, because they're so focused on learning the creative skills." "You can build your website all day, but if you don't know how to send people to that website, it doesn't matter."

**Source:** How To Make $1 Million A Year As A Digital Writer.md

In November 2023 he draws the practical consequence for beginners: it is unwise to take on too much at first, precisely because you must become the marketing, sales, operations and media department. "You are a media company, you're a marketing company, you're a sales company, you're everything." You cannot focus only on the skill you want to sell without learning how to sell it, nor without learning how to fulfill the service after you sell it. To create a brand, content, a product and promotions you need at least the fundamentals of marketing and sales, graphic design, web design, copywriting, content writing, email marketing and social media. "This is a blessing and a curse."

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

The agency story is one of the clearest cases in the corpus of how Koe's model was formed by experience: the departments he saw separated in an organization are the functions he later had to integrate in himself. The "blessing and curse" names the trade-off honestly. The blessing is control and margin; the curse is the breadth of skills required, which is why the one-person business favors the generalist of Chapter 20 and why Koe advises starting small (Section 28.5).

Justin Welsh, in the December 2021 conversation, identifies the department most often neglected. The most common misconception, he says, is "that if you have an appropriate niche and a product that solves a specific group of people's problems, that you're sort of all set." His example of such a niche: "I work with high-performance female sales leaders in the healthcare technology space ... I did that thing for 5 years." But "knowing the people whose problem you solve and being able to solve their problems are only two pieces of the pie. The other piece of the pie that you're forgetting is getting that attention ... making sure that people know who you are, what you do and why you do it." People underestimate "the amount of time you have to spend copywriting, learning how to be a great podcast host, learning how to host live events on video ... running a successful YouTube channel, building an Instagram following." "Doesn't matter if you know how to solve a problem; if people don't know about you, it's all a moot point." His conclusion: "one-person businesses are mostly about marketing, and I think that gets greatly overlooked."

**Source:** Justin Welsh Shows You How To Start & Grow A One-Person Business.md

The *three pieces of the pie* (who, solution, attention) are Welsh's, and they restate the product-and-people principle of Section 28.5 from the side of a founder's time. The two pieces most founders think about (the market and the solution) are the product side; the forgotten piece is the people side. Koe's own 2023 redefinition of the one-person business ("put yourself out there until enough people know who you are, what you do and why you do it") repeats Welsh's phrasing almost exactly, which suggests, though Koe does not say so, where it came from.

#### The levers

The last element of the model concerns growth. Once the pillars exist, what determines how much the business earns? Koe's answer changes form three times, and the corpus records the sequence.

In November 2022, in the "11 Lessons" video, the second lesson is that once you have a brand and a product (traffic and offers), "you need three levers." (1) *Growth*: continuously grow your brand and follower count, or you get stuck in the rat race of constantly reaching out to land clients, or of selling through a medium that is not your own traffic source. (2) *Nurture*: educate your audience to the point of becoming customers, through a newsletter. (3) *Monetization*: an offer or promotions on a systemized, consistent promotion schedule.

**Source:** 11 Lessons From Growing A 7-Figure One Person Business.md

In May 2024, as the sixth step of the "$1 million Creator," the levers become three activities of the value creator. (1) *Writing*: "attracting an audience of people by educating them on how your interest can help them achieve their goals"; the writing "can be repurposed into posts, a newsletter, a YouTube script and literally any other kind of content." (2) *Promotion*: "you won't make money if you don't promote your product: promote your email list on social media, promote your products on your email list." (3) *Iteration*: "pay attention to the writing and promotions that do better than others; improve them as you go to increase their effectiveness." "These are literally the only things that you have to do in order to reach $1 million as one person"; tactics, strategies and skills help you do them better, "but those are the main things."

**Source:** How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md

He then explains why three levers are enough, in an argument he calls simple math. "One, you have a product that sells; two, you have an audience that's growing. If you can continue growing the audience and selling the product, how much you earn is really up to you; it's just simple math." "If you can make $1 you can make 1 million; if you can gain one follower you can gain 1 million." "The only trap here is forgetting that you are creative": "if your audience stops growing, that's a problem; solve it. If your product stops selling, that's a problem; solve it. If you don't know how to solve it, that's a skill issue."

**Source:** How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md

In January 2026, as quoted above, the levers become two: content, which produces traffic, and the quality of the product.

**Source:** How I'd build a one-person business (if I started over in 2026).md

| Date | Levers | What they describe |
|---|---|---|
| 2022-11 | Growth, nurture, monetization | Functions of a funnel |
| 2024-05 | Writing, promotion, iteration | Activities of the creator |
| 2026-01 | Content → traffic; quality of the product | The two variables of income |

The three versions describe the same system at different levels of detail. The 2022 levers are stages a prospect passes through (being found, being educated, being sold to). The 2024 levers are what the creator does every week to keep those stages working. The 2026 levers are the two variables that the activities ultimately move. Koe gives no reason for the changes; they are consistent with his gradual simplification of the whole model, from four pillars to three and from many steps to "audience plus product."

The "simple math" argument deserves careful reading. Its claim is about linearity: if a process produces one follower or one sale, there is no structural reason it cannot produce a million, so the ceiling is set by the creator's ability to keep solving the problems that appear, not by the model. Its rhetorical figures vary. In February 2024 Koe says "if you can gain one follower you can gain $1,000; if you can make $1 you can make a million" (the first figure is possibly a slip in the transcript); in January 2024 another video says "if you can gain one follower you can gain 100,000"; in May 2024 the figure is a million for both. The idea is the same in all three. It should be read as a reframing that turns a stall into a problem to be solved, not as a prediction: growth processes on social media are not linear in practice (Chapter 26 describes the long flat stretch of beginner hell and the nonlinear jumps after it), and the claim is that the limit is a skill to acquire, not that the scaling is automatic.

The February 2024 passage gives the model its upper bound. Introducing a clip of Sam Altman (whose content does not appear in the transcript), Koe says he has been talking about the one-person business for a long time and plans to write a book about it. The one-person business can make "$5 to $10 million a year" if you understand and use technology and use social media and the creator economy as your vessel. Are there other ways to do business as one person? "I have difficulty believing so, because social media audience is leverage": no paid ads, no team; write content, build an audience, sell a digital product you do not have to fulfill much on.

**Source:** Zero To $1 Million As A One-Person Business (Working 2-4 Hours A Day).md

*Social media audience is leverage* (his phrase) explains why the upper bound is so high for a business without employees. Leverage, in the sense Chapter 25 developed, is output that does not scale with input: a piece of writing reaches a hundred people or a million with the same effort, and a digital product sells once or ten thousand times with the same fulfillment. A business whose traffic and product both have this property can grow revenue without growing headcount. The figure itself is Koe's estimate of what the model allows, not a typical outcome, and Chapter 30 (Section 30.6) collects the figures and timelines he gives for the stages along the way.

The next chapter turns from the definition of the business to its beginning: the routes of entry, the minimum viable offer, the case for starting with a service, and the permission to launch something imperfect.

### Exercises

1. **Run the six demands on your own situation.** Take a business, project or job you are engaged in now (or one you are considering). For each of the six demands Koe lists (evolve or die, expertise, results, sucking first, new habits of work, rest and play, a psychology-based skill set), describe concretely whether your current activity makes that demand of you, and what in your behavior would change if it did. Then decide whether Koe's claim that the one-person business is a *forcing function for every area of life* holds in your case, or whether some areas of your life would be untouched by it.

2. **Map a solved problem into a product outline.** Choose a problem you have actually solved in one of the Eternal markets. Apply, in order, the six-step sequence ("set a goal, find a problem, learn how to solve it, document how you solved it, distill it into a replicable process, give it to others"), then the three sub-steps of the holistic solution (desirable goal, starting point, unique path), then the four product questions (meaningful goal, steps, what to learn, what to practice daily). Where the frameworks ask for something your experience cannot yet supply, identify the gap and say whether it is a gap in your solution or in your documentation of it.

3. **Test "your path is your product" against a crowded market.** Pick a goal that millions of people share (losing weight, earning a first $1,000 online, learning a language). Describe three different paths people take to it and the kind of person each path would resonate with. Then argue for or against Koe's claim that, because paths differ, a market defined by a shared goal cannot saturate. Under what conditions would two sellers' paths be too similar for the claim to hold?

4. **Adjudicate "traffic before offer."** Koe says "we need traffic before we need an offer," recommends selling a service through direct outreach before having an audience, and says "you don't need to wait; you need something to iterate on." Write a short decision memo for a specific beginner (describe their skills, savings and available hours) recommending which to build first in the next ninety days. Justify the recommendation using at least two of Koe's arguments, and state what evidence after ninety days would show that the recommendation was wrong.

5. **Compare two definitions of entrepreneurship.** Set Koe's 2023 position (employee and entrepreneur as opposed trajectories; leave employment early) beside his 2025 position ("entrepreneurship is not a role or a title, it's a state of mind") and Sahil Bloom's "enterprising" employee. Construct one realistic case in which the earlier position gives better advice and one in which the later position does. What does the comparison suggest about the conditions under which a business of one's own is necessary rather than merely available?

6. **Diagnose a business with the pillars and levers.** Choose a solo creator or small online business you know well (or your own). Describe its brand, content and offer using two of Koe's formulations (for example, what/how/why and attract/nurture/implement). Then identify which lever (growth, nurture, monetization; or writing, promotion, iteration; or traffic and product quality) is currently limiting its income, and design one change to that lever. Explain why you chose that lever rather than another.

7. **Question the "simple math."** Koe argues that "if you can make $1 you can make 1 million," so that any stall is "a problem to solve or a skill issue." Identify two factors that could limit a one-person business's growth and that are not plausibly described as skill issues. For each, say whether Koe's framework could absorb it (by redefining it as a problem to solve) or whether it marks a genuine limit of the model. What is gained and what is lost by treating every stall as a skill issue?

8. **Write your own career diagram.** Using the four career-path diagrams in Section 28.4 as models, draw (in words or a simple list) the path from where you are now to a business that would carry your life's work. Mark which stages you have already passed, which skills each remaining stage requires, and where in the path a constraint you currently accept (time, money, visibility) could be converted, as Koe advises, into a design requirement rather than a reason not to start.
