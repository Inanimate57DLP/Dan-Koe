## Chapter 5 — Entropy and Order in Consciousness

Part II built a model of the person: an identity made of ideas, standards and, above all, goals, which filters what you perceive and which your mind defends as if it were a body. That model explains why change is hard, but it leaves a mechanical question unanswered. Why does a life that nobody steers drift in a predictable direction (toward anxiety, distraction, stagnation and a vague feeling of being lost) rather than staying where it is? Dan Koe's answer borrows a law from physics. Everything left to itself tends toward disorder, and the mind is no exception. This chapter reconstructs how he moves from entropy as a physical idea to *psychic entropy* as a description of the untended mind, why he treats chaos as the default state of consciousness and clarity as its opposite, what he means by "ordering consciousness" in yourself and in others, why he insists that "everything is information", and how he turns the whole picture into a practical discipline of *entropy management* in work and business. The next chapter will take the scarce resource at the center of this model (attention) and the device that organizes it (the goal) and examine them in detail. This chapter supplies the reason they matter: without a continuous input of directed energy, order decays.

A word on status before starting. Koe calls entropy "the supreme law of the universe" and uses thermodynamic vocabulary throughout, but what he builds with it is an interpretive framework for a human life, not a physical theory of the mind. He signals this himself when he calls his more abstract formulations "rather esoteric" or pairs them with practical advice. This chapter reports the framework in his terms and marks, where it helps, the difference between the physics he borrows and the use he makes of it.

### 5.1 Entropy, the Supreme Law of the Universe

#### What entropy is

The cleanest definition Koe gives comes from 2025: "Entropy is the measure of how messy, disordered or random something is. It's a part of the second law of thermodynamics, which in short states that in any natural process, the total entropy or disorder tends to increase over time unless effort or energy is put into keeping the system ordered" (**Source:** `The Deep Work Routine That Changed My Life.md`, 2025-05-18). Two years earlier he had introduced the same idea with more ceremony: "Let me introduce you to the supreme law of the universe which is entropy. Some call this Nature's tax, and in brief entropy is the measure of disorder in a system" (**Source:** `The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md`, 2023-01-01; the passage was reissued in `Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md`, 2024-03-21). The name "Nature's tax" is not his: he attributes it to an unnamed "some", and uses it as a synonym. Elsewhere he states the same law in plain language: entropy is "the gradual decline into chaos if energy is not put into a system", "the fact that everything falls apart" (**Source:** `My Honest Advice For Those Who Feel Lost & Confused.md`, 2024-05-30).

Across these formulations three elements recur, and the rest of the chapter depends on them:

1. **Disorder is the direction of travel.** Left alone, systems become messier, not tidier.
2. **Order has a price.** It can be preserved or increased only by putting in effort or energy. This is the "tax".
3. **The price is continuous.** It is not paid once. A system kept in order today will drift tomorrow unless energy keeps flowing into it.

**Complementary context:** In physics, the second law of thermodynamics says that the total entropy of an isolated system never decreases over time; local order (a living cell, a refrigerator, a tidy room) is possible only by consuming energy and exporting disorder to the surroundings. Statistical mechanics explains this as a matter of probability: there are vastly more disordered arrangements of a system's parts than ordered ones, so random change almost always moves toward disorder. Koe uses the law as an analogy for lives, minds, habits and businesses; he does not claim that a mind's "disorder" is thermodynamic entropy in the physical sense.

#### "If you do not create order for your life, you will decline into chaos"

What turns a definition from physics into "the supreme law of the universe" is the consequence Koe draws from it for a human life. The earliest version in the corpus appears in a 2022 video on reinventing yourself in six to twelve months. The *anti-vision*, the life you do not want (the concept developed in Chapter 7), usually comes, he says, from mindless action and inaction that lets "entropy take hold and just drag your life into chaos." Then the law: "That is the supreme law of the universe: if you do not create order for your life you will decline into chaos" (**Source:** `How To Get Ahead Of 99% Of People (In 6-12 Months).md`, 2022-12-25; reissued in `Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md`, 2024-03-21).

The remedy is stated in the same breath. Order has to be created deliberately, and Koe names three instruments for creating it: a vision, a plan (specified "day by day and week by week") and a system. Of the system he says that it "will prevent entropy in your life" and that it is "the thing that will take you from anti-vision to vision." He also names the kind of energy a system consumes: maintaining a system against entropy requires energy, "specifically mental energy." Someone who does not create this order is, in his phrase, a "victim of entropy."

This short passage already contains the architecture of the whole chapter. Entropy is the default force. A life that is not deliberately ordered does not stay put; it slides toward the anti-vision. Order is created by structures (vision, plan, system), and those structures are not self-sustaining: they run on mental energy, which is to say on attention. When Koe later says that "attention is the RAM" or that the goal is a lens (Chapter 6), he is describing the components of this anti-entropic machine.

#### Entropy goes further than impermanence

Koe distinguishes entropy from an idea that might seem identical: the Buddhist principle of impermanence. "It's the Buddhist principle of impermanence, that everything is changing, and with entropy it goes even further to say that everything is in decline unless effort is put into maintaining the system" (**Source:** `My Honest Advice For Those Who Feel Lost & Confused.md`, 2024-05-30). The difference is one of direction. Impermanence says that nothing lasts; it is neutral about where change leads. Entropy adds a vector: change, left to itself, runs downhill. This is why Koe can draw a practical imperative from entropy that impermanence alone would not justify. If things merely changed, you could let them change. If they decline unless maintained, then not acting is itself a choice with a predictable result.

**Complementary context:** Impermanence (*anicca* in Pali) is one of the three marks of existence in Buddhist teaching, alongside suffering (*dukkha*) and non-self (*anattā*). In its traditional use it is a contemplative insight meant to loosen attachment, not a claim about decline. Koe adapts it as a step toward entropy rather than discussing it on its own terms. (Impermanence returns in its own right in Chapter 39, where polarity and impermanence are treated as principles of a life.)

#### Why productivity systems exist

The most direct application Koe makes of the law is to explain why anyone needs a productivity system at all. Having defined entropy as "the measure of disorder in a system", he concludes that the productivity system he is about to teach "is important for the survival and evolution of our work because we create systems to aid in evolution." Without energy input, "systems tend toward disorder or chaos" (**Source:** `The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md`, 2023-01-01). The phrase "survival and evolution" is worth noticing. A system does two jobs. It defends what exists against decay (survival), and it creates the conditions to grow beyond it (evolution). Both jobs are needed because there is no third option of standing still, as the next subsections show.

In the summary of his book *The Art of Focus* (2024), entropy becomes the first of a series of principles, stated together with two others that follow from it:

1. "The supreme law of the universe is entropy; nothing is permanent — from emotions to ideas to your health to the systems that the education system relies on to secure your future."
2. "Systems thinking, or understanding the goal, process and problem of any crevice of reality, is how you rig situations in your favor."
3. "Psychic entropy, or disorder in the mind, means that we must create and maintain order in our lives through self-generated goals, a path to achieve them and problems to focus on right now so we don't get consumed by distractions."

(**Source:** `The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md`, 2024-01-18.)

The sequence is a chain of reasoning, not a list. Because everything decays (principle 1), the only way to keep anything in order is to understand the systems that produce it (principle 2), and because the mind is one of those systems, it too must be kept in order by the same structure: a goal, a path and a present problem (principle 3). The examples in principle 1 are deliberately wide: emotions, ideas, health and the institutions that promise a secure future. Nothing is exempt, including the default path criticized in Part I.

Principle 2 also records a change in how Koe defines a system. In 2023 a system had three components: an end goal, a process of steps to reach it, and *energy* "of specific quality" that keeps it from falling apart (his analogy was the body, which can be fueled with junk food but runs properly only on clean food; in work, the right energy is "focused prioritized action"). In the 2024 summary the third component has become *problem*: goal, process, problem. He does not explain the change. The two definitions can be read as complementary rather than contradictory. The 2023 version answers the question "what keeps a system from decaying?" (energy), which is the question of this chapter; the 2024 version answers "where should the energy go?" (into the problems that stand between the process and the goal), which is the diagnostic question developed in Chapter 6, section 6.6. In the newer formulation energy has not disappeared; it is what you invest in the problems.

#### The library, the bookshelf and the uncleaned room

Koe rarely leaves a principle abstract. Entropy, in particular, is carried by a family of household images that he returns to across four years, each time adding a layer. Taken together, they amount to a small theory of maintenance.

**The library and the home bookshelf.** A library has "a system for organizing books and making them easy to find", plus defined steps for employees to rent books out, take them back and reshelve them, steps that keep the system from "tending toward disorder or chaos." A home bookshelf is different. You take out one book, then another, and they end up scattered around the house, because nobody puts energy into maintaining the order, or because there was never an organizing system in the first place. The home shelf fails. The 2023 version of the example draws its lesson inward: what fails is not only the external arrangement but "the attention, the mental energy as well, necessary to maintain that system in the order in your mind." Hence the key sentence: "This isn't only the system that is external but it is how your mind interacts with that system, because that's how you order consciousness, that's how you bring clarity to your mind and allow yourself to act" (**Source:** `The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md`, 2023-01-01; reissued in `Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md`, 2024-03-21).

In 2025 the same library carries a different emphasis. As more people take, read and move books, an unmaintained library "is just going to become completely chaotic... a jumbled mess of books that are everywhere and nobody's going to be able to find anything. It loses its value." Maintenance can come from employees or, eventually, from "a robot that condenses the amount of energy that's needed to put the right amount of effort in" (**Source:** `The Deep Work Routine That Changed My Life.md`, 2025-05-18). Two new ideas enter here. First, disorder destroys *value*: a library whose books cannot be found is no longer a library in any useful sense. Second, the energy required to maintain order can be reduced by better tools, which anticipates the idea, developed in section 5.5 and in Chapter 12, of systemizing work so that the same order costs less effort.

Books add a further detail in a 2024 version: even durable objects decay without care. Spines warp, books get damaged, a volume is lost at a friend's house "if you don't put effort into maintaining the order of your bookshelf" (**Source:** `My Honest Advice For Those Who Feel Lost & Confused.md`, 2024-05-30).

**The uncleaned room.** The room is the image Koe uses most, and it is the one he maps most directly onto the mind. The earliest version (2022) is explicitly a metaphor for mental life: if your mind were a room and you put no energy into cleaning it, it would get dirtier over time. On the first day it may not look dirty, just a few things out of place. After a week, a month, a year, it is like walking into someone's room with clothes everywhere, the bed unmade, things all over the desk, "a complete mess". He then generalizes: "everyone is living in a pigsty of a mind" (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16). A 2024 version keeps the arc and adds the voice of the parent: "One day without cleaning won't be bad, but after a week, a month, a year, that's when your mom starts telling you that you live in a pigsty" (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md`, 2024-07-21).

The 2025 version spells out why small neglect is dangerous: "If you don't put effort into cleaning your room every day, slowly it will become a disgusting nest of filth." On day one there are a few shirts lying around, "not that bad", so you do not bother, and the room slowly gets "so dirty that you have to expend so much more effort to get it back to the ordered state." Then the application: "That's where most people's minds are right now. They're so distracted that it takes so much effort that they don't want to get undistracted" (**Source:** `The Deep Work Routine That Changed My Life.md`, 2025-05-18).

The 2024 bookshelf video adds the most radical layer. Many people had a mother who cleaned their room as children, or who told them to, "because she understood that if you did not clean your room it would just get dirtier and dirtier... until it just became uninhabitable." At that point the environment does not just reflect the mind; it shapes it: "it affects your mind so much that your mind in that environment becomes that environment; it becomes dirty and sloppy and can't think in a clear, corrected manner" (**Source:** `My Honest Advice For Those Who Feel Lost & Confused.md`, 2024-05-30). Here the room is no longer only a metaphor for the mind; it is a literal environment that conditions it, which connects this chapter to the account of reprogramming through environment in Chapter 4.

Read together, the variants yield a set of claims that the rest of the book takes for granted:

| Layer | What the image shows | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| Order needs a system | The library has a method; the home shelf does not | Library, 2023 and 2025 |
| A system needs continuous energy | Employees, a robot, daily cleaning | Library, room |
| The energy is partly mental | Order depends on "how your mind interacts with that system" | Library, 2023 |
| Disorder destroys value | A library where nothing can be found "loses its value" | Library, 2025 |
| Neglect compounds | A few shirts become "a nest of filth"; restoring order costs far more than keeping it | Room, 2025 |
| Decay is slow enough to normalize | Day by day it "becomes a pigsty and it's normal to you" | Room, 2026 |
| The environment becomes the mind | "Your mind in that environment becomes that environment" | Room, 2024 |
| Established order is cheaper to keep | Once habits and systems exist, "less energy is needed to keep it going" | Room/writing, 2024 |

The last row comes from the 2024 version of the room, where Koe immediately applies the metaphor to a writer: it takes time and experimentation to form the habits and systems that lead to an audience and an income, but once they are established, less energy is needed to keep things going (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md`, 2024-07-21). This qualification matters. Entropy is not a sentence to endless, equal effort. The cost of order is highest when it is first being built and when it has been allowed to collapse; in between, a well-designed system lowers it. That asymmetry is the basis of the distinction between building and maintenance in section 5.5.

#### There is no such thing as staying the same

The most consequential corollary of entropy, in Koe's hands, is that standing still is impossible. "To understand the first principle, you need to understand entropy. You must understand that by doing nothing with your life, you choose to slowly drown in chaos. You don't stay the same. You dig yourself deeper into a hole without trying, because the good life demands consistent effort toward your own goals" (**Source:** `The Deep Work Routine That Changed My Life.md`, 2025-05-18). He had said almost exactly the same thing a year earlier and attached a list of examples to it: "everything around you, the building you're in... your skin, your hair, the food in your fridge, the cleanliness of your bed, the cleanliness of your room, it's all going to tend toward chaos unless you put focus and effort into solving the problems that lead to it being better or it being maintained" (**Source:** `Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md`, 2024-02-18). Note the last clause. Effort does not mean generic busyness; it means "focus and effort into solving the problems" that keep a thing in order or improve it. This is the same move as the shift in his definition of a system from energy to problem.

In a 2024 video framing life as a video game, entropy becomes an adversary: "There's an enemy we are all battling against that leads to us being stressed, anxious and overwhelmed... called entropy" (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md`, 2024-07-21). The same passage gives a one-sentence summary of what defeating it looks like ("the flow state occurs when the content of your consciousness is composed of one challenging yet meaningful task") and of what losing to it feels like: most people feel they are drowning in "a cloud of chaos", in which ten negative thoughts can splinter into a hundred. His example of that splintering (a chest pain, then the thought of Grandpa's heart attack, then the ten heart-healthy foods you have neglected, then how an early death might affect your family) belongs to the mechanism of psychic entropy examined in section 5.2.

Why do people not notice that they are declining? Koe's answer is that decline is slow and its effects are delayed. Ask people who have plateaued what they are doing with their life and they answer confidently, "I'm just living life." They have not grasped the ramifications of staying the same "because they don't realize that there's no such thing as staying the same." The decline "doesn't show up until years later." The consequence he describes is generational: most people turn thirty or forty with the emotional maturity of a fifteen-year-old, because the world keeps pushing forward faster while their mind, body, spirit and finances stay put, which in reality means they are digging deeper into a hole (**Source:** `I'm 28. Here's How To Get Ahead Of Most 20 Year Olds.md`, 2025-07-27).

The hole is a metaphor in its own right. "The deeper you get into this hole, the light at the top is more difficult to see." And the question that follows: "If you're stuck in this 30-foot-deep hole, how are you going to dig yourself out of that?" (same source). The image combines two effects of entropy already present in the room metaphor: the cost of recovery rises with the depth of neglect, and the perspective needed to see a way out shrinks as you go down. The second effect will reappear in section 5.2, where psychic entropy narrows the mind until it cannot zoom out.

Koe's sharpest version of this argument is aimed at the comfort zone. "A job, or just your comfort zone in general, can be the early death of the best version of yourself", or rather "a slow and painful decline into chaos, if you understand what entropy is, especially psychic entropy." What traps people is the absence of "a continuously evolving hierarchy of goals that you choose, self-generated goals that you can continue pursuing." Without it, "you think you're just chilling, just standing still, when that's not true at all: you're declining, you're decaying," and the mind gets flooded with "feeling lost and overwhelmed and anxious and bored", until you are so stressed and narrow "you can't zoom out to see the new path that you should take to bring fulfillment back" (**Source:** `How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md`, 2024-05-26). The phrase "self-generated goals" links this directly to Chapter 1: the person who does not choose goals is not goal-less but pursuing assigned ones, and the comfort zone is what assigned goals feel like from the inside once they stop demanding anything new.

A concrete case of this decay is the vacation that turns into normal life. On a two- or three-week trip, the new place gives you dopamine; after three weeks it is normal, even boring, like home but without work, business, purpose or project. You want to return to something that lets you explore. If you do not have a focus, you look for that sense of discovery in your phone (social media, video games, Netflix), novelty at the click of a button, short-term dopamine that keeps you the same. "You feel like you're discovering", but you stay the same, and by the law of entropy you are not staying the same: "you are slowly decaying into chaos" (**Source:** `How To Make The Greatest Comeback Of Your Life.md`, 2024-12-15). Koe frames the example within a broader observation in that same passage: there is no single true path (or someone would have found it and everyone would be rich, happy and healthy), and life will never be permanently happy, because "happy doesn't make sense without sad", just as "a hand doesn't exist without an arm." He then widens the claim beyond psychology: the physical, biological, mental and spiritual planes all "contain this pattern" (same source). That sentence is what turns the vacation from an anecdote into a principle. Polarity is not a quirk of mood; in Koe's reading it is a structural feature of every level of reality, which is why no state, however pleasant, can be held still, and why the lasting answer is movement rather than the search for a permanent high. (Polarity and impermanence as principles of life are taken up in Chapter 39, section 39.3.) Novelty fades because it is defined against what is familiar; the only durable source of discovery is a project that keeps generating new problems. (The dopamine side of this argument is developed in Chapter 11.)

#### Why stagnation can be "wonderful"

If decline is the default, one might expect Koe to treat the onset of entropy as purely bad news. He does not. In a 2025 discussion of work in the age of AI, he advises treating your own work as "the opposite of your job": "It's work that evolves. It's work that demands continuous learning. It's work with a prerequisite that you have at least some of your life together." Then: "The minute you stagnate is the minute entropy increases. And that's a wonderful thing." It is wonderful, in context, because it is a signal: the felt rise of disorder tells you it is time to grow. "Staying the same is the enemy of a good life" (**Source:** `The Future Of Work (How To Become AI-First).md`, 2025-05-25). This reframing anticipates the later shift (examined at the end of this section) from entropy as an enemy to entropy as an opportunity.

The same logic underlies Koe's criticism of businesses built to cash in and fade. To help people do something that matters, "you can't stagnate; you must personally continuously improve and evolve." By stagnating, you are "declining into chaos, you are unraveling," and what you build will not last. It will be "a flash in the pan SaaS like most people are building right now." He is careful not to condemn such products outright: "Temple Run and Flappy Bird were great games," and a quick product can be "good quick pump and dump cash." His objection is to the life it implies: "but is that really what you're here for?" (**Source:** `The Death Of The Personal Brand (& The Future Of Creative Work).md`, 2024-10-06). In his use, a *flash in the pan* is a business or product that does not evolve and therefore does not last; the expression is common, the application to the entropy of a creator's work is his.

From here he derives something close to a moral obligation: "If you are here to help people, it is your obligation to pursue continuous improvement. Because if you don't, entropy increases." Life slowly gets worse until it is hard to dig yourself out. To people at a low level of consciousness this sounds like a drag; they would rather "drown in instantly gratifying pleasure", not understanding that "continuous improvement makes life overwhelmingly more enjoyable than the opposite." The job, as he lists it, is to share your point of view, improve each domain of life ("expand your mind, build your body, nurture your spirit"), and contribute back to the civilization "you suck resources from" through the modern form of value exchange: work, business, entrepreneurship (**Source:** `The Death Of Social Media (& The Future Of Content Creation).md`, 2025-09-07). The argument links three scales: the individual who must keep improving to avoid decline, the creator who must keep improving to keep being useful, and the civilization that supplies the resources and is owed a return.

The most uncompromising formulation is one of the "things to do" in a 2025 video addressed to people in their twenties: "If the overarching aim for the entirety of your life is not to self-actualize, then it's to self-sabotage." Every action should align with self-actualization; if it does not, you lack a deep reason for what you do. Most people do not think about the outcomes of their actions; they follow "a myopic focus on short-term pleasure that slowly beats them into a pulp" until, decades later, health problems catch up with them, their mind cannot adapt to a changing technological landscape, and their actions, never fully conscious, have never led to anything great (**Source:** `I'm 28. Here's How To Get Ahead Of Most 20 Year Olds.md`, 2025-07-27). He supports the claim only with a general reference to "decades of psychological research" on human needs and development, without naming any. The binary (self-actualize or self-sabotage) is entropy restated as a life principle: there is no neutral aim, because an aim that does not build is, over time, an aim that lets things fall apart.

**Complementary context:** "Self-actualization" is associated above all with Abraham Maslow, who placed it at the top of his hierarchy of needs as the drive to realize one's potential. Koe refers to Maslow's hierarchy explicitly elsewhere (see section 5.4), but in this passage he does not name a source.

#### Solving, not solved: forward movement

If stagnation is decline, the answer is movement, and Koe is precise about the grammar of it. "The only thing that can solve the problem is solving the problem. That means solving, not solved, not solve." (He calls "solve" future tense; what he is pointing at is the continuous aspect.) "It means you're doing something, you're solving, you're moving, because if you're not moving that means you're stagnant, and if you're stagnant that means you're not putting effort or energy into the system that leads to a potential end result that you want, that solves the problem. Therefore that's when entropy comes into play" (**Source:** `My Honest Advice For Those Who Feel Lost & Confused.md`, 2024-05-30). The emphasis on the gerund is the point. A problem is not dissolved by having the answer, intending to act, or having once acted; it yields only to energy being put into the system now.

Later in the same video, addressed to people who feel lost, he makes this the first step out: "The solution to most of your problems is just forward movement. It's investing energy into a goal, any goal, any self-generated goal, not one that's assigned to you, because if you aren't investing energy into a goal then you're stagnated and you will succumb to entropy and be dragged back into the situation that you're currently in" (same source). Three qualifications are packed into this sentence. The goal can be *any* goal: at the start, direction matters less than movement, because movement generates information (a theme of Chapter 6 and Chapter 14). It must be *self-generated*: an assigned goal is precisely what produced the stagnation. And stagnation is not a resting state but a pull backward: entropy "drags you back" into the situation you are trying to leave.

#### Being lost as a decision point

In 2026 the room metaphor returns with a new function. Koe now argues that there is "a benefit to actually being lost": "when you're lost, you have a decision to make. You either let entropy drag you into oblivion, which is that your life gets slowly worse and worse over time without you really noticing it," or you do something else. The room illustrates the first branch: you do not clean one day and a few shirts appear; the next day dust gathers; "eventually, day after day, it becomes a pigsty and it's normal to you. That's the dangerous part... It happens slowly over time in a way where you don't even notice it, and then it's so much harder to get out." The second branch: "make the decision to figure it out, experiment with new things until you find that one thing that's worth going all in on. The void is where success is born" (**Source:** `The Art of Strategic Thinking (How to Become Wildly Successful at Anything You Do).md`, 2026-08-19).

In this late use, *the void* names the state of being lost understood as a fork rather than a verdict. It is worth setting this against how Koe's treatment of feeling lost has moved over time. In early 2023 he presented feeling lost between chapters of life as normal, something to sit with; a few months later, in a video titled "If You're Lost", he called the lost phase "the most disastrous", "extremely dangerous" and, for most people, "an eternal struggle", while still granting that "there is wisdom in feeling lost": it means "there is potential to find your way." In 2025 he argued that people stay stuck in limbo precisely because they interpret being lost as a bad sign. And in 2026, as above, being lost became a decision point. The emphasis oscillates between normalizing the state and warning about it, and Koe does not announce a change of mind. What he does give, in the 2023 video, is the reason for the danger, and it is narrower than the state itself. People get consumed by thoughts, emotions and "low level responsibilities that prevent them from seeing or creating the light at the end of the tunnel", and "this is dangerous, very dangerous, because if you only have access to a **mental bubble** of certain thoughts and solutions then you may never find your way out" (**Source:** `If You’re Lost - Here’s How You Find Clarity For The Future.md`, 2023-06-04). The danger, in his own account, lies not in being lost but in staying lost inside a closed set of thoughts, without exposure to anything new. That reason is what allows the four positions to coexist: entering the state is normal and even useful; remaining in it, sealed in the bubble and letting entropy decide, is what makes it dangerous. The 2026 instruction to "experiment with new things" is the exit from the bubble the 2023 warning describes. (The lost phase as a stage between chapters of life is treated in Chapter 39.)

#### From enemy to opportunity: how Koe's view of entropy shifted

The decision-point reading is the latest stage of a broader change in how Koe positions entropy, and the change should be stated openly rather than smoothed over.

- **Entropy as threat (2022–2024).** Entropy is "an enemy we are all battling against"; the mind craves order, security and comfort; a life without deliberate order "will decline into chaos". Even in this period, however, the positive reading of chaos is already present. He had said in passing (2022) that "clarity stems from chaos, order stems from chaos." And in January 2024, in the summary of his book *The Art of Focus*, he calls entropy, "Nature's tax", not only the measure of disorder but "the driving force of evolution": everything falls apart unless effort reverses it, and that same pressure is what forces living things to change (**Source:** `The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md`, 2024-01-18; the passage is quoted in Chapter 6, section 6.6, for its account of systems).
- **Entropy as something to manage, and chaos as something to enter (2024–2025).** "Success is about entropy management": entropy is released when life is boring and constrained when it is overwhelming (section 5.5). In the same period a more active formula appears. In November 2024, describing how to become more intelligent, he prescribes: "reject the known path, dive into the unknown, set new higher goals to expand your mind, embrace the chaos and allow for growth" (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03), and he repeats the prescription almost word for word in December 2025 (**Source:** `How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md`, 2025-12-28; both passages are examined in Chapter 1). Here chaos is no longer only the drift to be held back; it is a place one chooses to go in order to grow.
- **Entropy as precondition (2025–2026).** After reading the chemist Ilya Prigogine (Koe says the simple story of entropy is "only half the story"), he argues that when life falls into chaos, an opportunity opens for a higher version of yourself to emerge, as ordered structures emerge from complexity; and, in 2026, that being lost is a decision point and "the void is where success is born."

The three stages are a map of emphasis, not a clean succession. The reading of chaos as a source of order and evolution coexists with the reading of entropy as an enemy from 2022 onward, well before Prigogine; what the later videos add is a theory of *why* chaos can be productive and a prescription to enter it on purpose. The only reason Koe gives for the shift is his reading of Prigogine ("that's only half the story"). What changes is the emphasis, not the law. The craving for order stays as a description of how minds work; entering chaos becomes, in some circumstances, a prescription. The two views apply at different scales: daily order protects focus and keeps the mind from splintering, while a deliberate passage through chaos can allow a life to reorganize at a higher level. This book treats the later, Prigogine-based reading where it belongs, in Chapter 38 (section 38.1), together with Ken Wilber's holons. In the present chapter, entropy is mostly what Koe first said it was: the default drift that order must be built against.

**Complementary context:** Ilya Prigogine (1917–2003) received the 1977 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his work on dissipative structures, systems far from equilibrium that maintain or create order by dissipating energy (convection cells and chemical oscillations are classic examples). His work is often summarized as "order out of chaos", the title of a book he co-wrote with Isabelle Stengers.

#### Study the formal systems

In 2026, Koe extends the law into a reading list. Among the formal systems he recommends studying are three that frame this chapter and the next. *Cybernetics*: "the study of feedback loops, control, and self-regulation in any system, mechanical, biological, or social" ("go down a rabbit hole with AI or Google"). *Systems theory*: "the study of wholes, interconnections, stocks and flows, emergence, and leverage points" ("buy a book on systems thinking"). And *thermodynamics*, especially the second law: "entropy always increases. Every ordered system requires energy input to resist decay. Your habits and routines will fall apart otherwise, aka clean your room." To these he adds a recommendation that crosses disciplines: read *Flow* by Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi, "even though it's not about thermodynamics" (**Source:** `The Art of Strategic Thinking (How to Become Wildly Successful at Anything You Do).md`, 2026-08-19).

The recommendation shows how Koe understands his own sources. Thermodynamics supplies the law, cybernetics supplies the mechanism of steering (Chapter 6, section 6.4), systems theory supplies the vocabulary of wholes and leverage, and Csikszentmihalyi supplies the psychology: what disorder and order feel like from inside a mind.

One term needs to be kept apart here. When Koe says "buy a book on systems thinking", he means the established discipline: the study of wholes, feedback, stocks and flows, emergence and leverage points associated with systems theory. That is not the sense in which he coined the phrase two years earlier. In the 2024 *Art of Focus* principles quoted earlier in this section ("Why productivity systems exist"), **systems thinking** is his own term, defined as "understanding the goal, process and problem of any crevice of reality", a practical habit of asking, of any situation, what it is for, how it works and where it is stuck, so that you can "rig situations in your favor." The two senses are related (both look at wholes rather than parts), but they are not the same thing: Koe's version is organized around a goal and a problem, which the academic field does not require, and it is the version this book uses. The disciplinary sense appears only in this reading list.

The "aka clean your room" is a wink at a familiar piece of self-help advice, but in Koe's frame it is a compressed statement of the second law applied to habits. The next section follows the line from thermodynamics to Csikszentmihalyi: what happens when the law of entropy is applied to consciousness itself.

### 5.2 Psychic Entropy and the Default State of Chaos

#### From the law to the mind

The step from physics to psychology is a single substitution. In 2022 Koe put it in the form of a gloss on the term itself: "psychic equals of the mind; entropy equals that systems tend toward disorder." If your mind is a system, and energy is not consistently fed into it to produce a desired end result, then "if order is not maintained in your mind, it tends toward disorder." He is explicit about the scope of the claim: "Anxiety, overwhelm, stress and any other negative emotion you can think of is the result of psychic entropy" (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16).

A 2023 formulation adds the condition under which the slide begins: "Entropy is that everything tends toward disorder; psychic entropy is that your mind tends toward disorder if not focused or if your mental system is not maintained" through conscious effort or energy. When we lose focus to a distraction, he adds, "we increase the chances of psychic entropy" (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (You Can't Escape The Matrix).md`, 2023-09-05). The pairing of the two conditions is important. Focus is the moment-to-moment form of maintenance; the "mental system" (vision, goals, priorities) is its structural form. Psychic entropy enters through a failure of either.

**Psychic entropy**, then, is Koe's term for disorder in the mind: the tendency of consciousness to drift into anxiety, overwhelm, boredom and scattered thought whenever it is not focused and its ordering structures are not maintained.

The term is not his invention, and his relationship to its source has changed. In 2022 he presented it as the central lesson of Csikszentmihalyi's *Flow*. In most later uses (2022 to 2025) he uses it without attribution, as part of his own vocabulary; in a 2023 video he quotes Csikszentmihalyi alongside it; in 2024 he glosses it with a coinage of his own ("mental disorder, not a mental disorder", below); and in 2026 he completes it with an opposite, *psychic negentropy* (section 5.3). The trajectory is from a borrowed concept to an enlarged personal vocabulary. Readers should keep in mind that when Koe says "psychic entropy" without naming a source, the concept still comes from Csikszentmihalyi.

**Complementary context:** In *Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience* (1990), Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi uses "psychic entropy" for states in which information entering consciousness conflicts with a person's goals (worry, fear, boredom, anxiety), disrupting the self and impairing its effectiveness. Its opposite in his book is "order in consciousness", the condition in which incoming information fits one's goals and attention can be freely invested in them; flow is its paradigm case. The word "negentropy" also appears in Csikszentmihalyi's writing (notably *The Evolving Self*, 1993), so Koe's later "psychic negentropy" revives a term with a prior history, although Koe names no source for it.

#### "Mental disorder, not a mental disorder"

In 2024 Koe gave the concept a deliberately provocative gloss. We are in a mental health crisis, he argues; we lack the curiosity, passion, purpose, autonomy and mastery that create a flow-sustaining life's work. Then the wordplay: "Most people have mental disorder, not a mental disorder, just mental disorder." He does not mean a diagnosed condition with a prescription (diagnoses are on the rise, he says, "mainly to profit off of this epidemic", an opinion he does not support with evidence in the passage), but literal disorder in the mind: psychic entropy. "Psychic equals of the mind, entropy equals systems tending toward disorder. Systems need consistent energy to thrive" (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md`, 2024-07-21). The phrase had antecedents: as early as 2022 he specified that he meant "disorder of the mind", not a mental disorder, which brings anxiety, overthinking and uncertainty to daily actions and leads to procrastination, or to not feeling (and therefore not reaping) the benefits of daily progress (**Source:** `Stop Trying To Be Unique... Do This Instead.md`, 2022-05-28).

The distinction has a clear purpose: it relocates most everyday suffering from the domain of pathology to the domain of skill. If most anxiety is disorder rather than a disorder, then most of it can be reduced by learning to order the mind. Koe makes this explicit in a 2024 argument for reading. Many people obsess over physical health but use "Band-Aid solutions to solve their disorder in consciousness." Yet "mental health is about sense making, understanding and clarity." Anxiety and depression, "even if from a chemical imbalance", and he is careful to say that what he proposes "won't cure" a chemical imbalance, "are the product of a narrow mind focused on a familiar past or predictable future, often negative" (**Source:** `This Simple Reading Habit Will Change Your Life (I Promise).md`, 2024-08-04).

That hedge should be taken seriously, and it should be stated more strongly than the passage does: Koe is offering an interpretive frame for ordinary distress, not a clinical account of mental illness. Nothing in the corpus presents psychic entropy as a diagnosis or as a substitute for treatment, and a reader dealing with a diagnosed condition should not read this section as advice about it.

The image he uses in the same passage is one of his best. When you are solely focused on the negative thing, you cannot see the puzzle: "you're focused on one pixel of a beautiful image, and that pixel just happens to be blurred and pixelated and ugly", until you zoom out and see the beauty of everything. He then redefines feeling lost in the same terms: it usually means you are in a chaotic life situation that you lack the knowledge or skill to make sense of. You are not where you want to be "because you can't lift the mental weight that allows you to get there" (same source). This is the logic behind his recurring claim that "the mind is a muscle": the capacity to make sense of complexity is trained, like strength, and a person who feels lost is, in this frame, someone facing a weight they have not yet trained for. (The training itself, through reading, learning and skill building, is the subject of Part VI.)

#### The mind craves order

Psychic entropy describes what happens when order is not maintained. Koe pairs it with a claim about what the mind wants: "The mind craves order; it hates chaos; it hates entropy." Yet it tends toward disorder if we do not maintain order, or do not understand how to navigate the chaos so that there is clarity. If we do not, "you start to go back to an unconscious state and let things affect you that shouldn't be affecting you" (**Source:** `Stop Trying To Be Unique... Do This Instead.md`, 2022-05-28). The phrase "go back to an unconscious state" connects this chapter to Part I: the unconscious, programmed state described there is, in mechanical terms, the state a mind returns to when its order lapses.

The craving for order explains a great deal of behavior that otherwise looks irrational. In a 2024 video, Koe uses it to explain why people choose the default path: because the mind tends toward disorder, "we crave order, security, comfort, goals, and clarity to achieve them." That is why you go to school, get a job and keep going regardless of how unfulfilling it is. The same craving shapes the information you consume: you surround yourself with physical and digital information that confirms who you currently are, not who you want to be. Anything outside your beliefs is perceived as a threat and triggers what he calls an "emotional mind closing reaction" that prevents you from seeing the truth. His practical remedy is modest: if you want to reprogram your mind, follow new people (**Source:** `Change Your Life In 365 Hours (The New Rich Focus On These Tasks Daily).md`, 2024-06-02).

This is an important bridge. Chapter 3 described the law of conceptual survival: identity defends itself as a body does. Here the same defense appears in thermodynamic language. An identity is a kind of order, and the mind will prefer a familiar order, even a confining one, over the disorder of the unknown. The school, the job and the familiar feed are not chosen because they are good but because they are ordered. The task is not to eliminate the craving, which is how minds work, but to satisfy it with an order you choose.

The order you choose has to be maintained daily. In a 2022 video on cheap dopamine, Koe ties his vision system directly to psychic entropy: "your mind tends toward disorder if you do not maintain the order in your mind with vision, goals, priorities, and reminding and acting on them daily." The consequence is concrete. If you stop for a week, you will be overwhelmed and anxious and wonder why you are not seeing results. The reason is simple: you stopped doing the things that got results in the first place and lost the momentum you had built up (**Source:** `Why Cheap Dopamine Prevents You From Becoming High Value.md`, 2022-10-09). The four verbs of the maintenance list (having a vision, goals and priorities; *reminding* yourself of them; and *acting* on them) show what "energy" means for a mind. A written vision that is never reread and never acted upon is a library with no staff.

#### Chaos is the default state

The strongest form of the thesis is not that the mind *can* fall into chaos but that chaos is where it starts. "The human mind bounces between order and chaos. Chaos is our default state. Clarity is the byproduct of ordered information that fills our consciousness. Our mind is clear when what we pay attention to is clear" (**Source:** `A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Life (In 6-12 Months).md`, 2023-07-25). Koe offers this as "an explanation for human behavior", and the last sentence of the quotation is its hinge: clarity is not a property the mind has on its own; it is borrowed from whatever the mind is attending to. A clear task makes a clear mind; a scattered feed makes a scattered one.

His evidence is a reported observation, whose source he does not name: people say that the most miserable part of their lives is Sunday between 10 a.m. and 2 p.m., when they have nothing to do. The point he draws from it is counterintuitive: "People say that they hate their jobs, yet jobs provide a clear state of mind that is arguably much more enjoyable than being at home alone with your thoughts." Humans fill their days with activities that structure the mind (playing games, designing, creating), preferably by investing attention in something useful (same source). Read with the previous subsection, the observation explains why the default path is so durable. A job one dislikes still supplies the one thing the mind craves most: an external structure for attention. Leaving it without an internal structure to replace it leads straight to Sunday morning.

**Complementary context:** Csikszentmihalyi's *Flow* reports experience-sampling findings of this kind: people tend to report more negative moods when alone with nothing to do, and many experience more flow at work than in their free time, even while saying they would prefer to be working less. Koe's "Sunday 10 a.m. to 2 p.m." detail is a specific version that this book has not been able to trace to its origin.

Koe also gives the default state a developmental reading. In a 2023 video on how to use one's twenties, his conclusion is that you realize your potential "by becoming aware of the rut that you were digging yourself in just by the default state of humanity — that's how you're born — where your consciousness is chaotic unless we order it" (**Source:** `Your 20s Are Meant To Build (How To Not Waste Them).md`, 2023-04-20). Awareness of the default comes first, as in Chapter 2's account of awareness as the first exit; the ordering follows, and in that video it takes the form of three things to build: the body, the mind and a business.

The *Art of Focus* summary (2024) gives the most complete account of the default state and its exit. "The default state of consciousness is chaos": unless the mind is engaged with a thought, a task or a stimulus, it wanders, and it wanders negatively, toward anxiety or boredom. Allowing randomness increases the potential for negativity: "for every good thought there are 100 negative thoughts" when there is no purpose, path or priority aligned with your ideal future to ground your attention. His example: you fantasize about making a lot of money but have no clarity on how, so the mind wanders to bills, work and student loans, splintering until you are "drowning in overwhelm." And the exit: "The path out is the path of mastery: the utter dedication to creating the best life for yourself through self-education, skill acquisition and daily practice" (**Source:** `The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md`, 2024-01-18).

The triad *purpose, path, priority* deserves a note. It names what grounds attention: a purpose (a reason to move forward), a path (a route that supplies clarity) and a priority (a problem to work on today). It is the mental version of the "goal, process, problem" definition of a system given in section 5.1, and it will return in Part IV as the structure of direction. The *path of mastery*, here, is the long-term form of that grounding: not a single plan but a lifelong commitment to learning and practice that keeps supplying the mind with ordered information. (George Leonard's notion of mastery, which Koe uses elsewhere under a similar name, is discussed in Chapter 15.)

#### Losing the coherent line of thought

What does the slide into chaos look like at the scale of seconds? Koe's description is one of his most precise. "When we are alone, psychic entropy ensues." And: "Psychic entropy is when we lose the frame or the order of our mind by letting go of a coherent line of thought." It happens more than you think. Sitting around alone with your thoughts, you may hold a coherent line of thought "for like two to three seconds"; then you catch on to some other thought, the thought process goes unconscious, and you cannot track where your thoughts will end up. "Our mind grasps at negative thoughts and then they multiply." The result is an unordered state of mind: feeling lost, overwhelmed and anxious (**Source:** `A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Life (In 6-12 Months).md`, 2023-07-25).

A *coherent line of thought*, in this usage, is a thread of thinking that holds the mind's frame together; when it is released, psychic entropy enters. The "two to three seconds" is an illustration from introspection, not a measurement, but it makes the claim testable in a modest way: anyone can sit alone for a minute and observe how long a deliberate train of thought lasts before something else takes over.

The cure follows directly from the diagnosis. If disorder is the loss of a coherent line, order is the ability to hold or recover one. "The cure to feeling lost, overwhelmed and anxious is knowing how to order your mind; it is knowing how to direct your attention." He names two tools: meditation, which teaches you to focus on your breath, and metacognition, which teaches you to question your thoughts. And he states the goal of both in a sentence that redefines solitude: "Learning to be alone is learning to order your mind in any circumstance for enjoyment" (same source). Being alone is the test case because it removes external structure. A person who can order their mind on a Sunday morning with nothing to do can order it anywhere.

#### How a thought splinters

The mechanism by which one negative thought becomes a hundred is what Koe calls *splintering* (he also speaks of thoughts "multiplying" or focus "splitting"). He gives several examples, each slightly different, and it is worth following them in order because together they show the stages of the process.

The first example concerns health. For every good thought, such as achieving good health, there are a hundred bad thoughts, in part because of information marketed to you, for instance by mainstream media. Without goals, a vision and clarity framing your mind so that distractions cannot penetrate, focus splits. You hear that "eggs are worse than cigarettes, they cause cancer"; you think, "I smoke, so that's worse; why even pursue this health goal?"; and you arrive at what Koe calls a *holistic but negative worldview*: "I don't have money for quality ingredients; I can't afford a gym membership." Then business enters: without clarity on how to start or run a business or fix money problems, the thought splits further. People get overwhelmed and anxious, he concludes, because they lack clarity, direction or aim, and have not educated themselves with enough quality information to focus confidently and make daily progress (**Source:** `Focus Is A Superpower (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md`, 2023-07-11). The coinage is apt: the worldview that results is "holistic" because it connects every domain of life, and "negative" because what connects them is a chain of reasons to give up.

The second example shows the moment the process starts. After identifying a problem in your life, things get scary: "the unknown creeps nearer, you're knee deep in the water and want to sprint back to shore." The thought of the problem multiplies because the mind has lost its constraints and boundaries: "psychic entropy, or disorder in the mind." You notice in the mirror that you are a bit overweight. Then you think about not taking off your shirt at the pool, about what your friends or partner think and how it affects the relationship, about all the hard things you would have to do to lose weight, the foods to give up and the exercise to do, and about what happens if you keep gaining: heart disease, chronic disease. Without a structure to order your mind (a plan, broken down into steps), that uncertainty is hard to bear, and most people shy away and quit (**Source:** `The Fastest Way To Ruin Your Life (Avoid This While You're Young).md`, 2025-01-12). The point here is that identifying a problem is not enough. A problem without a plan is an opening through which disorder enters; the plan is what supplies the "constraints and boundaries" that keep the thought from branching.

The third example, already mentioned in section 5.1, shows how quickly the branching runs into fear: a chest pain, then Grandpa's heart attack, then the ten heart-healthy foods you have neglected, then the effect an early death might have on your family (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md`, 2024-07-21).

Koe also offers an account of *why* attention splinters, framed in terms of time. "When we hold too much of the past or future within the contents of our consciousness, that's when chaos ensues, that's when entropy happens." You are split off from focusing on the system that actualizes your vision; attention is "manhandled" toward many things and splinters into chaotic negative thoughts, which dismantles the system your mind operates on "consistently, singularly, that brings meaning to your life." The alternative is singular focus on a vision, with the ability to shift focus in order to "collect high signal information" that helps you actualize it (**Source:** `How To Reset Your Life In 7 Days.md`, 2024-01-07). *High signal information*, in his usage, is information that serves the vision. The formulation is careful: singular focus does not mean refusing all other input, but filtering it. This connects with Chapter 2's account of psychological time: a mind pulled into a familiar past or a predictable future is a mind whose order has already been breached.

#### The puncture and the reversal

The most vivid of Koe's images for the onset of psychic entropy comes from 2023. Giving attention to a negative thought "creates this puncture in our system or our frame of reference", which lets many more negative thoughts in. It is like a plane door opening at altitude and everything getting sucked out, except that you should imagine it sucked *into* your mind. The mind narrows on the negative thought, closes off, and forgets that everything is fine; thoughts multiply, and focus bounces between them "like a pinball" (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (You Can't Escape The Matrix).md`, 2023-09-05). The *puncture* is the moment a frame of reference, which normally filters what enters consciousness, is breached by a single attended thought. (This is a different idea from the "puncture the known" Koe uses elsewhere for deliberately breaking out of a comfort zone.)

The same passage gives the reversal, and Koe calls it "very practical." Create the habit of *zooming out, gaining clarity and zooming back in*. First, realize what is happening. Then zoom out: think long-term, think of your vision and goals and of everything that has happened in the day, deconstruct the situation. Pull from all of it to create clarity. Then come back into what you were doing (same source). The structure is a loop: a narrowing caused by a thought is countered by a deliberate widening, which supplies the context that the negative thought had pushed out, followed by a deliberate narrowing onto the task. The zoom model is developed fully in Chapter 6 (section 6.3); here it is the first-aid technique for psychic entropy.

Koe's own summary of why most people do not use it returns to the room. "If you don't put effort into cleaning something like your room, eventually with time it's just naturally going to turn into a disgusting nest of filth. So, related to the mind, in psychic entropy, most people's minds are a disgusting nest of filth because they don't take the care to solve their own problems; they focus on them, it multiplies, and it ruins their life, because they can't zoom out and practice what I talk about in my book to learn how to navigate their mind" (**Source:** `Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md`, 2024-02-18). The distinction between *focusing on* a problem and *solving* it is the crux. Attention spent on a problem without a path toward its solution feeds the splintering; attention spent solving it (the gerund again) is the energy that restores order.

#### Clarity: a path from where you are to where you want to be

If chaos is the default, its opposite is *clarity*, a common word that Koe uses in a defined sense. In 2022 he opened a video by saying that if he could attribute his progress to one thing, it would be clarity, and that many successful people would agree. He equates clarity with "ordered consciousness", or "the ability to create based on a specific vision they have for the future of a project, for their life, for anything, macro, micro, on all planes of reality." Its mechanism is that "clarity is what makes focus seamless": it is what lets you channel your attention through focus. And the urgency comes from the times: in the age of information, technological advancement and distractions "being thrown at you from all directions", focus is "an absolute must", and clarity is its precondition (**Source:** `How To Create A Better Life For Yourself.md`, 2022-06-12).

A few months later he gave the operational definition: clarity is having a way, a path, to get from where you are to where you want to be. "You don't have a way of getting there, right? That is what clarity is" (**Source:** `If You Are High Value, Start A One-Person Business.md`, 2022-10-02). The absence of that path, the "lack of clarity", is what he names as the cause of overwhelm, anxiety and psychic entropy. The definition is structural: it does not locate clarity in a feeling of certainty but in the existence of a route. Overwhelm is, in this frame, the experience of a gap with no visible bridge, which is why Koe later prescribes attending to the first step instead of the whole distance.

In 2025 he gave clarity a two-part formula, the *clarity equation*, in the context of self-confidence. A lack of self-confidence is brutal: you question your actions and find no sustainable motivation, because everything is clouded by doubt. "The truth is a lack of self-confidence comes down to a lack of clarity." The first half of clarity is *perspective*, solved with a vision "that gets stronger with faith." The second half is *knowing what to focus on*, which is "not a matter of luck, it's a matter of strategy": you need a plan (**Source:** `How To Change Your Life So Fast It Feels Illegal.md`, 2025-03-02). Put as an equation, clarity = perspective (a vision, strengthened by faith) + knowing what to focus on (a plan). Both halves matter. A vision without a plan gives direction without a next step; a plan without a vision gives steps without a reason, and neither produces the confidence that comes from knowing both where you are going and what to do today.

The same year he named the connecting structure the *clarity bridge*. Presenting ways to digest what you read, he invokes the idea, which he attributes to Aristotle, that knowledge is for action, "because most people don't need more motivation. They need more clarity. You already have goals and ambitions but you never pursue them because point A, where you are, and point B, where you want to be, need a bridge of smaller goals and actions you can take to trek between the two. And without the clarity bridge, anxiety and overwhelm start to seep in" (**Source:** `How To Read Books Fast With AI (And Remember What You Read).md`, 2025-04-20). The bridge is made of smaller goals, which makes this the seed of the goal hierarchy developed in Chapter 8.

There is a limit to clarity, and Koe states it plainly. With a meaningful goal "out there that we don't have too much clarity on, and we will never have clarity on because it's so far away from us", there is a gap: you cannot know exactly what it will be like "because you don't have it right now." "So you have to bridge that gap by creating a system and gaining clarity on how to get there" (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16). This is an important qualification of the whole theme. Clarity about the destination is never complete; what can be clear is the next stretch of the path. The system is how one acts with full clarity on the near term and partial clarity on the far term.

Without clear goals, Koe argues, help becomes impossible in both directions. You cannot help yourself, because you do not know what you want, so you cannot start making the mistakes that lead to achieving it, discover what you don't want, and use that negative feedback to correct your actions over time. And others cannot help you, because you cannot communicate what you want, which sets you up for "a life of assumptions, expectations and never getting what you want", and you cannot find people with similar goals, observe and study them, reach out, befriend them and build a network that gets you there faster. Instead your mind is filled with goals such as "go to school, get a job, marry this person, watch Netflix every night, order junk food all the time", and lacks the opposite goals ("building your mind, body, spirit and business") that would let you spot opportunities in that domain (**Source:** `You Won’t Be The Same Person In 6 Months (Master Anything, Fast).md`, 2023-12-17). The argument is a preview of the next chapter's central idea: a goal is what makes certain information visible. Without it, both feedback and opportunity pass unnoticed.

#### Clarity over motivation

The practical consequence of all this is one of Koe's most repeated maxims. In "The 4-Hour Workday" (2023), the fourth of his ten commandments is to gain clarity on your *lever-moving tasks* (the few tasks that actually move a project forward, treated in detail in Chapter 12). Its core claim: "Most people think they need motivation when in reality they need clarity." The guiding question is practical: "How can you use one to two hours of your week to set yourself up for seamless work sessions?" (**Source:** `The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md`, 2023-01-01; reissued in `Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md`, 2024-03-21). In 2025 he paired it with a second reframe: "You don't need more motivation. You need more clarity. You don't need more time. You need more focus" (**Source:** `The Deep Work Routine That Changed My Life.md`, 2025-05-18). The pairing is symmetrical. The felt lack of motivation is really a lack of clarity; the felt lack of time is really a lack of focus; and both are addressed by understanding how the mind and attention work, the subject of the next chapter.

Koe describes the system he uses to generate clarity, and it is worth reconstructing because it shows the principle turned into a schedule. It has two parts.

1. **Weekend clarity-generating tasks.** "Every weekend, let's say Saturday, Sunday, I usually swap a few of my lever-moving tasks for a clarity-generating task." On Sundays, concretely, he outlines his newsletters, plans the week, writes down ideas for tweets, threads and other content, and works out what is needed to keep his products moving (his example is the course Modern Mastery and what content goes into it). The reasoning is that the work sessions of the coming week will be "seamless" only if the decisions about what to do in them have been made beforehand, at a time set aside for deciding.
2. **Daily walks.** Outside his hard-scheduled items, he takes several walks a day for clarity. Around 11 a.m. he goes on a walk of 30 to 45 minutes, listens to lectures and generates content ideas; sometimes, if he is not in a busy area, he writes or outlines on his phone. He refers viewers to another video for his full daily routine (the transcript renders its title ambiguously).

(**Source:** `The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md`, 2023-01-01; `Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md`, 2024-03-21.)

The two parts address different time scales. The weekend session orders the week; the walks order the day and refill the queue of ideas that the next sessions will draw on. Neither is "work" in the narrow sense, which is the point: clarity is produced by activities that look like rest or planning, and an hour of it saves many hours of hesitation.

In 2025 Koe added a tool to this system: the *clarity coach*, an AI workflow. After producing what he calls a "life reset map" (a written plan for his life, derived from his reading), he asks an AI assistant to "be my clarity coach: break down my life reset map and guide me through it." The output is a set of recommendations, a challenge and a reflection, which he refers to every day. He uses it alongside a "reading partner" workflow: the reading partner helps him take notes while reading; the clarity coach guides him and helps him "ask questions or overcome procrastination or limiting beliefs as I act on my day" (**Source:** `How To Read Books Fast With AI (And Remember What You Read).md`, 2025-04-20). In the vocabulary of this chapter, the clarity coach is a maintenance device: it supplies the daily "reminding" that keeps an ordered plan from decaying. (The reading workflow is treated in Chapter 16.)

#### Distraction as a problem in the system

Distraction is where psychic entropy becomes visible in a working day, and Koe treats it as a symptom of missing clarity rather than a failure of willpower. "The first point is that you aren't productive because you don't have clarity." It may seem obvious, he admits, but the mechanism matters: "you focus on one distraction, you don't correct yourself, and then slowly more distractions come in and make it incredibly hard to get back your focus" (**Source:** `The Deep Work Routine That Changed My Life.md`, 2025-05-18). This is the entropy dynamic applied to attention: one uncorrected lapse lowers the cost of the next, as one shirt on the floor lowers the cost of the second. A year earlier he had drawn the practical conclusion: "Distractions are problems in your productivity system that must be solved if you want to unlock laser focus." And he named two signals for identifying distractions and correcting yourself: boredom and anxiety (**Source:** `Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md`, 2024-02-18).

Treating distraction as a *problem in a system* has a consequence: it must be defined relative to that system. In 2023 Koe narrowed his definition accordingly. "Realize that distractions are subjective." Hanging out with friends, video games and partying are not bad in themselves; "it's bad if it's pulling you away from that one-hour time block." If you have one meaningful project and one hour a day for it, anything that pulls you away from that hour is a distraction. Once the hour is done, go be "distracted": at that point it is no longer a distraction and may be very important for maintaining the other pillars of your life. Work in the morning, knock out your priority tasks, and enjoy the rest of your day (**Source:** `Focus Is A Superpower (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md`, 2023-07-11). This is a refinement of an earlier definition in which a distraction was anything not aligned with the vision. Koe gives no reason for the change, and the two definitions can coexist at different scales: the broad one applies to the direction of a life, the narrow one to the hours of a day. The narrow one has the advantage of not turning leisure, friendship or play into enemies.

What makes typical distractions powerful? Koe's answer is that they fill a vacuum. When something pops up to distract you, sit with it and "filter it through a purpose filter": is this important, and how will I act according to this problem? Social media and the news "don't matter in your life"; the reason they matter so much to so many people, to the point that people build their identity around them, is that "they do not have a purpose to bring their attention back to; they do not have a hierarchy of goals to frame and structure their attention" (**Source:** `Society Is A Pyramid Scheme (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md`, 2023-01-15). The *purpose filter* is the practice of passing each stimulus through the question of whether it serves your purpose. Without a purpose, there is nothing to filter against, and whatever arrives wins by default.

The habit that makes the purpose filter work is the zoom loop already described, now framed as focus training. Hold the frame in the back of your mind, "the focus channel from which you view daily situations." The habit being formed is "zooming out, gaining perspective, and acting from a place where we can make better decisions" and perceive better, so that you can identify the problems in the way of your goals. You decrease distraction by "consistently and habitually bringing your focus back from distraction to what you deem as important", where *important*, *signal* or *significant* means whatever aligns with actualizing your goals. And this is, he adds, how you reap good dopamine: if your goal is "getting absolutely jacked" and you see a helpful tip on social media, "boom, that's meaningful dopamine": save it and implement it (**Source:** `Focus Is A Superpower (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md`, 2023-07-11). The example matters because it shows that the same stimulus (a social media post) can be noise or signal depending on the goal. *Meaningful dopamine* is reward that comes from information or effort aligned with your goals, as opposed to novelty for its own sake; the concept is developed in Chapter 11.

Clarity also protects focus passively, by narrowing it. In a 2023 video Koe lists "narrowed focus" as one psychological effect of having a clear project. When he works on something like his newsletter, "you know exactly what to do, you have the clarity to act, you have the education to understand, the prior resources, and a plan to actually make it work, and especially with a deadline, like me posting a newsletter every Saturday, I need to get that done. And when your mind narrows in, distractions can't really block it" (the sense is that distractions cannot break in). "This is another way to end just being a slave to distractions" (**Source:** `A Dangerous (But Effective) Strategy To Get Out Of A Rut.md`, 2023-10-02). The list (clarity, education, resources, a plan, a deadline) amounts to the full set of conditions under which attention closes around a task. Remove any of them and an opening appears.

Finally, Koe warns about a form of distraction that disguises itself as opportunity. In 2026 he describes someone on Instagram announcing "the latest Claude skill" you can download, which does this, this and this. You feel a missed opportunity because you do not use the tool that way and feel you need to learn it. It "could genuinely be helpful." But to protect your mind from manipulation, "you have to ruthlessly maintain your priorities and your values and your goals", because that skill may have nothing to do with them and you may get sucked in. "That's the definition of distraction" (**Source:** `Learn This Skill If You Want To Win In The Next 2-3 Years.md`, 2026-07-05). He calls the lever being pulled "survival tension": the fear of falling behind. The example shows the limit of the purpose filter as a passive habit. When the distraction is useful in general, only a firmly held set of priorities can decide that it is not useful *to you, now*. The purpose filter is not a list of banned inputs; it is a standing decision about what you are building.

### 5.3 Ordering Consciousness

Section 5.2 described what happens when the mind is left to itself. This section describes the opposite state, and the skill of producing it. Koe draws on two vocabularies here that sound almost identical and should be kept apart. *Order in consciousness* is Csikszentmihalyi's term, which Koe quotes, for the optimal state of inner experience. *Ordering consciousness* (or "the ability to order consciousness") is Koe's own phrase for an active skill: creating certainty, signal and meaning out of their opposites, in yourself and in others. The corpus does not say whether he treats the two as synonyms; the most faithful reading is that the second is the practice whose result is the first.

#### Csikszentmihalyi's order in consciousness

No external passage appears more often in this part of the corpus than one from *Flow*. Koe quotes it, almost word for word, in at least five videos between 2023 and 2025:

> "The optimal state of inner experience is one in which there is order in consciousness. This happens when psychic energy or attention is invested in realistic goals and when skills match the opportunities for action. The pursuit of a goal brings order and awareness because a person must concentrate attention on the task at hand and momentarily forget everything else."

The attribution varies. In one 2023 video the book is named only as "Flow" with no author; in others the transcript garbles the name ("mihi chiet mihi", "Mihi Chet mihi"); elsewhere he names Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi as the author of *Flow*. The passage itself is constant, and so is what he takes from it: three claims that fit his entropy framework exactly. First, attention is a form of energy ("psychic energy or attention"), so the energy that the law of entropy requires for maintaining order is, in a mind, attention. Second, order arises when that energy is invested in *goals*, and specifically *realistic* goals, matched to skill. Third, the mechanism is concentration: pursuing a goal forces attention onto the task and lets "everything else" drop away, which is precisely what prevents the splintering described in section 5.2.

What changes from one video to the next is the use he puts the quote to, and following those uses shows how central it is to his system:

| Year | Use Koe makes of the quotation | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | To argue that we must learn "how to focus your attention to reverse psychic entropy, or disorder in the mind", and to understand how identity comes into play | `The Daily Routine That Changed My Life (4 Habits Most People Ignore).md`, 2023-11-19 |
| 2023 (reused 2024) | To support the claim that goals order the mind; he notes that it "matches up quite well" with Maxwell Maltz's account of the mind as a goal-seeking mechanism | `You Won’t Be The Same Person In 6 Months (Master Anything, Fast).md`, 2023-12-17; `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03 |
| 2025 | To open the "more practical" step of treating work sessions "like quests in a video game" | `The Deep Work Routine That Changed My Life.md`, 2025-05-18 |
| 2025 | To introduce the step of organizing all the components of a life plan into a coherent plan and turning life into a video game | `How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md`, 2025-12-28 |

The Maltz connection is the most theoretically significant. In Chapter 3 the mind appeared, following Maltz's *Psycho-Cybernetics*, as a goal-striving machine that steers toward whatever target it is given. Csikszentmihalyi adds the experiential side of the same fact: when the machine has a target it can reach, consciousness feels ordered. Chapter 6 will join the two in the idea of the goal as a lens.

Koe also quotes two further passages from Csikszentmihalyi. In 2022, a description of control: "The mark of a person who is in control of consciousness [is] the ability to focus attention at will, to be oblivious to distractions, to concentrate for as long as it takes to achieve a goal and not longer, and the person who can do this usually enjoys the normal course of everyday life." His interpretation is that *enjoyment* is the main benefit of having a vision, having goals, making sense of the world and having clarity on the direction of your life (**Source:** `Stop Trying To Be Unique... Do This Instead.md`, 2022-05-28). The phrase "and not longer" is easy to miss and worth noticing: control of consciousness includes the ability to *stop* concentrating once the goal is reached. This is the psychological basis of the time-boxed workday Koe defends in Chapter 12; it also anticipates the subjective definition of distraction in section 5.2, where what is a distraction during the block is legitimate life after it.

In 2025, a passage on effort: optimal experience depends on the ability to control what happens in consciousness moment by moment, and so each person has to achieve it through their own efforts and creativity. Koe uses it to support the claim that a plan narrows attention: once attention closes around a task, distractions become harder to register (**Source:** `How To Change Your Life So Fast It Feels Illegal.md`, 2025-03-02). Two consequences follow. Order in consciousness cannot be supplied from outside; even the best plan works only if the person keeps directing attention through it, moment by moment. And it is a creative act, not just a disciplined one, which prepares the claim later in this section that creation *is* the ordering of consciousness.

**Complementary context:** Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi (1934–2021) was a Hungarian-American psychologist whose research, much of it based on the experience sampling method (participants report what they are doing and feeling at random moments during the day), led to the concept of flow: a state of complete absorption in an activity whose challenge matches one's skill and which offers clear goals and immediate feedback. *Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience* (1990) is the book Koe quotes; flow itself is the subject of Chapter 10.

#### The point of focus

Koe's own synthesis of these ideas, from 2023, reframes what focus is for. People who are not overwhelmed are taking on a challenge their skill matches (so they are not anxious) and not too low a challenge. The latter is the trap of the corporate job done over and over: it leads to boredom, then to the thought "I could be doing something better", then to a negative thought loop. "This is the point of focus: you have to learn to order your mind. You need to be able to create clarity from chaos," to have direction every morning, and to feel that you are making progress, so that you can tap into some degree of flow, for which you need environmental feedback that your progress is meaningful. He appeals to the reader's experience: you have felt "how amazing clarity and pure progress feels"; understanding how to recreate it is how you create daily enjoyment (**Source:** `Focus Is A Superpower (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md`, 2023-07-11).

The passage reverses a common view. Focus is usually treated as a means to productivity; here its point is the ordered state of mind it produces, of which productivity is a side effect. Its components are listed with some care: clarity created out of chaos (not received), direction renewed every morning (maintenance again), and a felt sense of progress backed by real feedback. Anxiety and boredom appear as the two ways of falling out of the balance between challenge and skill, which is Csikszentmihalyi's flow model in Koe's terms.

#### Psychic negentropy: meaning as ordered consciousness

In 2026 Koe gave the opposite of psychic entropy a name and used it to answer a question that had been implicit all along: what is meaning? When attention is fragmented, scattered and pulled in competing directions, that is psychic entropy; it feels like anxiety, boredom, restlessness: chaos. When attention is invested in a complex, challenging activity with clear feedback, that is *psychic negentropy*; it feels like flow, purpose, meaning: order. So "meaning isn't something you find out there in the world. It's a state of consciousness that emerges when your attention is ordered towards something complex enough to fully engage you" (**Source:** `I'm begging you to start writing essays (even if you hate writing).md`, 2026-04-05).

He calibrates the challenge with a numerical image: "If you are level 10, a level one challenge is boring and a level 20 challenge is daunting, but a level 11 challenge is just enough to lock you in." And he adds a second sense in which meaning arises from order: meaning is the experience of ordered consciousness, and it is *also* created through the process of ordering consciousness: taking chaos and creating structure, wrestling with complexity until it coheres. If you have ever been stuck in a rut, wrestled with where your life should go, and then had a burst of clarity that launched you into an intense season of life, he says, you have experienced this directly (same source). No source is named for "psychic negentropy" in the transcript.

**Psychic negentropy**, in Koe's 2026 usage, is the state in which attention is invested in a complex, challenging activity with clear feedback; it is felt as flow, purpose and meaning, and it is the opposite of psychic entropy.

This passage is the culmination of the term's evolution described in section 5.2: from a concept attributed to *Flow* (2022), to unattributed vocabulary (2022–2025), to a gloss of his own (2024), to a complete pair with a definition of meaning attached (2026). It also changes what the chapter's central contrast means. Order versus chaos was, at first, a matter of comfort versus distress. Here it becomes the difference between a meaningful and a meaningless life, and the "level 11" image ties that difference to a precise condition: a challenge just beyond current skill. The full theory of that edge, and of life as a game designed to stay on it, is the subject of Chapter 10.

#### Consciousness as the fabric of reality

Koe's most ambitious statements about ordering consciousness come from a 2022 video about making money as a creative, and they rest on a metaphysical claim that he states without argument: "Consciousness is the fabric of reality; everything that you are conscious of right now you are projecting meaning onto to create your own reality" (**Source:** `How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md`, 2022-06-25). Taken at face value, this is a philosophical position (close to the idealist traditions that treat consciousness as primary); taken as he uses it, it is a premise for a practical point. If what you experience is shaped by the meaning you project onto it, then the way consciousness is ordered determines the reality you live in, and the ability to order it is the most fundamental ability there is. The reader can accept the practical point without accepting the metaphysics; the corpus does not develop the metaphysical claim further in this context. (The limits of concepts and the mystical side of Koe's thought are treated in Chapter 40.)

From this premise comes the coinage: "The ability to order consciousness is the greatest skill of the 21st century." He defines it as the ability to create certainty from uncertainty, signal from noise, meaning from struggle. "It's polar thinking, it's polarity, it's two sides of the same coin": chaos and clarity. His illustration: put someone in an unfamiliar mental or physical environment, thrown off their routine, and they feel lost; put someone there who understands that environment and "can control consciousness within that environment", and they are much more at peace, because they have made sense of it (same source). **Ordering consciousness**, in Koe's sense, is therefore the ability to turn uncertainty, noise and struggle into certainty, signal and meaning. The example makes clear that the environment is identical for both people; what differs is the order each can bring to it. This is the positive version of the "mental weight" image in section 5.2: the person at peace is the one who can lift it.

Two weeks earlier, in another 2022 video, he had given the same skill a different name. He calls clarity "the greatest skill that you can develop of the 21st century: clarity and connection," and quotes one of his own tweets: "Clarity primes you to get into flow. Clarity primes you to make the sale. Clarity primes you not to overthink. The ability to order consciousness in yourself and others is the most valuable skill of the 21st century." And, more briefly: "Clarity is what brings action" (**Source:** `How To Create A Better Life For Yourself.md`, 2022-06-12). Three primed effects (flow, selling, not overthinking) and two directions (inward and outward) are packed into the tweet. The inward direction is everything this chapter has discussed so far. The outward direction, ordering consciousness in others, is the bridge from Koe's psychology to his account of content and business, and it is taken up at the end of this section.

A caution about the superlative is in order. "The greatest skill of the 21st century" is a phrase Koe has attached to several different skills over the years: simplifying complex topics (section 5.4), the ability to order consciousness, decreasing the time between idea and execution, persuasive communication, writing, agency and, most recently, an understanding of human nature. Koe himself has said that statements of this kind should be read metaphorically, as a way of "getting the point" across, not literally. The candidates are less rivals than descriptions of one capacity at different levels: ordering consciousness is the psychological core, and writing, persuasion and simplification are the forms it takes when it is aimed at other people.

#### Creation as ordering consciousness

In the same 2022 video, Koe defines creation itself in these terms. Nature, you, God, the universe, "whatever form or interpretation of the higher intelligence", is creative; things constantly create and grow internally ("an important buzzword"), generating their own energy. "Creation is the process of molding, controlling and ordering consciousness." Its raw material is tapped from the subconscious: the information you have been made aware of throughout your life, what you have been exposed to, "the energetic frequencies, if you will." Your job is to take that material, make the creation with your mind, and put it out into the world so that others benefit. We make sense of information, he adds, through our senses (**Source:** `How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md`, 2022-06-25).

*Creation*, then, is a common word that Koe uses in a defined sense: the process of molding, controlling and ordering consciousness, drawing on what the subconscious has absorbed and returning it to the world in a form others can use. The definition joins the two halves of this chapter. Psychic entropy is the state of a mind that is not ordering what it contains; creation is what a mind does when it orders what it contains *and externalizes the result*. This is why, for Koe, creating is not one activity among many but the general form of a healthy relationship with information.

He returned to the theme in 2023 with a definition of content. Content is "the things that are held or included in something; in other words, everything is content. Content is structured information." Information is how we learn, grow and adapt as a species; it occupies, shapes and comes out of our mind, and "we use the structure of our psyche to create content." Creation, in turn, is "to bring into existence; that's what humans do: they create, they solve problems, they build solutions. That is your edge." Humans who do not are "the robots or cogs in the machine of society": "to be human is to expand, transcend and create; to be robotic is to forget that you have this gift and allow your mind to mold to the whims of others with schooling and employment." Hence: "At its root, content creation is just being human." "Everyone is a content creator; some just choose to do it with intention and get paid to do so" on the internet, where physical boundaries do not limit reach. Creating is how you make your writing and speaking more valuable, contributing to and profiting from the global digital community "like our ancestors did their local physical community" (**Source:** `How Intelligent Creators Will Make Money In 2024 (The Build Teach Earn Method).md`, 2023-12-03).

The same passage introduces an analogy between the mind and the internet that Koe develops elsewhere. If your mind is a complex set of systems, like a computer, then the words you speak, write or communicate are like the media on the front end of the internet. In the summary of *The Art of Focus* (2024) he lays it out completely: "There is content in consciousness and content in the collective consciousness." The analogy has four terms:

| | The individual mind | The internet |
|---|---|---|
| **Front end** | Content in your consciousness: what you are aware of, and what you speak and write | Content in the collective consciousness: the media people publish |
| **Back end** | The unconscious programming that shapes who you are | The programmed operating system (code) that houses the content |

(**Source:** `The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md`, 2024-01-18; `How Intelligent Creators Will Make Money In 2024 (The Build Teach Earn Method).md`, 2023-12-03.)

The analogy does two things. It ties this chapter back to Part I: the "back end" of the mind is the conditioning described there, the program that shapes what reaches the front end. And it supports an argument about contribution: "Putting content into virtual reality is how you contribute to humanity through business" (same 2024 source). Koe quotes his own book on this point in another 2024 video: "Content is an extension of the personal and collective mind. We put out our ideas, beliefs and opinions that form a digital society, cultures and world. Content on the internet is content in the collective consciousness." He calls the passage "rather esoteric" and notes that the book pairs it with practical advice; he also calls himself a writer "because that's what I do every single morning" (**Source:** `This High-Income Skill Will Make You Irreplaceable (Full Guide).md`, 2024-01-28). The self-assessment is useful for reading this whole group of ideas: the front-end/back-end model and the "collective consciousness" are interpretive images, and Koe presents them as such.

#### Condensing information: the zip file

If creation orders information, its most valuable product is information that has been ordered *for someone else*, compressed so that they can absorb it with less effort. Koe makes this argument in a 2022 video about creators as a career path. Creators, personal brands, thought leaders and online educators condense information. He states that humans can process around 126 bits of information per second, the amount we are conscious of. His illustration: four years of an "outdated formal college curriculum" might be 60 million bits of information. Creators have obtained results and simplified them into a process that helps people get results faster; they put it into courses and content, so that what took 60 million bits now takes maybe 10 million, "and this process goes on; that's what creation is." Creating better solutions lets us expand our minds and develop intellectually much faster (**Source:** `If You Want To Secure Your Future… Follow This New Career Path.md`, 2022-05-21). The numbers for the curriculum are illustrative, not measured; Koe uses them to make the ratio vivid.

The distribution of condensed information is what media is for: "Media is how we distribute the valuable condensed information, like the zip file." The 10 million bits that you "expand in your mind" or "plug into your mind" give you what used to take 60 million (same source). The *zip file* metaphor is exact in one respect that is easy to miss: a compressed file is useless without decompression, and the decompression happens in the receiver. Condensed content works only if the reader has enough context to unpack it, which is why, in Koe's later writing advice (Part VIII), structure and simplicity carry so much weight.

**Complementary context:** The figure of roughly 126 bits per second as the limit of conscious information processing comes from Csikszentmihalyi's *Flow*, which derives it from earlier estimates of how many "chunks" of information attention can handle per unit of time; Csikszentmihalyi presents it as a rough order of magnitude. In Chapter 6, Koe's image of attention as RAM uses a different figure (about 50 bits per second), and that chapter discusses it in context. Both figures should be read as illustrations of scarcity, not precise measurements.

The connection to entropy is direct. A curriculum of 60 million bits is, for a learner, a large disordered body of information; a well-made course is the same body after someone has invested the energy to order it. The creator pays the entropy tax once so that each learner pays less. This is one of the earliest appearances of an idea that Chapter 31 will treat as Koe's definition of value: value as the reversal of entropy for someone else.

#### Ordering consciousness in others

The tweet quoted above said "in yourself *and others*." In the same 2022 video Koe explains why the outward half is what makes the skill so valuable: it is communication. If you can give someone clarity on a vision they want to actualize, on the goals to get there, or on something that "breaks them out and wakes them up to fulfilling their full potential", then "that is what we do, and we do it at scale online." He points out that he is doing it in that very video: giving viewers a vision and goals "to reap the experience that comes with that vision." And he asks creators, personal brands, solopreneurs and entrepreneurs to think of everything he says on two levels, personal and collective: as a society we also have a vision and goals, and political leaders, for example, have a vision for their campaign and give you goals to actualize it (**Source:** `How To Create A Better Life For Yourself.md`, 2022-06-12).

**Ordering consciousness in others**, in this sense, is giving people clarity on a vision and the goals that lead to it, and Koe's claim is that this, done at scale through content, is what creators and businesses actually do. He turns it into a method for brands. All the tactics, tricks and hacks people offer you about branding, he says, are "in reality telling you: create a vision for your brand." The questions follow: what are you helping people work toward? What intention are you giving them? What are the goals to get there? How does that show up in your content and products? For products: what is the desired outcome, the vision, the goals, and "how are you going to make that so seamless for them to make that behavior change in order to get that result?" "This is how you create anything; this is creation as a whole": you create a better life for yourself and for someone else, moving from vision to clarity to bringing it to life (**Source:** `How To Create A Better Life For Yourself.md`, 2022-06-12).

The method mirrors the inward process step by step. A person orders their own consciousness with a vision, a bridge of goals and a seamless path of action; a brand orders its audience's consciousness with exactly the same three things, offered from outside. A product, in this frame, is a pre-built clarity bridge.

Koe illustrates the same idea with an everyday case. When you argue with your partner, it is usually because you are not sharing the same perspective: you do not have the same goal top of mind, or the same problem top of mind, so you perceive the situation differently. "All of this, whether it be marketing for a product or creating content, you're trying to create a shared perspective, with goal, problem, clarity to achieve it" (**Source:** `Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md`, 2023-11-26). The argument with a partner is a failure to order two consciousnesses around a common goal; marketing and content are deliberate attempts to succeed at it. The *shared perspective* (a common goal, a common problem and clarity about how to address it) is what communication has to produce before any persuasion can work, a point Chapter 32 develops.

Koe reads his own audience through this lens. "There's a reason why my philosophical content introduces a lot of people to business. It's because they're learning it better from me, because we have a very similar worldview and it makes more sense to them." He is "not the most hyper-practical guy" walking you step by step through making a million dollars (though he still does that); he builds the philosophy in, so that "people who aren't yet in business but have the potential to do very well in business" learn best from him (**Source:** `Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md`, 2024-08-25). This is an observation about his own case rather than a general law, but it illustrates the principle: information is absorbed more easily by a consciousness already ordered along similar lines, so a creator who shares a worldview with an audience can transmit practical content that would not land otherwise. It is also, as Chapter 19 will argue, the logic of "you are the niche."

### 5.4 Everything Is Information

The previous sections spoke of ordering "information" in consciousness as if the word were self-explanatory. For Koe it is not a casual word but a foundational one. This section reconstructs the claim that everything a person deals with is information, the loop through which information shapes a life, the place of thinking in that loop, and the economic conclusion he drew from it in 2022: that information, not attention, is the new currency.

#### Everything is information, and everyone shares one Big Goal

The fullest statement comes from a 2022 video in his one-person business series (reissued in a 2024 compilation). Our genetic code, the light we see, the words on a screen: anything we sense is information that we use to correct or change our behavior. Touch fire, it is hot: that is information telling you to remove your hand. We process information, understand it, fail to understand it or misunderstand it, and use it to move better in the world. So better knowledge, better understanding and greater clarity of information lead to better decisions and potentially a life we love (**Source:** `You Have A $100,000 Product In Your Head (One Person Business Series).md`, 2022-12-04; `The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md`, 2024-02-06).

On this foundation Koe places a claim about motivation: "Everyone has the same Big Goal that they are trying to achieve, and that goal is to increase the quality of your human experience as much as possible." Even on social media, he notes, you are trying to feel something, a good feeling or, failing that, a bad one, "just to feel something." Then the loop: thoughts, emotions and actions (all forms of information) dictate our experience, and that experience is itself information, in the form of feedback. It is "a constant information loop" (same sources). The *Big Goal*, in his usage, is the goal everyone shares beneath their particular goals: raising the quality of their experience.

The *information loop* is the hinge of this section, and it can be laid out in four moments: information arrives through the senses; it is processed (understood, misunderstood or ignored); it shapes thoughts, emotions and actions; and those produce an experience that returns as new information. The Big Goal explains why the loop is not neutral: every person is trying, competently or not, to steer it toward a better experience. And the touch-the-fire example shows the loop working well: a single piece of clear feedback corrects behavior immediately. Most of life's feedback is less clear, which is why the quality of processing (clarity again) becomes decisive.

#### Life is "in formation"

Koe extends the claim with wordplay he treats as meaningful. "A game is a structured flow of information. Life is information, or in formation, and our quality of life depends on how we process that information" (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (You Can't Escape The Matrix).md`, 2023-09-05). Two ideas are compressed here. The first is that a game, which is the model of the good life developed in Chapter 10, is defined by *structure*: it is information arranged so that a player always knows the goal, the rules and the next move. A life experienced as chaotic is a flow of information without that structure. The second is the pun itself: information is *in formation*, still forming, never fixed.

The *Art of Focus* summary (2024) turns the pun into a principle and places it in a sequence with two others, continuing the numbered list quoted in section 5.1:

4. "The structure of the information we focus our attention on, or the content in our consciousness, determines our quality of life, but information is in-formation; it is ever-changing, impermanent. What you focus on must adapt and change on a daily, monthly and yearly basis."
5. "Zooming out to see the big picture of a situation allows you to see negative situations for what they are: not that serious."
6. "Self-experimentation is the only way to solve your problems for good, make novel discoveries in your life and gain true experience."

(**Source:** `The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md`, 2024-01-18.)

Principle 4 is the central thesis of this chapter in one sentence: the quality of a life depends on the structure of what fills consciousness. Its qualification ("information is in-formation") prevents that thesis from becoming a recipe for rigidity. Because information keeps changing, any particular order of attention will become outdated; what you focus on has to be revised at the scale of days, months and years. This is entropy seen from another angle: order is not only eroded by neglect but also made obsolete by change. Principles 5 and 6 supply the tools. Zooming out is the remedy for the puncture of section 5.2, now stated as a principle: from far enough away, most negative situations are "not that serious." Self-experimentation is the remedy for the obsolescence of principle 4: since no fixed order lasts, the only way to keep finding a good one is to keep testing.

#### Meaning is found by elimination

How do you find out what deserves attention in a flow of information that keeps changing? Koe's answer, from 2023, is a paradox resolved by trial and error. "The key to the good life is investing attention in what we deem meaningful, but what you deem meaningful is found by investing attention in what you eliminate as meaningless." The alternation between high and low, focus and distraction, trying and discarding "is the natural balance that keeps life enjoyable." But if you get stuck in a low, chaos ensues, and that, he says, is where psychic entropy comes in (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (You Can't Escape The Matrix).md`, 2023-09-05).

This principle softens what might otherwise sound like an austere doctrine of constant focus. Some distraction is not just tolerable but necessary: it is how you discover what does not matter, and so, by elimination, what does. The danger lies not in the low but in staying there. The same structure (movement between states is healthy; being stuck in one is entropic) reappears in section 5.5 as the release and constraint of entropy, and it is consistent with the "solving, not solved" principle of section 5.1.

#### Self-consciousness as split attention

One of the most common ways in which attention gets stuck is self-consciousness, and Koe offers a precise account of how it arises. The way to end the suffering of anxiety and boredom is "learning to control your attention, which is heavily influenced by the ego or the self." The mechanism: through attention you notice something; you become self-conscious "because you're comparing it to something"; you tend to highlight the differences between yourself and the thing compared; and this "split in attention causes you to use precious mental energy" that could be focused on the task at hand (**Source:** `How I Turned My Life Into A Video Game.md`, 2022-12-11; reissued in `Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md`, 2024-03-21).

His example shows that the comparison does not even need another person. You see a pimple in the mirror. You do not need to be standing next to someone with clear skin to have the idea and the perception of clear skin; the idea is enough. A desire for clear skin pops up that you cannot satisfy, because of the pimple. "You start to compare ideas of what should be, rather than accepting things as they are and refocusing your attention" (same sources). The answer to gaining control of attention, he concludes, is to treat life "like the game it is."

In the vocabulary of this chapter, self-consciousness is a specific form of psychic entropy: attention divided between what is and an idea of what should be. It is costly in the same way that splintering is costly, because it consumes the energy that order requires. The prescribed response has two parts, both visible in the quotation: *accept* things as they are (stop feeding the comparison) and *refocus* (return the energy to the task). The connection to Part II is also visible: the "ideas of what should be" are often the standards and self-images of the identity, so the ego that Chapter 3 described as a filter is here the source of a leak. The game frame, which Chapter 10 develops, is Koe's way of making the refocusing natural rather than forced: a game supplies a clear goal against which irrelevant comparisons lose their pull.

#### Thinking determines the outcome of your life

If life is an information loop, then the stage of the loop where information is made sense of, thinking, determines how the loop turns. Koe made this his thesis in two videos about "strategic genius" thinking (2025 and 2026), and it belongs here because it states the loop in its most explicit form.

"Your mind is how you interact with reality. You process information, you make sense of it, which is thinking, you make a choice, then you receive information as feedback, so you have to think again, and you respond to that feedback and repeat the cycle." Compressed: "Think, information, choice, repeat." And the conclusion: "Thinking determines the outcome of your life" (**Source:** `How To Think Like A Strategic Genius (5-Dimensional Thinking).md`, 2026-02-22). The loop has five moments:

1. **Information** arrives.
2. **Thinking** makes sense of it.
3. A **choice** is made.
4. The world returns **feedback**, which is new information.
5. The cycle **repeats**, with the feedback as input.

Because the loop repeats, errors compound. "Every moment where you engage in stupid thinking creates a compounding effect. You dig yourself into this rut so deep that it becomes impossible to even see the thing that will get you out." Later in the same video he adds that the loop "crystallizes. It becomes conditioned... repetitive, robotic, and then determines the outcome of your life", and that you interpret information "at your current level, line, altitude of thinking" (same source). The rut here is the same as the thirty-foot hole of section 5.1 and the "rut dug by the default state of humanity" of section 5.2, now explained through the mechanics of information: each poorly processed piece of feedback produces a choice that generates more of the same feedback, until the pattern is fixed and the way out is literally invisible from inside it. (**Complementary context:** "levels", "lines" and "altitude" are terms from Ken Wilber's integral theory, where they name, respectively, stages of development, separate developmental capacities and a color-coded measure of overall development; Koe's use of Wilber is examined in Part XIV.)

The opening of the 2026 video frames the thesis with an observation: "Some of the smartest people I know are incredibly stupid. And some of the dumbest people that I know are incredibly successful by today's standards. They're happy, they're healthy, they're wealthy." Why do smart people who overanalyze not achieve the life they want? Because "thinking is one of the most important things that you can practice in today's world. Thinking is the one thing that determines the outcome of your life." Koe deliberately avoids textbook categories ("first principles thinking or systems thinking or metacognition or anything else that you can ask ChatGPT about") because, he says, "you're here for a unique take." He reports having "spent multiple years studying different thinkers... dissecting how they think and trying to integrate that into my own way of thinking", and he structures the video as a progression from one-dimensional to five-dimensional thinking (**Source:** `How To Think Like A Strategic Genius (5-Dimensional Thinking).md`, 2026-02-22). That progression is the subject of Chapter 17; what matters here is the premise it rests on.

The 2025 video gives the premise a place in a larger account of freedom. "The mark of a free individual is that they do what they want with their life. And this requires them to learn how to learn, learn how to earn, and learn how to think." Thinking is the most important of the three "because it influences both how you learn and how you act, and both of those determine the strategy, or the path, to achieving a big goal." The causal chain of failure runs as follows: thinking is off, so acting and learning are off, so strategy is off; people "can't make sense of a big goal. They can't even comprehend something that's worth doing", and the result is the familiar answer "I don't know what I want out of life." The good news is that all three are skills, learnable "through practice, repetition, trial and error" (**Source:** `This mental model will make you think like a strategic genius.md`, 2025-07-20). The *free individual*, in Koe's usage, is one who does what they want with their life, and the definition makes freedom a matter of competence: of having the skills (learning, earning, thinking) that make self-direction possible.

The chain links this chapter's diagnosis to Part IV. "I don't know what I want" is not, in this account, a lack of desire but a failure of sense-making: the person cannot process the available information into a goal worth pursuing. That is psychic entropy at the level of a whole life, and it is why direction (vision, goals, purpose) has to be *built* rather than found.

Two further claims qualify the thesis. First, thinking is a practical skill: "If you want to think better, like a genius, then you're going to have to learn that skill just like you learn any other skill. You don't learn a skill by studying it. You learn it by practicing what you learned." He ends the 2025 video with a challenge to pursue the wisdom on your own, to look into Ken Wilber's work, to "have the curiosity", and he points to an earlier video on nine stages of ego development (**Source:** `This mental model will make you think like a strategic genius.md`, 2025-07-20). Second, genius is not erudition. "What we consider a smart person right now... is someone who has memorized a lot of facts in order to achieve some level of status, whether that be through a job or career." Because such people know so much, they are "very good at overanalyzing risk"; they think through all the options "to the point of doing nothing. So they don't do what they want in life." "Dumb people, on the other hand, take the stupid risks that change the trajectory of their lives." Hence "you probably don't want to be like most smart people, because smart people often aren't that smart." "Genius-level thinking is not about book smarts. It's about clarity" (same source).

The last sentence closes the circle with section 5.2. Clarity was defined there as having a path from where you are to where you want to be; genius-level thinking is here defined as clarity. The overanalyzing "smart" person is, in the terms of this chapter, someone whose large store of information has not been ordered into a path, so it splinters into risks and options and produces paralysis. The "dumb" risk-taker has less information but more order: a goal and a willingness to move, which generates feedback, which feeds the loop. This is an opinion with a polemical edge, not a study of intelligence, and Koe's "dumb people" are a rhetorical figure. The underlying point is consistent with everything above: what determines outcomes is not how much information you hold but how well it is ordered toward action.

Koe expresses the value he places on this kind of mind in a small personal observation. The best compliment a person can receive, he says, one he has heard a few times from specific people he has met, is "I like the way your mind works" (**Source:** `How To Reprogram Your Mind & Build Your Intelligence.md`, 2023-09-24). He uses it to open a video about building a mind that others admire and that can be monetized. The compliment is directed at neither the body nor possessions but at the way a person thinks, organizes thoughts and articulates them: at the order of their consciousness as it becomes visible to others. It joins the inward and outward halves of section 5.3: a well-ordered mind is admired because it orders the consciousness of the people who encounter it.

#### Information is the new currency

In 2022 Koe drew an economic conclusion from all this, stated against a popular slogan. "Attention isn't the new currency, information is", because information is all humans actually deal with: we process it with our senses, store it, and use it to create or destroy. He says he would like to believe that the "unconscious evolution-aligned intent" behind modern technology, mainly social media, is to give us information we would not otherwise have access to, increasing our potential. Then a chain:

> "Information leads to awareness of opportunity. Awareness of opportunity leads to profitable ideas. Profitable ideas lead to positive behavior change."

He acknowledges the dark side, that the manipulation of information can lead to death, destruction and scams, but chooses to focus on the good side. We live in the age of information, not the industrial age, and that has two faces: an abundance of ideas and opportunities, and an overwhelm of information (**Source:** `You Have A $100,000 Product In Your Head (One Person Business Series).md`, 2022-12-04; `The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md`, 2024-02-06).

The chain is worth reading slowly. *Awareness of opportunity*, the middle link, is Koe's term for what information makes possible: seeing a possibility you could not see before. Profitable ideas come from opportunities you can see; positive behavior change (in yourself and, through what you sell, in others) comes from those ideas. Each link depends on the order of the previous one: information that is not processed does not produce awareness; awareness that is not acted upon does not produce ideas. The two faces of the information age are also two faces of entropy. Abundance is raw material for order; overwhelm is what that same abundance becomes in a mind that cannot order it.

This position belongs to a particular moment, and Koe's choice of "currency" has changed over the years. In 2022 and its 2024 reissue the currency is information. In 2023 he said "ideas are the new currency" and spoke of capturing, holding and nurturing people's attention with ideas, and he also called collective attention ("mental real estate") "the most valuable resource"; in 2024, "ideas are the new oil." From 2025 the emphasis moves further: intelligence is "no longer a differentiating resource", "taste is the new intelligence", and, in 2026, "meaning is the scarcest commodity in civilization right now." The reason he gives is that each resource gets cheaper as it becomes abundant: information, then intelligence via AI models. The successive "currencies" are best read as layers of a single chain (information → ideas and attention → taste → meaning), each becoming the scarce one as the previous one becomes plentiful. The 2026 endpoint connects back to section 5.3, where meaning was defined as the experience of ordered consciousness: the scarcest thing is not information but the order that turns it into meaning. (The economic side of this progression is developed in Chapter 36.)

#### The information business and the skill of simplifying

From "everything is information" Koe derived, in the same 2022 video, a business model. He proposes a system for packaging the information in your head in a valuable way, to help others and to profit. To him "there's not a better business model", even though the *information business* is new: everything is information, the only goal is a higher quality of human experience, and you do not need a mega-corporation to provide it. If we were at peace living perfect lives, he reasons, we would not want more. Getting more people to the self-actualized and self-transcended stages of Maslow's hierarchy of needs would put us in a better position, because information lets people do more. "If I had the right information, I could go and build whatever I want to build." But someone has to build it first and teach him, or he has to do it himself; and if he is busy with his own work, he goes to someone else for that information. Not everyone will do this, he concludes; only a small fraction of people is even interested, but you can (**Source:** `You Have A $100,000 Product In Your Head (One Person Business Series).md`, 2022-12-04).

**Complementary context:** Abraham Maslow's hierarchy of needs (1943, later revised) arranges human motivations from physiological and safety needs through belonging and esteem to self-actualization; in later writings Maslow added self-transcendence above it. Koe uses the hierarchy in adapted form throughout the corpus; here, his point is that information is what helps people climb from basic needs toward growth needs.

The argument is a direct application of the information loop: if everyone pursues the Big Goal and information is what improves the loop, then selling well-ordered information is selling a better life. Koe's view of the vehicle for that information has changed considerably since 2022. In 2023–2025 he repeatedly called information products "the greatest product one can sell"; by 2026 he argued that static courses had reached the final stage of market sophistication, that the next level was an AI "implementation tool" built on a course's knowledge base, and that "small-scale software will be the new info product", while insisting that education as a function is too foundational ever to disappear. That evolution belongs to Chapter 33; the premise it modifies (that ordered information is the core of value) is the one stated here.

The same reasoning produces another of Koe's "greatest skill" statements. "Simplifying complex topics will be the greatest skill of the next century, because we live in an information age." There are so many ideas and career and life options that people are overwhelmed; new discoveries and knowledge are produced constantly and distilled into internet content; "we need people to organize and simplify it" (**Source:** `The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md`, 2024-02-06). Simplification is ordering consciousness applied to a public: it takes the overwhelm side of the information age and converts it into the abundance side. As noted in section 5.3, Koe has attached the superlative to several skills; this one and "the ability to order consciousness" are, read together, the same capacity seen from two sides, inward (ordering your own mind) and outward (ordering information for others).

### 5.5 Managing Entropy

The first four sections treated entropy as something to understand. This last section treats it as something to *manage*: a quantity to be raised or lowered deliberately, at the scale of a day, a season and a business. The ideas collected here are among Koe's most practical, and they prepare the systems of Part V (the 4-Hour Workday, rest and routine), which can be read as entropy management made concrete.

#### Success is entropy management

The central formulation comes from a 2024 video on building a life in "365 hours" of focused work. "Success is about entropy management." You must know how to release and constrain entropy in days, weeks, months and years:

- **Release entropy** when life gets boring and repetitive, by setting and pursuing new goals.
- **Constrain entropy** when life gets overwhelming, by deconstructing goals into smaller, clearer, more manageable goals.

(**Source:** `Change Your Life In 365 Hours (The New Rich Focus On These Tasks Daily).md`, 2024-06-02.)

*Entropy management*, in Koe's coinage, is this two-directional skill. It refines the picture built so far in an important way. Sections 5.1 and 5.2 could suggest that entropy is always the enemy and that the goal is maximal order. Here, too much order is also a problem: a life that has become perfectly predictable is boring, and boredom is one of the two signals of psychic entropy. The remedy for that kind of stagnation is to *introduce* uncertainty, by taking on a new goal whose path is not yet clear. Conversely, when uncertainty exceeds what you can handle (anxiety, overwhelm), the remedy is to impose structure by breaking the goal down until its next step is clear. The two moves correspond to the two ways of falling out of the challenge–skill balance described in section 5.3: boredom (challenge too low) calls for release; anxiety (challenge too high) calls for constraint. Entropy management is, in effect, Csikszentmihalyi's flow channel rewritten as a self-regulation practice.

The same video makes the principle concrete in the scheduling of a day. "If you can spend 8 hours building someone else's dreams, you can spend 1 hour building your own." It is silly, Koe argues, to save that hour for after work, when you are tired, prone to risky decisions, when entropy is high and the mind lacks the energy to focus. Psychic entropy "is low in the morning", so step one is to build your own project then (same source). The rationale draws on the craving for order discussed in section 5.2: the mind, which tends toward disorder, is most ordered at the start of the day, before the accumulated demands and distractions of the hours have breached its frame. Spending that window on someone else's priorities and giving your own the exhausted remainder is, in entropic terms, investing your best energy in another person's system. (The morning block and its structure are treated in Chapter 12.)

#### Problems are systemic, and attention gives them life

A 2024 passage deepens the account by changing how problems are seen. "Problems are not personal, they're systemic, and everybody faces them; it's when there is a blockage of the energy in the system that is our mind." Mental health, fixing the mental disorder that comes from psychic entropy, is about making sense, understanding and clarity. When you are learning a new skill, entropy is very high and must be constrained; when you stop, entropy creeps back in, is released, and must be constrained again through effort and energy. "This is a universal principle; it applies to everything. If you understand entropy and keep it at the top of your mind, it will change your life" (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md`, 2024-07-21).

Two practical implications follow. The first is a reframing of failure: if problems are systemic (blockages in the flow of energy in a mind-system) rather than personal (proof of some deficiency in you), then they call for diagnosis and adjustment rather than shame. This is the same move as "distractions are problems in your productivity system" in section 5.2. The second is a warning about learning: the high entropy of a new skill is not a sign that you are bad at it but the normal condition of acquiring it, and stopping is costly because the disorder returns as soon as constraint is relaxed.

The same passage then describes what high entropy does to perception. When entropy is high, a single negative thought can hijack attention, you cannot see the situation for what it is, "and then the mind game commences": you have to learn to play it. And Koe adds a phrase that he coins here: "Attention breathes life into everything it touches." Give your attention to a false reality that your mind creates, and you will act in a way that makes it real (same source). *Attention breathes life into everything it touches* names a self-fulfilling mechanism: what you attend to, you act on, and what you act on becomes your reality. In the context of psychic entropy it is a warning: the splintered, negative world of section 5.2 is not only felt but, through action, produced. In the context of the next chapter it is a promise: the same mechanism, aimed at a chosen goal, makes that goal real.

Section 5.2 noted another consequence of the mind's craving for order that belongs to entropy management: you surround yourself with information that confirms who you are now, not who you want to be, and anything outside your beliefs triggers an "emotional mind closing reaction" (**Source:** `Change Your Life In 365 Hours (The New Rich Focus On These Tasks Daily).md`, 2024-06-02). In terms of release and constraint, a feed that only confirms is a perfectly constrained environment. It feels orderly, but it locks identity in place. Following new people is a small, deliberate release of entropy into the information environment, enough to unsettle the existing order without overwhelming it.

#### Building and maintenance

Koe's most important distinction for managing entropy in work separates two kinds of effort. "There are two types of work. First is building: when you are building the foundation of something new, like a product, service or brand, this requires a lot of upfront work. The second is maintenance: when you are feeding the foundation you've built with a streamlined productivity system, like writing content, marketing yourself, building an audience, or fulfilling on your product or service. This requires 1 to 4 hours a day, depending on your skill level or choice of work" (**Source:** `Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md`, 2024-02-18).

| | Building | Maintenance |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Laying the foundation of something new | Feeding a foundation that already exists |
| **Examples** | A new product, service or brand | Writing content, marketing, audience building, fulfillment |
| **Effort** | Heavy and front-loaded | 1 to 4 hours a day, depending on skill and type of work |
| **Duration** | Temporary: perhaps six weeks, once or twice a year | Ongoing |
| **Entropic function** | Reverses the entropy of the business and creates new revenue | Keeps the existing order from decaying |

The distinction explains a common discouragement. People trying to change their lives or quit their jobs, Koe observes, "often get discouraged at the amount of work it takes to build, not maintain" (same source). They look at the effort of the building phase and assume it is permanent. It is not: "People don't understand that building isn't a permanent thing. It may be 6 weeks of working extra or working more on the weekends, and you're maintaining things along the way, but it's not permanent. That's not your permanent state. That's something you do maybe once or twice a year to reverse entropy of your business and keep yourself alive and relevant and to keep new revenue generating" (same source).

The phrase *entropy of your business* names the decline that sets in when a business is only maintained. Maintenance alone holds things in place for a while, but in a changing environment (information is "in formation") a business that does not periodically build something new slowly loses relevance and revenue. The building season is the energy input that reverses that drift. This is the household metaphor of section 5.1 at the scale of a company: daily tidying keeps the room livable, but every so often the room has to be rearranged for a new purpose.

The distinction also rests on the asymmetry noted in section 5.1: established order is cheaper to keep than to create. Koe states the same asymmetry as a property of skill: "Focus compounds, and you get better at what you're doing with time. When you build one thing it takes 2 weeks, when you build the second thing it takes 2 days, when you build the third thing it takes 2 hours. Nobody starts building the first thing that takes 2 weeks, so they never give themselves the chance to build the third thing that takes 2 hours" (same source). The numbers are illustrative, but the point is precise. The cost of building falls with each repetition, because each build leaves behind skills, templates and systems that the next one reuses. The two-week first build is the price of entry, and most people judge the whole path by it and never pay it.

#### Build, publish, maintain: the daily version

A few weeks after describing building as a once- or twice-yearly season, Koe described a daily routine that gives building a permanent place. He divides his workday into three time blocks of 60 to 90 minutes, separated by walks or making breakfast (around 9 a.m.), and ends the workday with the gym. "Option A" for the three blocks is *build, publish, maintain* (**Source:** `A Day In The Life Of Dan Koe (My Routine As A Writer & Founder).md`, 2024-04-02):

1. **Build.** "If you only have 1 hour, then you have to fill that with building... something that can potentially be profitable enough to allow you to do it full-time." For many people on the social media route, that means building an audience to monetize later; "anything aside from writing social media content and getting very good at growing on social media is a waste of time, aside from learning a skill." For Koe at the time, building meant writing books; in the past it had meant digital products, or his freelancing business and landing clients: "anything that moved the lever forward to my next phase of evolution in business."
2. **Publish.** Publishing to social media, which he says is "not a main priority" for him now "just because it's automatic."
3. **Maintain.** Maintaining past projects that earn income (his last book, his courses, the software product Cortex and client work for Cortex University), along with email, finances, the rest of the to-do list and *spillover tasks* (work left over from earlier blocks).

The order of the blocks follows the logic of entropy management: building, the most demanding and most future-oriented work, goes first, when psychic entropy is lowest; maintenance, which tolerates fatigue and interruption, goes last. The block for beginners with a single hour is telling: if there is only one block, it must be building, because maintenance of nothing is nothing.

There is a tension between this routine and the claim that building is not permanent. The corpus contains both: building as an occasional six-week season (February 2024) and building as the first block of every day (April 2024, and again in late 2024, when he describes the first block as a sprint on a build-once-sell-many project). Koe does not reconcile them. The most plausible reading, consistent with what he says in each place, is that they operate at different scales: the daily build block is the steady growth of the next project, while the building season is an intensive burst, a few weeks of extra hours, to launch something or reverse a business's decline. The contradiction is one of scale rather than of principle, but the reader should know that the corpus states both without connecting them. (The length of the blocks has also varied: from 90-minute blocks split into two 45-minute sprints in 2023, through blocks of 45 to 90 minutes, to the three blocks of 60 to 90 minutes described here. Chapter 12 traces this evolution.)

#### Seasons of work: a guest's view

Koe's distinction between building and maintenance has a counterpart in a conversation with the writer and entrepreneur Dickie Bush (2023), and the ideas in this subsection are Bush's, not Koe's. Reporting on the takeaways from his latest quarter, Bush says: "I'm in a consumption and delegation season, not a creation season", while Koe, he notes, is about to enter "almost purely a creation season." Bush knows what needs to be built for his next sprint (launching a program called Go Shredding and a coaching offer, hiring a larger team, learning "to manage versus do"), but he cannot build it himself; he has to learn the skill and find the people. He has not been writing much, which is uncomfortable, and his deep work time has shrunk; "the highest return on my time is not sitting for four hours writing right now." Management feels different from creation: "I'm always a Slack message away from making progress", whereas in creation "you cannot be creative all day... you have to step away." Coaching three customer success managers makes his days feel longer, and he reads that as a diagnosis: "that's how I know that it's a skill deficiency, because I feel busier, which means I lack the skill of prioritization, planning, delegation, execution" (**Source:** `Dan Koe & Dickie Bush On One-Person Businesses, Creative Workflows, and Lifestyle Design.md`, 2023-03-16).

Bush's frame adds two things to Koe's. First, the seasons are defined not by how much one works but by *what kind* of work has the highest return at a given stage: sometimes it is making things, sometimes it is consuming (learning) and delegating. A season of consumption is not a lapse into entropy if it is the stage the project requires. Second, the felt sense of busyness is information: feeling busier while doing a new kind of work signals a missing skill, which is a problem to solve rather than a condition to endure. Both points fit Koe's framework. A season is a deliberate release or constraint of entropy at the scale of months, and "I feel busier" is the systemic, not personal, reading of a problem.

In the same conversation Bush describes the device he uses to set the length of his seasons: a quarterly reflection. On the morning of the recording, he says, he realized that he had "12 blocks of 12 weeks until I'm 30" (twelve quarters; technically, he notes, thirteen-week blocks). Looking back at the previous twelve weeks, in which he had made great progress, "the sense of urgency I emerged with" was strong: the time ahead is "so short and only gonna feel shorter" (same source). The quarterly block is a unit of time large enough to build something and small enough to count. Counting the blocks that remain turns an abstract future into a finite set of seasons, which is a way of constraining entropy at the largest scale of all.

#### Bulking and cutting: new highs and higher baselines

Koe's own metaphor for the rhythm of growth in a business comes from bodybuilding. The fullest version appears in "The 4-Hour Workday" (2023) and is repeated in the 2024 compilation. Suppose you are making $10,000 a month and you reach a new high of $20,000 a month because you saw an opportunity and added more work, say six hours a day instead of four. Then "I have to systemize and come back down a little bit." Repeating this cycle, "you keep moving your baseline up, so your 4 hours of work are going a longer way and making more money, or whatever the end goal is, whether it's money or not" (**Source:** `The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md`, 2023-01-01; `Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md`, 2024-03-21).

"It's like bulking and cutting." When you bulk, you eat in a caloric surplus and are in a state of growth (here, business growth), and "you're going to tack on a bit of fat in your business: the things that kind of bog you down and make you have to work more." Then "when the cutting season comes" you cut back to, say, "15% body fat in business", and you can sit comfortably at four hours of work a day. "You don't just get stuck at one single part of the journey; you evolve" (same sources).

The metaphor maps onto entropy management precisely:

| Bodybuilding | Business | Entropic reading |
|---|---|---|
| Bulk: caloric surplus, growth | Seize an opportunity, add hours (4 → 6), reach a new high | Release entropy: take on more than the current system can handle |
| Fat gained during the bulk | "Fat in your business": the tasks and complications that bog you down | Disorder accumulated during growth |
| Cut: lose fat, keep muscle | Systemize; return to about 4 hours | Constrain entropy: impose order on the new level |
| Leaner physique at a higher weight | Higher baseline: the same 4 hours earn more | Order re-established at a higher level |

*Bulking and cutting* (Koe uses the image elsewhere for learning and other domains too) names, in this context, the cycle of growing by adding work and then systemizing back down. *Fat in your business* is the disorder that growth leaves behind. The metaphor's main lesson is that disorder during growth is not a failure: it is the expected cost of a surplus, and it is dealt with in its own season.

A related observation, from mid-2023, explains why the cycle has to be repeated. "Once you reach a new high monthly revenue in business, you're usually not going to sustain that for long. You're going to hit a new baseline, and then you'll hit a new high, and then the new baseline rises, and then a new high, the baseline rises, and that's how it goes." The way to hold on to what has been gained is to look back with the knowledge and experience you have built and ask "what are the things that I actually have to be doing to make sure that I sustain some level that I've built myself into", and then "you have to bake things into your day" (**Source:** `If You’re Lost - Here’s How You Find Clarity For The Future.md`, 2023-06-04). The *baseline* is the level you return to after a peak; the peaks themselves are not sustainable, but each one can leave the baseline higher, provided the activities that produced it become part of the daily routine. A peak is a burst of energy; the baseline is what the system can maintain without one.

#### Separate signal from noise and systemize the levers

How do you decide what to bake into the day? A 2023 passage gives the method. "As you're turning the final page... from a season of progress into a season of experimentation, when you hit a new monthly high and you know it's going to come back down, you need to be able to separate signal from noise: what are the levers that you have been pursuing in your day, and what just doesn't belong there? And when you focus on the levers that are actually getting you results, and you're able to systemize and make those habitual in your day, make them a part of your life, then you can start to sustain those higher baselines" (**Source:** `Happiness Is A Skill (How To Get Out Of A Rut).md`, 2023-02-26). Separating *signal from noise* here means distinguishing the levers that produce results from the activities that merely fill the day. The procedure has three steps: at a new high, review what you have been doing; keep the levers that produced the results and drop what does not belong; turn the levers into habits. The result is a higher baseline that does not depend on the extra hours of the bulk.

In another 2023 video Koe describes the outcome of this process in his own work, as the fourth step of a system for growth: systemize what works. As clarity grows, certain levers turn out to bring the most results, and techniques that give 1% better results than the next person's are "your edge, because that one percent compounds." His own example: he writes for 30 minutes every morning and repurposes the writing for every platform. That is what brought him followers and sales, and it supplies content for any business model, past and future. He saw writing as a lever for traffic and the newsletter as "an infinitely repurposable piece of content." At first it took longer, because he was not efficient and lacked the skill; now it takes 30 minutes to an hour each morning (**Source:** `Asking For Advice Is Keeping You Unsuccessful (Here's Why).md`, 2023-08-23). *Edge*, in this sense, is a small, systematized advantage that compounds; Koe uses the same word elsewhere, following David Deida, for something entirely different (the point at which you hold back your gift out of fear), and the two senses should not be confused.

The case is a complete illustration of the cycle. A lever (morning writing) was identified, systemized (one piece of writing repurposed everywhere), and refined until its cost fell (from longer sessions to 30–60 minutes): a higher baseline reached with the same or less effort. The reported duration of his writing has varied across the corpus, from 30 minutes in 2022–2023 to "1–2 hours every morning" in later years; the figures change without a change of principle, and the growing efficiency he describes here is consistent with the idea of raising the baseline.

#### Make the process more efficient: creativity, systemization and shower thoughts

The last piece of entropy management is the tenth commandment of "The 4-Hour Workday": make your process more efficient. Problems that come up while working, or that push the workday beyond four hours, "should try to be solved with creativity and systemization." This, Koe says, is another reason why it is crucial *not* to focus on work when you are not working: it opens your mind to creative problem solving, because "that's when your subconscious mind sends the insight or idea to your conscious mind and you have that aha moment." Shower thoughts are the familiar case: you get your best ideas in the shower "because you're not focused on work" (**Source:** `The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md`, 2023-01-01; `Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md`, 2024-03-21).

The 2024 version adds an example from Koe's own history. When client work spilled beyond four hours a day, the solution was not to work longer but to change the system: he had to build a larger audience for more traffic, create a product that did not require his time, promote and improve it until it was sustainable, and then use the freed time to take on fewer clients. He adds a caution against reading this as a quick fix: "This isn't instant. Nothing that I talk about is instant. Life isn't instant... you have to be on this path for life" (**Source:** `Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md`, 2024-03-21).

The commandment closes the loop of this section. Bulking adds disorder; cutting needs solutions; and the solutions come not from more focused hours but from the unfocused ones, when the subconscious is free to recombine what the focused hours have gathered. Rest is not the opposite of entropy management but one of its instruments. (Koe later connects shower thoughts to the brain's default mode network; that account, and the role of rest in his system, belong to Chapter 13.) The transition from client work to an audience and a product is also an early instance of the progression that Part X describes, from service to product, and of the principle that Chapter 12 formalizes as Koe's Law.

#### From entropy to attention

Throughout this chapter, one resource has appeared under different names: mental energy, psychic energy, focus, attention. It is what the second law's "energy input" means for a mind. Chapter 6 turns to that resource directly: why it is so scarce, how it can be widened or narrowed, how the mind steers with it, and why the goal (the device that has kept reappearing in this chapter as the source of order) works as a lens that determines what attention can see.

### Exercises

1. **Audit a slow decline.** Choose one domain of your life (health, finances, a relationship, a skill, a workspace) that you would describe as "staying the same." Using the household metaphors of section 5.1, reconstruct its last two or three years as a room: what were the "few shirts on the floor", when did the disorder become normal to you, and how much more effort would restoring order cost now than maintaining it would have? Then decide whether Koe's claim that "there's no such thing as staying the same" holds for this domain, or whether you can identify something in your life that genuinely stays the same without maintenance. What would such a case imply about the scope of his "supreme law"?

2. **Trace a splinter.** The next time you notice a negative thought multiplying, write down the chain as precisely as you can, in the manner of Koe's egg-and-cigarettes or mirror examples: the first thought, each branch, and where it ended. Then identify the moment of the "puncture": which thought was the first one you attended to? Finally, apply the reversal of section 5.2 (realize, zoom out to your vision and the day as a whole, deconstruct, zoom back in) and record what changed. Which part of the reversal was hardest, and why?

3. **Motivation or clarity?** Pick a goal you have held for a long time without acting on it. Diagnose it with the clarity equation (perspective: a vision you believe in; knowing what to focus on: a plan) and the clarity bridge (the smaller goals between point A and point B). Which component is missing? Design one "clarity-generating" session of one to two hours, modeled on Koe's Sunday routine, that would supply it. Then consider a counterexample: is there a case in which someone has full clarity and still does not act? What would Koe's framework say about that case, and is its answer convincing?

4. **Release or constrain.** Describe your current state of life or work as precisely as you can: are the dominant signals boredom and repetition, or anxiety and overwhelm, or a mix that varies by domain? For each domain, decide whether entropy should be released (a new goal) or constrained (a goal broken down into smaller, clearer ones), and specify the concrete move. Then compare Koe's 2024 view of entropy as something to manage with his 2025–2026 view of chaos as a precondition for a higher order (being lost as a decision point, "the void is where success is born"). In your situation, which view is more useful, and what would tell you that it is time to switch from one to the other?

5. **Distraction, defined.** Koe has offered two definitions of distraction: anything not aligned with your vision, and anything that pulls you away from your work block, after which the same activity may be important. Apply both definitions to a typical day of yours and list what each counts as a distraction. Where do they disagree? Defend the definition you would use, and identify one input (like the "latest Claude skill" example) that would pass a casual purpose filter but should fail a ruthless one.

6. **Order someone else's consciousness.** Choose a person or audience you want to help (a friend, a team, readers, customers). Following the method in section 5.3, write down the vision you would offer them, the goals that lead to it, and how you would make the behavior change as seamless as possible. Then identify where you and they might lack a "shared perspective" (a common goal, a common problem, clarity on how to act), as in Koe's example of an argument between partners. What would you need to establish first, before any advice could land?

7. **Question the thesis that thinking determines everything.** Koe claims that "thinking is the one thing that determines the outcome of your life" and that "smart" people often fail because they overanalyze risk. Construct the strongest case against this claim: what factors outside thinking shape outcomes, and are there situations in which careful risk analysis is exactly what prevents disaster? Then reread the claim through the information loop of section 5.4 (information, thinking, choice, feedback, repeat). Does that model absorb your objections, limit the claim's scope, or fail to account for them?

8. **Design a bulk and a cut.** Using the bulking-and-cutting metaphor and the build/maintain distinction of section 5.5, sketch the next twelve months of one project you care about. Where would a building season of a few weeks go, and what would it build? What "fat" would it probably add, and how would the following cut systemize the levers into a higher baseline? Identify one lever you already suspect is giving you a disproportionate return, and describe how you would test whether it is signal or noise.
