## Chapter 19 — You Are the Niche

Part VI ended with the creator: a mind that collects, connects and synthesizes ideas, develops taste and learns to articulate what it has understood. Before that mind writes a single post or sells a single product, it faces a question that almost every beginner in Koe's audience asks first and gets stuck on longest: *what should I be about?* The online business world has a ready answer, "niche down," and this chapter is Koe's long argument against that answer and for its replacement, "you are the niche."

The argument applies to the marketplace the model of the person built in Parts II and IV. Part II described identity as a network of goals and beliefs that a person defends and can change; Chapter 6 described the goal as a lens that decides what the mind notices; Chapter 7 built vision and anti-vision; Chapter 9 defined purpose as a problem worth solving and the life's work as a project. If all of that is true, then a business cannot be designed by first choosing a market segment from a list and then bending oneself to fit it. The direction runs the other way: the person's goals, problems, path and perspective already define a market, made of everyone who shares or once shared them. Koe's name for this market is the **niche of one**.

The chapter has seven steps. Section 19.1 describes the inherited belief system Koe calls the **business matrix** and the niche-down advice at its core, including what Koe concedes about it and what he rejects. Section 19.2 presents "you are the niche" and the **experience model**, in which the creator is at once the customer avatar, the brand, the content and the offer, together with the four pillars that translate a life into a business and the several versions those pillars went through. Section 19.3 explains why the ideal customer is one's past self ("build, write and sell to yourself") and records the limits that guests and Koe's own later experience set on that principle. Section 19.4 introduces the **Eternal markets** (health, wealth, relationships and happiness) as the universal niche in which everyone's problems live, and explains why a shared goal does not mean a shared path. Section 19.5 resolves the apparent contradiction between "be broad" and "be specific" with Koe's formula **broad catch, specific sale**: niche the product, not the brand. Section 19.6 gathers the exercises Koe and his guests offer for finding one's own niche, and the arguments about focus, consistency and obsession that surround them. Section 19.7 closes with **Book to Brand**, the method of outlining one's life as a book whose chapters become content.

The chapter prepares Part VIII. Chapter 22 will ask what to write, and its answer (value as ideas seen through the lens of a goal, one's own raw material, the topic tree) depends on accepting here that the writer's own life is the source. Chapter 20 extends the argument to the generalist. Chapters 27 and 28 return to the brand and the business as functions; this chapter is about where they come from.

### 19.1 The Business Matrix and the Niche-Down Advice

#### The business matrix

In Chapter 1 the Matrix was defined as an inherited web of beliefs, absorbed without questioning, that tells a person what to want and how to live. In a 2024 video titled "Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche," Koe extends the idea to commerce. "The business matrix in contrast to the social matrix is just a web of ideas and beliefs that illustrates how businesses are supposed to operate." It is the advice and education a beginner receives, usually from people online, about how to start a business, and its core formula is short: "Pick a skill, pick a niche, and do the same thing all over again." Koe's summary of where that formula leads is one of his most quoted lines: "Do work you don't care about for people you don't care about to live a life you don't care about."

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

The term **business matrix** is Koe's own, built by analogy with the social matrix. The analogy carries the whole diagnosis of Part I into the domain of business. A belief system is absorbed at the beginner stage, when a person has no basis for judging it, and it then operates as if it were a description of how things are rather than one option among many. Koe frames the problem as a question of learning stages. Humans learn by being exposed first to beginner knowledge, then to intermediate and advanced knowledge. The trouble is that very few people question the beginner-level knowledge from the start: "Are we absolutely sure it is the best way to go about things? Is it the most conducive to results or does it cause more confusion that makes people quit too early?"

The deeper objection is about motive. Most of the people Koe addresses started a business to get out of a particular kind of life. "You decided to start a business because you wanted to escape the mechanical and meaningless lifestyle, not to create a new one where you can just earn a bit more than you did before at a job." If the business is built by the business matrix, the escape fails: the person rebuilds the mechanical life of a job, with clients in place of a boss and slightly better pay. Those who break free of the business matrix, Koe says, "can do whatever they want." That phrase anticipates the main criterion of this chapter. A niche is judged not only by whether it makes money but by whether it leaves the person free to evolve.

#### Why "niche" became the central problem

Koe treats the niche question as unusually important for a practical reason: it is where the people he teaches actually get stuck. In January 2023 he called choosing a niche "the biggest problem ever in this Creator economy." Everyone talks about it, and in working directly with students in his programs Modern Mastery and Digital Economics, it came up as "a recurring issue time and time again," which is why he kept thinking about it until he had a solution to offer. A week later, in a video on authentic content, he gave a rough figure for that experience: he had worked directly and indirectly with over 5,000 creators, some one-on-one and some through courses. The two most pressing problems he saw were, first, creating one's "niche of one" or choosing a niche, and second, incorporating one's interests in a way that is interesting while still maintaining what he calls the "brand pillars": the content must still lead to income, or the person cannot do it full time, and it must still stand out, or "ChatGPT is going to replace you maybe." The cost of not solving these problems is concrete: "this makes people quit before things start getting good."

**Source:** The Most Profitable Niche Is You (Create Your Niche Of One).md; How To Write Authentic Content (Social Media Growth 101).md

The figure of 5,000 creators is Koe's own estimate of his reach, not a study, and it should be read as the experience base he claims for his opinion. What it establishes is that the critique of niche-down in this chapter is presented as the result of watching many people fail at a specific step, not only as a philosophical preference.

#### The traditional pattern and what Koe concedes about it

Koe's critique begins with a fair description of what he is criticizing. In "Niche Down Is Terrible Advice (For Smart People)" (2023) he describes the courses on freelancing and marketing he bought early in his career. Across all of them one pattern was apparent: get specific about whom you target. The pattern has four steps.

| Step | What the courses taught | Koe's example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Starving market | Identify a large market with permanent demand | Health |
| 2. Sub-market | Break it into a sub-market | Bodybuilding |
| 3. Niche market | Narrow in on a niche within the sub-market | Minimalist training |
| 4. Research the people | Collect data on the people in that niche to find their pain points and speak to them directly | Surveys, pain points, tailored messaging |

The promise of the method was that with it, "I had the secret": find a specific person, research them, know exactly what they need, and tailor a product or service to them with your skill set. Koe does not say this is false. "This is still it, it still works," he says. His complaint is that the whole of business strategy gets reduced to a slogan, "just Niche down bro, that's all there is to it," and that the slogan is applied to things it was never designed for.

**Source:** Niche Down Is Terrible Advice (For Smart People).md

This concession matters for reading the rest of the chapter correctly. Koe's mature position, developed in Section 19.5, is that the niche-down method is a valid tool for positioning a product and for paid advertising, where you can target a demographic directly. What he rejects is using it to define a brand, a body of content or a life's work. The title of the video says niche down is "terrible advice"; the content says it is good advice applied at the wrong level. The tension is real and the corpus records it: the same video in which the method "still works" is the one that calls it terrible.

#### What niching down costs

Koe's objections accumulate over several years and come from different angles. The first is psychological and concerns beginners. Niche-down "sparks the most overwhelm, anxiety and uncertainty for beginners." Beginners come to believe that if they do not find the most profitable niche they will fail, so they spend their time "flopping from Niche to Niche learning skills for that Niche, which is not a bad thing, and end up with no money or leverage to show for it, that's a bad thing." The sentence draws a distinction he will rely on later: the skills are not wasted, but the search for the perfect market prevents the accumulation of anything that compounds, an audience, a product, a reputation.

**Source:** Niche Down Is Terrible Advice (For Smart People).md

The second objection concerns the kind of work niche-down produces. In the January 2023 video he tells viewers that "you can completely forget the traditional Niche down advice of choosing like the most profitable Market with someone that you don't want to work with in any capacity using a skill that you don't want to use in any capacity." The alternative he offers is short: "you can create your own Niche." Behind the warning is his own history with a Facebook ads agency, which appears elsewhere in the corpus as one of his failed businesses; Chapter 37 tells that story.

**Source:** The Most Profitable Niche Is You (Create Your Niche Of One).md

The third and most structured objection comes in the 2024 video. Koe imagines picking a niche from one of the lists that circulate online and gives three reasons it fails.

1. **You don't care about these people.** "I don't care about these people. I would and did hate working with them." This is not sustainable for something as important as one's life's work, whose point "isn't to just have a new boss." Koe anticipates the objection that a business can simply be a way to make money. He agrees that it can. But if the end goal is to pursue one's life's work after generating cash flow, and one can pursue it by starting the business that way in the first place, "what's the dissonance there?" Choosing the profitable list-niche instead "just feels like laziness" and the wish for the get-rich-quick option.
2. **You haven't lived their problem.** "I don't have experience with their business. Most businesses fail because they try to solve a problem they haven't experienced. Remember that. Feel free to come back to me when you ignore this and fail."
3. **It prioritizes finding over attracting or becoming.** You learn a skill for someone else, search endlessly for people to reach out to, and spend more time on lead generation than on building leverage.

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

The third reason introduces a triad the rest of the chapter develops: *finding*, *attracting* and *becoming*. The traditional model asks the person to find customers. Koe's model asks the person to become someone whose development is itself the product, and to attract people who want the same development. The second reason, the failure rate of businesses that solve problems they have not experienced, is the seed of Section 19.3; the first, caring about the people one serves, is the seed of the "you attract who you are" argument.

A fourth set of harms appears in "Ideas Are The New Oil" (2024). "Niching down is a great way to increase competition, not decrease it." It is also good at making you "a slave to your client or someone that you don't like working with," and at leaving you unable to adapt as technology evolves. Koe clarifies what he means: he is not against all specificity but against niching down "in the traditional sense" of choosing a skill, going as deep as possible and targeting a narrow demographic. He grants that this may have value for paid ads, where one can type a demographic into a targeting tool. On social media, where the spread of content is random, it "shoots you in the foot": the creator gets no reach, cannot attract the niche anyway, and is eventually forced to broaden. His parody of the narrow demographic is deliberately absurd: "40-year-old soccer moms that love to drink Kool-Aid on the side of the game... then they drive the minivan home."

**Source:** Ideas Are The New Oil (How More People Get Rich In The Digital Age).md

The claim that niching *increases* competition runs against the usual logic, which says a narrower market has fewer competitors. Koe's reasoning, reconstructed from the surrounding material, is that a narrow niche defined by a skill and a demographic is easy for anyone with the same skill to enter, so it fills quickly with interchangeable providers, while a niche defined by a person cannot be entered by anyone else. Section 19.2 develops that second half.

#### Shiny object syndrome as a symptom

The 2024 video draws one more lesson from the niche search, phrased as a principle: "The endless shiny object syndrome you have with finding a niche is a blatant sign that business hasn't clicked for you yet. You don't understand it. You haven't failed enough. You haven't produced enough. You can make any niche work. That's why it's wise to create your own."

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

Two things are claimed here. The first is diagnostic: endlessly switching niches signals a lack of understanding, failure and output, not a lack of the right market. The second is a surprising inference: because any niche can be made to work by someone who understands business, the choice among list-niches is not the decisive variable, and so the person might as well choose the one niche no one else can occupy.

This use of "shiny object syndrome" should be read against Koe's other uses of the label, which the corpus records as an unresolved tension. Elsewhere, especially in 2022–2023, he defends shiny object syndrome as a necessary phase of experimentation ("I attribute my unique success to shiny object syndrome"), and in 2025 he criticizes parents, teachers and friends who use the label to dismiss people with multiple interests. The two positions can coexist if they refer to different behaviors: jumping from niche to niche in search of the most profitable market is one thing; exploring interests in order to find what one cannot stop doing is another. The 2024 line targets the first. Section 19.6 returns to the second.

#### Reality is not niched down

Alongside the practical objections runs a philosophical one, which connects the niche question to the critique of compartmentalization developed in Chapter 2. Its first statement comes in March 2023, in "The Future Of One-Person Businesses." School starts broad, with general studies, and then niches people into a compartment: a biology degree, a particular medical specialty. "That neglects the holistic nature of life." Online business repeats the pattern when it tells creators to niche down as far as possible to target one specific problem. Koe adds a personal qualification: building an audience and a personal brand is somewhat new, and after three years of doing it, plus his previous freelancing, he says he understands both sides and that the strategies for making an income are "vastly different." His conclusion is practical: if you niche down and stay in one small niche, you limit your audience growth and your future potential to pivot. The compressed principle is: "reality is not compartmentalized."

**Source:** The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md

In July 2023 he restates it more bluntly: "reality is not niched down, you are not niched down." He is "not huge on picking one niche and sticking with it and hammering it out," because it means missing out on much of life and on many of the ways one could serve an audience. He then gives a reason grounded in the identity model of Part II: the ideas one collects shape one's worldview, perspective and identity ("oh, I'm a writer, I'm a value creator"), and identity changes how one acts. A niche chosen from outside freezes a part of that process. The corpus notes that this sentence qualifies, rather than contradicts, his advice elsewhere to "target a profitable niche": what he rejects is the narrow niche defined by topic, not the niche defined by the person.

**Source:** The Value Creator (A New Internet Career Path For Intelligent People).md

The fullest version comes from "The Cure To A Mediocre Life" (August 2023), where niching down is presented as one symptom of what Chapter 2 called the default path, the trajectory a person is conditioned into from birth. The default path suffers from compartmentalization, specialization and niching down. "We are trained to focus on one dot instead of the lines that connect the dots." In school, biology, chemistry, mathematics and literature are taught in separate classes that never connect, and so the education lacks holism, creativity and "the practicality that gets abnormal results." After school the narrowing continues: teenagers must choose one of infinite paths before they have started real life. "There doesn't seem to be a greater recipe for misery than to focus on one thing for the rest of your life"; it strips curiosity and creativity. The result is a split between types of people that Koe compresses into a line: "Warriors lack brains and intellectuals lack balls." The prescription of the default path is school, job, partner, a few good activities squeezed in, retirement at 65 and no more work, when in Koe's view work is a necessary part of life that should involve investing attention in what one enjoys. "In school, pick a major; in business, pick a niche," each time neglecting the other areas that make the chosen one work, and results suffer. "The effects of compartmentalized learning destroy our individual potential."

**Source:** The Cure To A Mediocre Life (Become Multidimensionally Jacked).md

Read in sequence, the three statements form a chain. Reality is interconnected; a person is part of reality and is equally interconnected; therefore a business that is an extension of a person cannot be compartmentalized without amputating part of the person. This chain is the philosophical ground of "you are the niche," and Chapter 20 extends it into a full critique of specialization.

#### Ideologies of business

The same January 2023 video in which Koe introduces the niche of one contains a warning that applies the critique to business models themselves. "From a spiritual or philosophical lens, your brand is yourself; it is a concept." That concept can be the product of "a repetitive business ideology that has been conditioned into your head." If someone has only studied freelancing and never looked into, for example, how a Facebook ads agency works, so as to collect aspects of that perspective into their worldview, "it's gonna end in a lot of pain and suffering," because they will think their business model is the best "just like religions do or even sports teams do." The remedy is to step back, zoom out, and create the brand as something unique, "without attaching to any specific ideology," building "a holistic philosophy for your own brand."

**Source:** The Most Profitable Niche Is You (Create Your Niche Of One).md

The comparison to religions and sports teams is Koe's, and it frames attachment to a business model as an identity attachment of the kind Chapter 3 described: the model becomes part of who one is, and alternatives become threats. The corpus records a later moment of self-criticism that bears on this warning. In January 2026 Koe asks himself, of his own advocacy of the creator path: "am I just like promoting this dogma?" and answers that "the more I break it down, the more it seems like the one true way." The source notes the tension between that conviction and the earlier warning against attaching to a business ideology. Koe does not resolve it; he keeps the thesis and admits the objection.

#### The one-person business as nested holons

The last idea of this section gives the critique of compartmentalization its positive form. In a 2024 talk on deep work Koe borrows Ken Wilber's concept of the holon, something that is simultaneously a whole and a part of a larger whole, and applies it to the one-person business. "You are a whole that includes your interests; your interests are part and a whole of you, but they are also a part and a whole of your Niche, and then your Niche is a part and whole of the market, and then markets are kind of parts and wholes of social media."

**Source:** Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md

**Complementary context:** Ken Wilber, the integral philosopher, uses "holon" (a term from Arthur Koestler) to describe a world built of nested wholes, each of which "transcends and includes" the levels below it. Chapter 38 presents Wilber's framework in detail.

The nesting has a practical consequence for diagnosis. If one is struggling in any domain, there is usually a specific problem one can pinpoint; then one should "zoom out and see the bigger picture of how it connects with other things." A creator whose content is not working might find the cause not in the content but in the niche, or in the relationship between their interests and the market. "This is why multi-perspectival understanding and open-mindedness is so important... it allows you to solve creative problems by connecting the dots." The holon picture also shows why niche-down feels wrong to so many people: it asks them to start from the outermost layers (market, platform) and carve a place for themselves inside, when the structure actually grows outward from the person.

### 19.2 You Are the Niche and the Experience Model

#### The niche of one

The alternative to niche-down has several names in the corpus, and the corpus treats them as labels for the same thesis: "niche of one," "the niche of you," "the most profitable niche is you," "you are the niche," "become the niche," and, at different points, "I am my own niche," "the anti-niche," "nicheless" and "mission as niche." The central coinage appears in the January 2023 video "The Most Profitable Niche Is You (Create Your Niche Of One)." "Let's create your niche of one, or the niche of you. Here's a graphic: you are the niche."

**Source:** The Most Profitable Niche Is You (Create Your Niche Of One).md

The **niche of one**, as Koe defines it there, is a niche defined not by a market segment but by a person: by their unique path (the desire to change, the learning, the skills, the experience gained), by their broad ideal future, and by the problems they solved along the way. It "houses your content, products or services": it is the container inside which everything the creator makes sits. Koe's reasoning for why it works has two parts that he repeats in almost every version. First, every person's story is unique; it is "niche, individual and unique." Second, millions of people share any given person's interests, problems and desires, so the creator needs to reach only a fraction of them. The example he points to is his own Twitter account, which covered marketing, fitness, emotional management and aspirational content, not one topic.

The 2024 video opens with the thesis in its most compressed form: "The most authentic, profitable, and unique niche is you." Koe presents it as a conclusion reached "after years of pain," because the first thing one encounters on the journey to becoming "a free individual" is the instruction to niche down. The phrasing makes the idea autobiographical before it is theoretical. Two years earlier, in "11 Lessons From Growing A 7-Figure One Person Business" (2022), the same idea appears as Lesson 7, "the most profitable niche is you," with a historical argument attached. Traditional, salesy or sleazy branding, marketing and sales are coming to an end: "we've explored that crevice of reality," it had its time, and people are catching on and growing more wary of scams. "The age of authenticity is among us." To get ahead of the curve, Koe advises, productize yourself: turn the brand into a cohesive business (he refers to a graphic in his one-person business video that the transcript does not allow us to reconstruct).

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md; 11 Lessons From Growing A 7-Figure One Person Business.md

In a 2024 video on the future of work, Koe connects the thesis to his distinction between two economies. "You are the most profitable niche. The creator economy, not to be confused with the influencer economy, is characterized by individuals who pursue their interests and document their knowledge. Creators attract people to their vision, their story, their goals." And then, in the context of AI: "AI is not curious. You must give it the context of a vision, story or goal to work. Curiosity and generalism are your edge in attracting like-minded people who have problems you've solved in your life."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md

The distinction between the creator and the influencer is doing real work. An influencer, in Koe's usage, is someone whose value is attention as such; a creator is someone whose value is the documented pursuit of something. The niche of one belongs to the second: people are attracted not to the person's visibility but to the direction they are moving in. This is why the niche of one does not reduce to self-display, a point Koe makes explicit years later.

#### Where the term comes from: Justin Welsh

The phrase "niche of one" did not originate with Koe, and the corpus records its earliest appearance in another person's mouth. In a December 2021 interview, the solopreneur Justin Welsh describes his own approach: "I'm the brand, I'm the niche. The niche is me. And when the niche is me, I don't compete with anyone else." He tries "to teach people to build a niche of one. Build something where people like you because you're entertaining, humorous, funny, polarizing. Be the guy or the girl that people want to follow." His condition is: "as long as you're interesting, there can only be one of you." And his consequence draws on Kevin Kelly: "if you can build that niche of one, you get... Kevin Kelly, thousand true fans... you got a walking business there."

**Source:** Justin Welsh Shows You How To Start & Grow A One-Person Business.md

**Complementary context:** Kevin Kelly's essay "1,000 True Fans" (2008) argues that a creator needs only about a thousand people willing to buy whatever they make in order to earn a living. Chapter 25 discusses how Koe uses the idea.

Koe's response in the same interview is the first statement of his own version. Being your own niche is "a big thing I talk about a lot," he says, and as "a big spiritual guy... and like philosophical," he spins it this way: "your conditioning, every single... external stimuli you've been exposed to, your personality, the things that have built up over time, makes it so nobody can compete with you." The condition he attaches is crucial: this holds "if you learn specific skills to market and sell yourself on the internet, and understand those hard skills of content creation, writing, marketing, sales, et cetera."

**Source:** Justin Welsh Shows You How To Start & Grow A One-Person Business.md

This short exchange contains the logical structure of the whole thesis. Uniqueness is given: it comes from one's conditioning, the sum of everything one has been exposed to. In Part I, conditioning was the problem, the program installed from outside; here the same conditioning, once made conscious, becomes the asset, because no one else has the same history. But uniqueness alone does not make a business. Leverage comes from learnable skills: writing, content, marketing and sales. The rest of the book, especially Parts VIII to XI, is about those skills. "You are the niche" is therefore never an argument against learning; it is an argument about what the learning should be applied to.

#### Escape competition through authenticity

The idea that authenticity removes competition reaches Koe through two later sources, both of which go back to Naval Ravikant. In a January 2025 interview with the founders of Stan, a creator-commerce platform, co-founder John Hugh calls the niche "a really polarizing topic" and asks listeners to take his view "with a grain of salt." His overarching theme is "you are the niche." To the objection that there are already so many fitness creators or philosophy writers, he answers: true, "but there's none of you." He keeps a line from Naval in his head: "escape competition through authenticity." A fitness trainer faces a mature, competitive market, but no one else has *your* story: your particular physical ailments, two kids, learning to lose weight while picking up your kids, staying sane while working a nine-to-five. "At that level of authenticity and specificity no one else will beat you." That is "the learned and earned insight that you particularly have that is like your niche."

**Source:** How To Grow An Audience With Zero Followers & Build A Profitable Startup.md

John's example shows what "specificity" means in this framework. It is not the specificity of a demographic chosen from outside (soccer moms with minivans) but the specificity of a lived situation, which happens to define a demographic from inside: everyone else who is a parent of two with a job and a weight problem. The corpus notes that in the same interview John also warns against being too broad at the start and proposes a hyper-specific entry point; Section 19.6 returns to that tension.

In June 2025, Koe opens a method for building a personal brand by citing Naval's essay directly. Naval's argument, as Koe reports it, is that artists are by definition authentic, and so are entrepreneurs: "who's going to be Elon Musk? Who's going to be Jack Dorsey?" Their businesses and products are authentic to their own desires and means. Koe applies this to viewers who want to turn interests or skills into "a future-proof income source" without becoming "a hollow shell of a human being," putting themselves in a box, or building "another 9 to 5."

**Source:** How To Build A Better Personal Brand Than 99% Of People.md

**Complementary context:** Naval Ravikant is an investor and essayist whose "How to Get Rich (without getting lucky)" and related writings are among the sources Koe adapts most heavily; "Escape competition through authenticity" is one of Naval's aphorisms, built on the idea that no one can compete with you at being you.

#### Being the niche without knowing it

One of the most interesting observations in the 2024 video concerns why the niche-down advice persists even among successful creators who do not follow it. "The best personal brands do this without trying. And then usually they tell you to go into a niche because they don't realize that they are the niche. They don't realize what they're doing." Koe imagines their self-description: "I post about this thing maybe every other day, but I also talk about all these other things, but no, that's not even a part of my brand." He gives the phenomenon a name from learning theory: "That's called unconscious competence." These creators post whatever they consider important and try to illustrate why it is important, and the content does well "because if it's valuable to them, it's probably valuable to you or some people."

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

**Unconscious competence** is a common term (the last stage of the "four stages of competence" model, in which a skill is performed without awareness of how). Koe uses it with a specific sense here: successful creators are already the niche, but because they do it intuitively they misdescribe their own success and pass on advice that does not match their practice. The observation explains why so much contradictory advice circulates, and it is a methodological lesson Koe applies throughout the corpus: watch what successful people do, not what they say they do.

A few minutes later in the same video, Koe closes a related objection. People think they are not niched down if they talk about two or three interests when there are so many other interests out there. "You're very niched down by being the niche." The combination of a person's interests is already extremely specific; no one else talks about exactly that combination in exactly that way. Specificity is not lost by adding interests; it is produced by the intersection.

In a 2024 video on minimalist productivity, Koe answers the objection that "not everyone can do this." His reply: you can sell whatever you want; most local businesses are already online doing the same thing; marketing no longer runs mainly through direct mail and billboards (maybe sometimes), and the aim is high leverage. Everyone can do it "if they are the niche": if they do not conform to agency models and templated e-commerce stores, if they lean into their interests and personality, solve their own problems and sell the solution.

**Source:** Minimalist Productivity Work Less, Do More.md

#### Later versions: point of view, the mind in public and the AI context

Between 2021 and 2023, "you are the niche" rested on story, personality and conditioning. From 2025 the emphasis shifts, and the corpus records this as a cumulative refinement rather than a reversal. The new context is AI.

In May 2025: "You are the niche. You are the differentiator": your mastery, experience and way of looking at the world "through a perception forged by every bit of information you've processed over the entirety of your life." The argument now runs through AI's effect on products. "When AI makes 90% of the products the same, nothing really changes. People continue to buy from people and brands that they know, trust, and care about." The conclusion reshapes what a business is: "It's less about building a sales funnel and more about building a world that people can explore," a reference to his article "How to Build a World: The 2 Hour Content Ecosystem 2.0." Chapter 27 develops the brand as a world.

**Source:** The Future Of Work (How To Become AI-First).md

The "90%" is a rhetorical projection, not a measurement; the point is that if products converge, the differentiator moves from the product to the person.

In April 2026, in a video urging viewers to write essays, Koe gives the thesis an experiential condition. He has long said "you are the niche" because a person's most valuable resource is their point of view, and AI does not have access to it, since "every passing moment" influences it. But a point of view worth writing about cannot be built at a desk "on Claude 24/7." It requires novel experiences, living life, not "the optimized, efficient productivity life of just staring at your laptop all day." While everyone is "locked in to the agents, the new agents, the open claw," building things that get them nowhere, Koe's counsel is to reject that: "go out, experience life, build a good life, and then bring that online, because nobody else is doing that. That's how you become unique."

**Source:** I'm begging you to start writing essays (even if you hate writing).md

This version adds something the earlier ones lacked. In 2021 uniqueness was guaranteed by the past (conditioning); in 2026 it must also be renewed in the present, because a point of view stops developing if the person stops having experiences. The niche of one is not a fixed possession but a process that can stall.

In August 2026 Koe corrects a reading of his idea he attributes to critics, who think "you are the niche" means "posting about you, you, you." "No, you're documenting, you're placing your mind in public for people to explore. Your mind is not just your personality traits and... your story and the things about you. It's the ideas that create this web of interconnections that make how you say the ideas unique." The "second subconscious," his term for a well-kept external note system (Chapter 16), is, used correctly, an externalization of that web and serves as fuel for public work.

**Source:** How To Learn Anything 10x Faster Than Anyone.md

The three late statements can be put side by side with the early ones to show the evolution:

| Period | What makes you the niche | Representative statement |
|---|---|---|
| 2021–2023 | Conditioning, personality, story | "your conditioning... makes it so nobody can compete with you" |
| 2025 | Mastery and perception, as differentiator when AI equalizes products | "You are the niche. You are the differentiator" |
| 2026-04 | A point of view kept alive by novel experience | "go out, experience life, build a good life, and then bring that online" |
| 2026-08 | The mind as a web of interconnected ideas, placed in public | "you're placing your mind in public for people to explore" |

The later versions do not abandon the earlier ones; story and personality remain part of the mind. What changes is the center of gravity: from biography to ideas and perspective.

The phrase "niche of one" also served as a product promise, which shows how central Koe considered it. In a January 2023 promotion inside a video on distraction, he announces the first cohort of Solopreneur Sprints (starting February 7), designed to help participants "create your niche of one," write "20 plus pieces of foundational content" live with him while showing his thought process, and grow with a strategy that costs nothing: building relationships "in the new economy, which is the digital world." "It's not an emerging economy, it is the new economy." The same promotion mentions The 2 Hour Writer, his content-writing system built from six years of marketing and freelancing experience, and Modern Mastery (five dollars to join, then $27 per month, with a Discord, a strategy library, articles and roadmaps). The transcript offers only premises; it does not describe the method of the sprint itself.

**Source:** Distraction Is The Greatest Trap Of The 21st Century (How To Avoid).md

#### Nicheless: your identity is your niche

If the niche is the person, and the person changes, then the niche must change. Koe draws this consequence explicitly in the 2024 video. "Your identity is your niche. Or put more practically, your niche is what you are interested in and why it's important to your life." The practical version deserves attention: it defines the niche by two components, the interests and their *importance*, which is the material Section 19.7 will turn into content.

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

From this definition Koe derives a warning. Do not box yourself into hyper-specific skills or interests that you will outgrow "because you are human": you will grow bored, or it will no longer serve you, "like talking about agency work because you change what you do as your business grows." "Locking yourself into a niche makes it extremely difficult to develop yourself beyond it. Your business evolves as you evolve. If your business is a narrow niche, you limit your own potential."

The coinage that follows is **nicheless**: "When you are the niche and you are constantly changing and evolving because you're improving, hopefully, then you are nicheless. If you aren't constantly evolving and improving and your niche isn't changing, then you aren't changing and you're probably in the wrong type of business and it's not going to be sustainable."

The word is paradoxical on purpose. Being the niche and being nicheless are the same condition seen from two angles: from the inside, the niche is the person; from the outside, it never stays still long enough to be named as a market segment. The last sentence turns the logic of niche-down upside down. In the traditional model, a stable niche is a sign of a well-positioned business; in Koe's model, a niche that never changes is a sign that the person has stopped developing, which in the identity model of Part II means the person has stopped pursuing new goals. The examples Koe gives (Zuby and Hamza) are discussed in Section 19.4.

#### The experience model

So far "you are the niche" has been a claim about positioning. In October 2022 Koe gives it the form of a business model. In "The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself)" he argues that for people who want to do what they want and help those they can help most, traditional advice about branding, marketing, content and offers does not fit. He mentions the "value creator" as a style of one-person business (developed in a following video, and in Chapter 35 of this book) and calls what he is describing **the experience model**: "you are your customer avatar, you are your brand, you are your niche, you are your content, you are your offer, you are everything." The skills that matter most in this model are self-awareness and self-reliance, and those who thrive are the ones who take responsibility for their own future and help others get where they are going.

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself).md

In March 2023 he gives the experience model its shortest definition: you help people at point A, where you were before, get to point B, where you are now, with content and products in between containing knowledge, experience and systems. "And that's how you make money."

**Source:** The Best Online Business To Make $1 Million In 3-5 Years.md

In July 2024 he sets it explicitly against the traditional model. "The traditional business model involves creating a customer avatar based on someone with a profitable problem that you can solve": one identifies a problem and creates a solution. "The experience model turns you into the customer avatar. Your experience and story turn you into the niche; that way you can solve your own problems, attract people that are on a similar path as you, and help them do the same." The bonus is practical: "you don't have to spend countless hours doing market research to understand what will sell." The process is a sequence: "you pursue a goal, achieve it, talk about it, attract people with that same goal, and offer them a solution to reach it faster," through a product or a service, helping them "improve, but faster than you did."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md

| | Traditional model | Experience model |
|---|---|---|
| Starting point | A profitable problem in the market | A goal the creator is pursuing |
| Customer avatar | Built through market research | The creator (their past self) |
| Source of knowledge | Research and speculation | Lived experience of solving the problem |
| What is sold | A solution to someone else's problem | The path from A to B, faster than the creator walked it |
| Validation | Testing in the market | Already validated by the creator's own result |

The experience model is the business form of the identity model. In Chapter 8 a goal was what organizes a person's actions; here the same goal organizes a business, because the path to it generates the problems (which become content), the solutions (which become products) and the people (who become the audience).

#### The four pillars: goals, problems, systems, benefits

Within the experience model, Koe offers a framework for translating a life into the components of a business. It appears in the October 2022 video, recurs nearly word for word in the long 2024 compilation "The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide)," which adds the explicit label "experience model," and reappears as a graphic in "Life Is A Video Game (You Can't Escape The Matrix)" in September 2023. He calls it the **four pillars of the one-person business**.

| Pillar | Life element | Business function | Guiding question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your **goals** | **Brand** | What do you want out of life, and where are you leading people? |
| 2 | The **problems** standing in the way of your goals | **Content** | What roadblocks will your followers encounter, and how will you help them overcome them? |
| 3 | The **systems** you use to solve the problems | **Product** | How can they solve their problems faster? (Because that is what people want.) |
| 4 | The **benefits** of the journey, how it impacted your life | **Marketing** | Why should they care about your message? |

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md; Life Is A Video Game (You Can't Escape The Matrix).md

Each translation has its own logic. Goals become the brand because a brand, in Koe's sense, is a direction: people follow someone who is going somewhere they want to go. Problems become content because the obstacles on the way to the goal are exactly what others on the same path need help with; Koe calls this "a starting point," acknowledging that content involves more. Systems become products because a product is a compressed version of the method the creator found, sold to people who want to skip the trial and error. Benefits become marketing because the reason to care about a message is what it changed in the life of the person delivering it. For this last pillar Koe stresses "benefits-rich language" as "extremely powerful and persuasive," and gives his own example: the reasons he writes every day (to earn an income, to think more clearly, to systemize all his other content) became persuasive arguments on his landing pages.

The 2023 graphic adds a reason for the order: the brand builds leverage and readership, content nurtures that readership, the product monetizes it, and marketing makes monetization possible.

Koe compresses the framework into one sentence: "you pursue a goal, you solve your own problems, you teach others how you did it, and that's your business." He also qualifies it in the same breath. It is oversimplified and has many nuances; one must apply and test the principles oneself, and doing so produces unique, genuine knowledge. It takes time but "pays back tenfold." The alternative, hiring someone to build one's brand, "relinquishes all potential leverage and just power." The qualification is consistent with the self-experimentation principle of Chapter 15: the framework is a hypothesis to test, not a recipe.

One textual problem should be noted. In the 2022 video Koe announces "four pillars" but, when he walks through them a second time, numbers branding, content and offer as pillars one to three, and the transcript does not explicitly label marketing as pillar four in that second pass. The four-part mapping in the table is the one he states in the first pass and that the 2023 graphic shows.

#### The business as a system

A 2024 video gives the pillars a systemic reading that explains why they must be considered together. "A system is a group of parts that are connected or work together to form a goal-achieving complex whole; each part affects the system and depends on the other parts." Koe's analogy is the digestive system, in which the stomach and intestines work together. "Your business is made up of brand, content, product and marketing." Without a good product, itself a system, there is no income; neglect content and there is no traffic to sell to; if the product's landing page is weak, that is a subsystem one must diagnose and fix so as to put energy in more efficiently. "Systems become more efficient with time unless you neglect them."

**Source:** Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md

This reading connects the four pillars to the cybernetic model of the mind in Chapter 6, where a goal-seeking system corrects itself through feedback. A business built from the experience model is that same kind of system, and its weak point at any moment can be located by asking which pillar is failing. Chapter 28 develops the pillars as the functions of the one-person business; here what matters is where each comes from.

#### Later versions of the pillars

The four pillars were revised several times, and the corpus documents the revisions as renamings of the same architecture rather than contradictions.

In June 2023, in "The One Person Business Model 2.0 (Turn Yourself Into A Business)," Koe reads a tweet of his own: "for brand, tell a story; for content, make a map; for product, create a game." The tweet names three; the walkthrough adds the fourth, marketing, as "selling to yourself." As developed in the video, the pillars become: (1) brand: you are the niche, your identity, your story, your vision; (2) content: documenting your mind; (3) product: public personal projects; (4) marketing: selling to yourself. The transcript does not explain the phrase "create a game" any further.

**Source:** The One Person Business Model 2.0 (Turn Yourself Into A Business).md

In the August 2024 video the framework is reduced to three pillars and rephrased as an answer to the demand for specificity. "Niches are supposed to be specific. And what is more specific than this? A desirable goal that will change your life and your reader's lives, which is brand. A burning problem that will ease your suffering and your reader's suffering, which is content. A clear path, system or solution to bring you clarity and your reader's clarity, which is product."

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

This version is rhetorically important because it answers the niche-down camp on its own terms. Specificity is not abandoned; it is relocated. A goal, a problem and a path are more specific than a demographic, because they describe exactly what someone wants, what is stopping them and how to get through.

In September 2023, in "How To Reprogram Your Mind & Build Your Intelligence," a fourth formulation maps the self directly onto the business. "A personal brand is an extension of your character, the self you continue to develop. Your goals are what attract like-minded buyers. Your problems, when solved, are how you help others in the form of a product or service. Your ideas are the posts that occupy the mental real estate of your followers and shape their sense of self." Writing and speaking "your beautiful mind to the masses through social media is how you check the boxes of a fulfilling life." Koe calls this "turning yourself into the business."

**Source:** How To Reprogram Your Mind & Build Your Intelligence.md

| Version | Brand | Content | Product | Marketing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 four pillars | Goals | Problems | Systems | Benefits |
| 2023-06 "2.0" | Tell a story (identity, vision) | Make a map (documenting your mind) | Create a game (public personal projects) | Sell to yourself |
| 2023-09 self-to-business | Character (the developing self); goals attract buyers | Ideas as posts occupying mental real estate | Problems solved | (not named) |
| 2024-08 three pillars | Desirable goal | Burning problem | Clear path | (absorbed) |

The table shows two movements. The fourth pillar, marketing, is named differently in different versions and eventually disappears; the corpus records that by 2026 Koe speaks of "the three pillars, which used to be four." And the metaphors migrate: "map" is applied to the product in one 2023 version and to content in the 2.0 version. In the 2023-09 mapping, notice that the correspondences shift: goals no longer *are* the brand but *attract* buyers to it, and problems become the product rather than the content. Koe does not explain these changes. What persists across all versions is the claim that the business components are not invented but extracted from the creator's own pursuit: direction, obstacles, method and result.

### 19.3 Build, Write and Sell to Yourself

#### The simplification

If the creator is the niche, then the question "who is my customer?" has an unexpected answer. In the January 2023 video Koe proposes a three-part simplification of the whole one-person business: "One, build for yourself; two, write to yourself; three, sell to yourself." The reasoning is the same arithmetic that grounds the niche of one: "there are millions of people with the same interests, problems and desires as you, and you only need to find a fraction of them. The most profitable niche is you."

**Source:** The Most Profitable Niche Is You (Create Your Niche Of One).md

In April 2023, in "Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You)," he spells out each part: "Build the solution that solves your problems to perfection, the content that speaks to the person going through that problem, sell the solution with your personal experiences with that problem. Build, write and sell to yourself if you want to eliminate all competition." He then describes his own case without euphemism: "my entire business journey was selfish." He built for himself, wrote to himself, sold to himself: "my customer avatar was me. I built for him, I wrote to him." He wrote online what he wanted to read, the kind of content he liked on Twitter, Instagram and YouTube.

**Source:** Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md

The word "selfish" is provocative but precise. The model works because the creator's own problems are a reliable sample of other people's: if something solved the creator's problem, it is likely to solve the same problem for people in the same situation. Section 19.4 gives the reason Koe believes the sample is reliable: everyone's problems fall within the same few domains.

In July 2024 he restates the formula and shows how it maps results onto products. Results in fitness become a fitness program; results with focus, a productivity course; with a skill, a tutorial; with psychology, a journaling prompt; with spirituality, a meditation. "These products aren't all the same. They're made from experience, your story, what you found works for you." His example is Kinobody, a fitness creator who sells a two-workouts-a-week program built around his whole intermittent fasting philosophy, which is very different from what a bodybuilder with completely different goals would sell. They are in the same field and sell different solutions. "You attract people to the goal that you're pursuing because you are the niche... and people are going to be attracted to different people. That's how the creator economy works."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md

The Kinobody example is the market-level proof of the thesis: "fitness" is not one niche but as many niches as there are distinct paths through it, and each path attracts the people who want to walk it.

#### The shortcut: build what you would buy

The 2024 video phrases the principle as a lesson with a striking claim attached. "Build a product or service that you would actually buy, use, and benefit from. There aren't many shortcuts in business, but this is one of them. When you do that, you become the niche, and you can focus on marketing to your past and current self." The second half of the lesson extends the principle to writing: "Write the words that you would stop, read, and act on. When you know yourself, you get to skip ahead of the 99% of failed businesses that haven't experienced the problems they are trying to solve."

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

The "99%" is rhetorical, but the logic is the one already seen in 19.1: most businesses fail because they try to solve problems they have not experienced, and self-knowledge removes that cause of failure.

A December 2023 video on the "Build Teach Earn" method adds two elements. The first is a warning: "If you don't sell your own product, you will be assigned one to sell." The phrase echoes the thesis of Chapter 1, that if one does not choose one's goals, someone else's are assigned; here the same structure applies to products. Without lying to oneself, the creator must make something they would buy, use and benefit from. The second element explains what the shortcut saves: when you are the niche you do not need to worry excessively about market research, product-market fit or crafting the perfect marketing strategy. "Building an audience is a modern cheat code": your content is how you test ideas that turn into your marketing. Koe's example is posting ten tweets a day to test ten YouTube titles or thumbnail texts and using the most engaging one; he admits he has not done this himself but has seen people run ten Facebook ads at a time to choose a podcast thumbnail.

**Source:** How Intelligent Creators Will Make Money In 2024 (The Build Teach Earn Method).md

#### Experience, not speculation

In a September 2024 video on meaningful wealth, Koe states the epistemological basis of the model. "There are thousands of other people like you. I'm just simply talking about the problems I had in my life and giving out solutions to them; I attract an audience that way. That's what the best brands and businesses, in my opinion, do: their businesses are built on experience, not speculation or theory." They are built on a set of questions: "what do I know, what can I help the most with, what problems have I solved in my life, why are those important." And that, he says, is the marketing strategy: "build a product for yourself, market to yourself, write to yourself, attract people like you, because that's who you can help the most. So you know who you're going to target."

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md

A minute later he draws the strongest conclusion in this section. "Most businesses fail because they try to solve a problem they haven't experienced; they spin up some fancy startup idea in their head and then justify their failure with a dumb statistic like 99% of startups fail." If you pigeonhole yourself into a narrow niche and a problem you have no experience with, "there isn't really a path out of that." That is why he recommends becoming the niche, "because your business is only limited by your ability to develop yourself; your products evolve as you do because you solve new problems and you get better." The business has a simple loop: "Every business has an audience; it's traffic." "You test ideas with content, you turn those best ideas into products, you improve your products or create new ones until it succeeds." And then the claim: "You can't really fail if you are the business, because you're in full control of your improvement."

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md

This claim must be read precisely. Koe is not saying that every business built this way makes money; elsewhere he documents seven failed businesses of his own (Chapter 37). He is saying that the failure mode of the traditional model, being stuck in a market one does not understand with no path out, does not apply when the business is a function of one's own development, because the variable that determines success (one's improvement) is under one's control. It is a statement about the structure of the bet, and it presupposes the hard skills he named in 2021.

#### A worked example: the minimalist training program

Koe's most repeated example appears first in December 2022, in "You Have A $100,000 Product In Your Head," and is repeated almost verbatim in the 2024 compilation. Some people selling minimalist training programs are "raking in the cash." Their story is typical. They are not deeply into fitness the way a bodybuilder is; they do not want to train five to seven days a week, only two. So they research, experiment and create a program that gets them results, and then it makes sense to help others like them, "because you are the niche." Being in the gym two hours a week and still getting results is unique. And the marketing, the *why*, is already laid out: "I only have two hours a week to train, I still want to be fit and healthy, I still want to work on my business." For a person very similar to the creator, the marketing "plays itself out."

**Source:** You Have A $100,000 Product In Your Head (One Person Business Series).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The example shows every pillar from 19.2 at once. The goal (be fit with minimal time) is the brand; the problems on the way (limited time, conflicting advice) are the content; the program is the system turned product; and the benefit (fitness without sacrificing the business) is the marketing copy. It also shows why market research is unnecessary: the creator's own constraints define the customer.

In October 2024 Koe generalizes the example into the idea of productizing oneself. If you attract an audience with a personality and vision for the future similar to yours, "AKA You Are The Niche," then the projects you build to achieve your own goals "can easily be turned into products that other people pay for. That's how you productize yourself." He adds a second point aimed at people who only consume solutions: if you only use other people's fitness programs, meditation programs or writing systems without experimenting with your own, start experimenting, "so that you can pass down better ways to do things, and that's how things improve." Chapter 33 develops productizing oneself as a product strategy.

**Source:** The Death Of The Personal Brand (& The Future Of Creative Work).md

#### Why you are told to niche down, and why you are the customer avatar

The 2024 video contains Koe's most candid account of what the niche-down advice is actually for. You are told to niche down "so you can understand the minds of your readers and customers": create a customer avatar, identify a burning problem, position a solution toward them. "The solution is often the same as any other product on the market with small changes. The point is to make it perceived as valuable to a specific person by understanding them." He then offers what he calls a hack: "recreate what already exists, but better. And don't overcomplicate it." And a strong generalization: "All products are the same. Like, all self-help products are the same. All business products are the same. They're just positioned towards different people." That, he says, is why creating a niche matters.

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

This is the conceptual pivot of the chapter. Niching is about *positioning*, about making a product be perceived as valuable by a specific mind. Koe does not dispute that positioning is necessary. He disputes the method for achieving it. If the purpose is to understand a customer's mind, there is one mind the creator already understands from inside. Hence the next step of the argument. "I can talk about writing and someone else can talk about writing, but it's two completely different people, and that's what makes it unique." Koe targets his past and former self, the problems that self was trying to solve and the goals it was trying to achieve. Another writer may position toward soccer moms, because they are one or because they chose that niche. "Targeting yourself is just so much easier, and it goes a layer deeper than just making money": it involves self-improvement, self-awareness, building products that actually help, and working with people one enjoys. "Why don't we skip all of that and treat ourselves as the customer avatar, solve problems that we've actually experienced?" As the creator grows, the products evolve, and they build and distribute a new one.

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

Koe's 2024 definition of the term explains why this substitution works. "In essence, a customer avatar is the mind, worldview, or perspective of someone you are creating for or marketing toward." Section 19.4 develops the structure of that worldview. If a customer avatar is a worldview, then the creator's own past worldview is an avatar they know completely.

In January 2026 he states the inversion as a principle. The development path "flips the traditional model on its head. Traditionally you need a customer avatar, and you need to niche down to that customer avatar. But with this model, you are the customer avatar, and that makes things so much more palatable." "If you need market research, you just look at your story." "You pursue your goals in life and develop yourself, and by doing that, you have already validated the usefulness of what you will offer, and then you help the past version of yourself reach that same goal."

**Source:** If you have multiple interests, do not waste the next 2-3 years.md

Two statements from 2023 make the same point in a dialogue form Koe uses often. "But Dan, what about my customer avatar and market research?" "It's you. You are the niche, you are the customer avatar. Write the content and promotions that would speak to you." This replaces "boring market research" with creating one's own customers.

**Source:** Micro Education Businesses Are The Future & How To Start With Zero Dollars.md

The deepest version of the argument, from April 2023, ties the customer avatar to the identity model. Koe prefers "turning yourself into the customer avatar and marketing to your past self and attracting the people that are like you." It is easier and more fulfilling, because "you already have it in your head," and because "you don't have to work with someone you hate in order to make money; you just market towards your past self." The reason one should market to one's past self in particular is that one knows that perspective best, and "identity and perspective are very closely linked." The chain continues: perspective forms the foundation of content, writing and products; the people one attracts come to form a shared perspective; perspective is oriented toward goals and visions, so one leads people toward a goal or vision and can market products better, creating "a holistic business model that you truly enjoy."

**Source:** Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md

In March 2024 the mechanism receives its most explicit formulation, as a consequence of Koe's theory of value (Chapter 31). "Value is perceived based on a person's identity." This is why he recommends that "you become the niche": "you turn yourself into your customer avatar, you write to yourself, you build a product for yourself, you sell that product to yourself, the past version of yourself." This happens "on the internet with writing as content so that you attract the right people," so that one can be perceived as valuable "because that's where you cultivated the value." The mechanism: the people who share, or once shared, your identity are the ones who will perceive as valuable what you found valuable.

**Source:** Working Hard Won't Make You Rich (Do This Instead).md

With this, the argument of the section can be laid out as a chain. Value is perceived through identity. The creator's past identity is the one they know best. People who share that identity will perceive the creator's solutions as valuable. Therefore writing to and building for one's past self is not self-indulgence but the most accurate positioning available.

#### The ideal reader: past, current and future self

How does one write to oneself in practice? In October 2022, in "The Rise Of The Value Creator," Koe offers a method in steps. He is trying to find a name for "turning myself into a business, productizing myself"; his candidates are "mental monetization" and "the business of self," because "one person business is good, but that's kind of broad." Then he gives the instruction: "I want you to treat yourself as your ideal reader."

**Source:** The Rise Of The Value Creator (A Career Path For Polymaths & Self-Improvers).md

The **ideal reader**, in this method, has three temporal versions.

- **The past self.** Give advice that would have helped you overcome your problems faster, or encouragement. Koe admits his first example is "cheesy": "hey man, I know you're in a hard place... keep doing what you're doing." His second example is more useful: "I remember when I didn't understand this in marketing" turns into a post titled something like "here are the five marketing principles you need to understand."
- **The current self.** If you are going through a hard time, give yourself advice. If things are going well, tell yourself why, to reinforce the good behavior and to fix the bad.
- **The future self.** Write encouragement to keep going.

The precondition for this method, Koe says in the same passage, is solitude. "This is very big for value creators: solitude and thinking and free thinking, by going on walks, listening to what you want to listen to, contemplating, self-reflecting." Without that time, one cannot recall one's past low places with enough precision to write to them. The 2024 compilation repeats the passage, which connects to Koe's broader view (Chapter 13) that the subconscious brings ideas when the mind is given unstructured time.

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

In July 2023 he restates the method in contrast to the common advice of selling to an ideal customer. In his one-person business, "you are the niche": you write and sell to yourself, you are the customer avatar. What to write follows the three temporal selves: if you are going through a tough time, give yourself advice in your "public journal"; give your past self advice to overcome problems faster; give your future self encouragement. He adds an optional tool. In Digital Economics he has people take the Myers-Briggs personality test, while acknowledging that personality-test enthusiasts say there are "10 times better tests." The purpose is only to understand oneself "a tiny bit more, just so you can write better; you don't need to do this at all."

**Source:** The Value Creator (A New Internet Career Path For Intelligent People).md

#### You attract who you are

The Myers-Briggs exercise produced an observation that Koe repeats as evidence for the whole model. In a March 2023 conversation with Dickie Bush he explains that in Digital Economics everyone takes the test (he has heard it is inaccurate, but he finds it interesting) and treats their own type as their customer avatar to start writing to. When students join and ask Koe's type, which is INFJ, "50 plus" percent of them turn out to be the same. "You attract who you are, and that's who you can help best." Hence "you are the niche": help yourself and your past self, write through your current problems, give yourself advice, "use social media... like note-taking software" in which you place the ideas you are sharing. You attract people like you, especially if you share your interests more.

**Source:** Dan Koe & Dickie Bush On One-Person Businesses, Creative Workflows, and Lifestyle Design.md

**Complementary context:** The Myers-Briggs Type Indicator classifies people into sixteen types along four dichotomies; INFJ (introversion, intuition, feeling, judging) is one of them. Its scientific validity is widely questioned, which Koe himself acknowledges.

The "50 plus percent" figure is an anecdotal observation from his own program, not a controlled study, and the self-selection it suggests could have several explanations. Koe uses it as an illustration of the mechanism, not as proof.

The same conversation offers a more general frame. Koe says he loves that individuals are "becoming their own public schools, or building brands that are somewhat public schools that people can attend": "they all have different lenses from which they teach, and they all attract people according to those lenses." People resonate more with one creator's style of teaching "than some general curriculum or someone else's school," and this creates its own filter. Buyers buy again and will buy various courses, but eventually they gravitate into "your own little pocket or community of people, and then you're going to be able to become that community."

**Source:** Dan Koe & Dickie Bush On One-Person Businesses, Creative Workflows, and Lifestyle Design.md

And in "The Future Of One-Person Businesses" (March 2023), Koe draws the educational consequence. As a creator, leaning into the voice one learns best from, and, as a student, following the people one learns best from, improves the student-teacher relationship in the creator economy, because the creator attracts people with shared goals and people who learn best from them. "This starts to illustrate the depth behind my saying 'you are the niche.'"

**Source:** The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md

These three statements turn "you are the niche" from a marketing tactic into a claim about education, which Chapter 36 will develop as "creators as the new school." Each creator is a lens; the lens attracts those who see best through it; the fit between teacher and student improves because neither was chosen by a curriculum committee.

The corpus records a tension around the Myers-Briggs exercise. In the same March 2023 conversation, Koe also says that internet marketing is moving away from hyper-targeted avatar research toward broad idea-sharing (Section 19.5). Treating one's personality type as a customer avatar seems to reintroduce the avatar he has just dismissed. The tension is unresolved in the source, but the two claims operate at different levels: broad idea-sharing describes how content spreads on social media; the self-as-avatar describes whom the creator writes for and builds for. The avatar Koe rejects is the one built from research on strangers, not the one built from self-knowledge.

#### Writing to your younger self: the guests' versions

Several of Koe's guests offer versions of the same principle, and they add qualifications Koe himself rarely states. In the January 2025 interview with the founders of Stan, John Hugh describes his first step as figuring out "what kind of content you should make," which he defines as "the intersection between what you're really passionate about and what you're really good at," and restates as "what is something you would talk about for free and not get paid for." His practical filter was "what kind of content I could write for the younger version of me." He frames this as a way to avoid the trap of thinking "I got to build a business, how do I monetize" before one has even started. His example is his own: he cold-called his way into a job on Wall Street, was passionate about helping "any other underrepresented kid get their first dream job," and was good at writing a great résumé and at getting a cold email to convert into an informational interview. So he made content about exactly that.

**Source:** How To Grow An Audience With Zero Followers & Build A Profitable Startup.md

His co-founder Vitali, who calls himself "reasonably early stage" as a creator, adds a correction that changes how the principle should be applied. What made the biggest difference for him was "who the focus of your content is, whether it's you or whether it's your audience." Content that did not do well, both his own and that of other beginners, comes from "let me try to find that authentic self, let me talk about myself, me me me, this is how I think," and "the world is just: I don't care... they care about themselves." Building on John's idea, he says one still talks to one's younger self, but "in a way where the other person sees themselves... in the younger you." "Just the simple change of focus, and learning to do that consistently with intention, makes a world of difference."

**Source:** How To Grow An Audience With Zero Followers & Build A Profitable Startup.md

Earlier in the same interview Vitali describes his own slower, "tiptoeing" path into content from a conventional mindset ("you go to work... I just don't have time"). He became curious about storytelling and encountered the idea that "to be a great storyteller... you need to live an interesting life." He felt his life was boring: heads down, the same things sixteen hours a day while building a company. Over time he realized it was "very interesting if you learn to notice the interesting things and then you learn to just organically talk about them." His formulation of the path is: "living your life the way you want to, and then sharing its story, and then just finding what it is that people look up to you for, and productizing it over time." As one tells stories, people start knowing one for something and become curious about one's topics; product ideas and platforms follow. He calls this "the most organic kind of transition into content," one that does not require "making pivotal changes to their life all at the same time."

**Source:** How To Grow An Audience With Zero Followers & Build A Profitable Startup.md

The corpus records Vitali's correction as a refinement of the self-as-reader principle rather than a contradiction. Koe himself had described his writing as a public journal, written to himself. The guests insist on a distinction that the phrase "write to yourself" can hide: the self is the *source* of the material and the *bridge* of identification, but the reader must remain the *focus*. Writing to one's past self works only when the past self functions as a mirror in which others recognize their present situation. Koe's late correction in 19.2 ("you are the niche doesn't mean posting about you, you, you") moves in the same direction.

A more operational version comes from Dickie Bush, co-founder of the writing cohort Ship 30 for 30, in the March 2023 conversation. Ship 30's first-week framework for "what the heck do I start talking about" is the **two-year test**, and it has two directions.

- **Backward.** Inside your head are hundreds of thousands of ideas, frameworks and lessons developed over the last two years that you have internalized and now think are obvious. Unlocking them and realizing you are an expert in many things "is the gateway drug to creating on the internet." The exercise: list twenty things (skills you have built, hobbies you have started, life transitions, mistakes, jobs started, jobs quit, things learned) and write about them to the person you were two years ago, "who would do anything to have that information." If you distill well, "the internet guarantees there are millions of people on that exact same journey."
- **Forward.** If you are a complete beginner (Dickie's example is an eighteen-year-old), look at what you want to learn over the next two years (marketing, sales, branding, health) and "go curate the experts of those things." Curation gives you a foundation of ideas on which you later put your own spin; people now curate Koe and Dickie themselves, and Dickie did his own thirty-day curation of a podcast.

Both lists, Dickie says, are "your raw material."

**Source:** Dan Koe & Dickie Bush On One-Person Businesses, Creative Workflows, and Lifestyle Design.md

The forward direction solves a problem the self-as-avatar model leaves open: what does a person with no relevant past write about? Dickie's answer is that the beginner's material is the journey they are about to take, documented through the experts they study. This connects to "learning in public" in Chapter 14 and to beginner hell in Chapter 26.

#### Sell to yourself: the past self, one to three steps ahead

The product side of the principle is stated in a September 2023 video on micro-education businesses. "Create a product that you need or needed in your life": something that would have helped you get to where you are now, or that will help you achieve something faster. The examples are deliberately varied: clothes for style, courses for knowledge, a journal for productivity, "or anything in between." "Sell to yourself and you won't have to worry about competition." Koe contrasts this with what people get wrong: relying too much on the algorithm or on looks and playing that game, instead of building something "innately valuable," valuable because the creator has already validated it by using it and seeing it help their life, and then distributing it because they know it will help others.

**Source:** Micro Education Businesses Are The Future & How To Start With Zero Dollars.md

Later in the same video he answers the objection about saturation in one sentence: "But Dan, how do I eliminate competition and saturation?" "Build a product for your past self or current self, one that accelerates the progress made toward a meaningful goal." The phrase "meaningful goal" ties the product to the purpose chapters of Part IV; a product built on this principle is a piece of the creator's own project made available to others.

**Source:** Micro Education Businesses Are The Future & How To Start With Zero Dollars.md

In June 2026 Koe adds a constraint on distance. If one struggles with the question of whom to help, "the person you can help the most is your past self. And there are plenty of other people who are like your past self." One is pursuing one's best self, but one does not need to posture as that self, "because a lot of the time that doesn't relate to the person down here. Usually if you're one to two to three steps ahead, that's what you want." His example: telling an average person "you want to make five million bucks?" sounds dumb or like a scam, whereas "I learned this thing for how to make $100 a day, do you want me to teach it to you?" gets more openness. It could still set off alarms, but it is "a lot closer in proximity to where they are and what they believe is possible." The questions he suggests are: What situation were you in? How did you get into the interest that changed you? How can you help people go through the same transformation faster? "You just need a starting point. You need a direction"; it refines with time.

**Source:** If You Have Multiple Interests, Start A One-Person Business.md

The distance between creator and customer is one of the numbers that varies across the corpus without explanation. Justin Welsh in 2021 spoke of serving people two to three years behind; Koe has said "one to two steps behind" (2022, 2024), "one step behind" (2023, 2024), "two steps ahead" (2023) and "one to three steps" (2024, 2026). The reason he gives when he explains it is constant: proximity makes identification easier and avoids the anxiety of a gap the customer cannot imagine crossing. The variation probably reflects context (a course, a piece of content, a service) more than a change of view. Chapter 26 develops the "one step behind" principle for beginners.

#### Two alternatives: the starving market and selling to the rich

Koe presents selling to oneself as a choice, and he names the alternatives. The first is the common advice to build a product for a starving market and then attract that market as an audience. In the September 2023 video he concedes: "Yes, obviously this works." But it has four downsides: (1) hating one's customer base and not feeling motivated to work; (2) having no autonomy over what one writes and builds; (3) being unable to pivot into whatever topic or interest one becomes curious about; (4) being forced into a specific identity "which spills over into the entirety of your life."

**Source:** Micro Education Businesses Are The Future & How To Start With Zero Dollars.md

The fourth downside is the one most specific to Koe's system. In the identity model, the roles one plays reshape who one is. A business built around a market one does not belong to forces one to adopt an identity, the expert in that market, which then colonizes the rest of one's life.

The second alternative is attributed to a named figure. In January 2026 Koe notes that "a lot of the business gurus, like Alex Hormozi," whom he says he loves, "will tell you to sell to the rich." One may not have been rich in one's past, "and that's okay. That doesn't mean that this way doesn't work. It's just an alternative route" for people with "that deep desire to do something more creative and spend a lot of their time on what they deem meaningful," "not to say that the other way isn't meaningful." "It has to be somewhat of a conscious choice." And it does not limit one: Koe says he has "made a lot of freaking money doing this," has been working on a software company and is working on a nootropic company, opportunities he would not otherwise have had.

**Source:** If you have multiple interests, do not waste the next 2-3 years.md

**Complementary context:** Alex Hormozi is an entrepreneur and author of *$100M Offers*; his advice to target customers with high purchasing power is part of his broader framework for building offers. Koe treats him as a reference on offers and value (Chapters 31 and 33).

The corpus records a shift in how Koe frames this choice. In 2023, selling to oneself is presented almost as a universal solution: the way not to worry about competition. In 2025–2026 it becomes one of two legitimate routes, chosen consciously according to what one wants: creative fulfillment and meaning, or the most direct path to revenue. (In a 2025 passage he says he chose the first route for "the soul in my work.") The thesis is unchanged; its scope is narrowed and made explicit.

#### The limits of building for yourself: presence of the customer

The same 2025 interview with the founders of Stan contains the most important qualification of the self-as-customer principle, and it comes from people who practice it. Koe asks whether a design decision on their landing page came from trial-and-error data or from a firm, polarizing belief. Vitali answers with two foundations for deciding what customers need, as opposed to what they say they want.

The first is that "you need to have an opinion on what good looks like." In their case, that opinion comes from understanding software and having a vision of a good software experience, and it must be developed by intentionally educating oneself, because "the world evolves." The second is "really spending an excessive amount of time in the presence of the customer, talking to them." The shortcuts (a popup, a survey, multiple-choice questions, even long-form surveys) lose the information that lives in "the nuance of the conversation": "not just what people say but how people say it, what they don't say, the tone that they're using." Even watching someone set up their store live reveals problems they would never mention. Vitali's example: a user duplicates a page, the copy appears at the bottom of a list, they have to scroll and get lost; they would never report it, but it cost them ten minutes and needs fixing.

**Source:** How To Grow An Audience With Zero Followers & Build A Profitable Startup.md

John adds that staying in the presence of the customer is a tension he and Vitali must be "hyper-intentional" about. He explains why companies like United Airlines or Comcast have awful customer experiences: "the organization is stratified to such a hierarchy, which comes with hiring too many people and not being efficient," and one should ask "how often is the CEO literally in the pain gutters talking to customers." At scale, with many stakeholders, leaders ask "what is the most scalable use of my time," and an initiative that affects 20,000 people looks better than thirty minutes with one person "in the trenches." The equation is hard to balance, but John invokes an old startup saying, "do things that don't scale in order to scale." They deliberately keep "unscalable time," interviewing customers and making content themselves, because "that anchor and that root is worthwhile to take us back to our customer." "We bleed with our customers."

**Source:** How To Grow An Audience With Zero Followers & Build A Profitable Startup.md

**Complementary context:** "Do things that don't scale" is the title of a well-known 2013 essay by Paul Graham, co-founder of the startup accelerator Y Combinator.

Vitali's third point brings the discussion back to the self. "You got to use your own product." Koe uses Cortex, his writing software, every day; John and Vitali use Stan every day. "There's just nothing comparable to this experience where you're... trying to do something, you have an intent, and then you have tension, tension created by software." "I want to get things done at the speed of my thought," and if one has to open an app, "that's already difficult enough."

**Source:** How To Grow An Audience With Zero Followers & Build A Profitable Startup.md

These three foundations (an opinion of what good looks like, excessive time with customers, daily use of one's own product) refine "build for yourself" in an important way. Using one's own product is the self-as-customer principle applied to a product team; the other two are its necessary complements. A creator's own experience is the starting point, but it is one sample, and it must be corrected by the nuance only real customers reveal. The corpus records an unresolved tension here that will become central in Chapter 37. In November 2025, Koe reports that he did all his writing in Cortex and could not imagine living without it, and yet the product was not compelling to the market and did not look like a startup; friends of a collaborator said they would not use it. Building for oneself, which is the strength of the one-person business, can blind a founder to what others need. The Stan founders combine founder-market fit with intensive customer contact; Koe's own case shows what happens when the first runs without enough of the second.

### 19.4 The Eternal Markets

#### Why selling to yourself is not a small market

Sections 19.2 and 19.3 rest on an assumption that has not yet been examined: that the creator's own problems are shared by enough people to sustain a business. If a person's problems were idiosyncratic, building for oneself would produce products for a market of one. Koe's answer is a claim about human nature: underneath the variety of individual lives, everyone is pursuing the same few things and running into the same few kinds of problems. He gives this claim two forms. One is psychological and philosophical: everybody's niche is self-actualization. The other is commercial: the **Eternal markets**.

#### Everybody's niche is self-actualization

The phrase appears in December 2023, in "The Future Of Work Is Play": "Everybody's niche is self-actualization." Koe explains why this is not common knowledge: before the internet, people self-actualized in public through business, and the pattern was not visible as such. His illustration is the largest possible: those building rockets, Elon Musk and Jeff Bezos, are in "the self-actualization niche," taking humanity to Mars so the species can expand, helping the self, the collective ego or identity of humanity, expand "much further beyond the personal low-level problems that they started out with."

**Source:** The Future Of Work Is Play (How To Create A Digital Career).md

**Complementary context:** "Self-actualization" is Abraham Maslow's term for the highest level of his hierarchy of needs: the realization of one's potential. Koe uses it loosely, as the direction of growth that all genuine goals share, and he places it alongside "self-transcendence," a level Maslow added later in his work.

In June 2024, in "Ideas Are The New Oil," the claim is developed into an argument. Most people want the same things: more money, less insecurity, more sex, less loneliness, more purpose, less chaos. "We're all heading towards the same end goal." So "your niche is self-actualization" if one is a value creator (as opposed to an influencer, a theme page or a company account) pursuing one's life's work. What makes each person unique is not the destination but the curiosities, interests, skills and experience gained along the way.

**Source:** Ideas Are The New Oil (How More People Get Rich In The Digital Age).md

Koe anticipates an objection, "what if everyone goes down the same path?", and answers that everyone already is. If someone is not pursuing health, wealth, relationships and happiness, "they're distracted and they have someone else's goals implanted in their minds," because "the true you doesn't want to destroy itself." Such a person is distracted "by a line of code that someone else put in your mind." The image comes straight from the Matrix of Chapter 1: the default programming can override the natural pursuit of a good life, and the self-actualization niche is what remains when that programming is removed.

The same passage contains a clarification about the word "creator." "Creator" is meta, a way of being, and under it one creates one's own job title. Writing is Koe's route to financial freedom; someone else's might be budgeting or e-commerce. For health, Koe trains daily; others sell programs and nutrition advice; Elon Musk builds rockets so humanity can actualize as a species. Every business that provides real value, which Koe defines here as "true value equals transformation and problem solving," has self-actualization as its niche. And even when two people pursue the same goal, one person's 50,000 tweets or 1,000 videos will differ from another's. The sentence that compresses the idea introduces a coinage: "you are an **experience vessel**; you become a niche of one when you pass down that experience for others to utilize."

The "experience vessel" image reframes the niche of one in a way that complements the identity versions of 19.2. The person is a container in which experience accumulates; the niche of one comes into existence not when the container is filled but when its contents are passed on. A person who lives a unique path and never shares it has no niche; the act of transmission creates it.

In September 2025 Koe returns to the idea, calling it "a bit corny": "Everyone's niche is self-actualization." Everyone in what he calls the meaning economy (Chapter 36) is pursuing the same thing, a higher perspective, self-actualization, and "the uniqueness stems from their story." His own path went from web design to business to writing; someone else might pursue spirituality and philosophy; both move toward the same goal, "becoming a puzzle piece, a stepping stone for someone else to follow," someone others can be mentored by (not necessarily personally), learn from and benefit from. One's failures, life situation and surroundings are all unique: "You are the niche."

**Source:** The Death Of Social Media (& The Future Of Content Creation).md

The "stepping stone" image adds a social dimension. Each creator's documented path is a piece of a larger structure through which others move; the uniqueness of the path is what makes it useful, because it fits people whom no other path fits.

#### Broad identities as niches: Zuby, Hamza, Musk

The 2024 video "Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche" gives concrete cases of what a niche defined by identity looks like. Zuby (the musician and commentator) makes music and sells tickets to a show one day, talks politics another, sells a fitness ebook the next, "and he's doing perfectly fine." Hamza (a self-improvement creator) has an extremely broad niche, self-improvement, which "encapsulates literally everything." Koe's gloss: "Everything valuable or actually worth pursuing is self-improvement. Going to Mars is self-improvement on a collective scale. Elon Musk, his niche is self-improvement," but in the sense of "an expanded sense of self where... you have begun to identify with humanity as a whole. You've expanded your circle of concern, and so now your products are spaceships." Hamza talks about confidence, masculinity, dating, fitness and business; he is not hyper-specific. "He is the niche because he is a self-improver. That's his identity."

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

These cases illustrate the nicheless principle of 19.2 and anticipate the developmental maps of Chapter 38. The "expanded sense of self" is the same expansion of identity that Koe, following Wilber and Cook-Greuter, describes as development: as the circle of concern grows from the individual to the group to humanity, the problems one works on grow with it, and so do the products. Musk's rockets and a beginner's fitness ebook are, in this frame, positions on the same continuum.

#### The four Eternal markets

The commercial version of the claim is the concept of the **Eternal markets**, a term Koe uses from 2022 onward. Its clearest definition appears in the 2024 compilation of his one-person business material. The Eternal markets are where most people are trying to achieve lofty goals and where there are many problems in the way of those goals; that is where the most money is made, because "those are the profitable burning problems that people have." "The four Eternal markets are health, wealth, relationships and happiness." In the same compilation Koe contrasts two entry paths into business (Chapter 29): the skill-based path usually addresses only wealth, while the development-based path addresses the Eternal markets through the creator's own pursuit of goals. Later he adds that self-development content, such as mindset advice, falls under these markets.

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

In October 2022, the first appearance in the corpus, the list is built up rather than given: Koe places development-based content within the "Eternal markets," lists health, wealth and relationships, and then adds happiness. These are markets that always exist because they relate to persistent human problems.

**Source:** If You Are High Value, Start A One-Person Business.md

In December 2022, in "The One Person Business Roadmap," Koe maps the markets to levels of need, a passage the 2024 compilation repeats almost word for word. A personal brand is based on what one wants out of life, on what one leads followers toward. Everyone has the same desires, problems and goals, and all of them fall under the Eternal markets: health, wealth, relationships, "and a bonus is happiness." The first three are survival-based; in the same breath the transcript also calls them "self-actualization based," listing both labels together. Happiness is "self-transcendence based." These markets are where all burning problems exist, because "survival problems are the most burning problems." Starting a business usually fixes some part of survival; buying a nice car is usually "trying to survive via status." Targeting these relevant, profitable and evergreen markets is how one creates a compelling angle for marketing, products, brand or content.

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The double label for the first three markets is genuinely ambiguous in the transcript. The most plausible reading, given the structure of the passage, is that health, wealth and relationships span a range from survival (their urgent, burning form) to self-actualization (their developmental form), while happiness belongs to a higher register, self-transcendence. Koe does not resolve the ambiguity.

The example of the car shows how Koe reads consumption through this lens. Many purchases that look like luxuries are attempts to solve a survival problem by other means; status is a survival strategy in social form. This is why he calls survival problems the most "burning": they drive behavior even when they are disguised.

In March 2023 he adds a figure. He claims that 95% of people's problems revolve around survival-based problems: health, wealth, relationships and happiness (here all four are listed as survival-based). If you solve one of these for yourself and then create a system to help you do it (his example is a gym program you create for yourself), you have something to bring to market. The figure is Koe's estimate; he cites no source.

**Source:** The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md

The list changes across the corpus, and the changes are not explained. The following table summarizes the variants recorded in the sources assigned to this chapter, together with one documented elsewhere in the corpus.

Happiness enters the list when the frame is the good life and leaves it when the frame is the burning problem.

#### Subtopics: where interests live

In the June 2023 "One Person Business Model 2.0," Koe breaks the markets into subtopics that "culminate into interests." Health contains nutrition, training and mental health, which in turn contain bodybuilding, the ancestral diet, minimalist training, yoga and mindfulness. Wealth contains business, career development and finance, which contain freelancing, software as a service, résumé building, interview preparation, budgeting and "any modern skill." Relationships contain social dynamics, dating and marriage, which contain couples therapy, confidence and charisma, day game and how to approach people. All of these push a person to develop each area.

**Source:** The One Person Business Model 2.0 (Turn Yourself Into A Business).md

| Eternal market | Subtopics | Example interests |
|---|---|---|
| Health | Nutrition, training, mental health | Bodybuilding, ancestral diet, minimalist training, yoga, mindfulness |
| Wealth | Business, career development, finance | Freelancing, SaaS, résumé building, interview preparation, budgeting, modern skills |
| Relationships | Social dynamics, dating, marriage | Couples therapy, confidence and charisma, day game, approaching |
| Happiness | (not broken down in this passage) | — |

This breakdown is where the Eternal markets meet the traditional niche-down pattern of 19.1. The courses Koe criticized also start from a starving market (health) and descend to a niche (minimalist training). The difference is the direction of travel. In the traditional model, the descent is a search for an unserved segment; in Koe's model, the creator's own interests are already located somewhere in this tree, and the tree simply shows which universal market they serve. Chapter 22 builds a related structure, the topic tree, for organizing content.

#### Mirrors: the four pillars of you

Koe repeatedly identifies the Eternal markets with his own map of personal development. In a May 2023 video, "Most People Are NPCs," he advises starting "with what matters the most": your mind, body, spirit and finances. In business, the one-person business philosophy "flips it": the Eternal markets in which one finds the most burning, profitable problems are health, wealth, relationships and happiness, "same exact thing." The process follows: solve your own problems, create a solution for yourself, sell it to others as information, coaching, freelancing and so on; build and stack resources with a very low-cost business model; then do whatever you want (e-commerce, software, open a gym, "like I plan to do in the future"). "You need resources first, though, and you need to become high value by fixing the four pillars of you."

**Source:** Most People Are NPCs (Don't Waste Your Life On Autopilot).md

The phrase "four pillars of you" (mind, body, spirit, finances) should not be confused with the four pillars of the one-person business in 19.2 (brand, content, product, marketing); Koe uses "pillars" for both. The point of the mirror is that personal development and business are two descriptions of the same activity. The domains in which one improves oneself are the domains in which others will pay for help.

In December 2023 he gives a version for those who have not yet started. Start with the foundation of Mind, Body, Spirit and Business: educate yourself on solving the most burning, profitable problems in your life and in others'. "If you want to make money reasonably fast, you must solve your superficial problems and help others solve theirs." Learn, build and sell a product around fitness, relationships, mental health or financial freedom. By pursuing these goals one's path will be unique, because the knowledge one acquires is unique to oneself. And the motive is not only commercial: "do your part in raising the collective consciousness" by helping others transcend the problems that keep them in a lower state.

**Source:** The Future Of Work Is Play (How To Create A Digital Career).md

The word "superficial" is important. Koe is not saying that the deepest questions of life should be monetized first. He is saying that the problems most people pay to solve urgently (body, money, loneliness) are the right starting point, and that meaning can be brought in once survival is less pressing. Chapter 40 discusses the order body, mind, business that underlies this advice.

In September 2024, in a step-by-step video on wealth, the Eternal markets appear as a step in a process: "Step two is creating a solution to a logical, personal, professional or social problem; in other words, think health, wealth, relationships, happiness, the Eternal markets; that's where all real problems are."

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md

The corpus records that the names of these life domains oscillate: "mind, body, spirit and business," "mind, body, spirit, finances," "health, wealth, relationships, happiness," "body, mind, business," with relationships sometimes inside "spirit" and "business" sometimes replacing "finances." Koe does not explain the variations. They are best read as equivalent cuts of the same territory, chosen for the context: a business frame (markets), a daily-routine frame (pillars), or a developmental frame.

#### Shared goals, singular paths

If everyone pursues the same goals in the same markets, how can each creator be unique? This is the objection the Eternal markets seem to raise against the niche of one, and Koe answers it in several places.

In November 2023 he defines the relationship between goal and market. "A meaningful goal that you help people achieve is determined by solving a burning problem within human nature in the Eternal markets: health, wealth, relationships and happiness." The goal is meaningful because it is universal; it is not universal because it is generic.

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

In April 2023 he states the resolution in terms of scale. "Too often, especially online, we think that we're doing the same thing as everyone else when we're not; we're doing something slightly different, which in the grand scale of things is vastly different." Everyone has to pursue their goals in "these Eternal markets"; "you won't escape that," but the way each person pursues them will be completely different.

**Source:** Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md

The 2024 video gives the fullest version. "The goals and problems will almost always be similar to someone else's. Everyone has the same goals and problems in the Eternal markets: health, wealth, relationships, and happiness. But the path, the path is singular to you." Everyone wants financial freedom: one person takes the social media and online business route, with e-commerce and its skills, mindset and experience; another takes the investing and real estate route. Every other skill or interest learned along the way also contributes: if journaling, going on walks or playing video games were part of the path, "no matter how direct... it still played a role." The conclusion: "All of the beliefs, skills, and billions of bits of information you process along the way toward achieving your goals is the most unique niche in the world."

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

This is the key distinction of the section, and it can be stated as a structure:

| Element | Shared or unique? | Role in the business |
|---|---|---|
| Goals (the Eternal markets) | Shared by nearly everyone | Make the market large enough |
| Problems (burning problems in those markets) | Largely shared | Make the content relevant |
| Path (skills, beliefs, experiences, side interests) | Singular | Makes the creator unique and the product different |

The inclusion of indirect activities (journaling, walks, video games) in the path matters for the next sections. It means that interests apparently unrelated to what one sells are not noise to be removed but part of the niche, because they shaped the path. Section 19.6 develops this in practice.

#### The customer avatar as a worldview

The 2024 video uses this structure to redefine the customer avatar. "In essence, a customer avatar is the mind, worldview, or perspective of someone you are creating for or marketing toward." Structurally, a worldview or perspective is composed of (1) goals, (2) problems and (3) potential paths, plus other influences such as prior experiences, beliefs and skill levels.

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

The definition connects the marketing concept of the avatar to the model of the mind in Part III. A person's mind is organized by goals (Chapter 6); the problems are what the goals encounter; the paths are the options the person can see. To know a customer is to know these three things, and the creator who was once in the customer's position knows them from inside.

Koe then explains how content and products relate to the goals that define a brand. "Every piece of content you write and every product you build helps people learn the knowledge or skills that allow them to reach the goals of your brand. The bigger the goal, the more people you can attract and help. You help them reach the goal by solving problems or pain points. So your content and products argue why achieving a specific goal is important and give them the knowledge, tools, and education to solve the problem standing in the way." Goals give direction and motivation and attract people; problems are what one helps solve to change their lives.

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

The sentence "the bigger the goal, the more people you can attract and help" is the commercial reason for the breadth of the Eternal markets. It also introduces a tension the corpus records without resolving. In 2022 Koe said that if someone told him to "niche down toward a specific goal," he would agree, while noting that he himself prefers broad goals such as financial freedom; in 2023 he says the ideal future is "very big, broad" (Section 19.7) and elsewhere that purpose is "niche" while the method is holistic. One reading reconciles these: what is specific is the concrete transformation one offers; what is broad is the vision of life that frames it. Koe does not state this reconciliation himself.

#### Everyone's niche is the good life

A last group of statements makes the universality of the Eternal markets into a justification for a kind of honest selfishness. In April 2023: "All burning problems... in your life and others' all revolve around the same things: health, wealth, relationships, happiness, mind, body, spirit, business, you." Selfishness, fixing one's own problems, works "because other people have the same exact problems; those are the burning problems." "Stop avoiding this: solve your own problems, sell the solution. That's how you make money, at least at the start," and then one can bring meaning into it once one is not so worried about money.

**Source:** Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md

In the same video Koe describes how he started. "I didn't fantasize about building a billion-dollar company. I just started with what was right in front of me, which is improving my own human experience, because very few people do that." He adds: "95% of the population are by definition mediocre," which he says is not a judgment but simply what the label means. By improving one's human experience, creating something of value and becoming valuable, it becomes, in his words as transcribed, "almost impossible to make money"; the context makes clear he means almost impossible *not* to make money, because others want the same improvement but want to achieve it in a better way, learn from someone they resonate with, and be persuaded to take action.

**Source:** Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md

The "95% mediocre" claim is a definitional point (mediocre as "average," most people being by definition around the average), not a statistic, and Koe presents it that way.

In "Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business" (April 2023), after reviewing a set of creators, he observes: "Notice how every single person's niche is self-improvement or self-actualization," the broad niche, broken into topics within health, wealth and relationships. He takes this as confirmation that his theory holds: "solve your own problems, document the solution online, and improve others to raise the collective consciousness."

**Source:** Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md

And in March 2023 he gives the most candid version. "That's what everyone's trying to do, why not be honest about it": every person's niche is how to achieve the good life. He recalls a tweet he saw two years earlier (the source does not identify its author) saying that at the end of everyone's journey, they are a life coach. He admits it sounds cringe, but says it is true. His advice is to lean into it now; one does not have to label oneself a life coach, one can get creative, for example by calling oneself "the modern conqueror." "That is what a brand is."

**Source:** The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md

The four formulations (self-actualization, self-improvement, the good life, life coaching) say the same thing at different levels of seriousness. Every creator who documents a path through the Eternal markets is, in the end, teaching a version of how to live. The brand is the particular name and perspective under which that teaching is offered; Section 19.7 shows how Koe asks the creator to articulate it, starting from the question "how does one live the good life?"
