## Chapter 6 — Attention, Focus and the Mind as a Goal Machine

Chapter 5 established the background condition of the whole system: everything tends toward disorder, the mind included, and the default state of consciousness is chaos unless something orders it. That leaves a practical question open. If the mind drifts toward psychic entropy by default, *what* is the lever that puts it in order, and *what* decides the direction in which it is ordered? This chapter gives Dan Koe's answer in three movements. First, the lever: conscious attention, which he treats as a scarce, measurable resource (the "RAM" of the mind) and as the only thing over which a person has "root control". Second, the mechanism: the mind as a cybernetic system, a steering device that acts, senses, compares and corrects toward a target, and whose intelligence is measured by how well it does that. Third, the target itself: the goal, which in Koe's mature vocabulary is not primarily something to be achieved but a lens that decides what you perceive, what registers as a problem, what you learn and what system you end up building. Chapter 3 introduced the mind as a goal-striving machine from the side of identity (Maltz's guidance system, the homing mechanism, the heat-seeking missile). This chapter takes the same machine apart from the side of attention and control. The conclusion it reaches prepares all of Part IV: if the goal determines what you see and the system you build, then choosing the goal is the central decision of a life.

### 6.1 Attention Is the RAM

#### The supercomputer metaphor

Koe's most repeated image for conscious attention comes from computing. "I want you to think of your mind as a supercomputer," he begins in a 2024 video. In a computer, RAM (random access memory) is "one of the most important parts" because it determines performance: "the more RAM you use up with different programs running, open browser tabs and the performance requirements of what you have running, the slower your performance will be. This is no different from your focus or what you hold in your conscious attention" (**Source:** `How To Reset Your Life In 7 Days.md`, 2024-01-07). A month later he compressed the metaphor into a single sentence that he would reuse for years: "Your mind is a supercomputer and your attention is the RAM. Thoughts, regrets and tasks are the programs slowing your performance. Writing, mindfulness and focus are the reboot you can access at any time" (**Source:** `Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md`, 2024-02-18). In 2025 it opens his video on deep work in its shortest form: "Your mind is a supercomputer. And your attention is the RAM," with distractions playing the role of the browser tabs that make a computer crawl (**Source:** `The Deep Work Routine That Changed My Life.md`, 2025-05-18).

The metaphor is worth taking apart because each piece carries a claim. *RAM* is working memory, not storage: it is the space in which current operations run, and it is finite. The claim, then, is that conscious attention is not a vague faculty but a bounded workspace, and that whatever occupies it competes with everything else for the same capacity. *Programs* are processes that keep running whether or not you are looking at them; the claim is that most of what occupies attention is not chosen in the moment but left running in the background. *Performance* is what degrades as programs accumulate; the claim is that scattered attention does not merely feel bad but measurably reduces the quality of what you can think and do. And the *reboot* is a cheap, always-available operation that clears the workspace; the claim is that the remedy does not require changing your life first, only an act of writing, mindfulness or focus.

Koe is specific about what the background programs are. In the 2024 versions he lists the same six (**Source:** `How To Reset Your Life In 7 Days.md`, 2024-01-07; `Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md`, 2024-02-18; `Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md`, 2024-07-21):

| "Program" running in the background | What it is in Koe's description |
|---|---|
| Regretful past | Thoughts about past mistakes |
| Stressful future | Thoughts about things that might happen |
| Escape desires | "Desires for hunger and entertainment to escape those thoughts" |
| The internal cry | "An internal cry to break out of their conditioned way of living" |
| Mixed-priority tasks | "A list of mixed priority tasks that need to be finished" |
| Open loops | "Open loops of tasks they were supposed to complete but forgot about" |

The list has an internal logic that is easy to miss. The first two are about time: attention pulled out of the present toward a past that cannot be changed and a future that has not happened (the "psychological time" of Chapter 2). The third is a reaction to the first two: the urge to eat or be entertained is described as an *escape* from those thoughts, so the escape itself becomes one more program. The fourth is the identity-level discomfort that Parts I and II diagnosed: the felt sense that one is living a conditioned life one did not choose. Only the last two are what productivity literature usually means by distraction, and even these are about unfinished business rather than about notifications. The image therefore places ordinary productivity problems inside a larger picture: most of the RAM is consumed before the working day has begun.

The **open loop** deserves a word of its own because Koe uses the term in two related ways. Here it means a task you committed to and forgot, which keeps quietly consuming attention. Elsewhere (Chapter 12) the same phrase names the practice of postponing email and calls so that the day opens gradually from structured to unstructured. What unites both uses is the idea that anything left unresolved keeps a claim on working memory.

**Complementary context:** the observation that unfinished tasks stay more accessible in memory than finished ones is usually called the Zeigarnik effect, after Bluma Zeigarnik's experiments in the 1920s. Koe does not cite it here, but his "open loops" describe the same phenomenon.

The 2024 version adds the sharpest formulation of the consequence. "The modern mind has its attention split in infinite directions by default." Instead of being present with "singular focus" on a vision, we go about our lives "stressed out and near sickness", living in "a false reality created by split attention". If we think too much about the scattered past or future, "chaos ensues": the mind tends toward disorder if it is not kept in check, and we lose the sense of control that leads to enjoyment (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md`, 2024-07-21). This is the point at which the RAM metaphor joins the entropy model of Chapter 5. The "false reality" is not a metaphysical claim about the world but a description of experience: what fills consciousness *is* your reality, and when it is filled with a dozen competing programs, the reality you live in is fragmented, anxious and not of your choosing. The loss of "the sense of control that leads to enjoyment" is a nod to Csikszentmihalyi's definition of enjoyment as the experience of ordered consciousness, which Chapter 5 and Chapter 10 develop.

#### The phone in the morning: why the split mind feels terrible

The 2025 version of the metaphor drops most of the list and keeps one concrete scene. "For most people on an average day that precious focus is just chaotically bouncing between so many different things": the stressful future, "the boring present", the regretful past, the internal cry, the mixed-priority list. And the scene that starts it all: "The modern mind has its attention split in a thousand different directions the minute it wakes up and opens its phone" (**Source:** `The Deep Work Routine That Changed My Life.md`, 2025-05-18). Set side by side, the two versions show a clear difference: the 2024 version is the richer one, and the 2025 version reduces it, adding only the bits-per-second figure discussed below. What remains constant is the diagnosis that the split begins at waking and is self-inflicted.

A 2024 video on discipline explains *why* this produces a recognizable emotional state and not just inefficiency. "You only have so much focus you can invest into your goals every single day." As soon as we wake up we grab our phones and flood our minds with news, advice, doomsday predictions and people doing better than us, and "the only way to find relief is to keep scrolling to make ourselves feel like we are making progress." Then the key sentence: "The mind craves order and that's exactly why you feel terrible: your attention is not focused on a singular goal for the future so that distractions can't penetrate it." Your focus is split among goals other people tell you to achieve "to the point where you can't pay attention to your own" (**Source:** `It’s actually pretty easy to be self-disciplined (if you stop getting distracted).md`, 2024-05-12).

Three ideas are compressed here, and each will be developed later in the chapter. First, the bad feeling is not a moral failing but a *signal*: a mind that craves order registers disorder as discomfort (Chapter 5). Second, the content of the morning feed is not neutral information but *other people's goals*: news, advice and comparison each carry an implicit target (be informed, be optimized, be afraid, be like them), and attention spent on them is attention invested in goals you did not choose. Third, and most important for what follows, the remedy is not less information but a *singular goal* strong enough that "distractions can't penetrate it". The protection against a split mind is not a wall but a direction. Section 6.5 explains the mechanism by which a goal does this.

#### The bits of conscious attention: the numbers and their status

To make the scarcity of attention concrete, Koe repeatedly cites figures for how much information the conscious mind can process. They deserve careful treatment, because they appear in at least ten places across four years, they vary, and their source is almost never named.

The first appearance is explicitly borrowed. In a 2022 video recommending books, he names the main lesson he took from Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's *Flow*: the concepts of "psychic entropy" and consciousness, "which is kind of like a storage of your experiences in your mind." He then gives the numbers: humans "can process 126 bits of information per second" and over a lifetime "185 billion units of information or bits of information," part of which is stored in consciousness as memory, direct experience or things you are aware of (**Source:** `7 Books That Will Change Your Life In 2022.md`, 2022-04-23). A month later he repeats the 126 bits per second, "the things that we are conscious of", to support the value of condensing information for readers (**Source:** `If You Want To Secure Your Future… Follow This New Career Path.md`, 2022-05-21).

From late 2022 a second pair of numbers appears, now contrasting the conscious and the unconscious mind and no longer attributed to anyone. "Your unconscious brain can process and manage up to 11 million bits of information per second compared to your conscious mind being able to process 40 to 50 bits of information per second" (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16). A month later, in a single passage, he says "11 billion" and then "11 million" (**Source:** `Why You Can't Be Yourself (The Power Of Being Alone).md`, 2022-11-13); the transcript is internally inconsistent and the intended figure is presumably the 11 million he uses everywhere else. In 2023 he introduces the figures with an honest hedge, "there's some fact or something like that" (**Source:** `How To Reprogram Your Mind & Build Your Intelligence.md`, 2023-09-24). In 2024 the conscious range widens to "10 to 50 bits" and the lifetime total settles at "around 125 billion" (**Source:** `How To Reset Your Life In 7 Days.md`, 2024-01-07; `The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md`, 2024-01-18), after a 2023 version that gave 128 billion (**Source:** `Get Mad At Where You Are In Life (A Deep Explanation).md`, 2023-06-11).

| Date | Conscious rate | Unconscious rate | Lifetime total | Attribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022-04 | 126 bits/s | — | 185 billion bits | *Flow* (Csikszentmihalyi) |
| 2022-05 | ~126 bits/s | — | — | none |
| 2022-10 | 40–50 bits/s | 11 million bits/s | — | none |
| 2022-11 | 40–50 bits/s | "11 billion", then "11 million" | — | none |
| 2023-06 | — | — | 128 billion bits | none |
| 2023-09 | 50 bits/s | 11 million bits/s | — | "some fact or something like that" |
| 2024-01 | 10–50 bits/s | up to 11 million bits/s | ~125 billion bits | none |
| 2024-07 | 50 bits/s | 11 million bits/s | — | none |
| 2025-05 | ~50 bits/s | 11 million bits/s | 125 billion bits | none |

The order of magnitude is stable and the use is always the same: conscious attention is tiny compared to what the mind does automatically, and it must be protected. The figures themselves should be read as illustrations, not as measurements Koe stands behind. He never cites a study, and in one case says openly that he is repeating "some fact".

**Complementary context:** the 126 bits per second and 185 billion lifetime bits come from Csikszentmihalyi's *Flow* (1990), which builds the estimate from older work on attention spans and assumes seventy years of sixteen waking hours a day. The pairing of roughly 11 million bits per second (total sensory input) with a few dozen bits per second (conscious processing) circulates in popular psychology, usually traced to estimates by the physiologist Manfred Zimmermann and popularized in books such as Tor Nørretranders' *The User Illusion* and Timothy Wilson's *Strangers to Ourselves*. Both are rough, model-dependent estimates, not settled measurements. Note also that Koe's later lifetime figure does not follow from his later per-second figure: under Csikszentmihalyi's assumptions, 50 bits per second yields about 74 billion bits in a lifetime, not 125 billion. The lifetime number appears to be a blend of the two sources.

#### What the numbers are for: automatic mode and the unconscious

What matters is the argument the numbers serve, and here the corpus is consistent. The 11 million bits are, in Koe's words, "the things you've become accustomed to and no longer need to focus on": walking, breathing, sight, small hand gestures, "possibly even peeing", and practiced skills like playing tennis, shooting a basketball, building a business or making money, about which he adds, "they're all the same skills" (**Source:** `How To Reset Your Life In 7 Days.md`, 2024-01-07; `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16; `Why You Can't Be Yourself (The Power Of Being Alone).md`, 2022-11-13). Everything that does not receive conscious attention runs automatically. That is an asset, because it frees the scarce conscious channel for new things. It is also a danger, which Koe names **automatic mode**: if you do not remain aware, focused and intentional on a daily basis, you slip into running on the unconscious, and then "you have no idea where negative problems and emotions come from; you think they're just happening to you rather than you being in control of them." What you lack in that state is "the inner game or mental game" to get out (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16). Automatic mode is the attentional version of the autopilot described in Chapter 1: the same life on rails, seen from inside the mind.

The relationship between the two channels is not a simple opposition. In 2023 Koe describes them as running together, with the conscious one shaping the unconscious: the very limited conscious focus ("you have to protect that") "influences what gets stored in the unconscious", which in turn "changes your actions" and "heavily influences everything you do without you knowing it" (**Source:** `How To Reprogram Your Mind & Build Your Intelligence.md`, 2023-09-24). Read alongside the anatomy of identity in Chapter 3, this is the micro-mechanism of conditioning: what you attend to repeatedly becomes what you do without attending. A few weeks earlier he had drawn the positive implication. As you become more efficient with the 50 available bits, "meaning the unconscious or subconscious becomes trained to automatically process specific information", room is freed in conscious attention for "new, novel information and potential", which you can then invest in "what you deem meaningful" (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (You Can't Escape The Matrix).md`, 2023-09-05). Skill acquisition is, in this picture, the transfer of work from the narrow conscious channel to the wide unconscious one; and that transfer is what makes room for the next thing to learn. Chapter 15 builds its account of skill on this idea.

One passage goes further and treats the unconscious as a reservoir that can be put to work for chosen goals. "Imagine," he says, if you can direct 40 to 50 bits with conscious effort toward something, "the potential of 11 million more being available to you if you can align the goal that you create with what you truly enjoy." The condition is alignment: a self-created goal that matches who you are recruits the unconscious instead of fighting it. He adds that exploring what we usually forget or glance over would let us "peek into an entirely new way of viewing the world" (**Source:** `Why You Can't Be Yourself (The Power Of Being Alone).md`, 2022-11-13). This is a hypothesis about motivation, not a calculation; the number is used to dramatize the difference between working against your inclinations and working with them. It anticipates the argument of Chapter 10 that flow arises when goals and enjoyment coincide.

#### Your potential, your ticking time bomb

The lifetime total serves a different rhetorical purpose: it turns attention into a finite budget. In 2023 Koe says you can process about 128 billion bits of conscious information in your lifetime through attention, and that the information you perceive, and are open-minded enough to perceive, *is* your potential. At any moment you either waste it through distraction and a lack of conscious perception, or you use it "to craft a conscious character" to navigate reality and contribute to humanity (**Source:** `Get Mad At Where You Are In Life (A Deep Explanation).md`, 2023-06-11). **Conscious character** is his phrase for the identity you build deliberately with that budget, in contrast to the one installed by default.

The 2024 summary of his book *The Art of Focus* states the same idea more soberly: "Humans can process 10 to 50 bits of information per second with their conscious attention." The *what*, *how* and *why* behind the information we choose to process determine the quality of our lives: what we hold in attention, how we interpret it, and why we gave it attention in the first place. "We can consider the approximate 125 billion bits of information you process through focus in your lifetime as your potential," and he notes that a large chunk of it is already committed to grooming, errands and other basic responsibilities. Therefore "we must care deeply about where we direct focus" (**Source:** `The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md`, 2024-01-18). In the 2025 deep-work video the budget acquires a more dramatic name: 125 billion bits of conscious information in a lifetime, "that's your ticking time bomb." "You either invest a large portion of those 125 billion bits of information into a better future for yourself or you let distractions clog them up like browser tabs on your computer and waste your life" (**Source:** `The Deep Work Routine That Changed My Life.md`, 2025-05-18).

The framing does real work even if the number is soft. It converts a diffuse complaint ("I get distracted") into an accounting question (how much of a fixed budget is going where?), and it links the micro-level of a morning's scrolling to the macro-level of a life's potential. It also introduces the triad (what, how, why) that the rest of the chapter unpacks: *what* you attend to is the subject of section 6.2, *how* you interpret it is the subject of section 6.3, and *why* you attend to it at all, the goal behind the attention, is the subject of sections 6.4 to 6.6.

#### Focus is more than deep work: *The Art of Focus*

All of this explains why Koe uses the word *focus* in a broader sense than productivity writing does. In a 2023 conversation with Dickie Bush about his then-forthcoming book *The Art of Focus*, he warns that readers expecting a deep-work manual will be surprised. The book is meant to give people "a lens, or how to create their own lens": "it's both here's my lens, but I tried to create this in such a malleable way as possible that you can fit it to your own situation and fill in the missing pieces through experience." Focus "means so much more" than sitting down for deep work: "it's more so where is my focus at any given point in life, what am I noticing." Focusing on a bigger goal and holding it in the back of your mind "directs your focus to finding the things that will serve that goal" (the transcript renders it "survive"). "To me focus is life, because focus is conscious attention and we're in the age of attention." The book ties together business, spirituality, life and health. "It should be called like the art of life," he admits, but "focus" positions it better because he has noticed that "woo-woo" language turns people off, so he injects the same lessons "via something enticing like business." Dickie paraphrases the book as "the art of figuring out where you are honing your limited resources"; Koe agrees, but places it "higher level, less tactical" (**Source:** `Dan Koe & Dickie Bush On One-Person Businesses, Creative Workflows, and Lifestyle Design.md`, 2023-03-16).

The passage is useful for reading the rest of this chapter. It tells us that for Koe "focus" is a deliberate umbrella term: the commercially legible word for what spiritual traditions call awareness and what psychology calls attention. It tells us that the lens metaphor, which section 6.5 develops, was already the organizing idea of the book in 2023. And it tells us that his strategy of framing spiritual or philosophical ideas in business language is a conscious choice of positioning, not an accident; a reader who finds the business framing narrow should know that the author intends it as a vehicle.

### 6.2 Focus Is Conscious Attention

#### The definition and the claim of root control

Section 6.1 treated attention as a resource. This section turns the resource into a principle of control. In a 2022 video on anxiety and uncertainty, Koe gives the definition that anchors his whole use of the word: "In my eyes the definition of focus is conscious attention." He then makes a strong claim about its place in the hierarchy of self-improvement. When you peel everything back, "habits aren't the most important thing", nor whatever else self-help people say. "There is only one thing that you truly have a root control over that leads to every other thing you do, your thoughts, actions, on a long enough time scale, and that is focus." It is the only thing you can truly control, and it impacts everything you can only "somewhat control": thoughts, emotions and actions (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16).

**Focus**, then, is a common word used in a specific sense: not concentration on a task, but the direction of conscious attention at any moment. **Root control** is his coinage for the special status of that direction. The structure of the argument is a dependency chain. Thoughts arise partly on their own; emotions follow thoughts and circumstances; actions follow thoughts and emotions. None of these is fully under command. But what you do with your attention when a thought or emotion appears *is* under command, and over a long enough time scale that choice shapes which thoughts recur, which emotions dominate and which actions become habitual. Control is therefore indirect for everything except attention, and attention is the root from which the indirect control grows.

The claim has a cost the corpus itself records. Saying that "habits aren't the most important thing" subordinates habits to focus, while many other videos, including several discussed in Chapter 13, put habits and routines at the center. The tension is one of emphasis rather than contradiction: elsewhere he describes bad habits acquired without "mental housekeeping" (his term for writing down everything on your mind, restructuring it and ordering it on paper so that you have clarity to execute, a regular act of order against entropy; the practice is treated in Chapter 13) and the state of consciousness as a reflection of accumulated habits. The two views are compatible if focus is understood as the cause and habits as its sediment: habits are what repeated attention leaves behind, and changing them requires changing where attention goes. This is the reading this book adopts, but the author never states it in those words.

#### The same pattern in three traditions

Koe supports the primacy of attention with a convergence argument rather than with evidence. You hear about consciousness from ancient masters, spiritual gurus and entrepreneurs. Entrepreneurs talk about it through the lens of focus: "focus is the best thing that led to the most benefits in my life." Ancient masters talk about "non-duality and all this other stuff." Spiritual teachers say "consciousness, consciousness, consciousness." "There's so many different patterns here relating to attention because in reality that is the only thing you have" (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16).

The argument is typical of his method, which Chapter 17 calls pattern recognition across domains: when traditions that do not talk to each other arrive at the same emphasis, he treats the overlap as a sign that they are pointing at something real. Its strength is that it explains why "focus" in his work slides easily into "awareness" and "consciousness": he regards them as one phenomenon described in three vocabularies. Its weakness is that the convergence is asserted rather than shown; the three traditions mean different things by attention (the entrepreneur means prioritization, the contemplative means non-reactive awareness), and the argument works only at the level of a shared emphasis. It should be read as an interpretive frame, not as a demonstration.

#### Attention as frequency: the author's theory

In the same 2022 period Koe adds a layer that he himself flags as speculative. With the mind understood as a system, attention is "a way of processing information", which is "what humans do", and "information holds a certain frequency." He addresses the "woo-woo people" who say "everything is vibration" or "positive vibes only": "there is some science behind it, where what you pay attention to you are processing a frequency of energy." The practical conclusion follows from the systems view of Chapter 5: if your mind is a system, and systems need energy to be maintained, flow properly and lead to beneficial outcomes, then you need to be "very conscious with where you place your attention and how you interpret the information that is coming into your mind. Do not take this lightly" (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16).

A few months earlier he had called this his own theory, and tied it to the book he was writing: "My theory, and a huge part of the book I'm writing, *The Art of Focus*, is that focus and attention is how you tap into and channel and make a connection or kind of tune into the frequency of energy that you have available to you." This brings back a principle he had stated earlier in that video, "awareness begets awareness": if you are unaware of an energy or frequency, you first have to gain that awareness, because "the only thing you have available to your current mind are the things that you were made aware of" (**Source:** `How To Create A Better Life For Yourself.md`, 2022-06-12).

Two levels should be kept apart. The practical level is uncontroversial and fully consistent with the rest of the chapter: what you attend to and how you interpret it shape your state, so attention deserves care, and you cannot attend to what you have never been made aware of. The metaphysical level, that attention literally processes a "frequency of energy" and that "there is some science behind" the vibration language, is presented as his theory, and the corpus offers no evidence for the scientific claim. The phrase "kind of tune into" signals the hedge. The "awareness begets awareness" principle, by contrast, does a lot of work later: it is the reason section 6.5 insists that a goal opens awareness, and the reason the "fridge" metaphor says you can only cook with what you have been exposed to.

#### The attention spreadsheet

The clearest image of attention as the determinant of a life comes from *The Art of Focus* itself, which Koe read aloud shortly before its launch: "Imagine having a spreadsheet that displayed how much attention you've invested in any given area of your life (mind, body, spirit and business) that impact your relationship with the world and those around you. The spreadsheet would be a direct indicator of your quality of life" (**Source:** `Focus Is A Superpower (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md`, 2023-07-11).

The **attention spreadsheet** is a thought experiment, not a tool he asks you to build, but it can be used as one. Its premise is the root-control claim restated as accounting: if attention is the only input you fully control, then the distribution of attention across domains is the best single predictor of how those domains will look. Its four columns (mind, body, spirit, business) recur throughout the corpus as the domains of holistic development (Chapter 40). And it quietly redefines quality of life as something produced rather than received: not how good your circumstances are, but how much conscious attention you have invested in the areas that determine them.

#### What you can and cannot control: the compulsive thought

If attention is the only root control, the limit of that control needs to be stated precisely, and Koe does so in the same 2022 video. "Your unconscious is your unconscious. Things are programmed into your unconscious and you are not going to be able to get away from that." The programmed unconscious of Chapters 1 and 3 will keep producing thoughts you did not choose. "The only thing you can do is when that compulsive unconscious thought pops into your conscious mind, are you going to give it conscious attention?" And if you do, you can "mold your camera or your perspective to see it from a conducive angle that will lead to you achieving your goals or just not being an emotional wreck" (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16).

This is the operational meaning of root control. You do not control the arrival of the thought; you control two things about what happens next: whether you give it the scarce resource of conscious attention, and from which angle you look at it if you do. The camera image connects to Chapter 3's account of perspective as the camera and perception as the lens; the next section's "zoom" is the same camera adjusted in a different way. It also connects to the awareness practice of Chapter 2: noticing a thought as a thought is already the first exercise of root control.

From this Koe draws a principle he calls "the simplest thing that we can break it down into": "The cure to anxiety or overwhelm or stress or worry is to always act with intention." He then defines what intention means in this context: "This intention is to operate within your own reality and not project outside of it" (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16). **Act with intention** is thus defined negatively by what it excludes: projection. Anxiety, overwhelm and worry are attention spent outside the reality you can act on, in imagined futures, other people's opinions or circumstances you cannot change. Acting with intention brings attention back to what is within your own reality and directs it toward something. Section 6.5 will add the positive half of the definition: intention as "stretching towards a goal". Together they say that intention is attention that is both present and aimed.

#### The consequence: attention over hours

If focus is conscious attention and conscious attention is the scarce input, a practical consequence follows immediately: the value of work depends on where attention goes, not on how many hours are logged. Koe builds this argument against the hustle culture he grew up with, and he builds it with a mixture of borrowed testimony and his own reasoning, which should be kept distinct.

The borrowed testimony comes first. He quotes a tweet from the copywriter Tej (sometimes transcribed "Tes") Dosa, in two videos eight months apart: "I know an entrepreneur who works 14 hours a day and is stuck living in the basement. I know an entrepreneur who works five hours a day and has built and sold three companies for $100 million plus each. The value isn't in how many hours you work but where you focus your attention, awareness and consciousness." Koe stresses that "that last part is so, so, so crucial" (**Source:** `The 4 Hour Workday (How Creatives Work Less & Earn More).md`, 2023-06-18; `Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md`, 2024-02-18). The sentence matters to him because it uses his own triad (attention, awareness, consciousness) as the explanation of a business outcome.

The second testimony is from Sam Altman: "Focus is a force multiplier on work. Almost everyone I've ever met would be well served by spending more time thinking about what to focus on. It is much more important to work on the right thing than it is to work many hours. Most people waste most of their time on stuff that doesn't matter. Once you have figured out what to do, be unstoppable about getting your small handful of priorities accomplished quickly. I have yet to meet a slow-moving person who is very successful" (**Source:** `Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md`, 2024-02-18). **Force multiplier** is Altman's phrase; Koe uses it as evidence that the people he considers most successful value focus over hours.

**Complementary context:** the Altman passage comes from his 2018 blog post "Productivity". Note that it does not argue for working little; it argues that the choice of what to work on dominates the number of hours. Koe uses it for the first point, and elsewhere in the same video mentions that Altman is open about how long he works.

Koe frames both quotations with an observation of his own: "Most people think that they have to work like a millionaire in order to become a millionaire, but the first misunderstanding there is that most people don't know how millionaires work" (**Source:** `Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md`, 2024-02-18). He offers three examples: Altman, Dosa, and himself. The point is not that millionaires work little, but that the image of the millionaire's workday that circulates (endless hours, constant grind) is an assumption rather than an observation.

#### Doing mode and the badge of honor

The psychological side of the argument is a state Koe calls **doing mode**, a term he adapts from Alex Soojung-Kim Pang's *Rest*. He opens a 2024 video by reversing the usual causality: most people struggle to do good work *because* they work too much. Their mind is "always on", like a car running out of gas while being pushed down the road toward some destination. This produces doing mode, "a narrow-minded state". Left unchecked, it leads to a "hot ball of stress" in the chest whose source is hard to identify, a feeling of a closed mind, damaged quality time with others, an inability to appreciate "life's innate richness", and forgetting what a clear line of thought feels like. People in this state wear overwork "as a badge of honor" and feel they need to keep working on something without knowing what, because they are stuck in an **imaginary Rat Race**, chasing a destination they are not clear on themselves or one that someone else told them to reach (**Source:** `Minimalist Productivity Work Less, Do More.md`, 2024-08-11). He immediately qualifies that he is not saying long hours are bad in themselves; the target is the unexamined, destination-less version.

The connection to the rest of this chapter is direct. Doing mode is a chronic state of narrow, unconscious focus (in the terms of section 6.3's Focus Matrix, it lives in the stressed quadrant), and the imaginary rat race is what happens when attention is intensely invested but not aimed at a goal one has chosen (the theme of section 6.5). The car pushed toward a destination it cannot see is the cybernetic system of section 6.4 without a reference signal.

In 2025 he sharpens the critique into a statement about wisdom. Young people have been conditioned to see mindless grinding as "a status symbol disguised as an effective strategy". "Grinding is not wisdom or a strategy, it's a lack thereof." People who think they will grind 12 to 16 hours once they go full-time should know that "your habits now are probably going to be your habits later"; they simply do not know how to use time wisely. Most say "that's impossible" because they cannot muster the courage to say "I don't know how to do that." He supports the claim with his own output: two books, more than ten products and a software startup in progress, while being confident that most people "waste time or don't know what they don't know" (**Source:** `Harsh Truth You Don't Need To Grind 247 To Be Successful.md`, 2025-05-04).

The same video records the objection he was answering. A comment on X said, "that's easy for you to say... beginners need to grind." Koe answers with counter-sources: books like *4,000 Weeks* (he does not name the author) and *Rest* by Alex Soojung-Kim Pang; what Alex Hormozi says in some of his videos "if you actually listen"; and David Shapiro, who said building his career was a hard grind in one sense but was never really difficult for him and did not require working that much or that long. He describes two camps, the "hustle bros" ("work 16 hours a day or you're worth nothing") and those who say "you don't have to do that if you know what you're doing," and offers his content as "a better route" (**Source:** `Harsh Truth You Don't Need To Grind 247 To Be Successful.md`, 2025-05-04).

**Complementary context:** *Four Thousand Weeks* (2021) is by Oliver Burkeman; *Rest: Why You Get More Done When You Work Less* (2016) is by Alex Soojung-Kim Pang. David Shapiro is a writer on AI and post-labor economics whom Koe cites elsewhere.

#### "You don't need 12 hours a day": the claim and how it evolved

The strongest version of the anti-grind position appears in 2023. Anyone who says you need 12 hours a day "is either an idiot or they don't have perspective," or is describing their own experience, which is fine as long as it is framed that way. His argument is a reductio: if 12 hours a day were required to actualize your dreams, nobody with responsibilities would ever succeed, since adults have families, jobs and bills. He adds a concession that matters for later: if you are young, you have a huge advantage and should take advantage of it (**Source:** `Focus Is A Superpower (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md`, 2023-07-11).

This is one of the places where the author's position moved, and both versions should be visible. In 2023 the four-hour workday functioned as a general rule: three to five hours of focus as the maximum, four hours as a "dead stop", and twelve-hour days as a sign that something needed fixing with systems. By late 2024 he published a video titled "It's actually pretty easy to focus 12 hours a day" and described twelve-hour days that "just come naturally" in a phase of intensity; by 2025 he was saying that "productivity comes in seasons", four hours in one, sixteen in another, while sustained 12–16-hour days are "a sign of major dysfunction". He also explained the earlier rigidity: people read only the headline and misinterpret the message, and in early 2024 he admitted he had preached the four-hour workday "as if it served everyone" partly to be polarizing. The resolution he arrives at is to treat four hours as the sustainable baseline and long days as a legitimate season, provided they are neither permanent nor forced. Chapter 12 tells this story in full; what matters here is that the underlying principle never changed. In every version, what produces results is where attention goes, and long hours are acceptable only when they are an expression of clear, chosen focus rather than a substitute for it.

#### Mechanical work, creative work and the four productivity errors

Two further distinctions explain *why* hours and results come apart. The first separates kinds of work. "Mechanical work can be done for 16 hours a day with relative ease because it doesn't demand that much mental energy," but that is not where leverage comes from, and you still neglect the rest of your life. If you are arguing in your head that you can do lots of high-quality work all day, Koe says, it is probably mechanical work, and you are probably better off working less so you do not "procrastinate all the other work you should have done in other areas of your life for 40 years." Working a lot is not bad, but it is "the lowest form of leverage of the three" (**Source:** `Harsh Truth You Don't Need To Grind 247 To Be Successful.md`, 2025-05-04). The other two forms are not named in this passage; elsewhere in the corpus the comparison is between labor and the leverage of skill, media and systems (Chapters 25 and 34). The distinction between **mechanical work** and **creative work** connects directly to the RAM metaphor: mechanical work runs on the wide unconscious channel and can go on for hours; creative work requires the narrow conscious channel, which is exactly the resource that tires.

The second distinction is a diagnosis of how people get productivity wrong, stated as four errors "they may not explicitly say, but their actions show" (**Source:** `A Daily Routine To Work Less, Earn More & Enjoy Life.md`, 2024-12-29):

| Error | What people believe | What Koe implies instead |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Quantity over quality | Productivity is about how much you do | It is about the quality of what attention produces |
| 2. All hours are equal | "They are the same person in the last four hours of the day as they are in the first four" | Attention and energy degrade through the day; hours are not interchangeable |
| 3. Working on all things | Productivity means working on everything | It means working on "the one thing that brings all of the results" |
| 4. Tasks are levers | Every task contributes equally | Tasks must be separated from **levers**, the few actions that move outcomes |

His summary is a contrast between two people: "Person A can work 12 hours a day and make $50,000 a year, while person B can work 1 hour a day and make 5 million. The difference is skill, leverage and understanding, not how hard, long or organized you work." The figures are illustrative, not data; what the contrast does is relocate the explanation of results from effort to three things that depend on how attention has been invested over time. **Tasks versus levers** is the distinction Chapter 12 turns into a daily priority system.

The second error is illustrated in a 2024 video with a quotation from a speaker the transcript does not identify. When people tell him they work 15 hours a day, he is not interested; he is interested in "the quality of working hours, not the quantity." You are not the same in the first five hours of the day as in the last five: you are tired, and when you are tired you stop listening and your decisions become risky. Koe uses the quotation to frame the subject of that video, which is how to leverage the one hour a day you have (**Source:** `Change Your Life In 365 Hours (The New Rich Focus On These Tasks Daily).md`, 2024-06-02).

Finally, Koe reinterprets the most common piece of advice used to justify grinding. Successful people commonly advise young people to work as hard as possible in their twenties and give up most of life. He thinks most people misinterpret this: what they actually mean is "take bigger risks, bet on yourself and create your own path." That does not require 16 hours a day, because there is more to work hard on than your business: mind, body and spirit need developing, and relationships should not be neglected, because neglect loads "rogue thoughts" into your mind that dampen the quality of your work. In the same passage he invokes Parkinson's law: work fills the time allotted to it, and a real, urgent deadline gets work done (**Source:** `Harsh Truth You Don't Need To Grind 247 To Be Successful.md`, 2025-05-04). A **rogue thought**, in his vocabulary, is a stray thought opened by some unresolved matter that splits focus: one more program consuming RAM. The argument closes the loop with section 6.1: neglecting other areas of life for work does not even serve work, because the neglect returns as background processes.

**Complementary context:** Parkinson's law ("work expands so as to fill the time available for its completion") comes from C. Northcote Parkinson's 1955 essay in *The Economist*. Koe uses it repeatedly as the justification for short, deadline-bound work blocks (Chapter 12).

#### Work like a lion, not a cow

The most vivid version of the argument is a metaphor Koe takes from Naval Ravikant and then extends. Naval's point, as Koe paraphrases it: you and I are not like cows, meant to graze all day; we are meant to hunt like lions, and we are closer to carnivores than to herbivores in our development. As an **intellectual athlete** (Naval's phrase) you function like an athlete: train hard, sprint, rest, reassess, get your feedback loop, train more, sprint, rest, reassess. Expecting linear output by cranking the same hours every day is for machines; "machines should work 9 to 5; humans aren't meant to." Koe frames the whole quotation with a provocative premise: procrastination "is not laziness, it's how you're wired" (**Source:** `Harsh Truth You Don't Need To Grind 247 To Be Successful.md`, 2025-05-04).

Later in the same video he builds the metaphor into a comparison with his own additions (**Source:** `Harsh Truth You Don't Need To Grind 247 To Be Successful.md`, 2025-05-04):

| The cow | The lion |
|---|---|
| Grazes the fields | Hunts |
| Consistent long hours every day | Intense bursts of focused, high-energy work |
| Steady, predictable output | Long rest and recovery between hunts |
| Trades time for money linearly | Aims for leverage, where results are not tied to time |
| Shows up regardless of energy | Works according to energy and creativity cycles |
| Often ends in burnout and diminishing returns | Prioritizes impact over hours logged |

The original element he adds is an evolutionary account of *why* knowledge workers share the lion's wiring. Our brains crave the novelty and dopamine of discovering resources that aid survival. Ancestors who found berries, or prey for later, got dopamine, "the good excitement". For knowledge workers, "ideas are our new berries": psychologically, finding an idea is the same as hunting food. By today's standards, he concedes, a lion looks like "a massive procrastinator", and people will make you feel guilty about it and call it a lack of discipline; being bad at texting back or putting off homework, he says, is not a character flaw but how many people are wired. His three lessons: intensity beats duration; rest is the most productive form of work; results matter more than hours. **Ideas are our new berries** is his coinage, and it links this metaphor to the hunting psyche of Chapter 2 and to the berry bush of Chapter 3.

A year earlier he had already translated the lion into his own vocabulary and into a cycle. "Your psyche is wired to hunt, so, as Naval would say, work like a lion, or in my words, use tactical stress." He says he described this in his productivity framework, one he used to build his startup. The cycle: "sit, let vision accumulate, wait until you see the prey, sprint, get the work done, eat, enjoy life and rest." He warns that "this goes against everything that we've been conditioned to believe; it will take time to accept that this is how creatives work," and that "being boxed in a tight 9 to 5 schedule is a living hell for creatives and entrepreneurs at heart" (**Source:** `How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md`, 2024-05-26). **Tactical stress** has several meanings in the corpus (Chapter 10 treats the main one, the deliberate choice of a high-stakes situation); in this passage it names the lion's cycle of accumulation, sprint and rest.

The cycle can be read step by step in the terms of this chapter. *Sit and let vision accumulate*: a phase of open focus in which ideas stack up around a goal (section 6.3's open quadrant, section 6.5's stacking of ideas). *Wait until you see the prey*: the goal acts as a lens until a specific opportunity or task becomes visible. *Sprint*: narrow, conscious focus with a deadline. *Eat, enjoy life and rest*: the recovery that restores the scarce conscious channel. What makes this different from procrastination in the pejorative sense is the presence of the goal throughout; the corpus elsewhere distinguishes "mindful procrastination", which reserves energy for a deadline-driven sprint, from unconscious procrastination, which fills working time with what does not matter (Chapter 12).

The metaphor also appears from the mouth of a guest, which shows both its reach and its limits. In a 2025 conversation, John Hugh, co-founder of Stan, describes where he gets in his own way. It is "so easy to just get sucked into the urgent"; his co-founder Vitali pushes him with "do you really need to be taking this call yourself, or do you need to be thinking higher level for us?" Slack and email create "this dopamine addiction", especially for someone coming from finance. He does believe outsized outcomes require hard work, "but it's up to a certain degree"; his old mental model was "an input-output thing of work hours", from hundred-hour weeks in finance doing "drone level stuff". At his current scale, where the work is "almost entirely about creativity", he says he would be better off "going to Bali for 3 weeks and surfing and not working at all, and then after paddling back from a wave having 2 minutes of an insight", because of how much leverage creativity has; he admits he is not doing that. His paradigm is Naval's "work like a lion, not like a cow", and his formula is to find your balance "with your reality of the moment" and to be "maniacal about saying no to things and focusing": "the formula for success that I personally wish I followed more" (**Source:** `How To Grow An Audience With Zero Followers & Build A Profitable Startup.md`, 2025-01-28). The confession is valuable precisely because it comes from someone who knows the principle and struggles to apply it: urgency and notifications are strong enough to override a belief.

The same conversation contains the counterweight. Vitali, Hugh's co-founder, says he works all the time, and the two styles are presented as compatible. Koe himself, elsewhere, says that Alex Hormozi can work twelve hours well because it is his identity, and that "there is no right way to work", while in other videos he calls sustained 16-hour days a sign that someone does not value health and relationships. The corpus holds two registers side by side: a general critique of hustle culture and respect for an individual's genuine choice. They are not reconciled explicitly, but the line between them is the one this section has drawn: long hours that express chosen focus are one thing; long hours that substitute for it are another.

#### Outcomes over hours inside a team

The principle of attention over hours has an organizational version, which comes from Stan's founders and which Koe connects to identity. Asked how they structure employees' workdays, John Hugh answers: "we definitely don't track hours"; they are "entirely focused on outcomes." If someone is "100x and can just chill for the whole week" and ship best-in-class outcomes in twenty minutes, "awesome." But the people they attract "aren't people who ever want to be in a 9 to 5"; "if you're counting down the time until you clock out at Stan," it is not a fit, functionally or culturally, and they would help you find what sparks you. Their core value is "ownership", which he describes as "this deep level of responsibility." Because unlimited vacation "can be taken poorly", they set a minimum: at least fifteen days a year and at least three per quarter, with leadership setting the example by taking "a couple months off a year at least" (**Source:** `How To Grow An Audience With Zero Followers & Build A Profitable Startup.md`, 2025-01-28).

Vitali completes the picture with a definition of culture. Best-in-class people "never count the hours, they work through completion" and define themselves by the quality of their output; he recalls all-nighters in the office ("we are close... are we doing it, we are doing it") that built camaraderie, "maybe a little bit toxic for some people, not if you're having fun." "Culture is not what you say you stand for, and definitely not the values that are written, but the actions and behaviors of people on the team." His analogy is a **running club**: people run at different paces, and "our team runs at a very particular pace." One component of that pace is a 24-hour **iteration cycle**: do not come talk to anyone if you have not made progress in 24 hours on what you said you would do, and come prepared to report. When a hire fails, "everyone is running at the same pace but there's one person that keeps falling out"; after feedback, "they will either keep pace or they won't," and if not, "you have to correct a mistake" (**Source:** `How To Grow An Audience With Zero Followers & Build A Profitable Startup.md`, 2025-01-28).

These are the guests' ideas, not Koe's, and they belong to a startup context that Chapter 37 treats on its own terms. Koe reaches a parallel conclusion from his own model, though. He and his co-founder Matt have discussed how, in their past 9-to-5 jobs, they were "the worst employees ever," and that is on their mind when they hire. To them, "we just wake up and do the thing." An employee who does not fit would sabotage the business, not caring, delivering late, not listening, "no matter how much I motivate them as a founder or I train them or incentivize them." The reason: "their identity doesn't match the behavior required for our business" (**Source:** `How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md`, 2025-12-28).

Put together, the three passages show the principle of this section at the scale of a group. Hours are a poor proxy because what produces results is directed attention; directed attention cannot be bought with incentives or imposed with schedules, because it flows from identity and goals (Chapter 3); and culture, in Vitali's sense, is simply the observable pattern of where a team's attention actually goes. The running club is the attention spreadsheet of a team.

### 6.3 Zoom In, Zoom Out and the Focus Matrix

Section 6.2 established that attention is the root control. But attention is not just pointed somewhere; it also has a *width* and a *quality*. You can be absorbed in a single detail or aware of the whole field; you can be choosing where your attention goes or having it dragged. Koe builds two connected tools from these dimensions. The first is the image of zooming in and zooming out, which explains how problems grow and shrink in consciousness. The second is the **Focus Matrix**, a two-by-two model that crosses the width of focus with whether it is conscious, and maps each quadrant to a recognizable emotional state. Both are his own; both rest on a borrowed piece of neuroscience; and both are meant less as theory than as instruments for reading your state in the moment and changing it.

#### The zoom graphic

The image first appears in a 2022 video that opens with a graphic Koe made. The outer circles represent your perspective; the inner white circles represent the problem. On the left side, the more zoomed in you are on the problem, the bigger it becomes, "the more it becomes your reality." On the right side, the more you zoom out, the smaller the problem becomes, "because you can see that it is just a pixel that is a part of a beautiful image." A bar along the bottom indicates the consequence: the more zoomed in you are, the more your attention is immersed in the problem and the more it occupies your consciousness, "then the more anxiety, the stress and overwhelm you're going to have" (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16). The transcript describes the graphic only in outline; its exact layout cannot be reconstructed beyond what is said.

The mechanism follows from section 6.1. Conscious attention is a small fixed workspace. The more of it a problem occupies, the less room is left for anything else, including the information that would put the problem in proportion; and since what occupies consciousness *is* your experienced reality, a problem that fills the workspace becomes, for practical purposes, the whole world. Zooming out does not change the problem; it changes the share of the workspace the problem takes, which changes how it feels and what else becomes visible.

To show that zooming in is a real-time process and not a figure of speech, he proposes an experiment: drink a ton of coffee and watch your mind zoom in on the smallest things, "because of the stress response." Induce stress (too much caffeine will do), and you notice little problems, they immerse your attention, and you cannot even remember what you were supposed to do later today; "you can't remember the finer things in life in that state of stress" (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16). The **coffee test** is offered as a personal experiment, not a study, but it makes a testable claim: stress narrows attention onto problems, and narrowed attention, in turn, magnifies them. The loop runs in both directions, which is why the remedy has to act on attention directly.

#### Your problems are as big as you make them

From the graphic Koe draws a principle: "Your problems are as big as you make them." Problems are normal; everyone has them; and "there are always two sides to the same coin." If there is a problem, there is an opportunity on the other side "that you are not seeing because your mind is not open enough, because you're so laser focused on the problem itself." "Problems aren't special," and viewed "from the top of the mountain or from the big picture, they seem very small and stupid" (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16).

He gives the principle a pointed illustration aimed at creators and business people. Why are you so worried that a social media post, written on a given day at a given time in a given state of mind, is getting zero engagement, "when a child in a third world country has come to accept that he is not going to have water the next day and he is perfectly okay with that"? "How is that child in a better mental space than you?" He adds that he is not asking for an answer, only asking you to think about it. By gaining perspective, "you create the space to zoom your attention out, and from that the problem shrinks from the size of the world to the size of a breadcrumb" (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16).

A 2023 passage, reproduced in a 2024 compilation, applies the same move to the morning: "Problems are only problems if you interpret them as such from your narrow perspective. If you don't feel like getting out of bed, zoom out and see your problem for what it is": some people do not have legs and have to be lifted out of bed; some do not have a bed or food to cook in the morning. "You have a purpose to actualize and you need to perceive the situation in the way that allows you to act" (**Source:** `Society Is A Pyramid Scheme (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md`, 2023-01-15; `Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md`, 2024-03-21).

These comparisons are rhetorical devices, and they have limits a careful reader should notice. They work by changing the reference class: next to a child without water, an unliked post looks trivial. But the same device could be used to dismiss any suffering, and the author does not mean it that way; the final clause of the 2023 passage shows his actual criterion. The question is not whether your problem is objectively small, but whether the way you are perceiving it lets you *act*. Zooming out is justified when the zoomed-in view paralyzes you, and its purpose is to return you to action on a purpose you have.

The sentence "problems are only problems if you interpret them as such" also stands in tension with another strand of the corpus. Elsewhere Koe says that "problems never go away", and from 2025 he insists that thinking *starts* with a problem and that you should train your mind to think "problem, problem, problem" (Chapter 17). One view relativizes problems; the other makes them the engine of direction. The author does not reconcile them, but they apply to different things. The zoom-out move targets the felt weight of a problem that is blocking action (the urge to stay in bed, the anxiety over a post). Problem-first thinking targets problems as material for work: the real, specific obstacles between you and a goal. Section 6.6 adds the piece that connects them: a problem only becomes visible *as a problem* relative to a goal. Without a goal, every discomfort is equally heavy; with one, some problems become the work and others become pixels.

#### The ego's low note

In a 2023 video Koe explains *why* we zoom in on the negative, using a musical image. In the song of your life, highs and lows play together. What happens is "you choosing, your ego choosing, to perceive one of those, usually a low, a negative, and block out everything else that's happening around you." Then "all you see is negativity because you're just so narrowed in on it." What you have not done is "zoomed out and gained the resources necessary to zoom back in from a place of perspective and make forward progress in the world without a worry" (**Source:** `If You’re Lost - Here’s How You Find Clarity For The Future.md`, 2023-06-04).

Two details make this more than a restatement. First, the agent of the narrowing is the ego, which links the zoom to the conceptual survival of Chapter 3: an identity under threat fixes attention on the threat, and the fixation is a defense. Second, zooming out is not the end point. It is the way to *gather resources* in order to zoom back in, now from a place of perspective. The zoom is a cycle, not a preference for the wide view. The same cycle reappears below in the Focus Matrix, where narrow conscious focus is the productive state, and in section 6.4, where the cybernetic system has to step back to compare its position with the goal before it can correct.

#### Depth of focus: the two networks

Before presenting the matrix, Koe introduces the piece of neuroscience on which it rests. The second quality that fascinates him about humans, he says, is their "depth of focus." In psychology, the **task positive network** is active when you are focused on an external string of tasks, which is "good for productivity." The **default mode network** is active when you take your focus off the external (like work) and bring it internal; "this opens your awareness and allows better ideas to fill your brain, or negative ideas, but this is good for creativity." The aim is "this balance between narrow external focus and more internal open focus" (**Source:** `The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md`, 2023-01-01; `Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md`, 2024-03-21, where the same passage is reproduced). **Depth of focus** is his name for this axis.

Note the honest symmetry in the description: the internal, open mode lets in better ideas *or* negative ones. Open focus is not good in itself; it is the condition for creativity and also for rumination. That symmetry is the reason he needs a second axis.

**Complementary context:** the default mode network is a set of brain regions that is more active during rest, mind-wandering, self-reflection and imagination than during externally directed tasks; it was characterized in the early 2000s (Raichle and colleagues). Networks engaged by attention-demanding external tasks are often grouped as "task-positive", and their activity tends to be anticorrelated with the default mode network. Koe uses these findings as an explanatory frame for productivity and creativity; the link to specific emotional states in his matrix is his interpretation, not a claim from the research. Chapter 13 shows how he uses the default mode network to justify rest.

#### The Focus Matrix: how the model was built

The Focus Matrix appears in at least five videos between 2022 and 2024, each time as a graphic shown on screen and only partly described aloud. It is clearest to follow its development in order, because the terms shift before they settle.

The first version (June 2022) is presented as the "sixth layer" of a framework for a better life: control your attention and understand focus. The vertical axis runs from **deep focus** at the top (also called narrow or convergent focus) to **wide focus** at the bottom (open or divergent focus); the horizontal axis runs from unconscious on the left to conscious on the right. Its use is as a mental model to recall when you are stressed, anxious or reactive: see where your focus is going. Are you unconscious and in convergent focus, fixated on one negative thing that pulls that experience and its emotions into the present? Then ask "how can you move your focus from unconscious to conscious, and move it from narrow or wide" as the present situation requires (**Source:** `How To Create A Better Life For Yourself.md`, 2022-06-12).

Two weeks later he uses the matrix to make a point about spirituality and creativity. Many people do not take spirituality seriously, he says, but it is "another interpretation of the phenomenon that psychology is slowly starting to figure out": in an open, divergent, "conscious state of focus" you tap into creative abilities because you are not hyper-fixated on, say, a negative thought. "There is a separation between productivity and creativity": the narrower your focus, the less creative and the more productive; the more open and wide, the more creative, because you are expanding your mind and tapping into your subconscious and everything you have been made conscious of (**Source:** `How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md`, 2022-06-25). In this passage the transcript uses "convergent" and "divergent" inconsistently, at one point calling wide focus "convergent or open or wide"; the intended contrast is clearly narrow (productive) against open (creative).

**Complementary context:** "convergent" and "divergent" thinking are terms from the psychologist J. P. Guilford (1950s): convergent thinking narrows toward a single correct answer, divergent thinking generates many possibilities. Koe uses them as synonyms for narrow and open focus.

In November 2022 the axes are renamed. The vertical axis now runs from **contraction** (narrow, deep focus) at the top to **expansion** (wide, open focus) at the bottom; the horizontal axis from **conscious focus** on the right ("you are aware of what you're focusing on") to unconscious on the left, where your attention is being "manhandled" and you are not in control of it. Emotions are shown in each quadrant on screen but not read aloud. Narrow focus is not bad, he stresses; it is very useful for productivity. But when you are "lasered in" on a thought, emotion or belief you do not like, your mind is closed off: the "other piece of the puzzle" that would stop you from being angry cannot register in awareness. Expansion means opening focus and consciousness to "seek to understand rather than judge", to "seek to observe and discern rather than judge and react and label." Most people, he says, live in a perpetual state of narrow, contracted focus, and he guarantees that the emotions on the unconscious side are ones you do not want. "It is as simple as learning to focus, but that goes ten times deeper than you think" (**Source:** `Why You Can't Be Yourself (The Power Of Being Alone).md`, 2022-11-13).

His illustration in that video is small and memorable. When someone notices a typo in one of his tweets ("there" for "their") and that is all they focus on, they miss the complete message, the lesson, the essence of the post. The recommendation is to expand your focus and try to understand the essence of what is being said in any situation, pulling the lesson out rather than lasering in on the one thing you do not like, because what you focus on "composes the contents of your consciousness, so it's your reality" (**Source:** `Why You Can't Be Yourself (The Power Of Being Alone).md`, 2022-11-13).

In January 2023, and again in a 2024 compilation that reproduces the passage, the matrix is presented as a guide, with the reason for building it stated plainly: "there are negative aspects to focus; it's not all productivity and creativity." The left side (unconscious, narrow or open) leads to stress, anxiety, reactivity, annoyance, feeling lost, feeling uncertain, feeling overwhelmed. The right side (conscious, narrow and open) is "more efficient, task oriented, associated with the flow state, present, creative, relaxed and joyful." Use it once you become aware of your current state: "if you feel lost or you feel stressed or you feel anxious, there's a reason behind that," and you can move your focus toward the positive side, "usually by eliminating distractions and focusing on the task at hand" (**Source:** `The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md`, 2023-01-01; `Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md`, 2024-03-21). In the 2024 compilation he later explains the negative narrow side with an example from deadlines: one problem leads to the next, and focus opens into drowning in problems.

The assignment of each state to a specific quadrant is fixed only in September 2023, in a video that connects the matrix to Csikszentmihalyi's "order in consciousness": ordered consciousness creates degrees of flow, and to order consciousness is to focus attention on something external (a conversation, a task) or internal (a thought, an emotion), both of which change how the brain operates; he mentions the task positive and default mode networks and "a few other networks" that vary with open versus narrow focus. Then the four quadrants are named (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (You Can't Escape The Matrix).md`, 2023-09-05):

| | **Narrow focus** (contraction, deep, convergent) | **Open focus** (expansion, wide, divergent) |
|---|---|---|
| **Conscious** (you direct attention) | Efficient, task-oriented, flow state, deep work | Present, creative, relaxed, joyful |
| **Unconscious** (attention is "manhandled") | Stressed, anxious, reactive, annoyed | Lost, uncertain, overwhelmed, lacking clarity |

"Just by understanding this, you can identify where your focus is, how you feel, and start to move within the matrix to where you want to feel." Earlier versions, which list the emotions only by side, are consistent with this assignment, and one of them anticipates it by associating unconscious narrow focus with being locked on one problem and unconscious open focus with drowning in many. Taken together, the versions support the table above; the earlier transcripts alone would not have allowed the quadrants to be reconstructed with certainty.

#### How to read the matrix

The matrix is simple, but several of its implications are easy to miss.

First, neither width is good or bad in itself. Narrow focus is the flow and deep work of the top-right quadrant *and* the reactive fixation of the bottom-left; open focus is creative presence *and* lost overwhelm. What separates the good states from the bad ones is the horizontal axis: whether attention is being directed or dragged. This is the matrix's version of the root-control claim in section 6.2: the decisive variable is consciousness, not concentration.

Second, the two unconscious states map onto the two failures already described. The stressed, reactive quadrant is the zoomed-in problem of the graphic and the doing mode of section 6.2: one thing occupies the whole workspace and the ego defends against it. The lost, overwhelmed quadrant is the split modern mind of section 6.1: attention scattered across a dozen programs, none chosen. One is too much of a single thing, the other too little of anything.

Third, the matrix gives two different moves, which the corpus describes separately. One is *horizontal*: from unconscious to conscious, by noticing your state ("there's a reason behind that"). The other is *vertical*: from narrow to open or the reverse, depending on what the situation demands. Productivity calls for conscious narrowing; creativity, rest and the recovery of perspective call for conscious opening. Moving well means doing both: first becoming conscious, then choosing the width.

Fourth, it explains why the remedy he names most often is so mundane. "Eliminating distractions and focusing on the task at hand" moves you from the bottom row to the top, usually from the lost quadrant to the efficient one, because a single concrete task is the quickest way to give scattered attention a direction. Chapter 12's daily system is built around that move.

#### Narrowing on purpose: tools, rules and frames

If conscious narrow focus is the state for work, the practical question is how to enter it. In the 2023 video that fixes the matrix, Koe answers that this is what productivity hacks are actually for. If you have work to do, narrow your focus on a specific task and use tools: set a timer, which "narrows your mind"; have a goal; create a priority task; break it into actionable steps, because knowing exactly what you are doing narrows the mind further. Some people put on headphones, a hat, close all the blinds: anything that narrows everything in so you can get into a deep flow state (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (You Can't Escape The Matrix).md`, 2023-09-05). The list moves from the inside out: time (timer), direction (goal, priority), clarity (steps), senses (headphones, hat, blinds). Each one removes a possible competitor for the workspace.

A year earlier he had framed the same idea in the language of games, which Chapter 10 develops. "In the real world, your rules are your values": on the macro scale, acting in alignment with your values and constraining attention to what is aligned with them makes life more enjoyable. On the micro scale, you can create rules: on a walk, never step on a crack, or walk 2,500 steps. And "clarity comes from eliminating environmental distractions", which means creating a **frame**: for deep work, close the browser tabs, set a Pomodoro timer, have clear steps, use noise-cancelling headphones. He used to hate walks, though he knew they were healthy; little rules turned a mundane activity into an enjoyable one (**Source:** `How I Turned My Life Into A Video Game.md`, 2022-12-11). The point of the crack rule is not the rule itself; it is that any constraint narrows attention, and narrowed, conscious attention is enjoyable. A frame is a constraint applied to the environment instead of to the mind.

**Complementary context:** the Pomodoro technique, devised by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s, structures work into timed intervals (traditionally 25 minutes) separated by short breaks. Koe's attitude to it varies across the corpus: here he recommends it as a frame for deep work; elsewhere he prefers longer blocks.

The opposite move has its own heuristic. "When in doubt, you can always bring your attention to the present moment; that will widen or open or diverge your focus." Focusing on the breath makes you more present and lets thoughts pass, "kind of like meditation." The same can happen on a walk, he says, whether you are listening intently to an audiobook or in divergent focus, with "spatial awareness of your present conscious bubble" (**Source:** `How To Create A Better Life For Yourself.md`, 2022-06-12). The passage hedges ("kind of") and its audiobook example mixes intense listening with open focus, so it should not be over-read. Its useful core is the default move: when you cannot tell what your state requires, return to the present, which reliably loosens a narrow, unconscious fixation. The **present conscious bubble** is his image for open awareness of your immediate surroundings.

#### Zooming out as a way of thinking

The zoom began as an emotional tool for anxiety. Over the following years Koe expanded it into something broader: a habit of thought that he places at the center of intelligence, decision-making and spiritual practice.

In 2024 he names the move **universal thinking**. When you are stuck in overwhelm and not in a good state to learn, you need to zoom out: "I call this universal thinking." Most people live in closed or narrow-mindedness because they are always stressed and do not know how to zoom out, rest more and "nurture that stress" to open their minds. Without doing this consciously, they are stuck in a cycle: they get stressed or anxious, focus on it and magnify it, instead of zooming out far enough to see it as "a little pixel of a larger image that is reality," a picture that "doesn't hold much weight" (**Source:** `The Fastest Way To Acquire High-Value Skills (And Finally Change Your Life).md`, 2024-02-11). He illustrates it with what he calls "macro and micro." Looking down at an ant, it is tiny; he does not really care about it (he has probably stepped on more than he would like, "not that he dislikes ants"). Or a breadcrumb next to himself, a man of six foot five: the breadcrumb seems insignificant. "Do this with your mind": because it is not physical, you can mold it; you have control. "You can zoom out, see anxiety for what it is, a small little problem, and move past it" (same source).

In 2023 he had already given the move its widest formulation: "Literally the key to life is just zooming out, expanding your focus, expanding your mind, collecting more perspective. The highest perspective is the one that includes them all." The reason he gives is about what we want from life: we do not want to get stuck scrolling social media, having meaningless sex "in more ways than physical", and winning arguments in the comments that lead nowhere; we want to act on goals, build something beneficial in reality and impact others in ways that bring enjoyment. Later in the same video he restates the idea: "the highest perspective is all perspectives, and being able to hold both of those and pull truth" (**Source:** `A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Life (In 6-12 Months).md`, 2023-07-25). The **highest perspective** is not a superior opinion but the capacity to hold several perspectives at once; this is the perspective-collecting "perspective vessel" of Chapter 3 described from the side of attention.

The step from perspective to action is a principle he calls the most important step of a 2023 framework: "Think macro, act micro, or the art of zooming out." "You are a perspective vessel, and your actions, whether you are conscious of it or not, have a ripple effect on the world." He half-remembers a "fun fact" (the butterfly effect, though his recollection that a butterfly's wings could "destroy an entire iceberg or something" is admittedly imprecise). As you open your mind to embody a holistic perspective, your actions should follow: think big picture, forget petty details, and act. And expect resistance and problems when you open your mind; become aware of them, solve them, make mistakes, because that is how you improve (**Source:** `Distraction Is The Greatest Trap Of The 21st Century (How To Avoid).md`, 2023-01-22). **Think macro, act micro** keeps the zoom's two-way character: the wide view sets direction, the narrow view executes.

**Complementary context:** the "butterfly effect" comes from the meteorologist Edward Lorenz's work on chaotic systems in the 1960s–70s, popularized by his 1972 talk asking whether the flap of a butterfly's wings in Brazil could set off a tornado in Texas. It describes sensitive dependence on initial conditions; Koe uses it loosely as an image of ripple effects.

Zooming out also governs decisions. In a 2024 side lesson, Koe says your life is determined by your decisions, past and present. You will have to do things others perceive as bad, shallow or superficial because they cannot see the big picture: "they criticize you now and praise you later." When deciding, zoom out and think through all the scenarios the decision could create. His analogies: "one stock can be down while the index fund is up"; "you can have a cheat meal and still be healthy." Most people get neurotic about the cheat meal, especially very healthy people, which makes them even less healthy than having it would. Even if the decision turns out to be a mistake, "welcome to being human": that is the only way to decide better next time. "Indecision, stagnation and not pursuing something greater is the most bad decision of them all" (**Source:** `4 Frameworks To Write Follow-Worthy Content (+ Kortex Template).md`, 2024-09-08). The stock and the index are the zoom in financial form: the part can be falling while the whole rises, and judging by the part is the zoomed-in error. The cheat-meal example adds that zooming in on a detail can harm the very whole it is supposed to protect.

#### Philosophy, spirituality and "as above, so below"

Koe connects the habit of zooming out to his reading choices. "That's why I like reading philosophy and spirituality, because it's big picture thinking": it primes your brain to zoom out and see the entire picture instead of getting hyper-focused on a technical detail that will not help. "It's like a big picture strategy where then, when you come back in, you have that feedback to know if you're moving in the right direction" (**Source:** `7 Books That Will Change Your Life In 2022.md`, 2022-04-23). The final clause is the important one: the big picture is not an escape from detail but a reference against which detail is evaluated. This anticipates the cybernetic model of section 6.4, in which a system can only correct its course if it can compare its position with a target.

The 2024 summary of *The Art of Focus* pushes the idea into explicitly metaphysical territory. He quotes the book: "When we harness the creative ability of our minds to view life from above, we can discover, collect and harness profound insights that bring us peace and progress below." Your life feels meaningless, he says, because you are not grounded in the present moment. This is not about organized religion or a belief system; it is "tapping into the highest perspective there is to become more creative than you ever have." The argument is a chain: creativity is the key to becoming irreplaceable; creativity depends on the openness and expansion of your mind; therefore understanding the universe as natural law puts you ahead of 99% of people. "As above, so below" (**Source:** `The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md`, 2024-01-18).

**Complementary context:** "as above, so below" is a paraphrase of a line from the *Emerald Tablet*, a text of the Hermetic tradition, and is presented as the "principle of correspondence" in *The Kybalion* (1908), which Koe recommends elsewhere. In its original setting it expresses a correspondence between the cosmic and the human levels; Koe uses it as a figure for the relation between big-picture understanding and everyday action.

The corpus records a tension around this recommendation. In the same period Koe recommends books on metaphysics and Hermetic philosophy, and elsewhere criticizes people who study "this esoteric crap" instead of fixing what is in front of them. The two positions are not contradictory if the target of the criticism is evasion: big-picture study serves someone already acting, who uses it to orient the next step, and harms someone who uses it to avoid acting. That is the same condition the zoom has carried all along: you zoom out in order to zoom back in.

#### Multiple whys: zooming out on other people

One application of the zoom concerns how we interpret other people's actions. "Why do you go to the gym and eat healthy foods? To be healthy, yes, but there's more": you want to increase your perceived status so you can attract better opportunities, advance your career and make more money. "Why am I speaking to a camera right now? To help you out, yes, but there is always more than one reason for someone to do something." On social media people say "you're only saying this because of this." His example: a bodybuilder posts a photo of his physique and commenters say "that was only possible because of steroids." "You can instantly filter out high and low consciousness people in the comments," because the low ones "can't zoom out" to see that "it actually took some work and effort and more discipline than that person has ever had in their life." Later in the same video he generalizes: "reasoning can't be reduced to a singular why"; "there is always something deeper and unconscious pushing us"; "your reasoning behind your actions becomes more holistic as you expand and transcend to new levels of mind" (**Source:** `People Dumber Than You Are Making Millions.md`, 2023-11-12).

**Multiple whys** is his name for the principle. It is the zoom applied to motive: reducing someone's action to a single cause is the interpersonal equivalent of fixating on one pixel. The link to the levels of mind of Chapter 4 is explicit: the more developed the mind, the more causes it can hold at once, both in others and in oneself.

#### Struggle is universal: zooming out to find a vision

In a 2023 video for people who feel lost, Koe presents zooming out as the way through. "Zoom out, or expand your focus, or just realize that struggle is a universal principle and that everyone experiences it; it's not only happening to you." Humans have a similar psychology and experience the same emotions; "that's how we relate to each other." "Everyone is going through a different phase, a different chapter, a different book, a different sub-book." Once you see that and have "that higher consciousness" (he mentions, while saying he does not want to "bring religion into this", Jesus Christ as "a high consciousness individual, like Buddha and other leaders"), with "that level and that empathy", and try to sustain it through your days, things become easier and you are less stressed, and "you allow ideas to culminate, you allow inklings to kind of stack up into this vision that you can go and pursue and lead people towards and create in alignment with" (**Source:** `If You’re Lost - Here’s How You Find Clarity For The Future.md`, 2023-06-04).

This passage adds something new to the zoom: it is not only a way to shrink problems but a way to *generate direction*. The open, conscious state allows faint intuitions ("inklings") to accumulate until they form a vision. That is the lion's "let vision accumulate" from section 6.2, and it is the reason the open quadrant of the matrix is called creative. Chapter 7 returns to the idea that a vision is often discovered by accumulation rather than decided.

In the same video he uses a vertical image to explain why people cannot see their own mistakes. "If you're consistently digging yourself down and going lower levels into unconsciousness, then it's much more difficult to look back up and see, okay, these are the mistakes I made." "Instead you have to climb your way up, and then you can see from the top down: oh, these are the mistakes I made." He calls it "a massive problem": so many people are living on **mindless autopilot**, "surface level living", "digging themselves deeper and deeper", and it is much harder to get out (**Source:** `If You’re Lost - Here’s How You Find Clarity For The Future.md`, 2023-06-04). The climb is the zoom turned into altitude: perspective is a height from which errors become visible, and unconsciousness is a hole from which they do not. This is the bridge to the next section, where seeing one's errors becomes the definition of intelligence.

#### Why zooming out needs structure

The last piece comes from a guest, and it explains why all of this does not happen naturally. In a 2025 conversation, Sahil Bloom recounts that his practice of a structured "life dinner" with his wife went viral and drew detractors: "you shouldn't have to create a structure to talk about things in your relationship. Your relationships are going to die if you need to create a structure." His reply: if you do this naturally after kids and marriage, "I am so happy for you. But I would guess that that is not true." The reason: "you don't do this with yourself. How often do you take time on a daily basis in your own life to zoom out and think about the bigger picture? No one does that. So why can we expect that we'll do that naturally in our relationship?" Conversations "end up being the short-term daily things, because that's the life you're living in, the weeds. You're living first person view in your video game. This is all about zooming out, seeing the bigger field, seeing the forest, so that you can actually strategically make sure that you're walking in the direction of the place where you're trying to go." Koe adds his own view: he loves routines and rituals because they "break out of the normal day to day," and systemizing something that is "supposed to be authentic" can be "an incredible tool to make things more authentic" (**Source:** `The Time Billionaire (A Concept That Will Change Your Life) With Sahil Bloom.md`, 2025-01-30).

**First person view** is Sahil Bloom's phrase, and it fits Koe's video-game metaphor (Chapter 10) exactly: in a first-person game you see only what is in front of you, and the map is a separate screen you have to open deliberately. The point generalizes the whole section. Zooming in is the default because daily life is lived in the weeds; zooming out is a counter-default that requires a ritual, a structure, a practice. Without one, the narrow, unconscious quadrant wins by default, which is exactly what the entropy model of Chapter 5 would predict.

### 6.4 Cybernetics: Intelligence as Steering

The first three sections described attention: its scarcity, its root status, its width. They left implicit what attention is *for*. This section supplies the answer through a model Koe adopts from cybernetics and develops between 2024 and 2026: the mind is a steering system that pursues a target by acting, sensing where it is, comparing that with where it wants to be and correcting. On this view, intelligence is not a stock of knowledge but the quality of that steering, and learning is not the intake of information but the reduction of error relative to a goal. The model gives the rest of the chapter its spine. It explains why attention needs a target (a steering system without one has nothing to compare against), why errors are information (they are the signal the system steers by), and why the goal, examined in section 6.5, is the most consequential variable of all.

#### "The only real test of intelligence"

Koe's 2024 video on intelligence opens with a definition he later attributes to Naval Ravikant: "the only real test of intelligence is if you get what you want out of life." He says there is a science to intelligence, but most people get it wrong because they think it is about book smarts and performing cognitive tasks; those play a major role in success, "but there is much more to the story" (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03). In a 2025 video he returns to it as "the classic viral quote from Naval" and builds both his success formula and his explanation of cybernetics on it (**Source:** `How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md`, 2025-12-28).

The definition is pragmatic and outcome-based. It does not ask how much you know or how fast you reason, but whether your life moves toward what you want. That makes it a natural bridge to cybernetics, because cybernetics is precisely the study of systems that reach targets. Koe states the bridge directly: if intelligence is getting what you want out of life, then cybernetics is "the science, or the way that you can study, in order to do that" (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03).

#### The word and the frame

He introduces the term through its etymology. "Cyber comes from the Greek word *kybernetes*" (the transcript garbles it), "which means to steer, or good at steering." Cybernetics "illustrates the properties of an intelligent system; it measures the intelligence of a system." From this perspective, intelligence is "your ability to steer or correct your steering toward a goal." "Cybernetics is also known as the art of getting what you want." And it is not a technical specialty he expects you to study: "cybernetics is a frame, it's a worldview, it's a perspective that you can adopt to understand quite a bit of the world" (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03). In the 2025 restatement he distinguishes the two levels of the frame: for humans, cybernetics is "the art of getting what you want"; for a machine, it describes an intelligent system that course-corrects toward a goal. He adds, with explicit uncertainty, that cybernetics was the original word for AI, but that someone (he does not remember who) disliked it and renamed the field "artificial intelligence"; if Naval's definition is right, cybernetics helps you get what you want faster (**Source:** `How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md`, 2025-12-28). In 2026 he gives the Greek again, now as *kubernetes*, "steersman or helmsman" (**Source:** `How To Learn Anything 10x Faster Than Anyone.md`, 2026-08-09).

**Cybernetics**, then, is a third-party term that Koe adapts in two directions: as a worldview for life (the "art of getting what you want") and as a criterion of intelligence. **The art of getting what you want** is presented as an established alternative name for the field; the corpus does not say where it comes from.

**Complementary context:** cybernetics was founded as a field by the mathematician Norbert Wiener, whose 1948 book *Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine* took its name from the Greek *kybernētēs*, "steersman". It studies feedback and control in machines, organisms and organizations; negative feedback (a signal that reduces the gap between actual and desired state) is its central concept. The term "artificial intelligence" was coined by John McCarthy for the 1956 Dartmouth workshop; McCarthy later said that one reason for choosing a new name was to avoid having the work framed as cybernetics. The two fields developed largely separately, so Koe's recollection that one "renamed" the other captures a real historical connection only loosely. Chapter 3 introduced the psychological application of the field through Maxwell Maltz's *Psycho-Cybernetics*.

#### The cybernetic loop

The core of the model is a loop, which Koe presents in 2024 with a graphic of a boat steering left and right to reach a lighthouse. Cybernetics "is pretty much to have a goal, to act toward that goal, to sense where you are, to compare to the goal, and act again based on that feedback." It is "listening to the feedback mechanism of making mistakes, or just getting blown off course, say, by life." He gives three examples of such systems: a ship blown off course that corrects toward its destination; a thermostat that senses a change in temperature and switches on; the pancreas, which secretes insulin after blood glucose spikes (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03; `How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md`, 2025-12-28). The graphic itself cannot be reconstructed from the transcript beyond this description.

Then he applies the loop to the mind. Since your mind is "a system that houses many complex systems," it is "the meta system from which you operate and act within life; it's the operating system for life." In practice: have a goal, act, notice "I'm over here and this is where the goal is," and ask what you can do to move toward it "rather than continue going in the wrong direction like most people." Most people set the goal, head off in one direction, and then quit or end up trapped: "that is unintelligent." "Acting, sensing, comparing, and understanding the system from a meta perspective is fundamental to high intelligence" (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03). **Meta system** here means the mind as the system that contains and runs all the others; in a different 2023 usage the same phrase named society as the system that captures attention when you adopt its goals. The two senses should not be confused.

To illustrate the opposite of a steering system, he introduces a famous line: "The definition of insanity is doing the same thing over and over and expecting a different result." He notes that it is attributed to Einstein "if he actually said it", since Einstein "is notorious for having quotes assigned to him", which does not take away from how smart and revolutionary Einstein was. He uses it because "it reflects cybernetics": repeating the same action without correcting it in response to feedback is the definition of a system that does not steer (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03).

**Complementary context:** there is no evidence that Einstein said this; the earliest known appearances of the saying are in 1980s recovery literature (it appears in a Narcotics Anonymous text in 1981). Koe's own doubt about the attribution is well placed.

The loop can be laid out with the vocabulary of the whole chapter:

| Step of the loop | What it requires | Where it appears in this chapter |
|---|---|---|
| Have a goal | A target state, chosen or assigned | Section 6.5 (the goal as lens) |
| Act toward it | Directed attention turned into behavior | Section 6.2 (focus as root control) |
| Sense where you are | Perception of the current state | Section 6.3 (zoom, the matrix as a reading of one's state) |
| Compare with the goal | Seeing the gap from a "meta perspective" | Section 6.3 (zooming out to see mistakes from above) |
| Act again on feedback | Correction instead of repetition or quitting | This section (iteration and persistence) |

#### The 2026 refinement: four named components

In 2026 Koe returns to the loop in a video on learning and gives it a more formal anatomy. "Learning is a cybernetic process." The **helmsman** is "the perfect image of a feedback system. The helmsman doesn't just point the ship in a direction once; he constantly reads the current state, like the wind, the waves or the heading, and then he compares it against the intended course, and adjusts." That is trial and error, and "so few people have the direction that allows them to notice errors in their life." Then he names the components of intelligent systems (**Source:** `How To Learn Anything 10x Faster Than Anyone.md`, 2026-08-09):

| Component | Definition in Koe's words | In a human life |
|---|---|---|
| **Reference signal** | "The goal, the target state" | The goal you hold |
| **Sensor** (perception) | Reads the current state | What you notice about where you are |
| **Comparator** | Computes the gap between target and current state; "the difference is the **error signal**" | Recognizing a problem or a mistake |
| **Actuator** (behavior) | Acts to reduce the error | What you do next |

"Cybernetics is more of a frame of how to view reality, to course correct in a better direction." His examples recur: the thermostat that turns on the heat when it is too cold ("not a very intelligent system"), the pancreas that secretes insulin when blood sugar rises; "your body is one system composed of so many different intelligent systems."

The refinement is not just a relabeling. Naming the comparator and the error signal makes visible something the 2024 version left implicit: a mistake is not a failure of the system but its *input*. Without a reference signal there is no gap; without a gap there is no error signal; without an error signal the actuator has nothing to correct. The sentence "so few people have the direction that allows them to notice errors" states the consequence. People without a goal do not make fewer mistakes; they simply cannot see them as mistakes. This idea becomes the "retention chain" below and the thesis of section 6.6, that problems do not exist without goals.

#### High and low intelligence

From the loop Koe derives a definition of intelligence that he repeats, with small variations, from 2024 to 2026. "You can judge intelligence based on the system's ability to iterate and persist with trial and error." "High intelligence is the ability to iterate, persist and understand the big picture." "The mark of low intelligence is the inability to learn from your mistakes": low-intelligence people get stuck on problems rather than solving them; they hit a roadblock and quit, like a writer who fails to build a readership and quits because they cannot try new things, experiment and figure out a process that works. To think there is no effective process you can create "is verifiably false"; such beliefs are limiting beliefs, "hence being low intelligence." There is a path to your goals; you just are not aware of it, because "you haven't taken the step that reveals the next step, and then the next step," and when you take a wrong step you lack the awareness to course-correct (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03). The 2025 version repeats the definition nearly word for word and previews a future video on why artists hate AI, which in his observation points to the same definition (**Source:** `How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md`, 2025-12-28).

**High intelligence** and **low intelligence** are therefore defined by behavior in the face of feedback, not by capacity. Three components are named: *iterate* (try again differently), *persist* (do not quit at the roadblock) and *understand the big picture* (have the meta perspective needed to compare). The phrase "the step that reveals the next step" is a compact statement of why acting precedes clarity: the path is not hidden because it is unknowable but because it only becomes visible from positions you have not yet occupied. Chapter 7 develops this idea into the claim that you will never know exactly what you want in advance.

A month before the 2024 video, he had stated the same principle as the "arguably most important" step of a framework for focus: if you understand cybernetics, intelligence or systems, you need to iterate and improve with feedback; "that is the main sign of high intelligence." Low-intelligence people and systems hit negative feedback or roadblocks and do not progress, or repeat the same thing without results because they are not clear on their goal, and so they quit or get stuck. "High intelligence is defined by your ability to learn; it's your ability to achieve any goal on a long enough time scale, because you understand that achieving any goal is possible if you work towards it" (**Source:** `It's actually pretty easy to focus 12 hours a day (if you do this).md`, 2024-10-13). Note the diagnosis embedded in the description of failure: the system repeats itself "because it is not clear on its goal". In cybernetic terms, an unclear reference signal produces a noisy error signal, and a noisy error signal cannot guide correction.

In 2025 he extends the claim. "You can achieve any goal you set your mind to" cannot be disproven within reason. Intelligence is realizing that there is a series of choices leading to the goal, and that "ideas are hierarchical": "you can't go from papyrus to Google Docs in one leap." Even if a goal is impossible now, you may lack resources that will be invented in a few years; "any problem can be solved on a large enough time scale." And "most of your excuses for not achieving a goal are a sign of low intelligence," because you have more than enough resources: you can buy ChatGPT for $20, or Claude for $20, which he prefers (**Source:** `How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md`, 2025-12-28).

These statements should be read for what they are. "Any goal on a long enough time scale" and "any problem can be solved" are motivational principles framed as near-certainties; the qualifiers ("within reason", "on a long enough time scale", "within the laws of physics", below) carry much of the weight. What they assert, defensibly, is a stance: treat the absence of a known path as a property of your current position, not of the goal. What they do not establish is that every goal is achievable by every person, and the author does not claim to have shown that. The phrase "ideas are hierarchical" has a precise and useful meaning, though: progress is cumulative, each step builds on the previous one, and a goal that looks impossible may simply be several steps away.

#### Trial and error: the first rocket and the toddler

Koe's favorite argument for the power of trial and error is historical. How did people figure out the very first thing, before the how-to guides and step-by-step processes people are now obsessed with? ("Give me the step-by-step bullet points and I'll do it. No, you won't.") Was there a guide for the first rocket? "Through the process of trial and error, you can do anything within the laws of physics." Successful people tried and failed, did not let failure convince them the thing was impossible or disorient them into quick pleasure; they set a new direction from reality's feedback and found the needle in the haystack. "The mark of an intelligent system is that they course correct according to feedback. They have a lighthouse and they don't quit when they get blown off course." Entrepreneurship, for him, means engaging in your natural state, the process of creation, "pursuing unknown goals that demand failure to achieve", the single trait of highly successful individuals, for whom failure is constant, necessary and daily (**Source:** `How To Become So Valuable AI Can Never Replace You.md`, 2026-06-28). The **lighthouse**, the goal toward which an intelligent system corrects its course, links the 2024 graphic to this 2026 argument.

A 2025 passage, in a video about which human capabilities remain once AI can do much of the work, places humans in the same frame. One capability he calls *selection*: we can come up with any idea, but can we find the good ones? Without learning from mistakes it is hard to make cumulative progress; it "wouldn't be fun to start over from scratch if we wanted to build an electric car after a gas car." "As universal cybernetic systems, that's what humans are, we can become more efficient at navigating idea space to avoid wandering lost. We error correct. We make mistakes and fix them." He adds that there is no fundamental difference with artificial general intelligence on this point (**Source:** `The Most Important Skill To Learn Right Now.md`, 2025-12-21). **Universal cybernetic systems** is presented as his phrase, though the corpus notes that the passage may draw on an unnamed source. Its point is that error correction is what makes progress *cumulative*: a system that keeps its corrections does not restart from zero.

The most intimate illustration is childhood. As a child you had biological goals: walking, talking, speaking, in order to survive. Simple now, they were not always. Your mind received negative feedback from its environment (parents scolding you, guiding you, teaching you to walk by doing it themselves), which made the systems in your mind more efficient over time. You went from crawling, to stumbling, to walking like a toddler with little balance, to, hopefully, walking well; an Olympic gold medalist can flip through the air and stick the landing because they practiced achieving that goal long enough (**Source:** `You Won’t Be The Same Person In 6 Months (Master Anything, Fast).md`, 2023-12-17). The argument has a sting that should not be missed: any goal plus practice plus feedback produces an efficient system, and that is equally true of mediocre goals. The machinery that taught you to walk will just as efficiently teach you to scroll, to avoid risk or to stay on the default path, if those are the goals it is given. This is the neutrality of Maltz's "success mechanism or failure mechanism" (Chapter 3) restated in cybernetic terms, and it is why the choice of goal, not the effort of pursuit, is the decisive variable.

#### Learning as a cybernetic output process

The 2026 video draws the most practical consequence of the model. "When most people try to learn something new, they focus on content. They hoard material and try to absorb it with spaced repetition or flash cards or courses and learning the fundamentals. They assume learning is an input process." He is careful not to dismiss these tools: "I love courses. I take them all the time." "But in reality, learning is an output process, because output demands input" (**Source:** `How To Learn Anything 10x Faster Than Anyone.md`, 2026-08-09).

**Learning is an output process** is his coinage, and the cybernetic frame explains why it is not a paradox. Output (building, writing, attempting) is the actuator acting toward a reference signal. Acting produces a gap between what you wanted and what you got; that gap is an error signal; and the error signal specifies exactly what input you now need. Input chosen this way is relevant by construction. Input absorbed without output has no error signal to attach to, which is why, in his view, it does not stick. The corpus records that he had been circling this idea for years under different names (intention plus execution, "become a builder", the Clarity Catalyst, "learn as you build"); the 2026 version keeps the core (a project, learning when you hit problems, teaching) and changes the theoretical frame from dopamine and novelty to cybernetics. Chapter 14 tells that story.

The same video explains why most learning fails, with an example that makes the error signal tangible. "The reason that most learning fails is because there's no negative feedback loop." If you do not have the goal of getting in shape, "a deep, intrinsically generated goal, not a superficial goal you're pursuing to fit in, like a New Year's resolution", then a night of heavy drinking "won't register as a problem or negative feedback, because it doesn't impact your non-existent goal of performing on your morning run or gym session." "You can go to work just fine with a hangover because you don't give a [damn] about how well you do at your job." The principle applies to business, relationships and health. With a goal like a bench press record or a marathon, drinking would affect it; if the goal really means a lot to you and you drink anyway (he says he is "not completely against" drinking), training suffers and you reach your goals slower or never, and eventually you realize "I need to stop doing this." "That's called learning" (**Source:** `How To Learn Anything 10x Faster Than Anyone.md`, 2026-08-09).

The **negative feedback loop** in this sense is the mechanism by which an error registers as a problem, and it only exists relative to a goal deep enough to be affected. From this he builds what he presents as a chain: "A goal creates the error signal. The error signal creates the filter. The filter creates relevance (so whether it's useful, because most people fill their mind with noise and things they will never use), and then the relevance creates retention" (**Source:** `How To Learn Anything 10x Faster Than Anyone.md`, 2026-08-09).

| Link | What it produces | What happens if it is missing |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | The error signal (a gap between target and state) | No error to detect |
| Error signal | A filter (what matters now) | Nothing distinguishes important from unimportant |
| Filter | Relevance (whether information is useful) | The mind fills with noise "you will never use" |
| Relevance | Retention | Nothing is kept |

The **retention chain** is the 2026 formalization of an idea that section 6.5 traces back to 2022: the goal as a filter for information. What the chain adds is causal order: retention is not a property of memory technique but the last link of a chain that starts with a goal.

He closes the circle with a critique of memorization, which opens the same video. "Why would you want to memorize every word of a book in the first place? I know why. You want to sound smart. It's an unconscious status symbol for you. You want to impress others with your articulation and intelligence, but you don't realize that sounding smart comes from understanding, not memorization. And understanding requires a much different approach to learning." Schools tested your ability to memorize, hours of study to pencil in the right answer, "when that's not how life works at all." Everyone wants knowledge in order to make better decisions and reach a more advantageous position with money and power so they do not have to worry about daily survival, "but memorizing facts is the least effective way to get there" (**Source:** `How To Learn Anything 10x Faster Than Anyone.md`, 2026-08-09). The critique of school here connects to Chapter 2; the alternative, understanding, is the subject of the definitions of intelligence below.

#### Learning as you move: Devon Eriksen

Two years earlier, a conversation with the novelist Devon Eriksen gave the cybernetic view of learning its strongest argument, from a guest. Asked whether it is possible to know what you need to learn, Eriksen answers that "the further into the future you have to look, the harder it becomes to get everything right." He might know what he needs to learn in the next two months, have some idea for the next two years, and have no idea for the next twenty: "I'm going to find that out as I learn what I need to learn in the next two years." So deciding at age five, in kindergarten, what a child needs to learn will be wrong, "because the world is going to change." "In order to follow a moving target, you have to have a feedback loop." The way to structure learning is not "I make the best plan at the outset" ("my plan sucks, because I don't know anything yet") but "I need to get moving and learn as I go." What we should cultivate in children and call education is "the tendency to move and the ability to learn as we move." Koe adds a phrase from investing: "time in the market beats timing the market" (**Source:** `The Most Important Skill To Learn In The Next 10 Years With Devon Eriksen.md`, 2024-12-08).

Eriksen continues with a warning about expectations. You are not going to make a million dollars in your first year as an online content creator; you are not going to have the most popular show on YouTube; you are not going to write a book, "no matter how good it is", that sells a million copies in its first year when no one has heard of you. "You just have to get going, you just have to move forward and make your mistakes and learn as you go, and agency is the willingness to do that" (same source).

**Moving target** and **learn as you move** are Eriksen's phrases, and his definition of **agency** as the willingness to move, err and learn along the way is one of several in the corpus (Chapter 35 collects them). In cybernetic terms, his argument is that when the reference signal itself moves (because the world changes and because your own goals evolve), no plan made in advance can substitute for a loop. The plan is a guess; the loop is what corrects the guess. Koe's own model absorbs this point: section 6.5 will show him insisting that goals are "points of view that must be refined on a consistent basis."

#### What intelligence is beyond book smarts

The pragmatic definition (getting what you want, iterating, persisting) is not the only one in the corpus. In the same years Koe offers a cognitive and holistic definition, and the author presents both together without ranking them.

The cognitive one appears in a 2024 video on reading. "Intelligence is not about how much you know, it's about how much you understand." He quotes Naval again: "if you understand it you don't need to memorize it; if you don't understand it you don't want to memorize it." "Intelligence is holistic": someone very intelligent has "a higher ability for holistic pattern recognition"; they make sense of the world by connecting the dots and have "a deep sense of insight that leads to intuition", which lets them make intelligent decisions (**Source:** `This Simple Reading Habit Will Change Your Life (I Promise).md`, 2024-08-04). In the same passage he names what intelligence is not. "Book smarts are often used as a disguise to overanalyze risk and not act on anything": not pursuing a new goal, not diving into the unknown, "that is quite dumb." You become narrow-minded about the risk, focus on it and talk only about it, and "program yourself into a stupid identity" (same source). **Book smarts**, in his sense, is academic intelligence used as a defense against action. The zoom of section 6.3 explains the mechanism: overanalysis is a narrow, unconscious fixation on risk, and the identity it programs is the one that never acts.

He then adds a principle that links intelligence to the generalism of Chapter 20. "Intelligence is not getting trapped in parts, but swimming in wholes." Someone who studied a popular business strategy may be effective at it, but the person with a deep understanding of business as a whole "will be able to create their own strategy and sustain their success regardless of how the industry changes." "Intelligence stems from generalism, not specialism": being a specialist often puts you in direct competition with other specialists, and the specialist's frame prevents learning in connected domains, which is what allows creativity. "Intelligence is holistic, creative and metaphysical", and so the books worth reading are those that open the mind: on holism, creativity and metaphysics (**Source:** `This Simple Reading Habit Will Change Your Life (I Promise).md`, 2024-08-04). **Parts versus wholes** is his formula; its relation to the specialist–generalist debate, in which his position oscillates and changes name over the years ("specialized generalism", "generalized specialists", "specialized generalist"), is treated in Chapter 20.

The 2024 video on intelligence uses a camera image borrowed from Chris Langan, whom Koe introduces as "the man with the highest IQ": "most high IQ people have the magnification but not the aperture." Koe's gloss: having a high IQ is not the be-all and end-all of intelligence; "if your mind is a camera, there is more than the ability to zoom in" (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03). **Magnification** and **aperture** map exactly onto the zoom of section 6.3: magnification is the capacity to zoom in on detail; aperture is the width of what the mind lets in. The claim is that intelligence requires both, and that raw cognitive power supplies only the first.

**Complementary context:** Chris Langan is an American autodidact who became known in the 1990s through media reports of extremely high scores on IQ tests, and who developed a speculative "Cognitive-Theoretic Model of the Universe". Koe cites only the camera remark.

Later in the same video he gives two more formulations. One: "Intelligence is questioning your beliefs, realizing deep generalized patterns, and realizing that all is relative except for those deep generalized patterns that can be universally applied" (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03). **Deep generalized patterns** appear only here; they are the principles that survive the relativism of particular perspectives, which is why the generalist who sees across domains can find them. The other is his recap of the whole video: "Intelligence isn't about book smarts or magnification, it's about expanding your mind towards truth, it's about holistic pattern recognition and your ability to zoom out so far that all boundaries and limits are dissolved. Through conscious development, and having the awareness of when you're closing yourself off to growth and getting trapped in any stage, you can reach the unitive stage" (same source). The "unitive stage" refers to the last stage of ego development in Susanne Cook-Greuter's model, which Chapter 38 presents; he adds that *The Art of Focus* covers his steps for moving through most of the stages.

A 2023 metaphor gives the holistic definition its most concrete image. Reductionist science throws a frog in a blender and observes its parts, losing the holistic nature of the frog and limiting its discoveries, instead of observing the frog in its environment and how it moves (even an artificial environment lacks other frogs, travel, its own behavior). The alternative is to take the universe into account "like a cone coming down from the sky": "truth is this big picture narrowed in on one small aspect of reality," making discoveries "like a spotlight." Koe says explicitly that this is metaphorical, and the **cone** recurs in his work: creativity requires a high level of understanding focused on specific problems "like the cone", and "big irrational goals followed by small rational steps" also follows "the cone shape that dictates truth" (**Source:** `The Cure To A Mediocre Life (Become Multidimensionally Jacked).md`, 2023-08-30). The cone reconciles the two widths of the Focus Matrix in a single shape: a wide base of understanding narrowing to a point of application. It is "think macro, act micro" drawn as geometry.

How do the two families of definitions relate? The corpus registers them as coexisting, not as a replacement: the pragmatic one (getting what you want, iterating, persisting) and the holistic one (understanding, pattern recognition, zooming out until boundaries dissolve) appear in the same video and are not ranked. A guest disagreed with both: Devon Eriksen holds that IQ-type intelligence is biological and cannot be increased, and he notes that Koe disagrees because he defines it "more broadly"; in that conversation both agree that agency, not intelligence, is the real bottleneck (Chapter 35). In 2025 a third formula appears, "taste is the new intelligence" (Chapter 18). But the agreement with Eriksen is not the author's last word on the weight of intelligence. In December 2025 Koe gives a success formula with three ingredients: agency, opportunity ("which many people like to mistake as privilege because they lack the other ingredients") and intelligence. Each limits the others. With high agency and low opportunity, "it doesn't matter how likely you are to act", the goal will not bear much fruit; with opportunity and agency but low intelligence, "you'll never fully benefit" (**Source:** `How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md`, 2025-12-28). Here intelligence is not demoted behind agency but kept as an ingredient in its own right, and, given Koe's broad definition, one that can be expanded. The tension stays unresolved in the corpus: Eriksen's intelligence is fixed and secondary; Koe's is a separate requirement that can grow. The two positions can coexist only if Eriksen's narrow, IQ-type intelligence is distinguished from Koe's broad one, and the author does not draw that line himself. (The three-ingredient formula is developed in Chapter 35.) The cybernetic model offers one way to see how the first two fit together. The pragmatic definition describes the loop's *performance*: does the system reach its targets? The holistic definition describes the quality of its *comparator*: how much of reality can it take in when it measures the gap? A system with magnification but no aperture compares its position against a narrow slice of the world; a system that can zoom out sees the gap in context, which is "understanding the big picture", the third element of the pragmatic definition. On this reading, the two definitions are two aspects of good steering, but the reconciliation is this book's, not the author's.

Finally, Koe warns against expecting intelligence to arrive quickly. To reach "the articulation of Jordan Peterson or the thinking ability of Alan Watts," you have to commit: "you aren't just going to watch this video and see five jumps and stages; it takes years to get through the stages of development." You need a practice: "you need to study an hour a day, you need to build something and apply what you learn." That is not much to ask for a trait that "determines much of the enjoyment and success in your life": navigating your mental health, making as much money as you want, understanding reality and getting what you want out of it. Intelligence, he adds, "can't be summarized in a 1 to 5 minute YouTube video" (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03). The practice he prescribes is the loop itself: study is the input, building is the output that generates error signals, and application is the correction.

#### Reality as harsh mentor

The cybernetic model has a consequence for how a life should be arranged, which Koe states in 2026 as "the most esoteric" and yet "the single most important thing": choose a **vessel** (the medium through which you act: a job, a business, a craft) whose feedback is as close to reality as possible. "The most dangerous lifestyle is one removed from continuous trial and error." Being removed from error correction removes you from challenge, discovery and hard-earned wisdom, which are what lead to growth and fulfillment. This applies to jobs, where challenge normalizes, but also to entrepreneurs who carry over the **employee mindset**, always needing to be told what to do or a handbook to feel confident. Many entrepreneurs, he says, are not actually engaged in trial and error, "this very enjoyable process that truly leads to success" (**Source:** `How To Become So Valuable AI Can Never Replace You.md`, 2026-06-28).

In cybernetic terms the argument is straightforward. A system learns at the rate at which it receives error signals. A job with fixed procedures and delayed, filtered feedback (a manager's annual review) starves the loop; the critique of employment in Chapter 2 becomes, here, a critique of feedback latency. And the employee mindset is a disposition that survives the change of vessel: someone who waits for instructions has replaced the comparator with an external authority.

A week later he states the positive version. You can read every persuasion book and psychology textbook and think you know why humans act as they do, "but if you have not tested that knowledge against reality, then you do not know what you're talking about." You need feedback: put something out and see how people respond; write, speak, create a product and sell it. "Entrepreneurship equals self-improvement because the market and reality become your harsh mentor." That is why he recommends writing: "writing is clear thinking" (you refine and clarify your thinking on the page), and there is "a dual mechanism": when you post what you have written you get direct feedback on its value, and since to be valuable it must be attention-grabbing and persuasive, you practice and refine the skill of understanding human nature (**Source:** `Learn This Skill If You Want To Win In The Next 2-3 Years.md`, 2026-07-05). The **harsh mentor** is the market understood as an error signal that cannot be argued with. Knowledge that has never met it is, in this frame, untested hypothesis.

The most radical formulation comes in the same 2026 period, in the context of surviving mass replacement by AI. "That's how you survive mass replacement: you build your own thing and you commit to it and you accept no other option." How do you accept no other option? "You just accept trial and error as your god. Reality as your god, because that's what God is, is reality." He acknowledges that the phrase will be "extremely misinterpreted" (**Source:** `How To Become So Valuable AI Can Never Replace You.md`, 2026-06-28). **Trial and error as your god** is a deliberately provocative coinage. Its meaning, consistent with everything above, is that the final authority over what is true, what works and what to do next should be the feedback of reality, not a guide, a plan, an expert or a hope. The theological wording is his; it is best read as a statement about ultimate authority in decision-making, not as a theological claim, though the author does not clarify further.

The "commit and accept no other option" half of the statement is in tension with an earlier strand of the corpus. In 2021, a guest (Justin Welsh, following Daniel Vassallo) defended many small bets over going all in, and Koe partly agreed. From 2025 he speaks of "concentration of force" as the only way to get rich and of giving yourself no other option. The tension is not resolved explicitly; the two positions can apply to different people (an operator running several small businesses versus someone trying to build one thing that survives replacement). What the cybernetic model adds is that commitment and trial and error are not opposites: committing to one vessel is what keeps the loop running long enough for its corrections to accumulate. Quitting is the one move that guarantees the error signal is never used.

### 6.5 The Goal as a Lens

Section 6.4 established that the mind steers toward a reference signal and that everything else in the loop (what is perceived, what counts as error, what is learned) depends on it. This section examines the reference signal itself, and it is the idea the corpus returns to more often than any other in this chapter. Koe's claim is that a goal is less a destination to be reached than a lens through which everything is perceived. It determines which information registers, which opportunities become visible, which passages of a book you underline, which problems you notice and therefore what you learn and who you become. Chapter 3 presented a first version of this from the side of identity: the homing mechanism, the heat-seeking missile, two people reading "two entirely different books". Here the idea is followed through all its layers: what a goal is, how it frames perception, why it should be focused on rather than the problem, how it filters information and learning, and how it can be used deliberately.

#### Everything you do is goal-directed

The starting point is a claim about behavior, stated in several forms. In 2023: everything you do, physically and mentally, is goal-directed; "if you are not absolutely at one with the present moment, then it is goal directed" (**Source:** `The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md`, 2023-03-12). Later that year: "At any given moment we are acting towards a goal, whether you're conscious of it or not." Most people act "towards the unconscious goals that society assigns to them, like going to school, getting a job, retiring at 65," marrying someone they do not enjoy because they are supposed to, or staying a doctor because it is expected. "Even right now I'm clearly pursuing a goal of making a YouTube video"; "unless you're doing absolutely nothing, you're pursuing a goal" (**Source:** `A Dangerous (But Effective) Strategy To Get Out Of A Rut.md`, 2023-10-02). In 2024, the claim reaches the smallest movements: goals are "the conscious or unconscious goals that influence every single action you take, like taking one step forward or going to the gym." Literally taking a step: "you are acting towards a goal. You are moving in a specific direction. Everything you do is associated with some goal. It's just unconscious. Self-generated goals put you in control of your life." He paraphrases, with some uncertainty, a line attributed to Carl Jung, that if you do not make the unconscious conscious it will determine your fate and control your life, and adds: "But it's just presence, mindful living, becoming conscious" (**Source:** `Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md`, 2024-08-25).

**Complementary context:** the line Koe paraphrases is usually quoted as "Until you make the unconscious conscious, it will direct your life and you will call it fate." It is widely attributed to Carl Jung, though it is a popular condensation rather than a verbatim sentence from his works.

This is the teleological thesis of Chapter 3 (behavior is pulled by goals, not only pushed by the past), now stated as a premise for what follows. Two features of the formulation matter here. First, the single exception: being "absolutely at one with the present moment". Presence is the only state the author describes as not goal-directed, which is why the open, conscious quadrant of the Focus Matrix and the spiritual practices of Chapter 40 occupy a special place in his system. Second, the alternative is never "no goal" but "an unconscious goal": the choice is not whether to pursue goals but whose. From this premise the 2023 passage draws the first psychological effect of a conscious goal: it "creates a frame of reference". Your goal "has to stay top of mind the entire time", and a conscious goal gives you a frame from which to "filter signal from noise" and the important information. In that video the frame is part of what Koe calls tactical stress (launching yourself into the unknown "out of the pursuit of a goal that is in the unknown"), which Chapter 10 treats in full.

From the same premise Koe derives a long-term project. Since every goal you pursue teaches you something, "make it your life's work to create a holistic library of knowledge for everything you are doing in life." Your goals keep evolving, you keep moving forward, and you pass down the lessons learned: ideas, beliefs, opinions, actionable advice. He guarantees that many of your favorite creators do this, even unconsciously, and that it will make them stand out for years (**Source:** `The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md`, 2023-03-12). The **holistic library of knowledge** is the record a goal-directed life leaves behind, and it anticipates the idea of building in public and the creator's life as a public journal (Chapters 14 and 21).

#### What a goal is: an aim, a point of view, a lens

If everything is goal-directed, the definition of "goal" becomes the central question, and Koe's definition in 2025 is deliberately different from the one in productivity literature. In January he starts from a sentence of Jordan Peterson's, "what you aim at determines what you see," which he says aligns with what he has been discussing for the past year: how goals shape perception (**Source:** `The Fastest Way To Ruin Your Life (Avoid This While You're Young).md`, 2025-01-12). Then he gives his definition. You have heard that "systems are greater than goals" and that goals should be "clear and specific" or SMART, but most people do not understand what a goal is: "a goal is an aim." It is not a static point where you achieve something; "a goal is simply a tool to identify a problem, it's a point of view." Most people pursue goals for acceptance, status and survival rather than for "the happiness that stems from resistance being overcome or problems being solved." Later in the video he adds that goals can and should evolve with better information; they are not to be achieved at all costs; a problem requires a solution arrived at creatively by making guesses and correcting errors; and "goals are points of view that must be refined on a consistent basis" (same source).

A month later the formulation sharpens. "Your mind absorbs the relevant information according to the goal that is at the top of your mind." If your goal is a degree and a job, that is "the main overarching homing mechanism for your mind that's been coded into your head" for eighteen years; it is unconscious, and you simply do it. But then "your mind is narrowed, it has a narrow point of view. That's what a goal is: a point of view. It's not something that you achieve, it's a way of viewing life." And when those goals frame what information you can pick up, "your destiny is to become replaceable, because you're not going to learn the multiple interests that allow you to be free" (**Source:** `How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md`, 2025-02-23). In May he puts the definition at the start of a framework for gamifying life, whose first step is designing the game: "Most people have a very mediocre definition of what a goal is. A goal is an aim. A goal is not something that you must achieve at all costs. A goal is a lens from which to make decisions. The goals themselves are supposed to change and evolve as you become more experienced." Long-term goals, he adds later, "are just the direction you're heading in; you don't have to achieve that thing specifically" (**Source:** `How To Gamify Your Life (And Reinvent Yourself... Fast).md`, 2025-05-08). And in December he gives the most complete version: "Real change requires changing your goals", not setting a surface goal (which may itself serve an unconscious goal that harms you), but "changing your point of view, because that's what a goal is. A goal is a projection into the future that acts as a lens of perception, which allows you to notice information, ideas, and resources that aid in you achieving that goal" (**Source:** `How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md`, 2025-12-28).

**Goal**, then, is a common word with a specific meaning in Koe's mature vocabulary, and the four 2025 formulations can be combined into one definition: a goal is a projection into the future (*aim*) that functions as a *point of view* and a *lens of perception*, which makes certain information, ideas, resources and problems visible and others invisible, and which is meant to be refined as experience accumulates rather than achieved at all costs.

| Formulation | What it emphasizes |
|---|---|
| "A goal is an aim" | Direction rather than a fixed endpoint |
| "A tool to identify a problem" | The goal makes problems visible (section 6.6) |
| "A point of view", "a way of viewing life" | The goal is where you see from, not what you see |
| "A lens from which to make decisions" | The goal is a criterion for choosing |
| "A projection into the future that acts as a lens of perception" | The goal works now, by changing what you notice |
| "Points of view that must be refined on a consistent basis" | The goal is provisional and evolves |

The corpus records a tension around this definition that should be stated plainly. On one side, Koe says repeatedly that goals are not for achieving: the massive goal is set not "for practicality or even achievement" but for vision, direction and filtering; "winning the game of life isn't an outcome but a process"; goals at one year, one month and one day "aren't for achievement" but are macro and micro lenses. On the other side, in late 2024 he says "the destination is what determines the journey", that the goal is "vastly more important than the system", and "it's not about the journey, it's about the destination." Section 6.6 presents those statements in full. The author's own resolution is that journey and destination are "inseparable". A consistent reading is available: the destination defines the direction of the system, while each concrete goal functions as a lens that is adjusted or abandoned as information arrives. The lens view does not say that achievement is irrelevant; it says that a goal does its most important work *before* it is achieved, by changing what you see.

#### Two readers, two books

The most frequent illustration of the lens is reading, and Koe returns to it so often that it functions as the canonical example. In 2023: "Goals can't exist without the awareness of a problem. Goals and problems create a frame for your perspective; your perspective determines what information you perceive as important." A person with money problems and society's goals sees a new job opportunity as important; a person with money problems and self-generated goals sees a business opportunity. Both read the same book (the Bible, a self-help book, fiction) and interpret it completely differently, highlighting different quotes because they are relevant to their situation (**Source:** `The Daily Routine That Changed My Life (4 Habits Most People Ignore).md`, 2023-11-19). In 2024: "A person with the assigned goal of society to retire at 60 years old will see fewer business opportunities than a person with a self-generated goal of financial freedom at 25 years old." "Two people with different goals will have radically different highlights of the books and content. They will notice the things that aid in their goals whether they are aware of it or not" (**Source:** `Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md`, 2024-08-25). And in a video on reading the same year: intention is necessary "because every single person interprets a book differently." Someone aiming to be a better CEO can read philosophy and find it applicable; someone wanting a better relationship can read the same book and progress in that domain. So "any book, conversation, article or information can help you achieve a goal," and you need not limit what you read, although some kinds of books help much more than random choices (**Source:** `This Simple Reading Habit Will Change Your Life (I Promise).md`, 2024-08-04).

In 2025 he gives the image a structure. The goals that compose your mind, identity and perspective, "often in that order, like a set of **concentric circles**", filter what you perceive. Someone with the goal of school followed by a job will not notice an opportunity to start a business and pursue their life's work; someone starting a business will not notice ideas for getting good grades, because they do not need grades. Both can read the exact same book and read it differently, skipping different paragraphs, sentences and lessons, each unconsciously extracting what reinforces the system leading to their goals; "they're reading it from a different mind." Identity shapes everything you perceive (**Source:** `The Fastest Way To Ruin Your Life (Avoid This While You're Young).md`, 2025-01-12). The concentric circles connect this chapter to Chapter 3: goals are at the center, they compose identity, identity shapes perspective, and perspective filters perception. The lens is not an accessory placed in front of the eye; it is the innermost ring of who you are.

Two personal examples show how far the filter reaches. "If I want to start a business, I really want to start a business, that's the only thing I can think about, then everything I perceive, the conversations I have, the books that I read, even if it's some weird self-help book or fiction book, I'll pull lessons out of it that apply and help me achieve my goals." People going to school or a job do the same, but for those goals (**Source:** `How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md`, 2025-02-23). And the clearest case: one person reads a book with the goal of getting a job and pulls out information related to a better job; someone losing weight reads a spirituality book and it is framed by that goal, giving them "mental and spiritual strategies to deal with the low energy that comes from being deep in a cut." "If you read *The Power of Now* when you're prepping for a bodybuilding competition, that's going to help you manage the emotions," while for someone struggling to start a business it helps with that. "That's what I mean by the goal frames your perception." He adds a corollary that works as a diagnosis: "if you're not getting anything from reading or from watching podcasts, and you're just watching them for entertainment, it's usually because you're not trying to pursue a meaningful and self-generated goal" (**Source:** `How To Become So Self-Disciplined It Feels Illegal.md`, 2025-08-24). *The Power of Now* is the book that, in Chapter 2, triggered the author's own awakening; here it reappears as an example of how the same text serves different lenses.

**The goal frames your perception** is his recurring phrase for the whole phenomenon. The corollary is the useful part for a reader: if information seems useless, the problem may not be the information but the absence of a goal through which to read it.

#### Information means everything you perceive

A 2024 passage widens the concept of information beyond reading. "Your perspective, goals and problems frame any and all information and opportunities available to you." By information he does not mean books or podcasts but anything perceived: sounds, sights, smells, tastes, touches, the content of consciousness, "our experience, the only thing that is real." He repeats the figures from section 6.1: the conscious mind processes 50 bits per second, the unconscious 11 million. And he draws the conclusion that joins the two halves of this chapter: to enrich everyday experience, notice novel opportunities, achieve the impossible and create more flow, "we must expand who we are by fundamentally changing the goals our mind operates on", which influences what composes the 50 bits of processing in any given second (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md`, 2024-07-21).

This is the hinge between attention and goals. Section 6.1 said conscious attention is a narrow channel; section 6.2 said directing it is the only root control. Here the question "directed by what?" receives its answer: by the goals the mind operates on. You cannot widen the channel, but you can change what passes through it, and the instrument that decides what passes through is the goal. That is why Koe says that changing your goals changes your experience itself, not merely your plans.

#### The goal chain: from interpretation to identity

Koe repeatedly describes the effect of goals as a chain of consequences. In 2023: "Goals change how you interpret situations, which influences your actions, which programs your identity, which compounds over years into the good life." In the same passage he gives the corollary about problems that section 6.6 develops: "you can't solve a problem unless you're aware of it; you can't become aware of a problem unless it impacts a goal." Most people do not have goals, are afraid of making mistakes, and so do not give themselves a chance to improve any aspect of their lives (**Source:** `You Won’t Be The Same Person In 6 Months (Master Anything, Fast).md`, 2023-12-17). In 2024 he gives the chain's justification for studying perception at all: "how your goals frame how you perceive situations determines most of the decisions that you make, most of the choices that you make, and if you are the culmination of your past choices, then your choices today are going to create your future" (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03).

| Link in the chain | What it does |
|---|---|
| Goal | Frames how you interpret situations |
| Interpretation | Determines most of your choices and actions |
| Actions | Program your identity |
| Identity | Compounds over years into the life you have |

Chapter 3 showed the same chain from the other end ("you are the culmination of your past choices"). Here it runs forward: the goal is the first link, and changing it is the only intervention that propagates through all the others.

He makes the chain concrete with everyday behavior. If your goal is to quit your job, your Google searches change "from best careers to go into in 2024 to best business models to start in 2024"; you go down the rabbit hole and follow new people on social media, "if you actually want to achieve the goal." If you are bound to fail, or want to fail, you will not do these things, "or you're going to interpret them from a skeptical lens and program your mind in the opposite direction to stay comfortable" (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03). The last clause is a warning: the lens can be set against the goal you profess. A declared goal contradicted by an unconscious one (staying comfortable) produces a skeptical lens that collects reasons not to change. That is the conceptual survival of Chapter 3 operating through perception.

A 2023 example adds the role of algorithms. If he sets a conscious goal to make $100,000 in business and commits to it with tactical stress (moving, or buying something he cannot yet afford), "everything I see is going to be through the lens of how is this going to serve my goal." Scrolling with the goal of entertainment, "I'm going to ignore any valuable information that could benefit my future"; building a business, "I'm going to filter through the memes and get to the value." You condition yourself into "that state of being an education": you see a valuable account, watch more of its videos, the algorithm starts to feed you more of them, "and then eventually **character begets action**: it's going to shape who you are over time, the information that's circulating in your head and is top of mind, and it will influence your actions" (**Source:** `A Dangerous (But Effective) Strategy To Get Out Of A Rut.md`, 2023-10-02). "Character begets action" is his coinage for the last links of the chain: the information circulating in your head shapes who you are, and who you are produces what you do.

A 2024 passage explains the same feedback with the algorithm from the other side. Once a book connects ideas and gives you clarity to act, you start acting and make some progress, which is the beginner stage, and then you can notice intermediate-level strategies that previously would have been "annoying things popping up on your feed": Alex Hormozi videos, his own videos, "here's how you land a client with this specific strategy," sales strategies. "All of the content, the good educational content, is closed off to you until you actually start the thing", and this goes beyond social media. Once you pay more attention to that content, the algorithm shows you more of it, reprogramming your mind and conditioning you "into a better identity of starting a business." Social media and the algorithm can reinforce very bad or very good habits (**Source:** `This Simple Reading Habit Will Change Your Life (I Promise).md`, 2024-08-04). The idea that good content is "closed off" until you start is the lens applied to stages: each level of action opens the perception of the next level of information. It is the "step that reveals the next step" of section 6.4, seen from the side of input. Chapter 11 develops the algorithm's double edge.

#### Focus as a trainable muscle framed by perspective

The 2024 summary of *The Art of Focus* presents the lens inside the book's first pillar, focus, and gives the most systematic statement of how goals, perspective and attention relate. Focus is a muscle that can be trained: just as you condition the body with physical training, you condition the mind with mental training. Focus has a frame, called perspective or worldview, conditioned by the information processed throughout your life, and perspective restricts what you can perceive and interpret. It is constructed from three things: (a) the goals you plan to achieve, (b) the paths you know to achieve them, and (c) the problems that must be identified to achieve them. If you do not know which goals you are pursuing, you pursue the ones society assigned you. Your focus is limited to the aspects of reality your perspective allows "unless you create goals that launch you into the unknown." And "a problem that you consider a normal part of your life won't reveal itself as a problem", so you will have nothing to learn, discover or act on. The training method: set and pursue interest-based goals that let your mind become aware of new information that leads to your evolution. Hence the need for radical open-mindedness: in a constant state of close-mindedness, stress and narrow focus, opportunities pass you by and you blame anything but yourself. Over time, the trained mind filters signal from noise, biased toward information that brings prosperity (**Source:** `The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md`, 2024-01-18).

**Focus is a muscle** is one of his recurring formulas (Chapter 15 treats the related "mental bodybuilding"). What this passage adds is the structure of the frame:

| Component of perspective | What it contributes to focus |
|---|---|
| Goals you plan to achieve | Direction: what attention is for |
| Paths you know to achieve them | Range: which routes you can even consider |
| Problems that must be identified | Content: what you notice as needing work |

The triad goal–path–problem is one of the author's foundational principles, and Chapter 8 develops it as a planning tool. Here it describes the frame of attention. Two of its implications deserve emphasis. First, the frame limits perception *unless* the goals launch you into the unknown: familiar goals reinforce the existing frame, while unfamiliar ones force it to expand. That is the cognitive reason for the big, uncomfortable goals of Chapter 7. Second, "a problem you consider normal won't reveal itself as a problem" anticipates the thesis of section 6.6: without a goal that exposes it, a problem is just the way things are.

#### Focus on the goal, not on the problem

If goals and problems together frame perception, which should attention dwell on? In a 2024 video for people who feel lost, Koe gives a clear answer. "The last reason you feel stuck or trapped is that you haven't expanded your mind beyond the problem, so it's impossible to solve; if your mind is only focused on the problem, that means it's not focused on a solution." "If you have a goal but you're not clear on how to get there, your mind does this magical thing called pattern recognition, where you pick up information in the environment that helps you achieve the goal." "If your main focus is the problem, you're going to further dig yourself into the problem." If you "consciously condition yourself to continue focusing on the goal", the mind picks up ideas that move you toward it (**Source:** `My Honest Advice For Those Who Feel Lost & Confused.md`, 2024-05-30). Then the mechanism, in its fullest form: "When you focus on the goal, even if you don't know what it is, and you continue focusing on that goal, your mind will pick up ideas that help you achieve the goal." When you read any book, "your mind will bias the information and ideas that you highlight that will help you achieve the goal," and the same happens in conversations and while scrolling. "Everyone that has different goals on their minds are going to highlight different things in books, they're going to pick up different things in conversations, their perception is going to be framed to be advantageous toward the end goal that they're pursuing, and if you're pursuing the problem, then that's a self-fulfilling prophecy that you're going to fail" (same source).

**Pattern recognition**, a psychological term, is used here in the specific sense that runs through the corpus: the mind's capacity to pick out of the environment what serves its goal. The logic of the "self-fulfilling prophecy" follows: if the lens is set on the problem, the problem becomes the goal the mind serves, and it collects more evidence of the problem. This is the same mechanism Chapter 3 described ("if you're always focused on negative outcomes, those outcomes become your reality, because those outcomes are the goals your mind is helping you achieve").

He immediately guards against a misreading. Conditioning yourself to focus on the goal "is like a form of positive thinking, but not the delusional positive thinking of, like, oh just think positive all the time, because evil exists, negativity exists; that's a stupid way to go about life and it leads to more negativity than just being negative at times and learning to deal with that" (**Source:** `My Honest Advice For Those Who Feel Lost & Confused.md`, 2024-05-30). The distinction matters. Goal focus does not deny problems or forbid negative emotions; it decides where attention *rests*. You can acknowledge the problem fully and still keep the goal as the reference signal. In cybernetic terms, the problem is the error signal and the goal is the reference signal; a system that stared only at its error signal, without the reference, would have nothing to steer toward.

This passage is the clearest case in this chapter of the author changing position, and both versions should be visible. In October 2022, step one of his framework for people who feel lost was "set a meaningful goal"; in early 2023 he defined purpose as the most urgent problem. In March 2024, two months before the passage above, he said the opposite: "stop focusing on the goal", because "a problem creates a goal" and a goal without a felt problem lacks gravity; become aware of the problem, sit with it and dissect it. In the same March video he also said that goals "don't start out magnetic" and that interest is built by investing energy. Then, in May 2024, came the passage quoted above: focusing on the problem digs you deeper; focus on the goal, and "step one: just have a goal", which he called a minimum viable goal. The reason he gave for the March position was that if the problem is not conscious enough, the goal is useless and you end up like "the 99%". The author never reconciles the two. They can apply to different moments. Awareness of the problem is the way to *choose* a goal when you do not yet perceive anything as wrong (Chapter 7's brutal awareness and anti-vision rely on this); focusing on the goal is the way to *solve* the problem once you are moving, so that attention collects solutions instead of more evidence of the difficulty. Read this way, the problem is where a goal comes from, and the goal is where attention should then live.

#### The reticular activating system

In 2026 Koe gives the lens a neurological name and calls it the most important thing he knows about creativity. "To be creative, you need something to create." He tells three stories. During spelling tests as a child, after learning a new word, "I would just start hearing it everywhere": in passers-by, in overheard conversations, on his computer in video games. He saw a new car, looked it up, and the next day saw "like 10 of them." And when he made starting a business an actual prioritized goal, he began noticing business opportunities he did not know existed, "because I was made to believe that getting a 9-to-5 job was my only option." "This phenomenon is the single most important catalyst for creativity. It's the **reticular activating system** in our brains, like a homing mechanism, a heat-seeking missile going towards the heat. And it's based on what's important to you, the unconscious and conscious goals that occupy the frame in your mind." On the default path, "you're really only going to notice things that help support you going down the default path. But if you change direction, you truly change direction, and it's not as simple as just saying 'I'm going to change direction', then you start to notice things that aid in you going that direction" (**Source:** `If you've lost your creative genius, watch this.md`, 2026-03-28).

A much earlier example is the same phenomenon in a lighter key. When you notice a new car for the first time, you start to see it everywhere. When the Jeep Gladiator came out, he and his editor Devin both thought it was "the stupidest looking car," and now, every time they see one, Devin says "hey, a Jeep Gladiator", about two hundred times by now. Applied: if you were in love with the idea of building out the project of "your mind, body, spirit and business," you would start seeing opportunity everywhere; "if you don't have that intention, all of those opportunities are just slipping right under your nose" (**Source:** `Learn New Skills FAST (How I Remember Everything I Learn).md`, 2022-11-27).

**Complementary context:** the reticular activating system is a network of neurons in the brainstem whose best-established role is regulating arousal, wakefulness and the sleep–wake transition. Its popular description as a filter that makes you notice what matters to you is a simplification that goes beyond the neuroscience; the noticing phenomenon itself (a new word or car model suddenly appearing everywhere) is usually explained by selective attention and is known as the frequency illusion or Baader-Meinhof phenomenon (see Chapter 3). Koe's use of the RAS should therefore be read as a name for a phenomenon he observes, not as an established mechanism. The phenomenon itself, that goals shape what is noticed, is well supported by research on selective attention.

What is new in the 2026 passage is not the mechanism, which Chapter 3 already described as the homing mechanism, but the claim about its scope. Koe now calls it the "single most important catalyst for creativity", and the reason is that creativity, in his model, is the connection of noticed material (Chapter 18). If the lens determines what is noticed, it determines the raw material from which anything can be created. The qualification at the end is also important: changing direction is "not as simple as just saying 'I'm going to change direction.'" A declared goal does not reset the filter; only a goal that has actually become a priority, that occupies "the frame in your mind", does.

#### Conditioning your own perception: the avocado

If goals shape perception automatically, can perception also be shaped on purpose? A 2025 example says yes. You can "start to condition how your mind views the world"; "this kind of sucks, but it's also a very cool thing, because you have control over it." Find a new food, like avocados, and look up the benefits: "I should eat more avocados now." Look up the negatives and you would probably find something that makes you hate the thought and never eat them again. "Avocados is a stupid example here, but you can do that for almost anything." This is "why the diet and nutrition world is all messed up": you can find negative and positive aspects of everything, "and what you choose to focus on determines what you're probably going to eat." Apply it to your whole life: look up the negatives of where your current habits lead, and "you have a lot of control over how your mind perceives reality to move you closer toward your goals and love the things that you want to do" (**Source:** `How To Become So Self-Disciplined It Feels Illegal.md`, 2025-08-24).

The example is a deliberate use of the mechanism that the skeptical lens of the job-quitting example used against the person. The choice of what to research is an act of root control (section 6.2): it decides what information enters the narrow channel, and that information then shapes attitude and behavior. It also carries an implicit warning about intellectual honesty. If perception can be steered by selective research, then the same technique that helps you love what serves your goals can make you believe whatever you want. The author presents it as a tool for behavior change, not for finding the truth about avocados; a reader should keep those uses apart.

#### Intention: stretching toward a goal

The concept that connects the goal to everyday attention is **intention**, which Koe defines with its Latin root in mind. "Intention means that you are stretching towards a goal." It is not mindlessly doing whatever you want. He addresses an objection: what if someone wants to murder someone or jump off a cliff? That is not what they really want; mental illness and similar cases he will not address, because he does not know enough about them. For the average person, he assumes, most people want to act toward a better future and live it, so they really can do whatever they want "once they know what they want" (**Source:** `How To Do Whatever The Fck You Want.md`, 2023-01-12). This is the positive half of the definition whose negative half appeared in section 6.2 ("operate within your own reality and don't project outside of it"). Intention is attention that is present and aimed.

**Complementary context:** the Latin *intendere* means "to stretch out" or "to direct toward" (as in stretching a bow toward a target), which is the image behind Koe's "stretching towards a goal."

In the same video he describes the procedure he considers the lifelong core of the whole system. You cannot walk around unconsciously doing what others want for the rest of your life. "You have to make the conscious decision to start making conscious decisions" and be aware of every action you plan to take and where it leads: act with intention. The steps: (1) set some form of goal for the future; (2) reflect on the past to understand and gain clarity on how to reach it; (3) self-educate (learn, study, use the internet) to "bridge those clarity gaps"; (4) start executing; (5) give yourself a bigger time horizon, not two weeks. This is the process to repeat throughout your entire life (**Source:** `How To Do Whatever The Fck You Want.md`, 2023-01-12).

| Step | Purpose |
|---|---|
| 1. Set a goal | Install the reference signal |
| 2. Reflect on the past | Gain clarity on how to reach it from where you are |
| 3. Self-educate | Close the **clarity gaps** between goal and path |
| 4. Execute | Start the loop of action and feedback |
| 5. Lengthen the time horizon | Give the loop enough time for corrections to accumulate |

The sequence is the cybernetic loop with a human face: goal (reference signal), reflection (sensing the current state), self-education (learning what the comparator says you lack), execution (actuator), and a long horizon (persistence). **Make the conscious decision to start making conscious decisions** is a recursive formula worth noticing: the first conscious decision is the decision to stop running on assigned goals. **Clarity gaps** are the distance between a goal and the knowledge needed to pursue it, closed by self-education; Chapter 14 develops the term.

A 2023 video adds that one goal is not enough for a lifetime. "You need a conscious goal for every part of your life, or every chapter of your life, because a goal provides vision and orders your mind." A goal gives you "a lens to not only build toward, but what to perceive and learn information from." His example: with the goal of building a business you start building it, which is "like lifting emotional weight, in a sense, because it's going to be hard", and to fuel that you need to learn new information so you have the skill and understanding to do it. Later he adds that the goals must be "conscious, self-generated goals, not ones that are assigned to you by a job, school or society" (**Source:** `How To Reprogram Your Mind & Build Your Intelligence.md`, 2023-09-24). The phrase "a goal provides vision and orders your mind" is the direct link to Chapter 5: the goal is what reverses psychic entropy, because it gives attention a single direction around which the contents of consciousness can be organized. The "chapter of life" anticipates the model of life as a series of chapters in Chapter 39: each chapter needs its own conscious goal, and the end of a goal is the beginning of a new lens.

#### Signal from noise: how goals filter information

If a goal is a lens, its most practical function is filtering. Koe states the principle in 2023. There is an overabundance of information, but it is overwhelming only to those without goals and intentions behind their learning. That is how you "learn everything": you "filter signal from noise" in every piece of information, aligned with conscious goals you set, not goals others set. From birth people project goal after goal onto you, shaping your behavior and the lens through which you perceive and learn; those goals are how you survive (**Source:** `The Cure To A Mediocre Life (Become Multidimensionally Jacked).md`, 2023-08-30). The usual complaint about information overload is thus reversed: the problem is not too much information but too little direction. **Signal from noise** is the phrase he uses throughout for this filtering function.

He draws the consequence for advice. "Learn and build; that way you eliminate useless information," because everyone tells you what to do, projecting what they deem important, "what they see as signal", which "to you is 95 percent of the time noise." If you are not where you want to be, "nothing is as important as your own goals: not the news, not your friends' relationship problems, and definitely not who's getting elected" (**Source:** `A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Life (In 6-12 Months).md`, 2023-07-25). The 95% is a rhetorical figure; the point is that signal is not a property of information but a relation between information and a goal. What is signal for one person is noise for another, and most of the advice in circulation is someone else's signal.

From this principle come several practices, stated across 2023. The first is a **rotation of information**. Pursuing confidence-related goals (business, the gym, talking to new people, a new group of friends) requires educating yourself as you pursue them, with information coming through "the filter of you achieving those goals", which he describes as pattern recognition: if you are trying to talk to new people while listening to audiobooks on social skills, you have something fresh to experiment with. For everything you have ownership over (gym, business, skill set and mindset, relationships) "you should have a rotation of information", and you should not consume anything that does not contribute to those goals. Entertainment, such as Netflix to wind down at night, is fine; but in the morning, rather than scrolling, "you need to have inputs that contribute to the outputs that you want" (**Source:** `You Aren't Successful Because You Care Too Much (How To Stop).md`, 2023-08-08). The morning recommendation answers directly the morning phone of section 6.1.

The second is a scheduled dose. "With the goal of escaping wage slavery at the top of your mind, you can schedule a minimum of 20 minutes dedicated to consuming information that will help with that goal." The sources: "buy books, use your Audible credits, save podcasts, follow new social accounts and add new videos to your watch later on YouTube." "Get out of your house, have a notes app open on your phone and write down your discoveries." "As you immerse yourself in the information you will feel the dopamine in your brain on the hunt for a better life" (**Source:** `How To Escape Wage Slavery (Entrepreneurship Is Modern Survival).md`, 2023-08-01). The "hunt" connects to the lion of section 6.2 and the berries of Chapter 3: goal-directed consumption is described as hunting, and the dopamine as the reward of finding what serves the goal (Chapter 11 discusses how Koe uses dopamine).

The third is specificity. Write down ten to twenty specific skills, interests or topics that will actualize your vision, because you need clarity on what to do every day. "The key to all of this is specificity." Do not write "web design"; write "how to create landing pages that are actually useful to businesses." Do not write "the gym"; write "diet and training principles to lose 10 pounds of fat in six weeks." Do this ten times at a minimum. And if you cannot see how a skill, interest or curiosity serves your vision, remove it (**Source:** `A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Life (In 6-12 Months).md`, 2023-07-25). Specificity matters because a vague topic filters nothing; a specific one tells the lens exactly what to look for.

Specific lists raise an obvious worry: is studying many things at once not the "shiny object syndrome" that self-help warns against? Koe answers: "Focus on one thing long term, and focus on the multitude, the thousands of things short term that will allow you to get there. That's not shiny object syndrome, that's being smart and diversifying your study" (**Source:** `Micro Education Businesses Are The Future & How To Start With Zero Dollars.md`, 2023-09-17). **Diversifying your study** is his name for this. The distinction is structural: shiny object syndrome is many things without a unifying goal; diversified study is many things *filtered* by one long-term goal. The lens is what turns breadth into coherence, which is also the definition of the generalist in Chapter 20.

In 2025 the practices are gathered into a procedure he calls **interest-based education**, presented as the second step of reprogramming the mind. (1) Take note of your current problems with how you live, typically in health, wealth, relationships and finances (what is wrong with your health and fitness, how you look, your finances, your social life?), and pick one. (2) Explore the internet and social media "as a curator, not a consumer." (3) Experiment with various methods without becoming dogmatic. (4) Dedicate at least 30 to 60 minutes a day to learning that directly correlates with your self-generated goals. (5) "Throw your mind into an entirely different environment and let your beliefs be challenged." The key: "you reprogram your unconscious by aligning your actions and learnings with a goal that was not assigned to you." And the whole process in one line: "goal, interpret information, act, make mistakes, correct mistakes, repeat. If you can do that, you'll be successful" (**Source:** `You're Stuck In The Matrix, Here's How You Escape.md`, 2025-10-05).

The one-line summary is worth setting next to the cybernetic loop of section 6.4, because it is the same loop with the lens inserted in the right place:

| Interest-based education (2025) | Cybernetic loop (2024–2026) |
|---|---|
| Goal | Reference signal |
| Interpret information | Sensor, filtered by the goal |
| Act | Actuator |
| Make mistakes | Error signal |
| Correct mistakes | Comparator and new action |
| Repeat | The loop continues |

**Curator, not a consumer** names the attitude of step two: the difference between consuming and curating is whether a goal is selecting the input. Chapter 16 develops the same idea as the step from consumer to researcher.

#### Aim for your learning

The same principle applied to studying produces one of Koe's sharpest criticisms of learning advice. Having watched countless tutorials and podcasts on how to study, he finds that "the missing piece is that they just don't go over why you're learning in the first place." People choose something to learn that does not connect to their current skills or the life they want. "This is dangerous: you won't want to learn if there is no deeper meaning or clarity behind it, you will need more discipline, and you'll probably continue to hate learning like you did in school," or you will feel it is only for a job you did not care about. Step one is to "create an aim for your learning", so you feel progress toward "self-generated goals, not the goals society assigned to you", which, with a reference to Csikszentmihalyi's *Flow*, he calls "a major key of life enjoyment." "People who enjoy learning something can be so much better than people who are just innately smart" (**Source:** `How To Learn Anything 10x Faster Than Anyone With AI.md`, 2025-03-09). **Aim for your learning** is his term. Its argument connects to Chapter 3's thesis that discipline is needed only when identity and goal are misaligned: learning without an aim requires discipline because nothing pulls it.

Two years earlier he had placed the aim at the start of a six-step method for retaining "the most knowledge possible" (**Source:** `Learn New Skills FAST (How I Remember Everything I Learn).md`, 2022-11-27):

| Step | Content |
|---|---|
| 1. Set an intention | A goal, purpose or "why" held at the top of awareness |
| 2. Schedule a creativity block | For example, a 30-minute walk |
| 3. Create a note-taking system | To capture ideas that arise away from work |
| 4. Build every aspect of your life | Fall in love with the process and with infinite games |
| 5. Teach what you know | The protégé effect |
| 6. Turn this into your life's work | Research, sustain passion, build, teach, "projectize" and then productize yourself |

Only the first step belongs to this chapter; the others are developed in Chapters 13 (creativity blocks), 14 (building and teaching) and 16 (note-taking). But the first step is the foundation of the rest, and he explains why. Set a goal, create a purpose, have a why behind your actions, "however you want to frame it": an intention to improve your life or actualize your potential. It must be held at the top of your awareness if you want to apply what you consume to improving any area of life. It sets your focus on the future and lets you channel everything you experience into that vision; you view things "from the lens of your vision", so you tend to see them in a positive light. He concedes that you can still see things negatively and that this is a lifelong process. And he states the condition under which it works: "everything you consume starts to fuel your vision, if that vision is genuinely important and you are dedicated to achieving that" (**Source:** `Learn New Skills FAST (How I Remember Everything I Learn).md`, 2022-11-27). The condition matters as much as the claim: the lens works only for a goal that genuinely matters. A goal held for appearances does not filter anything, which is the same point the RAS passage makes about not simply "saying" you have changed direction.

A 2023 passage explains why so much reading seems to leave no trace. "Step one is drown in new information to program your mind, because most people are drowning in information that has nothing to do with their goals; they don't have a vision, goal or problem to interpret situations through." "Chances are you've already read life-changing information, but it didn't register in your awareness because you didn't have a goal to apply it to." "You could have read a book that was absolutely life-changing to another person, but then you go and read it and you're like, it was kind of a good book, it wasn't that good" (**Source:** `How To Escape Wage Slavery (Entrepreneurship Is Modern Survival).md`, 2023-08-01). This is the retention chain of section 6.4 described from experience, three years before it was formalized: no goal, no relevance, no retention. **Program your mind** here means deliberately flooding attention with information tied to a goal, so that it registers.

What if the goal itself is still faint? Then, he says in 2024, "you need to just have this faint image of a goal or vision that you want to actualize, and now you need to start stacking ideas that bias that goal or vision." Change and expand your mind by reading new books and having "intentional conversations, not just mindless conversations with your friends, like, oh, did you play that new video game." Start conversations with friends, parents or partners "framed around the goal you're trying to achieve, so that you can gain more clarity on how to achieve it, and if you even want to achieve that goal." Conversations include "actual verbal conversations, or conversations with a book or content, or with your own mind" (**Source:** `My Honest Advice For Those Who Feel Lost & Confused.md`, 2024-05-30). **Stacking ideas** is the procedure for sharpening a lens that is still blurred. Two things stand out. The goal does not need to be clear for the lens to start working; a faint image is enough to bias what you collect. And the conversations serve a double purpose: to learn how to reach the goal and to test whether you even want it. The lens is also how a goal gets revised.

In 2025 the principle hardens into a criterion of utility. "What's the purpose of you learning?" If you learn because you are bored and it serves no utility, does not increase your value and does not become part of who you are, why learn it? "If you aren't going to do it every day for the rest of your life," either recognize that you have been doing something you do not plan to continue and work on eliminating or replacing it, or do not learn it at all. He grants exceptions: for entertainment or a short-term hobby, "sure, that's fine", but if you are not where you want to be, short-term hobbies will not help. "You have no business learning Photoshop if you aren't actively working toward a goal that requires it"; if you are learning it because you are bored, "go and read a fiction book instead. You don't learn skills for entertainment. You learn them for utility" (**Source:** `How To Master Any Skill Fast (Give Yourself 2 Weeks).md`, 2025-08-10). The "every day for the rest of your life" criterion is deliberately severe and should be read as a heuristic for prioritizing skills, not as a rule against curiosity; the explicit exception for entertainment and hobbies shows that the target is aimless skill acquisition presented as self-improvement.

A 2026 remark about his software product applies the same logic to tools. With Eden, "there's only so many tutorials we can create before it's up to you to just figure out how to do what you want to do. The software is a tool, like a wrench. You can't use a tool if you have nothing to use it for." When people ask how to use it, his first question is "What are you trying to do? Are you trying to write content? Okay, do you know how to write content? Have you educated yourself? Have you started?" Only then can he give a proper answer (**Source:** `How To Learn Anything 10x Faster Than Anyone.md`, 2026-08-09). The question "what are you trying to do?" is the reference signal asked for explicitly. Without it, "how do I use this?" has no answer, because usefulness is relative to a goal.

#### You can only cook with what's in the fridge

The lens decides what you notice, but you can only notice what you have been exposed to. Koe captures this second limit with a metaphor he took from another YouTube channel, one that, he says, only makes "reasons to read" videos (he does not name it). "You can only cook with what's in the fridge." "Your mind won't notice new life-changing opportunities if it doesn't have the goals that open your mind and expand your awareness." Surrounded by people pursuing credentials (a college degree for a high-paying job), your mind notices only information that serves that goal. If you consciously re-educate yourself with new books, you are exposed to novel ideas that let you create new goals; "your awareness surface area expands"; you connect new ideas and notice an opportunity you would not have cared about before, which motivates you to start something like a business. "Then and only then" can your mind notice the higher-level ideas needed to progress in that domain. And if your fridge does not expand, "you eat the same boring meal every single day", which is not necessarily bad (the same meals help with clarity on a diet), but eating "the same mental junk food" every day makes you gain "mental fat" (**Source:** `This Simple Reading Habit Will Change Your Life (I Promise).md`, 2024-08-04).

The metaphor adds a dimension the lens alone does not cover. The lens selects from what is available; the fridge is what is available. And the two interact in a cycle: new input creates the possibility of new goals, new goals open the lens to new input, and so on. **Awareness surface area** (his term) is the size of the fridge: the more skills, education and exposure you have, the more problems and opportunities you can identify. **Mental junk food** and **mental fat** are the downside: repetitive, low-value input that fills the fridge without nourishing.

In 2026 he revisits the metaphor and shifts its emphasis, in an explicit self-correction. "The way I used to think is that I need to consume information in order to have ideas. So if I struggle to write or to have ideas, I felt like I just wasn't consuming good information, which is kind of true. But creativity is rarely an input problem. I love the saying that you can only cook with what's in the fridge, but most people's fridges are just overflowing with, like, candy and ice cream and trash" (**Source:** `If you've lost your creative genius, watch this.md`, 2026-03-28).

The two versions should be held together, as the author himself does ("which is kind of true"). In 2024 the problem is an empty or monotonous fridge: people who read nothing new cannot form new goals. In 2026 the problem is an overflowing fridge full of junk: people who consume constantly but without a lens have plenty of input and nothing to cook with. Both are failures of the same relation between input and goal: too little input to form goals, or too much input not filtered by any. The 2026 version also stands in tension with his advice elsewhere to hunt for ideas in little-known books (Chapter 22): input still matters, but the bottleneck is usually excess and lack of processing. Chapter 18 develops the processing side.

#### The lens as niche, originality and utility

The last group of passages turns the lens from an instrument of perception into an instrument of creation, and anticipates Parts VII and VIII. In 2023 Koe presents "create a lens" as the first step of a method for value creators, with a graphic whose layout is reconstructed from his narration: at the bottom you consume information; your vision, goals and problems shape how you perceive it; you extract parts of it to create. His example: if money is a top-of-mind, stressful problem, then whatever book you read (on spirituality, on academic writing, on anything), a sentence will resonate with the money problem, and you can reframe it for an audience trying to make money. Someone else would extract a completely different lesson depending on their goal, problem or vision. So "you need to build a project or have a goal or have a vision for your future," because "leaders attract followers." "This is your niche and brand; it is how you frame everything you read and write." Value is what helps people overcome problems or achieve goals, and your brand attracts people with a shared goal or vision (**Source:** `The Value Creator (A New Internet Career Path For Intelligent People).md`, 2023-07-19).

**Create a lens** is the practical name for the whole section's idea. Its consequence for creators is that the lens *is* the niche: what makes your content distinct is not your topic but the goal through which you read everything, and the people it attracts are those who share that goal. Chapter 19's "you are the niche" and Chapter 22's "value is ideas through the lens of a goal" both rest on this passage.

The same lens makes ideas original. "The goals that you're working towards are going to form all of the ideas that you have. You can read a book, pick an idea, and the way you make it original to you is you think of it through the lens of what you're trying to achieve in life." His example, which he admits is "a bad example, off the top of my head": reading "you should read more books," he takes his goal (to become smarter and more articulate) and attaches it: you need to read more books because they lead to you being intelligent, articulate and so on, which creates an idea of his own (**Source:** `The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md`, 2024-07-28). Originality, on this account, is not the invention of new content but the passage of existing content through a particular goal. Chapter 18 develops the idea further.

Finally, a 2022 remark identifies what turns a passing thought into usable material. Without a place to use a thought, you might not remember or even experience certain things. "That's the difference between a thought and an idea: utility." When you have somewhere to publish, a thought you have while out and about becomes "oh, I could write about this online"; you organize it and ask how it can help people. You start turning everyday experiences into life lessons that solidify in your mind, help others, attract an audience and affect people "where it truly matters, and that is their direct human experience" (**Source:** `If You Want To Secure Your Future… Follow This New Career Path.md`, 2022-05-21). The **thought versus idea** distinction is the lens at its smallest scale: a goal (here, publishing) gives a thought somewhere to go, and having somewhere to go is what makes it register, organize itself and stay. It is the retention chain of section 6.4 applied to one's own mind, and the reason later chapters treat a weekly writing practice as a perception-training device as much as a publishing one.

### 6.6 The Goal Determines the System; Without a Goal There Is No Problem

The previous sections assembled the pieces: attention is the scarce input, the mind steers toward a reference signal, and the goal works as a lens that determines what is perceived. This last section closes the model with two theses that follow from those pieces and that prepare everything in Part IV. The first is that a system (the habits, routines and processes that productivity literature puts at the center) cannot exist without a goal, because the goal is what determines the system. The second is that problems and bad habits do not exist as problems except relative to a goal in which energy has been invested. Together they say that the goal is upstream of both what you do and what you see as wrong, which is why choosing it is the central decision.

#### What a system is

Koe's definition of a system first appears in 2023 and is reproduced in a 2024 compilation. A system has (1) an end goal or outcome, (2) a process or steps to reach that outcome, and (3) it must be fueled with energy that has specific qualities. The body is his analogy: you can fuel your body with junk food, but that energy "doesn't really meet the demands that your individual body needs"; clean foods with proper nutrients keep it up. In productivity, if the outcome is "a stable and revenue generating business," "you are going to have to fuel that system with the correct energy in the forms of **focused prioritized action**" (**Source:** `The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md`, 2023-01-01; `Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md`, 2024-03-21).

He adds a structural property: systems "lay over each other, they're just layers upon layers." "A paragraph is a goal to the system of sentences, and words are a system that create sentences." And he proposes an exercise: "just start thinking of everything as a system: notice the goal, notice the steps on how they're going to get there, and then notice the energy required to maintain that system without it falling apart" (same sources). Later in the 2023 video he gives one more property: once systems are refined and repeated, they form grooves in the neural networks of the brain and become automatic, and by layering system over system you "automate the path to the good life" (**Source:** `The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md`, 2023-01-01).

A 2022 passage generalizes the definition and links it to entropy. Not only the mind: "your body's a system, your relationships are a system; everything that leads to a desired outcome is a system; anything where you are trying to receive or achieve a goal is a system." When systems are not maintained with energy or effort (for the body, the food you eat; "whatever the input is to lead to the output"), and not in a positive way, they become more disordered over time, "and it is going to require much more energy and effort to fix that system" (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16).

Each property connects to an earlier part of the book. *Energy of specific quality* is the maintenance against entropy of Chapter 5: a system left alone decays, and the wrong input decays it faster. *Focused prioritized action* is the attention of section 6.2 described as fuel: the energy a work system needs is directed attention, not hours. *Layers* explain why goals and systems nest: what is a goal at one level (the paragraph) is a component at the next (the essay), which is the structure of the goal hierarchy in Chapter 8. And *automation* is the transfer from the conscious to the unconscious channel of section 6.1: a refined system no longer consumes RAM.

In January 2024, in the summary of *The Art of Focus*, the definition changes, and the change is informative. The second pillar of the book is energy: "Specific goals demand a specific amount of mental energy to actualize." Locked into the default path, you spend mental energy on society's goals, which makes it hard to invest in your own. Entropy ("**Nature's tax**", as some call it) is the measure of disorder and "the driving force of evolution": everything falls apart unless effort reverses entropy, and energy is required to maintain order. Then the new definition: "A system has a purpose, a path for achieving that purpose, and problems to invest energy in along the way." Society has had years to construct systems for the attention of the masses. To avoid an average life, dedicate time to creating and maintaining systems in all areas: create a **hierarchy of goals** across health, wealth, relationships and happiness, and invest mental energy in them often, if not daily; otherwise you will focus on the default path "and end up like everyone else; that isn't pretty" (**Source:** `The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md`, 2024-01-18).

| Version | Components of a system | Role of energy |
|---|---|---|
| 2022–2023 | Goal (outcome), process (steps), energy of specific quality | A component: the fuel that keeps the system from falling apart |
| 2024 (*The Art of Focus*) | Purpose, path, problems | No longer a component: what you invest *in* the problems along the path |

The third component moves from energy to problems, and energy becomes what is spent on them. The author does not explain the change. Both versions can be kept: the 2023 one describes maintenance against entropy (a system needs the right fuel), the 2024 one is a diagnostic tool for any situation (what is the purpose, what is the path, where are the problems?). The 2024 version is also the triad goal–path–problem that frames perspective in section 6.5, now presented as the anatomy of a system. That convergence is the point: the same three elements that determine what you perceive determine what you build.

#### Systems cannot exist without goals

From this definition Koe derives an objection to one of the most repeated sentences in self-improvement. "Systems are greater than goals" went around thanks to James Clear's *Atomic Habits*, and people took it literally and preached it across the self-improvement internet. "Systems can't exist without goals," he objects, since a system is what leads to a goal. Then he reasserts the primacy of goals: "If you don't know what you want, you will be told what you want and you will believe it"; "80% of living the life you want boils down to creating your own goals, while most people are mindless slaves to society's goals." Later in the same video: "humans are goal-oriented creatures, and goals create systems; systems don't exist without goals" (**Source:** `You Won’t Be The Same Person In 6 Months (Master Anything, Fast).md`, 2023-12-17). The target of the critique is how people read Clear, not necessarily Clear himself.

In 2024 he uses a different sentence from Clear to make the relationship two-sided. Clear writes that "winners and losers have the same goals"; Koe concludes: "a system can't exist without a goal; a goal won't be achieved without a system." He defines **systems for progress** as "a series of actions or processes that lead to a specific result; we see these in the forms of habits, routines, rituals, practice and repetition in the areas of your life that are important." And he draws the distinction that matters: "we are talking about conscious systems in alignment with a self-generated goal, not unconscious known systems toward assigned goals; there's a big difference there" (**Source:** `Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md`, 2024-07-13).

**Complementary context:** in *Atomic Habits* (2018), James Clear argues that goals are about the results you want and systems are about the processes that lead to them, and that if you want better results you should "forget about goals" and focus on systems; his remark that winners and losers have the same goals supports the claim that goals alone do not distinguish them. Clear does not say goals are useless; he says they are insufficient. Koe's objection is to the slogan as it circulated, and his own position ("a goal won't be achieved without a system") is close to Clear's.

The final distinction is where Koe's view adds something beyond the debate. Everyone already has systems: the commute, the evening scroll, the job's routines. The question is not whether to have systems but whose goal they serve. An **unconscious known system** toward an assigned goal is the default path in operational form. A conscious system aligned with a self-generated goal is the alternative. Building habits without first choosing the goal only makes you more efficient at pursuing whatever goal was already installed. The corpus records how this position developed: from 2023 the goal's primacy over the system is reinforced, and from 2025 a further note is added that "all systems fall apart or they evolve" and that "systems don't last forever" (even his own four-hour proposal is a system that breaks when necessary), so that principles come to matter more than any particular system. Chapter 13 follows that thread.

#### The destination determines the journey

In late 2024 Koe pushes the argument further, and in doing so takes a position against the most popular consolation in self-help, "enjoy the process." "The goal determines the system; the destination determines the journey. Goals and the destination are greater than systems and the journey. It's not about the journey, it's about the destination, because without the right destination you're going on the wrong journey." You can randomly enjoy the process, or set a destination and still enjoy the journey more as you head toward it. He likens this to Jordan Peterson saying you need "a positive aim for your life": the guiding light, your vision, the destination, which determines your life and the enjoyment you get from it. You can enjoy the journey chaotically, "but I don't think that's the wisest thing to do" (**Source:** `It's actually pretty easy to focus 12 hours a day (if you do this).md`, 2024-10-13). **Positive aim** is Peterson's phrase; Koe adapts it as the destination that gives a journey its direction.

A month later he restates the principle at the start of the video on intelligence. "If you want to become more intelligent, start with the goal you're pursuing. Goals are what determine the system; in other words, the destination is what determines the journey." "The goal and destination are vastly more important than the system or journey, because they create it." He anticipates the objection from a thinker the transcript renders unclearly, most likely Alan Watts: "sure, you can argue that the end of a melody isn't its goal, as [Watts] would say, but the goal of the artist was to create the melody." Everything you do (watching the video, moving your eye a fraction of a centimeter, taking a step, scratching your nose, building a billion-dollar business) "is the act of pursuing a goal, creating the melody or journey or process in its path" (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03).

**Complementary context:** Alan Watts often compared life to music, arguing that the point of a piece of music is not to reach its final chord, and that a life lived only for its destination misses the point. Koe's reply does not deny that the melody's value is in the playing; it relocates the goal from the melody to the musician, who had to intend the melody for it to exist.

He then addresses the psychology of the process directly. "The destination is a point of view that allows you to appreciate the journey." People say "it's all about the process" *after* they have achieved the goal, after they have the perspective; you are usually not sitting in the suffering of the process thinking "this is great." You can have gratitude, but if you do not achieve the goal "you're just stuck in this perpetual loop of suffering." "You rarely enjoy the process until you reach the goal, and that's okay, that's how it's supposed to be; that's reality pushing you toward achieving the goal." "You need a bit of suffering to appreciate the periods of peace. If you were happy all the time, it would become normal to you; happiness would stop making sense. All true happiness stems from a perspective of previous sadness." His conclusion: "the journey and the destination are inseparable" (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03).

The phrase **the destination determines the journey** appears only in these 2024 passages, and it sits in visible tension with other statements in the corpus. Ten months earlier he had said: "You win the game of life by finding enjoyment, not pleasure (big difference)" in as much of your experience as possible, and your experience is the present moment. He then introduced a sentence with the word "quote", without naming a source (possibly his own writing): "winning the game of life isn't an outcome but a process"; you are either winning right now, regardless of your emotional state or your status in the social hierarchy, or losing by projecting out of the present moment (**Source:** `How To Reset Your Life In 7 Days.md`, 2024-01-07). And section 6.5 showed that from 2025 he insists that goals are points of view, "not something you achieve".

Both sides should be stated as the author left them. In January 2024 winning is a process, located in the present; in October and November 2024 the destination matters more than the process and you rarely enjoy the process until you arrive. His own conclusion, "inseparable", suggests a way to read them together, which this chapter's model makes precise. The destination is what *creates* the journey (no melody without the intention to make one) and what *gives it meaning* (the point of view from which the suffering makes sense); the present-moment enjoyment of the January passage is the experience of being on a journey that has a destination. Without a destination, the "process" is not a process but drift, which is why the 2024 argument and the lens definition of 2025 are compatible: a goal can be a lens, not something to be achieved at all costs, and still be what determines which journey you are on. The tension that remains is one of emphasis: whether to stress the destination that orients or the present that is lived. The corpus does not resolve it, and Chapter 39 returns to it as the balance between peace and progress.

#### Problems are not problems without a goal

The second thesis of this section is stated most completely in 2023. "If you don't invest energy into a goal, you won't feel the pain of not reaching that goal." If getting in shape is not a serious goal that shapes your other actions, you will not see the impact of alcohol on your gains, your body, your physique or your energy. If you have not invested energy or progress in starting a business, you will not realize that alcohol is holding you back. If a thriving relationship is not a top-of-mind goal that is "part of your identity" (not "oh, that sounds nice"), you will not realize that good communication is what composes it, and that alcohol, mindless entertainment and junk food destroy it by lowering your performance. Without awareness of what leads to something good, "your bad habits don't seem bad, because you don't know how much progress you can make." Most people lack a clear vision of what they want, so the negative impact of their actions goes unnoticed; bad habits do not seem worth quitting because you do not have, or do not prioritize, responsibilities that "deserve you at 100% capacity." And the rule: "if the importance of those responsibilities outweighs the pleasure of your bad habits, you'd stop without question" (**Source:** `You Won’t Be The Same Person In 6 Months (Master Anything, Fast).md`, 2023-12-17).

In 2024 he restates it as a principle about problems in general. "You can't become aware of a problem unless you have the goal, and if you don't invest energy into the goal, you won't feel the pain or identify the problem of not reaching that goal." "Problems aren't problems unless you have a corresponding goal. If you don't have any worthwhile goal, you aren't able to see the plethora of problems that, once solved, lead to a better life" (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03). **Invest energy into a goal** is the condition he names: not merely having a goal but having put effort into it, so that obstacles cost you something.

The thesis is the cybernetic model of section 6.4 applied to everyday life. A problem is an error signal; an error signal is the gap between a reference and a current state; and without a reference, there is no gap. The added condition, invested energy, explains why a nominal goal is not enough: a goal you have not invested in does not hurt when it is blocked, so the error signal is too weak to register. This is also the 2023 version of the 2026 retention chain, and the ground of the goal chain's corollary in section 6.5 ("you can't become aware of a problem unless it impacts a goal"). It reframes motivation: the reason people do not change bad habits is often not weakness but the absence of anything those habits are visibly damaging.

He illustrates it with his own story. He occasionally has a few drinks at dinner; in college he drank much more. He realized how much drinking took from his goals at a particular time: cutting weight for the summer and writing his book. He had never seen drinking as a problem "because I never had anything to dedicate my time and attention to in the morning." After only two or three drinks he would wake up foggy, ideas would not come, and he would sit "for an hour staring at a screen when I'm supposed to be getting 5 to 10 pages for my book done," which felt terrible. When he tried to run first thing in the morning and power through with discipline, it was "so much harder" to achieve the goal and lose weight, quite apart from how alcohol interacts with the body. Then a qualification that is characteristic of him: he is "not a fan of a static and permanent lifestyle or routine"; he likes change and being able to "loop back to problems from a higher level of development." For the next few months, since those goals are not active, he likes having a few drinks on the weekend with friends (**Source:** `How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md`, 2024-11-03).

The story makes the thesis concrete and also shows its relativity. The same two drinks were not a problem before the book and the cut, became one while those goals were active, and stopped being one again when the goals changed. Nothing about the drinks changed; the reference signal did. The phrase "loop back to problems from a higher level of development" anticipates the cyclical model of life in Chapter 39: problems are not solved once and for all but revisited as goals change. The corpus records a broader, unresolved tension about alcohol between total abstinence while building something (a position he agreed with in a 2023 conversation with Dickie Bush), an "all or nothing" difficulty he describes in 2025, and the moderation in this story. In his own account, the answer depends on the stage.

#### Bad habits are only problems relative to your own goals

The same principle, applied to the habits people feel guilty about, produces a set of statements that can sound permissive and are not. In 2025: a goal or a problem, one does not exist without the other, but most people do not know what goal to pursue, or have assigned goals, which makes problems hard to notice. Partying, sleeping in and bad habits are not problems unless they block a goal. The goals of the **known path** (school, job, retiring at 65) do not require much effort or creativity, "so you can afford to be a degenerate": show up hungover, put in minimal effort, because the goals are not demanding. With your own strong plan or vision acting as a frame, you notice problems in your lifestyle: getting drunk wrecks your morning's work, you lose progress, you miss the weekly goal (the newsletter, the content, the product, the productive meeting). "You have to show up for your vision; you don't have to show up for the life you were assigned" (**Source:** `The Fastest Way To Ruin Your Life (Avoid This While You're Young).md`, 2025-01-12).

The sentence explains a phenomenon Chapter 3 attributed to identity: why people on the default path seem undisciplined. They are not lacking discipline; their goals do not demand it. Undemanding goals make bad habits affordable, so the habits are not problems, so nothing changes. A demanding goal of one's own is what turns the same habits into visible costs.

Two earlier passages define the conditions under which leisure is fine. In 2023: "If you don't know why you are doing something, if you don't have a reason, then why are you doing it? Why are you putting the food in your mouth?" He is not saying that drinking alcohol, Netflix or video games are bad. If you are conscious of why (to take a break, to reset, to gain creativity for the week, or "just have a **forced low** in life"), there is no problem. "The problem is when you don't notice the destruction that your habits are causing." When you are conscious of it and allow that bit of destruction, knowing you will do the things that reverse it and keep progressing, that is not bad (**Source:** `Most People Won’t Change (How To Recreate Yourself).md`, 2023-05-19). A **forced low** is his term for a deliberately chosen dip, such as rest or a reset, that does no harm because it is conscious and bounded. And in 2024 the shortest formulation: "**Awareness is a cure**. You don't have to quit all of your bad habits, you just have to view them and their consequences through the lens of your vision for long enough" (**Source:** `Disappear And Come Back Unrecognizable (12 Rules To Change Your Life).md`, 2024-04-14).

"Awareness is a cure" combines the two main ideas of this chapter. Awareness (root control, section 6.2) is necessary but not sufficient on its own; what makes it curative is the *lens of your vision* (section 6.5) through which the habit is viewed. Seen through a vision you care about, the habit's consequences become an error signal, and an error signal sustained "for long enough" produces correction without any act of prohibition. This is the cybernetic loop doing the work that willpower is usually asked to do.

In 2025 the principle reaches its most permissive-sounding form. "Do what you want without permission from someone else. Go to the party, get drunk, start the business, scroll on your phone all night, because denying those desires is only going to bind you to them." The catch: "you need to be able to realize when those things are a mistake." Getting drunk every night is not a mistake if you have no meaningful responsibilities each morning; it is not hurting a goal. "Managing parties and alcohol becomes a lot easier when it impacts something more important than parties and alcohol." Since school and a job are not more important to you, you do it anyway. "You need your own goals. And you can only generate those goals by getting absolutely fed up with where you are and rejecting everything you thought was true. You need to start from scratch" (**Source:** `12 Rules To Change Your Life In 12 Months.md`, 2025-09-14).

The argument has a precise structure under its provocative surface. Prohibition imposed from outside binds you to the desire, because the desire becomes the focus of attention (a narrow, unconscious fixation, in the terms of section 6.3). What frees you is not denial but a more important goal that the desire visibly damages. And such a goal cannot be borrowed; it has to be generated, which, in his account, requires being "fed up" with where you are, the brutal awareness that opens Chapter 7. The corpus records a nuance that came later: in 2026 Koe advises "starving the old self" of the normal feedback it receives, widening the gap between impulse and action. The two positions are compatible if the distinction is between repression imposed from outside, which he rejects, and the interruption of automatic behavior from inside, by conscious choice, which he recommends (Chapter 11).

#### Where the model leads

The chapter's argument can now be stated in one sequence. Conscious attention is a narrow, finite channel (6.1), and directing it is the only root control a person has (6.2). Its width and its quality can be read and changed, zooming in to work and out to regain perspective (6.3). The mind that directs it is a steering system, whose intelligence is the quality of its loop of acting, sensing, comparing and correcting (6.4). The reference signal of that loop, the goal, is a lens that determines what is perceived, learned and created (6.5). And because it determines both the system you build and the problems you can see, the goal is upstream of everything else (6.6).

The conclusion is the premise of Part IV. If you do not choose the goal, one is assigned, and the assigned goal will shape your attention, your perception, your systems and your sense of what is wrong just as efficiently as a chosen one would. The question is therefore not whether to have goals but how to choose them well: how to see the default trajectory clearly enough to reject it, how to articulate a direction you cannot yet fully see, how to break it into goals that work as lenses at every scale, and how to turn the pursuit into a game that produces flow. Chapter 7 begins with the first of those steps.

### Exercises

1. **Audit your RAM.** For three consecutive mornings, write down, before you touch your phone and again thirty minutes after, every "program" you notice running in your attention, using Koe's six categories (regretful past, stressful future, escape desires, the internal cry, mixed-priority tasks, open loops). Compare the two lists. Which programs were already running before any input? Which did the phone add? Which, if any, could a single "reboot" (writing, mindfulness or one focused task) actually close, and which would require a change of goal to close?

2. **Locate yourself in the Focus Matrix.** Over one working day, record your state at five random moments and place each one in a quadrant of the matrix (conscious or unconscious; narrow or open). For each moment spent in an unconscious quadrant, identify what moved you there and what move (horizontal, vertical, or both) would have taken you to the quadrant the situation required. Then assess the model: was there any state you could not place, or any moment where the quadrant's label did not match how you felt? What does that suggest about the model's limits?

3. **Run the cybernetic loop on a stalled goal.** Choose a goal you have pursued for some time without much progress. Describe it using the four components of 2026 (reference signal, sensor, comparator and error signal, actuator). Which component is weakest? Is the reference signal vague? Are you failing to sense where you actually are? Are you seeing the gap but not acting on it, or acting without comparing? Design one change to the weakest component and explain, in Koe's terms, why it should improve the loop.

4. **Two readers, one text.** Choose a short text you know well (a chapter, an essay, a long post). Reread it twice: first through the lens of a goal you currently hold, then through the lens of a very different goal (for example, one held by someone close to you, or one you held five years ago). Mark what each reading highlights. What does the comparison show about the "goal as lens" thesis? Is there anything in the text that both readings ignored, and what would it take to notice it?

5. **Problem or no problem?** List three habits you are ambivalent about (they cost you something, but you have not changed them). For each, name the goal, if any, that it currently damages, and estimate how much energy you have actually invested in that goal. Using section 6.6, explain why the habit has or has not registered as a problem. Then decide, for each one, whether the right move is to change the habit, to invest more energy in the goal so the habit becomes visible as a problem, or to accept it as a conscious "forced low." Defend each decision.

6. **Question a principle.** Koe says that high intelligence is the ability to "achieve any goal on a long enough time scale" and that "most of your excuses for not achieving a goal are a sign of low intelligence." Construct the strongest case you can against this claim, then the strongest case for it. Under what conditions does the principle stop applying? Is there a way to reformulate it that keeps what is useful and drops what is overstated?

7. **Goal first or problem first?** The author said in March 2024 to stop focusing on the goal and become aware of the problem, and in May 2024 to focus on the goal because focusing on the problem digs you deeper. Describe one situation in your own life where the first advice would be right and one where the second would be. What, in each situation, determines which one applies? Write a short rule of your own that would tell someone which to use.
