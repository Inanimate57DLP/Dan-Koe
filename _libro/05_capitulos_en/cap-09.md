## Chapter 9 — From Shallow Purpose to Life's Work: The Project

Chapter 8 ended with two redefinitions that change what "having a purpose" means in Dan Koe's system. Purpose is not a grand mission discovered through reflection but the most urgent problem in your life right now, and passion is not found but created by investing energy into a goal until it pays you back. Those definitions leave three questions open, and this chapter answers them in order. The first is a question of *legitimacy*: if your most urgent problem is money, a body you dislike or a breakup, is it acceptable to start there, or is that "shallow"? Koe's answer is that you must start there and cannot skip it (9.1). The second is a question of *trajectory*: if purpose is the problem you are solving now, how does it change as those problems get solved? Here the author offers a developmental map, the levels of purpose (survival, status, creativity, contribution) and its workplace version (job, career, calling) (9.2). The third is a question of *form*: what does pursuing a purpose look like on an ordinary Tuesday? The answer is the project, which Koe treats as the practical unit of change, the thing that turns a vision into something you can work on for an hour in the morning (9.3). The chapter closes with the project at its largest scale, the life's work, and with the author's claim that in the present era its most natural vessel is a business built on the internet (9.4). That last section anticipates Part X, where the business itself is examined, and the whole chapter prepares Chapter 10, where the project, the goal hierarchy and the levels of challenge are reassembled into a game.

### 9.1 Start Shallow: Why You Cannot Skip Levels

#### Fix yourself first

The section opens with a sequencing claim that runs against a familiar moral intuition. The intuition says that caring about others is nobler than caring about yourself, so the earlier you turn toward others the better. Koe says the opposite about *order*, not about value. Increasing your own quality of life is "what every single person I believe should aim to do before they aim to do other big things." It "doesn't make sense to go and put all of your cognitive power towards someone else's desires or building something outside of yourself instead of fixing yourself first" (**Source:** `Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md`, 2023-04-30).

The word "fix" is chosen deliberately, and he insists on its literal force: "When I say fix I do mean fix yourself." The justification comes straight from the diagnosis of Part I. By eighteen, he says, we are "socially conditioned to the end of hell," carrying an "insane amount of bad habits." The conditioning described in Chapter 1 is not a neutral starting point but damage that has to be reversed before you can approach other pursuits "from a better place." The four domains he names for that repair are mind, body, spirit and finances; before doing big and great things, and *in order to do them better*, each individual should put those four in order.

The reasoning is a dependency argument, not a selfishness argument. Your cognitive power is the instrument you would use for any larger project. If it is still running the conditioned program, the larger project inherits that program's distortions. Fixing yourself first is therefore not a detour on the way to contribution but the construction of the instrument that contribution will need. This is the first appearance in the chapter of a principle that will recur in every section: higher levels depend on lower ones, and neglecting the lower undermines the higher.

#### "Depth comes after you solve the shallow problems"

Koe formulates the same principle as a phrase that the lexicon records as his own coinage. Discussing whether his approach of attacking big, practical problems is shallow, he concedes the charge and turns it: "Yes, welcome to human life. Depth comes after you solve the shallow problems in your life and others" (**Source:** `Value Creation The Single Skill That Built My $1M Solo Business.md`, 2023-04-09).

The sentence has two halves. "In your life" refers to your own development; "and others" refers to how you help people. On the second half he is blunt: "nobody cares about mindfulness or existentialism or spirituality when they can't pay their bills." If someone's mind is "narrow, stressed and focused on bills and getting a girlfriend," which is his description of a typical eighteen-year-old, you cannot address them from the level of meaning. You have to work them beyond the surface problems, and you cannot let them skip those problems either. "These are the stages of development that you have to raise people up; your ego has to develop." The last clause links this directly to Chapter 3, where the ego was presented as something to be developed rather than eliminated: here the developmental path of the ego runs through the shallow problems, not around them.

#### The expanding circle of care, and "smart but dumb"

In 2026 Koe gives the principle its most theoretical form, drawing on a model of individual development as "an increasing circle of care or concern… from egocentric to ethnocentric to worldcentric to cosmocentric or reality itself." You are born selfish, then you identify with your tribe or group, then with humanity or the world, and further outward (**Source:** `How To Think Like A Strategic Genius (5-Dimensional Thinking).md`, 2026-02-22).

**Complementary context:** the sequence egocentric → ethnocentric → worldcentric → kosmocentric is Ken Wilber's, and "transcend and include" is Wilber's formula for how each stage relates to the one before: a higher stage goes beyond a lower one while containing it. Koe does not name Wilber in this passage; the attribution is inferred from the vocabulary and from the fact that he cites Wilber explicitly elsewhere in the corpus.

What Koe does with the model is to use it as a critique. "People try to skip levels. They don't understand transcend and include. They try to identify as this world-centric person who just cares about everyone's equality, but they don't understand that you also have to care about your group and yourself because those heavily impact how much you can care about the world." He compresses the dependency into a proverb ("You can't fill someone's glass if your own is empty") and into a harsh label: people who identify as world-centric virtuous people without having taken care of themselves and their group are "smart but dumb." "You do not sacrifice your own values or your group values for the sake of these world values that sound so noble when it doesn't work without the [former]." In the same passage he places a parallel physical sequence beside the mental one ("atom to cell to molecule to organism," in the order he states it), to show that each level exists only on top of the previous ones.

The distinction he is drawing is between *identifying* with a stage and *being* at it. A wider circle of care that has not passed through the narrower ones is, in his reading, a claimed identity rather than a developed capacity, and it tends to fail exactly where it matters: when caring for the world requires resources, stability or competence that the person never built for themselves.

#### Better something superficial than nothing

If depth comes after the shallow problems, the practical consequence is that you should not be embarrassed to begin with a shallow goal. Against the excuse "I don't know what to do," Koe says you need to take "that first shallow level thing," because "purpose is found through solving your own problems and busting down through those layers from shallow to deep." Then the line that made the idea memorable: "I don't care if you start going to the gym because you broke up with your girlfriend, I don't care if you start trying to make money because you want a Lamborghini" (**Source:** `Why Cheap Dopamine Prevents You From Becoming High Value.md`, 2022-10-09).

The argument behind this permission is epistemological. "The only way you are going to figure out what you truly want is by doing what you think you want and then realizing that's not what you want." This is the experimental route to knowing what you want that Chapter 7 developed (you never know exactly what you want; exposure reveals it), applied to purpose. Once you have pursued the shallow want, two continuations are open. You can keep pursuing the same thing but "developing a different why because you find it important," or you can pursue something else "from a more enlightened state or from a better position." Neither continuation is a failure: "It's okay to start over from a better position because you are going to do it better that way."

A year later he condenses the point into a lesson he draws from bodybuilding: "it's better to pursue something superficial than it is to do nothing with your life." Again two outcomes are possible, and both are gains: "you either learn what you don't want so you can pursue what you want, or you find depth, meaning and spirituality along the way" (**Source:** `A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Life (In 6-12 Months).md`, 2023-07-25). The structure of the argument is worth noticing. It does not claim that superficial goals are secretly deep; it claims that pursuing *any* goal produces information, and that the information is valuable whichever way it points. Doing nothing produces no information at all.

#### Material goals as portals

Koe applies the argument to himself without much defensiveness. Many people, himself included, are introduced to the world of self-improvement and entrepreneurship through the pursuit of cars and houses, and he says he would be lying if he claimed he wasn't, and isn't still, interested in them. What he discovered by going down that path is that it is not "completely materialistic": pursuing a superficial goal took him down "a deeply spiritual path of highs and lows and failures," in which he learned to master his mind, to become an actual entrepreneur and to solve creative problems. His philosophy of money, he adds, has since shifted toward money as a tool to build what does not yet exist (**Source:** `The Future Of Work Is Play (How To Create A Digital Career).md`, 2023-12-31). The phrase he uses, "a superficial goal that takes you down a deeply spiritual path," is the compact version of the whole section.

There is, however, a condition, and without it the permission becomes a trap. People think they want the nice car, the nice job, travel, "but once they do those things the shine, the superficial shine, wears fast." So they keep convincing themselves that the next nice thing is what they want, "but they never use those nice things as a portal into something deeper; they never dig deeper and let the material object shine a light on the true problem that they're trying to solve." They get the car, the cushy job, the fancy vacations, and "never seem to evolve or push deeper or realize what their nature really wants, what they really want, not what their ego wants" (**Source:** `If Your Life Is Spiraling Out Of Control, Here's The Solution To Feeling Lost.md`, 2024-03-10).

The term *portal* (the lexicon lists it as his, from this single passage) carries the mechanism. A material goal is not the destination; it is a door through which you can see a problem you could not see before. Achieving the goal "shines a light" on what you were actually trying to solve: status, acceptance, security, boredom. The person who uses the object as a portal looks through it; the person who does not simply buys the next object. The distinction between "what your nature wants" and "what your ego wants" is not developed further in this passage, but its use is clear: the ego is satisfied by the shine, which wears off; the deeper want is revealed only if you ask what the shine was standing in for.

#### Shallow and selfish problems first, and the inner core

In 2024 Koe announced that he would make a video about his life philosophy, to which he gave a name the transcript renders as "kism" (the exact word is ambiguous in the source and should not be relied upon). The preview states the core: "you need to probably be shallow and selfish when you're just starting out in order to reach the deeper metaphysical and meaningful," because unsolved shallow problems "weigh you down" until they are solved. Focus on the problem "staring you in the face" (health, money, competency) before "more profound, complex issues that require a more developed mind to benefit from" (**Source:** `Minimalist Productivity Work Less, Do More.md`, 2024-08-11). The last clause matters: the deep problems are not dismissed; they are said to *require a more developed mind to benefit from*. Tackled too early, they do not produce depth; they produce confusion or pretense.

The same year he gives an example that shows how a "deep" problem can turn out to have a shallow root. Lacking self-esteem looks like a psychological problem, but it may be a health problem: "maybe you lack muscle mass or dislike how you look," a surface-level problem. Referring back to his own video on finding your purpose by solving your own problems, he says you must solve the shallow external problems first and then "bust through to your inner core to get to the meaning and purpose," because that core has been "shrouded by self-comparison" (**Source:** `The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md`, 2024-02-06). The *inner core* is the place where meaning and purpose live; self-comparison is what hides it; and the shallow problems are what keep self-comparison active. Solve them and the comparison loses its fuel.

The passage also connects the principle to business, which section 9.4 will develop. You cannot ignore shallow problems, because most people struggle with them, and once you have solved one yourself you can help others with it: learn the gym, find a unique take or your own system for getting in shape. He immediately adds a caution against cynical use of the idea: "have some common sense and be smart about what you're selling." Solving a shallow problem for yourself gives you something to offer; it does not license selling a solution you have not really found.

By 2025 the idea has hardened from a prerequisite into a source. The pursuit of greatness, he observes, is often shot down by people who never do anything great, who see it as shallow or materialistic and spotlight only money or fame ("You're a bad person because you want to make money or be famous"). He says he does not want to be famous and does not consider himself famous; his influence is a by-product of creating value, such as a YouTube video. Then: "In my personal opinion, the deepest and most meaningful things come from the pursuit of materialistic or shallow things. Because you can only reach the depths once you've scratched the surface." He adds that pursuing only spirituality neglects the other domains (material, money, mind, body, health), all of which a well-rounded individual needs (**Source:** `I'm 28. Here's How To Get Ahead Of Most 20 Year Olds.md`, 2025-07-27).

The evolution record of the corpus classifies this as a refinement rather than a change of position. In 2022–2024 the shallow is a *prerequisite* for the deep (solve it first, then go deeper); in 2025 it is the *source* of the deep (the deepest things come from the pursuit of shallow things). The two are compatible, but the second is stronger. It is also explicitly marked by the author as "my personal opinion," and it should be read that way: it is a claim about where meaning tends to come from, based on his experience, not an empirical finding.

#### Spirituality as a status hat

The principle has a mirror image, and Koe states it as a warning. "Most people want to start with the deep spiritual and metaphysical problems that reside in their core." If you have not done even the slightest self-help work (going to the gym, training, fixing nutrition and habits, acquiring high-value skills and knowledge), "you're in for a bad time." His diagnosis of people who try to be spiritual without that groundwork is that they are "doing it for superficial reasons": they wear spirituality "as a status hat" and use "spirituality as a nobility card to avoid making any form of contribution to humanity by pursuing goals" (**Source:** `Most People Won't Change (How To Recreate Yourself).md`, 2023-05-19).

The irony is intended. The person who skips the shallow in the name of depth turns out to be the shallow one, because depth itself has become a status symbol. The *status hat* and *nobility card* (both his coinages, from this one passage) are two functions of the same move: the hat displays superiority, the card excuses inaction. The connection to the circle of care is direct: the "smart but dumb" world-centric identity and the spirituality-as-status-hat are the same error at two scales, a higher identity claimed without the lower development that would make it real.

#### Selflessness requires selfish values

The ethical core of the section is a tweet that Koe reads aloud in 2022: "selflessness requires selfish values." The selfish values he means are the "self-" virtues: self-control, self-reliance, self-reflection, self-education, self-awareness, self-employment, self-management, self-experimentation, "and so on." The argument is short: "it's impossible to be selfless when you are dependent on others for your survival." He has seen many people who want to be selfless "out of the gate," wanting to give value before they have created any by prioritizing themselves. And he adds a balancing note that keeps the slogan from becoming a license: it requires balance, not being "a jerk all the time" (**Source:** `Why You Can't Be Yourself (The Power Of Being Alone).md`, 2022-11-13). The title of the video makes the link to Chapter 3 explicit: the first step to being yourself is prioritizing yourself.

"Selfish" here is a common word with a special sense. It does not mean indifference to others; it names the cluster of capacities by which a person becomes able to stand on their own. In this sense the selfish values are the conditions of selflessness, because only someone who is not dependent can give freely rather than give in exchange for survival.

Two years later the same values reappear as the entry requirement of his business model. "It is completely in your hands." If you do not value self-reliance, personal responsibility or taking control of your own life, "this isn't for you yet," until you realize that "if you want true happiness those are going to have to become values of yours" (**Source:** `The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md`, 2024-02-06). In the same passage he mentions that his analytics show an audience mostly between eighteen and thirty-five, with some outliers in their forties and fifties whom he applauds; the detail is incidental, but it confirms that he is addressing people at the beginning of the sequence this section describes. The claim about "true happiness" is a strong one and is offered as conviction, not as argument; within his system it follows from the view that happiness depends on agency, which Part XII develops.

#### The body underpins everything

If higher levels depend on lower ones, the lowest level in a human life is the body, and Koe draws that consequence explicitly. "The destruction of the lower leads to the destruction of the higher," he says, calling it a major tenet of thinking in wholes and parts. His examples run across scales: destroying the earth destroys humans and the level of mind with them ("literal suicide"); a cancer cell or virus that takes over sickens and, if unfixed, kills the organism; removing a sentence from a paragraph often makes the paragraph nonsensical (**Source:** `You Need To Be Extreme If You Want Your Life To Change.md`, 2025-06-29). Koe does not name Wilber here; the adaptation of Wilber's holon tenet is inferred from the vocabulary of wholes and parts.

The application is personal and uncompromising. You live in your body; it "should be your full-time job" to learn about it, train it and treat it with respect, and anything else is "literal suicide." A lack of health "shoots upward" into your mind's creative ability, the quality of your work and the depth of your relationships: "It's all connected." The practical instruction is modest by comparison: commit to one workout, whatever you are most pulled to (a running program, weightlifting, Pilates, yoga); the point is to pay attention to the body "that lets you be here every day."

His guest Dickie Bush supplies the same idea from the side of experience. Fitness, Bush says, "is a funnel into everything else": when you take ownership of your fitness and overall health, it becomes "a gateway drug to business, to taking just full ownership of everything." Both hosts trace their business paths to fitness journeys and to fitness creators, and Bush later observes that when members of his writing program start writing every day, "every other area of your life improves, you start to take your fitness more seriously" (**Source:** `Dan Koe & Dickie Bush On One-Person Businesses, Creative Workflows, and Lifestyle Design.md`, 2023-03-16). The "gateway drug" formulation is Bush's; Koe later applies it to personal growth leading into business (Chapter 28).

Koe also defends the body's priority against a common way of framing mental distress. Asked whether feeling lost and anxious is "the whole case of the chemical imbalance," he answers yes, "but I don't think people understand what they're saying when they actually say that." Some people do have a chemical imbalance. But "everything you put into your body is a chemical," so if the chemistry of the system is not maintained "through the means available to you, whether it be natural, unnatural, whatever it may be, chemistry is chemistry," disorder occurs: "there is a blockage there" (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16). The argument should be read carefully. He does not deny clinical conditions, and he explicitly allows "unnatural" means; his point is that the phrase "chemical imbalance" is often used as if chemistry were something that happens to you, when much of it is the result of what you eat, how you move and how you live. That is an interpretation, not a medical claim, and the corpus offers no evidence beyond it.

In 2025 he adds physiological detail, presented as facts about exercise: "30-plus minutes of exercise provides steady and sustainable dopamine release. Physical movement is one of the most reliable flow triggers. It creates BDNF, or brain-derived neurotrophic factor, supporting neuroplasticity. Strength training lowers the risk of heart disease, diabetes, cancer, and early death. Plus, muscle protects against frailty as you get older. More muscle equals higher BMR equals you can eat more without gaining fat, and it boosts mitochondrial density and efficiency, which equals more energy." To the objection that lifting is risky he answers, "yes, if you're stupid or don't know what you're doing," and the steps he gives are to learn about it, go to the gym, and use the training program he co-created with his trainer (Superhuman 90) (**Source:** `A Full Dopamine Detox Protocol To Reset Your Life In 30 Days.md`, 2025-10-26). The commercial mention is part of the source and should be read as such.

**Complementary context:** BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) is a protein involved in the growth and maintenance of neurons; BMR (basal metabolic rate) is the energy the body uses at rest. The author states the benefits without citing studies; the general association between exercise and these markers is widely reported, but the specific formulations are his.

The corpus records a shift of emphasis here. In 2023, in conversation with Dickie Bush, Koe said that the details of diet barely matter, called excessive self-optimization a "destructive self-improvement game," and defended a degree of "degeneracy" against what he called the "Huberman cult." By 2025 he describes caring for the body as a full-time job and gives concrete protocols. He gives no explicit reason for the change, and he keeps his criticism of dogmatism; the 2025 protocols are presented as a baseline rather than an ideology. The two positions are compatible if one reads the first as a critique of obsessive optimization and the second as a demand for the minimum without which nothing higher works.

#### The author's lesser purpose

Koe's own life illustrates the section, and he tells the story as an instance of the pattern rather than as an exception to it. As a kid he was "tired of not being able to get girls even though I wasn't trying" ("every young dude's thing"). After his first breakup he thought, "okay, I'm going to get back at her… and just go to the gym and get jacked." In hindsight he finds it funny, but he is clear about the mechanism: "I went to the gym because there was a big pain in my life and I wanted to solve that." He calls this a *lesser purpose*. Solving it took time, and "you may not know if it's solved or not," but once it is being solved, the next purpose presents itself, because "you're just slowly and consistently solving better problems" (**Source:** `Society Is A Pyramid Scheme (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md`, 2023-01-15; repeated almost word for word in `Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md`, 2024-03-21). The phrase "solving better problems" is the bridge to Chapter 8: if purpose is the most urgent problem, the quality of your purpose rises with the quality of the problems you are able to have.

The corpus contains several versions of when he started training: after his first breakup (2023, 2024), in eighth grade with a teacher he names as Mr. Schroeder (2024), from age fifteen (2024), in high school with a book on nutrient timing (2025). These are narrative variations that are compatible with one another, and none of them changes the point.

A second story shows what a dominant goal does to everything around it. In his first or second year of lifting, in after-school weights in high school, he and his friend Jerry competed daily: "All we cared about was bench." He reached a 275-pound maximum by year two, "extremely fast because that's all I cared about. My nutrition followed that. My life followed that. All I was watching on YouTube was how to lift more." Ten years later he no longer cares about benching 315 (he estimates his current maximum at around 285, perhaps 315 untested); the point of the story is the second sentence, which he immediately applies to skill acquisition (**Source:** `How To Master Any Skill Fast (Give Yourself 2 Weeks).md`, 2025-08-10). This is the "goal as lens" of Chapter 6 seen in a teenage body: once one goal dominated, nutrition, schedule and media consumption reorganized themselves around it without separate decisions.

A third story shows the lesser purpose turning out to contain a general pattern. For most of his life he studied fitness and nutrition in his free time. When he started building his business and seeing results, he recognized it: "This is progressive overload." In training, progressive overload means "slowly increasing the weight over time. You're not trying to jump up in huge leaps and bounds… You increase by 2.5 lb or 5 lb. And the nutrition you eat has to fuel the recovery to facilitate that, and you use that as a metric for how much you're growing"; consistent strength gain "is a great proxy for muscle growth," and "all of the variables have to come together in order to build muscle." In business, likewise, "you need all of the variables to come together, and you're not aiming for a viral explosion of sales like many of the startups are. You're aiming for the slow, consistent growth, and year after year you look back and you're like, holy crap, this has been an exponential line" (**Source:** `The Art of Strategic Thinking (How to Become Wildly Successful at Anything You Do).md`, 2026-08-19). The shallow pursuit did not just lead to a deeper one; it trained a way of seeing that later transferred to a domain he could not have anticipated.

Finally, he identifies what keeps an achieved shallow goal from going stale. The reason he is "so far ahead in many areas," he says, is that he never stopped learning about the gym; he has not reached a point where he knows everything. At least once a day (he immediately corrects himself: "not every day") he listens to a ten-to-twenty-minute video on fitness, business, mindset, spirituality or philosophy, all mixed, a "constant flow of energy" to experiment with. He insists this is not optional. The main failure he sees is people achieving a goal such as a good body, maintaining it, and then finding it repetitive and mundane, with nothing new to experiment with and "no good dopamine going into your head because you've stopped learning" (**Source:** `You Aren't Successful Because You Care Too Much (How To Stop).md`, 2023-08-08). This is the failure mode that the portal idea predicted: an achieved goal that is not used to open a further problem loses its shine. Continued learning is one way of keeping the portal open. Chapter 10 will describe the same phenomenon in the language of games: when challenge stops rising, boredom follows.

### 9.2 The Levels of Purpose

#### A developmental map of purpose

Section 9.1 established a direction (shallow before deep) without saying what the stages are. In a 2024 video on money, Koe supplies them in what he calls "a concept of mind called the levels of purpose." The video starts from money because, he argues, "your perception of money is determined by your level of development," and "the solution to your money problems, or psychological problems, is to advance through each level." The central sentence describes a sequence: "The purpose of money changes as you develop yourself: it starts as a way to survive, becomes a method to acquire status, then becomes a fuel for creativity and expression, then finally transforms into a way to help others advance through similar stages, related to money or not." The four levels are therefore survival, status, creativity and contribution (**Source:** `You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md`, 2024-09-29; all quotations in this section through "transcend and include" are from this video unless another source is given).

Two generalizations follow immediately. First, "these levels don't only apply to money; they apply to almost everything that you do in your life." Second, your "main purpose is what determines how you perceive situations." The second claim connects the framework to the cybernetic model of Chapter 6: a purpose is a goal at the top of the hierarchy, and the goal is a lens. Moving from one level to the next is therefore not only a change of motive but a change of what you are able to see.

| Level | What money (or any pursuit) is for | How you enter it | How you leave it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Survival | A way to survive | Default; stress can return you here at any time | Solve material needs; develop a skill; get a job |
| Status | A method to acquire status, power and influence | Security around money plus the realization that you need others to perceive you as valuable | Notice quickly the impact of superficial purchases; correct the mistake |
| Creativity | Fuel for creativity and expression | Status achieved; mistakes become new problems; craving for autonomy | Enough skill and experimentation to see you can deeply affect others |
| Contribution | A way to help others advance through similar stages | The pull to share what improved your life | Not an exit but an integration: work, rest and play collapse into one |

The table is a reconstruction from the video's sequence. The "how you leave it" column for contribution is an interpretation: Koe does not describe an exit from the fourth level, but he does say the levels reset when you start something new (see below).

#### The gym across the four levels

The first example he gives is the one 9.1 has already prepared. "You go to the gym for status and survival first: you go because you want to look good, you go because you want to be accepted by others. Once you achieve that purpose, then it transforms into something creative: you start going for longevity, you start discovering your own why behind it. And then contribution: you start passing down what you know because you know so much and you know you can help other people, and that's what brings you fulfillment." The example matters because it shows the levels operating within one activity. The behavior (going to the gym) does not change; its purpose does, and with it the meaning of the activity. This is exactly the "different why" that the 2022 passage in 9.1 described as one of the two possible continuations of a shallow pursuit.

#### Level one: survival

"Every belief we listed before is pursuing the goal and purpose of survival," Koe says, referring to the limiting beliefs about money catalogued earlier in the video. The defining feature of the survival level is what it does to attention: "You won't notice a money-making opportunity in the survival stage because your attention is solely consumed by the house, the mortgage, being tired from your job, the relationship problems that splinter off because of those, and barely having enough money to think." This is the "attention is the RAM" model of Chapter 6 applied to economic stress: the background programs of survival occupy the workspace, and opportunities that are objectively present never register.

He stresses that survival is a state, not only a phase: "This isn't a static stage; you can go into it at any time: if you're extremely stressed you're probably in survival mode," unable to notice opportunities or find "the same zest in life." If you are stuck there, "you have no idea what life has to offer." In this stage "you take any job you can get because you have to survive," and when "your goals are the product of your conditioning you don't have many options for work you enjoy." Yet he refuses to moralize: "This isn't a bad thing." "You need to solve your material needs before you can move forward": work your way up, develop your skill, get a job. The survival level is not something to despise but something to master.

The idea has a 2022 antecedent that frames the same point as a competition. Most people, he wrote then, cannot pursue curiosity consistently or full time because their attention is "manhandled" by base psychological needs; he refers to the hierarchy of needs and describes self-actualization as "a need, not a want, for living the good life." Therefore "your future isn't about competition with others, it's about competition with your distracted mind." An exceptional future belongs to those who master their survival, pursue their genuine curiosity and pass down what they learned. The sequence he gives is: improve yourself, solve your own problems, master your survival, document your journey, pursue your curiosity, and separate yourself from the crowd, with the caveat that "how you do this is unique to you" (**Source:** `The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md`, 2022-12-18). Read beside the 2024 framework, this passage already contains three of the four levels in embryo: survival mastered, curiosity pursued (creativity), and learning passed down (contribution). Status is the level that the 2024 version adds.

**Complementary context:** the hierarchy of needs is Abraham Maslow's model, in which physiological and safety needs must be reasonably met before higher needs (belonging, esteem, self-actualization) become motivating. Koe does not name Maslow in this transcript. His levels of purpose are an adaptation, not a reproduction: they are organized around the purpose of money and of work rather than around needs, and they add an explicit "contribution" stage beyond self-actualization.

#### Level two: status

The second level is the one most likely to provoke resistance, and Koe anticipates it. "There's never been a time where weak, average or mindless people weren't treated like cattle"; they were "herded around because they didn't have the skill or independent thought to do something themselves; they didn't have the autonomy." "The only people who do well in society are those who gain power and influence." He then asks the listener to "throw all of your definitions of power and influence out the window," because "if you have a bad relationship with money you have a bad relationship with those words." Power and influence "aren't always evil." "The best way to get power is through empowerment and education and persuasion instead of manipulation; there are ethical ways to gain power." And the decisive premise: "You can only impact and influence and contribute to people, contribute to humanity, if you have power." He is explicit that this is a stipulated definition for the purposes of the video: "if it's not power then think of a different word."

The function of this argument within the framework is to make status a *necessary* stage rather than a moral lapse. Contribution, the fourth level, requires the capacity to affect others; that capacity is what he calls power; and status is the stage at which it is acquired. A person who tries to jump from survival to contribution without passing through status repeats the error of the "smart but dumb" world-centric identity of 9.1: wanting to help without the means to help.

Entry and exit are both specified. "You reach the status stage when (1) you have a sense of security around money and (2) you realize the need for others to perceive you as valuable." You cultivate a skill set that gets you a better job or lets you start a business; that income lets you pay bills, focus on other areas and make "moderate to extravagant superficial purchases" (the new car, the house). And "the way to get out of the status stage is you need to realize the impact of those things on your life fast." There is an exception that keeps the criterion from becoming puritanical: "if you're a mechanic and you love cars there's nothing wrong with getting a car; that's where you find your meaning in life." But if the car is "just this superficial status symbol for you" and you don't realize it yet, "the way out of the status stage is to realize your mistake and try to correct it." The exit criterion is the portal idea from 9.1 restated: the purchase is a mistake only if it is a dead end; noticing quickly what it did (and did not do) for you is what turns it into a door.

The corpus records one place where this criterion meets the author's own biography. Early in his business he rented an apartment far above what he could afford. In 2022 and 2023 he told the story as a bold move that preceded his first big month; in 2025 he reframed it as a deliberately imposed deadline (a purchase that forced him to develop skills), explicitly distinguishing it from lifestyle creep and status spending. The magnitude of the rent also varies between tellings. The reframing is consistent with the status-stage criterion: what matters is not whether a purchase is expensive but whether it serves growth or only display.

Against "non-critical thinkers" who "see this as bad but don't understand perception or psychology," Koe gives a general principle to justify the status stage: "humans must notice the surface before they notice the depth." His illustration is from relationships: "I don't care how great of a personality you have, people do care about looks before deciding to get into a relationship with them." Perception starts at the surface, so the surface is not optional. The principle restates "depth comes after you solve the shallow problems" from the side of the perceiver rather than the developer: just as you must solve your shallow problems before your deep ones become reachable, others must perceive your surface before your depth becomes visible to them. The lexicon groups the status, creativity and contribution stages as terms that appear only in this 2024 video.

#### Level three: creativity

"Once you've achieved some level of status your mind will start to transform; you'll begin to realize all of your mistakes; those mistakes are new problems to solve, a new purpose to pursue." The transition is described in terms that Chapter 8 prepared: a new purpose appears as a new problem. "You may even feel the need to reject everything you've acquired." "The money gets old, sleeping around gets old, the cars and watches get old." But the transition is not automatic: "you can get trapped in any one of these stages." The trapped case is "the typical stereotype of the rich businessman on a yacht with all the watches," stuck in status "and abusing it." For those who move on, "money gains more meaning the more you develop yourself through these stages," and "you'll start to crave autonomy; you'll realize how slow the progression becomes as you advance higher in the status game, and want to take on harder challenges."

The creativity stage has a characteristic task. "At this point your focus must shift to developing a philosophical sense of mastery around a few pursuits." He returns to bodybuilders: "going to the gym starts as a pursuit to feel good about themselves; after a few years they either quit or reorient toward a new, deeper destination that makes the journey more meaningful." They find joy in feeling good from healthy food, understand deeply "how their health choices impact their future," and "gain fulfillment from the process of making tiny improvements in the gym." "They shift from vanity to therapy, they shift from looks to longevity." The formula *from vanity to therapy, from looks to longevity* describes a change in what the activity is for, and it explains why the same person can keep doing the same thing for decades without boredom: the activity has been re-founded on a deeper purpose.

The work of this stage is described as a move from borrowed to original knowledge. "In the status stage most of what you learn and do is assigned to you by other people: your parents, your boss, your job." Once you gain traction with your skill set, see your value and crave autonomy, "you take your expanded knowledge and you start to create your own way of doing things, and this is where the magic starts to happen; this is where you become irreplaceable." "You've tried different training programs, business models and coping strategies to the point of realizing the patterns and principles between them all; you've unlocked a level of mind that allows you to navigate with grace between them." The instruction is radical: "Your job in the creativity stage is to simply create: create more things, create everything; absolutely reject what you've been told and start to do things from scratch to see if you can create a better way. Break free from the dogmas and ideologies and processes you've adopted from others; build novel solutions from scratch."

Two connections are worth making. "Assigned" knowledge links the creativity stage to the assigned goals of Chapter 1: the move into creativity is the moment at which a person stops running someone else's program in the domain of skill. And "patterns and principles between them all" anticipates Chapter 15's account of mastery and Chapter 18's account of originality: you become original not by ignoring existing methods but by trying enough of them to see what they share and then building from that shared core. The phrase "level of mind" connects back to Chapter 4: creativity is a stage reached by raising the level of mind, not by deciding to be creative.

#### Level four: contribution, and the concentric circles

"With a combination of a high value skill set and enough creative experimentation you realize that you can deeply impact others' lives and feel the pull to do so." This is the fourth level. Koe then gives the framework its spatial image: "If we think of levels of purpose as concentric circles, like one circle on the outside of another, four circles total, there's a pull to go deeper." He identifies the pull with "that inner voice," "the voice telling you that you were meant for more in life," and "by listening to that you start to bust through these purposes: you have to make money, then you have to gain status, then you have to be creative, and now it's time to contribute to other people and help humanity."

The metaphor of concentric circles corrects a possible misreading of the table. The levels are not rungs on a ladder you climb away from; they are layers you move inward through. Survival is the outermost circle, the one you encounter first and the one that surrounds everything else; contribution is the center. The "pull to go deeper" is the experience of being drawn inward.

The contribution stage has a distinctive phenomenology. "In this stage you feel a deep desire to share the things that have improved your life; you begin to see life from a new perspective and wonder why others are constantly distracted with menial things; you can see where their life is heading but they can't." And the domains of life fuse: "The contribution stage is where the used-to-be separate domains of your life collapse into one." You no longer see work as "somewhere that you clock in and clock out," rest as "a treat that you can only indulge in once work is done," or play as "a hobby that lasts 30 minutes at night." "You see all of them as necessary counterbalances to one another; work, rest and play become difficult to distinguish." "Rest becomes a way to regenerate your creative ability for your work; play becomes what you do for work; work is so deeply integrated with your life that anything you do can be passed down in the form of products or information." "Your entire life begins to revolve around how you can best contribute to the world." This description is the bridge to Part V: the 4-Hour Workday, the treatment of rest and the idea that "the future of work is play" are, in this frame, the practical shape of a life at the contribution level.

At this level the person becomes what Koe calls "a perspective vessel for reality: you make sense of the world from your mind and pass that perspective for others to adopt and benefit from." "You hunt for and gather information, synthesize it with your experience and distribute it to those who want to benefit from it." "You become less of a leech: you don't only consume and take from reality for your selfish personal game, but you create, share and contribute back to the world." The *perspective vessel* appeared in Chapter 3 as a description of every person (each of us is a vessel through which reality passes and is interpreted). Here the term gains an active sense: at the contribution level the vessel does not only receive and interpret but also distributes. The "hunt and gather" vocabulary links to the hunter psyche of Chapter 2, and "less of a leech" reverses the consumption-without-creation pattern that Chapter 11 will diagnose. In the video Koe points to his one-person business playlist and to a forthcoming video on "the death of the personal brand"; the latter is one of the sources of section 9.4.

#### The law of transcend and include, and the reset

Two properties make the levels a developmental model rather than a simple ranking. The first is that they follow what Koe calls "the transcend and include law": "you never escape the survival stage; you can go back and fall down and be in survival mode for a certain amount of time; you can go through these cycles, but you transcend and include: you go up a stage and then you include a lot of the principles from survival in that stage because you can't do it any other way." His analogies are structural: "it's like matter to life to mind, or a word to a sentence to a paragraph," "a whole and a part." "If you were to remove matter from the earth, life and mind would be gone." The conclusion, that without survival and status, creativity and contribution cannot exist, is the reverse side of "the destruction of the lower leads to the destruction of the higher" from 9.1. (The transcript literally says "can exist," but the reasoning requires "can't"; this is an ambiguity of transcription, not of argument.) In this transcript Koe does not attribute "transcend and include" or the whole/part idea to anyone.

**Complementary context:** as noted in 9.1, "transcend and include" is Ken Wilber's description of how holons (wholes that are also parts of larger wholes) are organized in developmental sequences. Koe uses it elsewhere with attribution and here as a "law" without a source. Chapter 38 treats Wilber's maps in full.

The second property is that the levels *reset*. "They reset when you start a business: when you quit your job to start a business you're back in survival mode; you need status to acquire the right connections and resources; you begin to crave autonomy from things like client work and tasks you can delegate; you free up mental energy to be creative; eventually you will realize that your main purpose is to help other people." The reset clarifies how the model is meant to be used. It is not a map of a single lifetime arc, after which one lives permanently at the center, but a cycle that repeats whenever you enter a new domain or a new game. This is consistent with the "chapters" of life described in Chapter 39 and with the infinite game of Chapter 10: every new game starts with survival.

#### How the levels reconcile two definitions of purpose

The levels of purpose resolve an apparent tension in the author's definitions of purpose. In 2023–2024 he insisted that purpose is the most urgent problem in your life right now and that "your purpose is not to spread love and be selfless" if you have not developed yourself (Chapter 8). In mid-2024 he wrote that "hopefully a purpose is a goal that has a positive impact on others." The evolution record of the corpus classifies this not as a retraction but as a refinement: the levels integrate both formulations. Purpose starts as your own problem (survival, status) and becomes contribution later, and "you cannot skip steps." Both definitions are true, at different stages.

#### Job, career, calling

Later in the same video Koe applies the levels to work, in a second triad that the lexicon also lists as his: job, career and calling. He defines the middle term first. "A career is a commitment to development in your work. A career demands that you pursue a hierarchy of challenging roles and tasks. Psychologically this brings long-term order and clarity to your mind. With each level of challenge life becomes more complex and interesting; new paths for knowledge and skill acquisition become apparent." And he adds a condition that links it back to 9.1: "If you want to progress further in a career, to an extent you have to have your life together."

The definition is generous to careers in a way that the corpus's critique of employment (Chapter 2) might not lead one to expect. A career supplies what Chapter 5 called order in consciousness and what Chapter 8 called a hierarchy of goals: rising challenges, clarity about the next step, expanding knowledge. Its limit is not that it lacks structure but that the structure is not yours.

The triad follows the same law as the levels: "a job is not a career but a career is a job; a job and a career are not a calling but a calling is a career and a job." The calling is then defined: "A calling is work you can't pull yourself away from and others can't help but pay you for." "A calling can't be assigned to you"; it "cannot be pursued under the orders of another"; it "cannot be defined by a set amount of working hours because your mind is always working on it." "A calling is found at the point where improvement turns into obsession." And it needs protection: "A calling is something others won't understand, something that must be cared for, protected and maintained by the one pursuing it, like a gift that others could accidentally steal."

The definition contains two tests, one internal and one external. The internal test is inability to stop ("can't pull yourself away from"); the external test is that others pay ("can't help but pay you for"). A passion without the second is a hobby; paid work without the first is a job or a career. The phrase "where improvement turns into obsession" locates the calling not at the start of a pursuit but at a threshold reached through it, which is consistent with Chapter 8's thesis that passion is created by investment rather than found.

The three are matched to three kinds of person: "Jobs are great for young people who don't know what they want to do yet and simply need to survive. Careers are great for those who want a bit more satisfaction in life because they understand the need for challenging work as a forcing function for self-development. And a calling is for those who know they were meant for more, the select few who are willing to take the plunge into the unknown and take full responsibility for the outcome of their life." The phrase "meant for more" repeats the "inner voice" of the concentric circles, which shows that the two triads are designed to map onto each other.

| | Job | Career | Calling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who it suits | Young people who need to survive and don't yet know what they want | People who want challenge as a forcing function for development | Those who know they were meant for more and accept full responsibility |
| Who sets the goal | The employer or client | The organization's hierarchy of roles | You; it cannot be assigned |
| What it gives | Skills, industry knowledge, income | Long-term order and clarity; rising complexity | Work you can't stop and others pay for |
| Its risk or limit | Complacency; crystallized identity | You don't control the goal; flow drivers are weak | Others won't understand it; it must be protected |
| Relation to the others | — | Includes the job | Includes job and career |

The job's risk deserves the fuller account Koe gives in a separate 2024 video. "I am not against jobs by any means; I see them for what they are and they are a stepping stone." "A job is useful for acquiring skills and industry knowledge but it can also harm one's psyche and breed complacency." The chain he traces is precise. In a job you almost inevitably "stop solving new problems" and get stuck "solving the same problems over and over again." Those are problems "you didn't choose to solve in the first place," assigned to you "because the goal that allowed the problem to exist was assigned to you by your employer or your client." So you get bored and start wasting time. But by then you have "so many responsibilities in life and such a crystallized identity that your survival depends on those repetitive boring problems." Bad habits keep you "low energy, narrow-minded and stressed," so new problems, such as "blocking out work to actually do what you want in life," don't even register "unless someone makes you aware of them" (**Source:** `How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md`, 2024-05-26). The argument uses the whole model built so far: goal → problem (Chapter 8), assigned goals (Chapter 1), crystallized identity (Chapter 3), the goal as perceptual filter (Chapter 6). The job's danger is not the work but the fact that its goal is not yours, and a goal you did not choose produces problems that do not develop you.

In 2026 Koe adds the career's limit in the vocabulary of games and flow. "You have job, career, calling, each different level of purposes." With a career you can climb higher, but "you're not the one in control of the goal," so you are not in charge of the level of challenge or of what it represents; it is like being told which quest to pursue in a video game when you want to be on the other side of the map. You are not working on your own projects. And "curiosity, passion, purpose, autonomy, mastery, those are the five drivers of the flow state"; in a career or job you are much less likely to have any of them (**Source:** `How To Become So Valuable AI Can Never Replace You.md`, 2026-06-28).

The five drivers have their own history in the corpus. In 2022 Koe cited them as Steven Kotler's "five intrinsic drivers," at first with some doubt about the attribution; in 2024 he attributed them to Kotler and reordered them as his own top-down adaptation; by 2025–2026 he presents them as "the five drivers of the flow state" without a source. The list is identical throughout. The attribution, then, should be given to Kotler, with the adaptation and the use made of it here (as a diagnostic of why careers rarely produce flow) given to Koe. Chapter 10 treats the drivers in full.

**Complementary context:** Steven Kotler, a writer on peak performance, describes curiosity, passion, purpose, autonomy and mastery as intrinsic motivators that, stacked together, make flow more accessible. The video-game image ("the other side of the map") anticipates the life-as-a-game framework of Chapter 10.

Finally, Koe draws the conclusion that links the triad to section 9.4. Referring to "the levels of purpose" and to a previous video on money, he says that your life's work, at some point in "the job to career to calling evolution," will require you to start a business. "If thinking of a business or a one-person business as independent work helps you swallow that pill, then please do that." And if words like "internet" and "business" make you close off because you haven't been exposed to the information, that is when you should think critically and look at the depth behind them (**Source:** `The Death Of The Personal Brand (& The Future Of Creative Work).md`, 2024-10-06). The levels themselves are not explained in this transcript; it presupposes the earlier video. Section 9.4 examines this claim against other statements of the author that seem to contradict it.

#### You make money to create

The levels of purpose describe a change in what money is for. Koe has a slogan for the turning point, and its history in the corpus is instructive. In 2023 he recommends starting an education business by pursuing your purpose, because education has minimal startup costs and huge profit margins, and argues that you need to build an income first so you can invest it in creating more. He quotes his own tweet: "you don't create to make money, you make money to create." From there you create an independent income source that you can spend building whatever other business you want, "or you can just do nothing, you can retire" (**Source:** `Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md`, 2023-04-30). The *education business* and *independent income* are his terms, and both are developed in Part X.

In 2025, in conversation with Sahil Bloom, he attributes the idea to someone else: "one quote that I'm going to butcher by Walt Disney: you start creating to make money, and then you start making money to create. So the perception of it kind of shifts." He applies it to himself: "I started on social media mainly because I wanted to make money… It was like a lead generation mechanism for my freelance business at the time, and then slowly pivoted over time." Later in the conversation he applies it to Elon Musk: "He shifted from creating to make money to making money to create… You need the resources to reach the specific level that you want to… make progress in humanity" (**Source:** `The Time Billionaire (A Concept That Will Change Your Life) With Sahil Bloom.md`, 2025-01-30).

Two things change between the versions. The *attribution* moves from his own tweet to a paraphrase of Walt Disney that he admits is inexact; he does not explain the change. And the *form* moves from an opposition ("you don't create to make money; you make money to create") to a temporal sequence ("you start creating to make money, and then you start making money to create"). The second form fits the levels of purpose better: creating for money belongs to survival and status, making money to create belongs to creativity and contribution, and the passage from one to the other is the development the framework describes. The 2023 form, read literally, would forbid the first stage; the 2025 form includes it. That is the same transcend-and-include logic applied to the slogan itself.

### 9.3 The Project as the Practical Unit

#### Why build at all: the creator's nature

The levels of purpose describe where development goes; they do not say what a person does on a given morning to move along them. Koe's answer, repeated across five years with remarkable consistency, is: work on a project of your own. Before defining the project, he grounds the need for one in a claim about human nature. His chain of reasoning in 2022 runs as follows. Animals such as chimpanzees use tools, but no other being has constructed the world it lives in with its mind. Humans constructed the school system, government, religious beliefs and language itself "from nothing but images and symbols"; at some point we had no language. What we had was consciousness, attention, focus and the tools to connect ideas, make them tangible, communicate and form relationships. He notes in passing that these constructions have traps and downsides (they control many people's lives, the theme of Part I) but leaves that for another occasion. His conclusion: "humans are the only being that can have an idea and make it real," and one key to a high quality of life is leaning into your nature as a creator and builder, actually building things "rather than watching other people build things and doing nothing about it" (**Source:** `If You Are High Value, Start A One-Person Business.md`, 2022-10-02).

The argument is a version of the book's title, *create or be created*. The same capacity that built the structures that program people (Chapter 1) is available to each individual, and a life that does not use it is lived inside other people's constructions. The project is the everyday form of using it.

In 2024 Koe borrows a description of what working on your own project feels like from a writer he calls only "Paul" (presumably Paul Graham, though the transcript gives no surname). Working on your own project, Paul says, is not exactly happier; better words are "excited" or "engaged." You are happy when things go well, and often they don't. So why do it? Because for people who like working this way nothing else feels right: you feel like an animal in its natural habitat, doing what you were meant to do, "not always happy maybe, but awake and alive." Koe presents this as the first step of using rest well ("build your own thing") and adds his own observation: getting lever-moving work done is very difficult if you only work on assigned projects (**Source:** `Minimalist Productivity Work Less, Do More.md`, 2024-08-11). The quotation corrects a likely expectation. The project is not offered as a source of happiness but of aliveness, which is closer to what Chapter 10 will call flow and what Chapter 39 will distinguish from happiness.

#### A personal project is not optional

The strongest version of the claim is that you are always working on a project; the only question is whose. In 2024 Koe tells how he came to it. He realized that most people giving career and life advice had not achieved what he wanted; "in their closed mind the reality I wanted to create was impossible," yet he kept looping back to the projects he loved "for my sanity." "Most people don't have a personal project that will create the future they want; most people don't realize a personal project is not optional." The only other option is "to live by your programming": pursue the goals society set for you and work on the projects that lead to them. And the line that he would reuse for years: "if you don't spend 1 hour building your dreams, you will spend 8 hours building someone else's for life" (**Source:** `This High-Income Skill Will Make You Irreplaceable (Full Guide).md`, 2024-01-28).

Later the same year he spells out the mechanism. "If you don't have a project to build, chances are you are already working on a project, someone else's, one that was assigned to you by a teacher or employer. You'll spend 8 hours building their dreams but won't spend 1 hour building your own." The cost is not only time but control of perception: if you are always building someone else's project, "you are not in control of your mind because your mind is framed by the project that you're working on": "your mind literally interprets the world based on the goal that it has in its mind." If your only goal is to wake up and go to the job, "most of your time is spent trying to numb the pain from that thing"; you are not building toward anything more. If instead you have a business you pour effort into every morning, "you experience a lot more serendipity in your life because you notice new things that can be applied to your business" (**Source:** `The Cheap Dopamine Epidemic Stop Ruining Your Life.md`, 2024-11-24).

This is the most important connective argument of the chapter, because it shows that the project is not a productivity device added on top of the theory but a direct consequence of it. Chapter 6 established that the goal is a lens that decides what you perceive. A project is a goal made concrete. Therefore whoever owns your project owns your lens. Numbing the pain (the cheap dopamine of Chapter 11) is what a mind does when its lens is set by a goal it does not care about; serendipity is what a mind experiences when its lens is set by a goal it chose.

In *The Art of Focus* Koe makes the same point the thesis of a chapter he titles "The Project." He describes a pattern in his own life: he always carved out time to build something of his own; schoolwork, client work and assigned job projects were necessary but did not bring fulfillment. "The source of most people's lack of fulfillment is that they've never learned or built something that they chose to learn or build." The inputs and outputs of the mind, he argues, are enslaved from birth by the conventional path ("learn this, build that") to the point that people come to hate the only two things (learning and building) that would free them. "If you don't make a choice, society chooses for you." For him, a perpetual stream of evolving personal projects worked on first thing in the day brought novelty to his days, and the discoveries, insights and progress across domains were "the source of my zest for life" (**Source:** `The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md`, 2024-01-18). The diagnosis is subtle: the school path does not make people hate learning in general; it makes them hate *assigned* learning and *assigned* building, and since those are the only kinds they have known, they conclude that they hate learning and building as such. The cure is not more discipline but a first experience of chosen learning.

As early as 2023 he had described the attentional side of the same problem. People lack an intrinsic goal, so they adopt someone else's goal "because it is clarity someone else created," as a job is; they give it their mental energy and align their work with it. Eventually they are "so deep in this meta system called society that their attention is just enslaved in that," and they have no time to create something for themselves, so everything takes much longer (**Source:** `The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md`, 2023-01-01). The phrase *meta system called society* is his (the lexicon distinguishes it from a later, different use of "meta system" for the mind as a system of systems). The point is that a job is attractive not only because it pays but because it supplies *clarity*, the scarce good of Chapter 5, and clarity borrowed from someone else's goal is still clarity. That is why it is so easy to accept.

The same year Koe draws the decisive distinction between two kinds of clarity. "Clarity is the key to enjoyment, but enjoyment is not the only piece of the puzzle." You can enjoy a life someone assigns to you (a nine-to-five, doing what people tell you), but it does not bring fulfillment. "Sustainable long-term clarity comes from two sources": self-generated goals in alignment with a *conscious future*, and society-generated goals in alignment with an *unconscious future*. "One is created, one is assigned." "If we don't want our life to be the product of someone else's vision we must create our own" (**Source:** `A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Life (In 6-12 Months).md`, 2023-07-25).

| | Self-generated goals | Society-generated goals |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Created by you | Assigned by others |
| Future they serve | A conscious future | An unconscious future |
| What they give | Clarity, enjoyment and fulfillment | Clarity and possibly enjoyment, but not fulfillment |
| Typical vehicle | Your own project | Someone else's project (a job, a school assignment) |

The distinction between *enjoyment* and *fulfillment* is the hinge. Koe concedes what critics of his position often say (that many people enjoy their jobs) and then argues that this does not answer the question, because enjoyment comes from clarity and clarity can be borrowed, while fulfillment comes from authorship and authorship cannot be.

#### The main character and the portfolio of failures

Koe gives the person who lives by self-generated goals a name taken from fiction and gaming. "A main character is an individual with a clear vision for the future, self-generated goals to actualize it and non-negotiable actions they must take on a daily basis." Such a person is aware that the conventional school-career-retirement path leads to the same mediocre lifestyle observable "in 99% of the population"; understands that they must take control of their life; knows they are not entitled to anything; and accepts that they must create their own success "whether the odds are for or against them" (**Source:** `Most People Are NPCs (Don't Waste Your Life On Autopilot).md`, 2023-05-08). The three components map directly onto the structure of Part IV: vision (Chapter 7), self-generated goals (Chapter 8), and non-negotiable daily actions (which, as this section will show, take the form of an hour a day on one project). The main character is the opposite of the NPC introduced in Chapter 1; the lexicon records an earlier form ("main character / player") in which the emphasis was on conscious imitation and navigating the game.

Why do people not choose their own path, if the argument is so clear? Koe's answer in a 2024 passage is: lack of confidence, and confidence comes from a specific kind of investment. "You aren't confident because you haven't invested in your portfolio of failures. You spent too much time residing in the comfort of other people's paths in life," failing to "break free from the masses, think for yourself, set your own goals and achieve them regardless of how long it takes." "You aren't confident because you don't have results in anything aside from what's already been done" (**Source:** `It's actually pretty easy to be self-disciplined (if you stop getting distracted).md`, 2024-05-12). The *portfolio of failures* (his coinage, used in slightly different senses between 2023 and 2024) is the accumulation of attempts on your own path. The metaphor is financial: you invest in it, and it compounds into confidence.

The process he gives, labeled as the first step to self-confidence ("deconstruct your goals"), is a compressed version of Part IV. Sit with your thoughts: what do you really want, the same boring path as everyone else? If not, "pay attention to what you don't want in life by observing the actions of most people and where those lead" (the anti-vision of Chapter 7). "Do the opposite of everyone else." Think about your future and deconstruct it into goals and actions (Chapter 8). Educate yourself every day with material that helps achieve those goals; block out time for focused work to build out your vision; then start. The closing principle explains why the portfolio has to be built rather than imagined: "Self-confidence comes from doing what you think is uncomfortable over and over again and realizing it wasn't that bad in the first place."

#### What a project is: one idea, several definitions

Koe has defined "project" many times, and the definitions form a sequence that the corpus classifies as a progressive operationalization. "Project" is a common word, but he uses it with a specific sense that accumulates across these definitions; it is worth reading them in order.

The first, from 2023, is a chain that derives purpose from structure. He always had a project to solve one of the problems in his life (money, fitness, a creative hobby such as Photoshop): "I was solving a problem, something, a hole in myself that I tried to patch with an external project that could go on to be something more." And: "a project is an organized way for achieving a goal, therefore it has a purpose, and therefore by working on it you are living with purpose, and that has a slew of benefits" (**Source:** `Solve Your Own Problems And Sell The Solution (Your Niche Is You).md`, 2023-04-30). The syllogism is the key to the chapter's title. If a project is the organized pursuit of a goal, and a goal is a purpose, then working on a project *is* living with purpose; purpose stops being something you must find and becomes something you do. The image of patching "a hole in myself" with an external project also connects the project to 9.1: the shallow problem (money, fitness) is the hole, and the project is the patch that "could go on to be something more."

The same year he widens the frame to the whole person. Turn the goals from your notebook into projects. "You are the meta project; you are your life's work." You as a project are composed of interconnected planes that make life enjoyable: mind, body, spirit and money. Money is necessary: "the bridge of commerce, the lifeblood of society," the means by which you bring the future you want to life and share it so others benefit and you contribute to humanity. In business there are the *eternal markets* (health, wealth, relationships, happiness), which connect to mind, body, spirit and business, and "this is where burning profitable problems lie." Treat each domain of life as a project to identify and solve problems; "the project is the solution"; once you create the project, "you turn yourself into the business." Improving your life with conscious effort toward goals and making tangible projects you can pass on creates "a life of impact, money and fulfillment" (**Source:** `Focus Is A Superpower (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md`, 2023-07-11). This passage already contains the whole arc of the chapter: domains of life as projects (9.3), projects as products (end of 9.3), and the self as the business (9.4).

In the summary of *The Art of Focus* (2024) the "meta project" becomes the "infinite project": "A project is a tangible hierarchy of goals that orders the mind, and you are the infinite project. You are your life's work. You are a project that houses projects, each with a purpose, path and priority that, when actualized, allows you to evolve." And a new image: "A project is how you throw an anchor in the unknown and pull yourself through a difficult current of learning experiences to complete the project" (**Source:** `The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md`, 2024-01-18). The evolution record treats "meta project" → "infinite project" as a renaming of the same idea with one addition (each sub-project has a purpose, path and priority, the Purpose-Path-Priority triad of Chapter 8). The author gives no reason for the change. "Infinite" fits the infinite game of Chapter 10 better than "meta" did: the project that is you is never finished.

The *anchor in the unknown* image deserves a moment. In Chapter 7 a big goal was described as an anchor tossed into the unknown, something that gives direction without needing to be reached. Here the anchor is the project, and the image is dynamic: you throw it ahead of you into uncertain water and pull yourself toward it through a current of learning experiences. The project gives the unknown a fixed point.

In 2025 the definition becomes cognitive. "A project can be anything: your health can be a project, your body can be a project, your relationship… your business… an image in Photoshop… a website for your portfolio can be a project. A project is simply a structured way of achieving a goal or making progress toward a goal." Its function is "to further narrow your frame of reference so your mind biases the right information as you learn," which keeps optimizing pattern recognition (**Source:** `How To Learn Anything 10x Faster Than Anyone With AI.md`, 2025-03-09). A later 2025 version adds the neurological mechanism as Koe understands it. As you read, study or converse, "good dopamine will spurt into your brain to signal that information is important for the completion of the project." "Your subconscious will munch on problems and send relevant ideas to your conscious mind. This is what creatives call shower thoughts. It's when the default mode network in your brain is active when you are at rest." His example is Charles Darwin, who worked on his projects in focused blocks and then went on long walks, during which potential solutions popped into his head (**Source:** `A Full Dopamine Detox Protocol To Reset Your Life In 30 Days.md`, 2025-10-26).

**Complementary context:** the default mode network is a set of brain regions more active during rest and mind-wandering than during focused external tasks; it is associated with spontaneous thought. Koe's account (the project primes what the subconscious works on, and rest lets the results surface) is his interpretation, consistent with the "two networks" model he uses in Chapter 6. The Darwin example is biographical and, according to the corpus record, possibly reached him via Alex Soojung-Kim Pang's *Rest*.

Later in 2025 the definition becomes practical. "A project is when a plan and a strategy meet action." Don't focus on the plan or the strategy, he says; they are byproducts. Act, build, iterate on them, "because the plan and a strategy without action and without iteration are just a list of things that aren't going to happen." "The bridge between where you are and where you want to be is a series of projects that reflect the value you've developed in yourself." Because a completed project means you have solved a problem in your own life, you can turn it into a product that helps others; "a project is the only qualification you need to start earning an independent income" (**Source:** `12 Rules To Change Your Life In 12 Months.md`, 2025-09-14). This connects to a broader change the corpus records in Koe's view of planning: in 2022 he advocated extremely specific weekly plans to avoid falling victim to entropy; by 2025 the plan is an "evolving blueprint" or "a guess that starts out dumb and becomes educated," subordinate to action. The project definition absorbs that shift: the plan is now inside the project, and the project is defined by action.

The 2026 version adds publication. "The life you want lies at the end of a series of meaningful projects." "A project is simply a structured set of goals and milestones. It's a way of creating something tangible to work toward a goal." You achieve goals by creating a project, completing it "and preferably publishing the project": a product released as a product, writing published on social media "to get feedback from the market." "Your mind is to be treated as a project, as is your body, your relationships, or your financial situation." "A project creates a frame for your learning by filtering out unimportant noise. Anything that does not directly move the project forward is not worth learning right now… because you won't retain it anyway." Hence the instruction: "Don't start studying. Don't start watching tutorials. You start the project. You start with an image of what you actually want to do in the world first, and then you take the first step" (**Source:** `How To Learn Anything 10x Faster Than Anyone.md`, 2026-08-09).

| Year | Definition | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | "An organized way for achieving a goal," therefore living with purpose | The project as the form of purpose |
| 2023 | "You are the meta project; you are your life's work" (mind, body, spirit, money) | The self as a project of projects; link to business |
| 2024 | "A tangible hierarchy of goals that orders the mind"; "you are the infinite project"; an anchor in the unknown | Order in consciousness; renaming meta → infinite; purpose, path and priority per project |
| 2025 | "A structured way of achieving a goal" that narrows the frame of reference | The project as cognitive filter (dopamine, default mode network) |
| 2025 | "When a plan and a strategy meet action" | Action over planning; project → product; independent income |
| 2026 | "A structured set of goals and milestones," preferably published | Publication and market feedback; "don't start learning" |

The evolution record judges these complementary rather than competing: the project moves from being a form of purpose, to a cognitive frame, to a published action, and each later version keeps the earlier ones. The 2026 definition matches the 2025 one and adds "publish."

#### The project as lens: reprogramming the mind

With the definition in place, Koe's arguments for the project fall into three functions: it is a *lens* that changes what you perceive, an *anchor* that holds your attention and your learning, and a *magnet* that draws ideas toward it. The three are aspects of one mechanism, but he develops each with its own images.

In a 2024 protocol for escaping cheap dopamine, the second step is "focus on a meaningful project," and the reason is order: "A project is ordered; you need to order your mind; your projects frame your attention." When you are building something, that is what you are focused on; "distractions are hard to penetrate if the project is actually meaningful"; "that's how you get into the flow state, that's how you become obsessed." "Projects are your building blocks for a better future" (**Source:** `The Cheap Dopamine Epidemic Stop Ruining Your Life.md`, 2024-11-24). The claim that distraction cannot easily penetrate a meaningful project is the positive counterpart of Chapter 11's analysis of distraction: you do not defeat cheap dopamine mainly by resisting it but by having something whose pull is stronger.

In the same passage he cites Naval Ravikant: "go do something great and your network will instantly emerge." The quotation supports the project step by reversing the usual order of networking: building something great attracts the connections you need, rather than networking first and building later. The line is Naval's; the use of it as an argument for prioritizing your own project is Koe's.

The passage adds a second argument, built on a nutritional metaphor. Even with good inputs (after curating your information diet), you must produce good outputs, "otherwise it just creates more entropy in your mind." If you don't balance consumption with creation, "you still get mentally obese, you still gain mental fat, because you're doing the nutrition, you're eating the food, but you're not exercising." Dopamine does not only come from consuming information: "your habits, what you do, conditions your mind in a positive or negative direction." The choice is binary: either work on a project that acts "as a puzzle piece to your ideal lifestyle" or fill your boredom with memes and pleasure that lead to disorder. The *mentally obese* metaphor (his coinage; Chapter 11 develops it) shows why curation alone is insufficient: input without output accumulates as disorder, exactly as Chapter 5's entropy model predicts, and the project is the exercise that metabolizes it.

In 2026 Koe states the lens function most fully. "You need a meaningful project to work on. You need a problem to solve… a business to build… an essay, a design, whatever. You're creating a lens by which you reprogram your mind." The justification is a chain that recapitulates Parts I–III in one sentence: "if you are the culmination of the ideas you've accepted into your head, and the ideas you accept are based on what you deem important, and the only things that were important to you were the school, job, and retirement that industrial culture permeated into your parents, teachers, and peers, then the primary way to pursue a rare life is to simply question and choose what is important to you." The project is how you make that choice operative. With one, "even a conversation you overhear on the street becomes creative fuel. You read a book, and a sentence pops out to you, but when another person reads it, they don't get the same effect" (**Source:** `If you've lost your creative genius, watch this.md`, 2026-03-28).

The chain is worth laying out, because the conclusion is easy to accept without seeing why it follows. (1) You are the sum of the ideas you have accepted (Chapter 3's identity as a network of ideas). (2) You accept ideas according to what you deem important (the goal as filter, Chapter 6). (3) What you deem important was set by industrial culture through the people who raised and taught you (Chapters 1–2). (4) So to change who you are, you must change what you deem important. (5) A meaningful project is a concrete way of deeming something important. (6) Therefore a project reprograms the mind, not by willpower but by changing which ideas get accepted. The book passage that "pops out" for you and not for another reader is the observable sign that the filter has changed.

#### The project as anchor

The anchor function has two sides: it holds attention, and it holds learning.

On the attentional side, Koe gives the project a specific place in his daily routine. In 2024 he describes the hierarchy: "Your identity is shaped by your vision for the future; your vision is the all-encompassing goal that you are pursuing in your life; it is at the top of your hierarchy; everything falls under it. So now you need a project, a tangible project… you need a project to work on in the first 1 to 2 hours of your day." He gives three reasons. First, "you need something to build that will result in your ideal future; if you aren't building your future then that future isn't going to be built." Second, "you also need something that exposes you to problems to solve, because if you aren't building something then the only problems that you're encountering are the ones that your mind fabricates." Third, "you need clarity for your work so you have a place to anchor your attention so it doesn't go to the negative thoughts and fabricate problems" (**Source:** `Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md`, 2024-02-18).

The second and third reasons together are a compact theory of rumination. A mind is a problem-solving machine (Chapter 8). If it has no real problems, it does not stop solving problems; it invents them. The project gives the machine real problems, and the fabricated ones lose their grip because attention has somewhere better to go. This is why the project belongs in the first hours of the day: it sets the lens before anything else can.

The 2026 version of the argument comes after a seven-day reset protocol and uses a nautical image. "Without a project, it's like your mind is a boat just drifting in water. The 7-day protocol we just did helped calm the storm that was previously there rocking your boat. But even if the sea is calm now, and you don't have a direction, you're just a boat floating. You're just going to stay there. It's nice, but at some point, you're probably going to want to do something" (**Source:** `If you've lost your creative genius, watch this.md`, 2026-03-28). The metaphor separates two things that are easy to confuse: calm and direction. Detox protocols, meditation and rest (Chapters 11 and 13) produce calm; they remove the storm. They do not produce direction. A calm mind without a project is pleasant and static. The project is what turns calm into movement.

On the learning side, the anchor is what experience attaches to. "A project is important because you have an anchor for the things that you're learning." If he is not building a Photoshop art project, and he has a life experience that reveals an idea that would go great in it, "I'm not even going to notice that part of life." Then the strong conclusion: "If you don't have a project you aren't living meaningfully because you don't have anything to tie your experiences to." Going through the same day in, day out, building nothing of your own, "working on someone else's projects for your job for your entire life, your life lacks meaning" (**Source:** `The Fastest Way To Acquire High-Value Skills (And Finally Change Your Life).md`, 2024-02-11). Meaning, in this account, is not a feeling added to experience; it is the connection of experience to something you are building. Without the anchor, experiences happen but do not accumulate.

#### The outline as magnet

The magnet function is the anchor seen from the side of ideas, and Koe develops it through the outline, the tool he uses as a writer. In 2025 he describes how, when writing a story, the outline acts "as a magnet for ideas or a frame from which you view life." When he writes a newsletter, a script or a book, on walks, in conversations or while thinking, his ideas magnetize toward it, which is why his note-taking app, Cortex, has a "capture" function. "That's how you notice ideas in reality, is by having a project to apply those ideas to." Without the project or goal, many ideas and opportunities pass you by because your mind doesn't care: "there's no dopamine signaling for the survival of that project." He then widens the image: life is not a finished book but an infinite story; reaching a goal is a chapter, and you write a new one. Goals are not final peaks either: once you are at one mountain peak you see another, visible only from the first (**Source:** `The Fastest Way To Ruin Your Life (Avoid This While You're Young).md`, 2025-01-12).

The phrase "dopamine signaling for the survival of that project" connects the magnet to the law of conceptual survival from Chapter 3: a project is a concept your mind is trying to keep alive, and like any concept it recruits attention and information for its own survival. You can use that mechanism deliberately by giving your mind a project worth keeping alive. The mountain image, meanwhile, returns in 9.4, where Koe uses it to define business.

The technique is old in Koe's work. In 2022 he presented it as a content-creation tip that doubles as a life method. When he has a slight vision of a video but sits down and nothing comes, he outlines first. The outline sets "anchor points": "you've kind of set, like, anchor points; you've ordered some consciousness in the unknown." Then, as you go about daily life, being aware of these points lets you notice things that apply to them. He outlines a video, goes for a walk, listens to an audiobook or talks to people, and connects those experiences to it. "It really doesn't matter what I'm reading": he could read about mechanical engineering and something will stick out "because everything's the same, everything's connected," giving inspiration and filling "the holes of my vision, my outline." And it does not only happen while consuming: when you form an intention to fill the outline out, "your subconscious will start to pick up and send information to your conscious mind to fill that out" (**Source:** `How To Create A Better Life For Yourself.md`, 2022-06-12).

"Ordered some consciousness in the unknown" is the most precise formulation of what the outline does in Koe's vocabulary. Chapter 5 defined clarity as order in consciousness; the outline creates a small patch of order in a region that is otherwise unknown, and that patch then organizes incoming information around itself. The claim that "everything's connected," so that mechanical engineering can feed a video about self-development, is the author's interpretation of his experience and should be read as such; the more modest claim (that a primed mind notices relevant material it would otherwise miss) is what the method requires.

He immediately extends the outline beyond content. "This isn't just for content, this could be your life": think about the situation you are walking into when you get home and see your kids. Is it going to be positive or negative, how is the conversation going to flow, "what reality are you going to create"? You create an outline of that, an outline of your vision (**Source:** `How To Create A Better Life For Yourself.md`, 2022-06-12). The example is small, but it shows that "project" and "outline" in his vocabulary are not limited to work: any situation you approach with an intended shape can be outlined, and the outline then shapes what you notice and do in it.

In 2024 he names the outline in this role an *experience anchor*. The outline does not need to be perfect: he starts by brain-dumping all his ideas on paper, and "once that's there then it becomes an experience anchor," so when he goes into the world, reads social posts or books, ideas that fit the project stick out and he adds them, piecing the outline together until the outline deadline, then working on the project itself. Writing his book, he says, made his life meaningful: almost every idea he met was applicable to the book, producing "constant good dopamine" (put it in the book, write it in the outline, research more, find more ideas), "this constant back and forth of serendipity that made me feel like a child again," restoring a sense of wonder. Then the sentence that compresses this section: "If you lack meaning, it's because you probably lack your own project, one that you assigned to yourself." And a warning to those looking for "that one thing that will get you results over and over": stop (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md`, 2024-07-21).

#### The author's six months of flow

Koe offers his own experience as the strongest evidence for these claims, and it should be read as testimony rather than proof. In a 2024 video he describes a period of about six months in which everything he noticed felt novel and made him feel good. He attributes it to three conditions: he had a vision for the future, he had a project to actualize it (building products and writing his book), and everything he consumed (self-education, new experiences) could have its "creative firepower" taken and fueled toward the project. "That's all my life was, was just input, output, input, output. I was just a vessel for reality to flow through in a channeled way towards a goal" (**Source:** `How To Reset Your Life In 7 Days.md`, 2024-01-07).

The image of the *vessel* here (the lexicon records "vessel (input–output)" as a distinct sense) is the individual counterpart of the "perspective vessel for reality" of the contribution stage. In both, reality flows through a person; the difference is that in the input–output state the flow is *channeled toward a goal*. Without a project, input accumulates (the mental fat above); with one, input becomes output.

The same mechanism, he argues, is the path to confidence and to other people's interest. Showing up daily to build a project "that lives into the future" is how you become confident and interesting. "This is the unconventional, if not the only, path to self-confidence": having value you can give others, because underneath it shows competence, time invested, and that you acquired the information and perspective to see situations with nuance, which is to say, experience. "When you are hell-bent on creating your vision for the future, people notice and they want to join you, they want to follow you" (**Source:** `Focus Is A Superpower (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md`, 2023-07-11). Together with the portfolio of failures, this gives two complementary accounts of confidence: from the inside, it comes from repeated uncomfortable action that turns out survivable; from the outside, it is the visible trace of value built over time. Naval's "your network will instantly emerge" is the social consequence of the same thing.

#### Choosing a project

If a project is necessary, which one? Koe's first answer is a refusal. "What should your project be? I have no clue. I can't tell you that. Stop outsourcing your critical thinking and just life to someone on the internet." What he can say is that "it should probably be a business, as that's the vessel for your purpose; it is the catalyst for the good life that breaks you out of robotic living. Entrepreneurship is modern survival and your psyche is wired to hunt; you aren't meant to be a monkey in a cubicle." And alongside the business, "the other domains of your life are the projects in your life: your health, your relationships" (**Source:** `Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md`, 2024-02-18).

Both halves of the answer matter. The refusal is consistent with the self-generated goals of this section: a project someone else chooses for you is, by definition, an assigned project. The recommendation ("probably a business") rests on two arguments from earlier chapters: the hunter psyche of Chapter 2 (*wired to hunt*, his phrase for a mind evolved to notice novelty useful for survival, which a cubicle starves), and the idea that in a modern economy survival itself is obtained through the market, so that building a business is the contemporary form of the hunt (*modern survival*). The status of the recommendation is opinion, and he marks it with "probably." Section 9.4 shows that he is not fully consistent about it.

In 2025 he gives a default for those who do not know what to build, as the third step of a "war mode" protocol: build mind, body and business, which he calls "the only things that matter" and the singular priorities that make the vision real. *Mind* is how you interpret risks and opportunities to make better decisions. *Body* is how you look, feel and are perceived. *Business* is how you contribute value to the world. You live with your mind and body, so studying and mastering them, and not being controlled by them, should be your full-time job; they are what let you transform the world and help others. Business is "the modern way to package up the creative ability of your mind and give it to others" to sustain your ideal life, and health and energy are your capacity to invest attention in your life's work without distraction. The action: create a project for each (adopt a training program, start the business, acquire knowledge in pursuit of both) (**Source:** `How To Change Your Life So Fast It Feels Illegal.md`, 2025-03-02).

The list of life domains varies across the corpus: mind, body, spirit and business (or finances); health, wealth, relationships and happiness (the "eternal markets"); mind, body and business. The evolution record classifies these as renamings without explanation, equivalent cuts made for different purposes (a three-item list as an action plan, a four-item list as a general vision). The mind–body–business triad used here is the action-plan version; it drops spirit and relationships not because they matter less but because, for someone who does not yet know what to build, these three are where a project can start tomorrow.

In 2026 Koe gives explicit criteria for "a good frame," which he treats as synonymous with "a project worth creating." First, it must be *unsolved for you*: "It doesn't have to be completely original or novel, but it must be a challenge. There must be something you don't know the answer to yet, which allows your subconscious to become a magnet for relevant and useful ideas." Second, *it has to matter to you*: "Your pattern recognition is powered by emotional investment. So a project you chose because it looks good on paper, like a high-paying degree that you don't actually care about, won't activate the same radar as the one that genuinely keeps you up at night." Third, it must be *shareable*: "it has to take some form. It has to exist in reality. It can be words, visuals, code, a conversation, a business, or a meal. You have to take the abstract thoughts in your head, ground them in reality, and test their worth." He adds a nuance about the relation between imagination and usefulness: in thinking, let the mind be "like a kid again who wants to build a teleportation device"; when you put something into reality, "it has to be useful. But don't let that stunt how creative you can think" (**Source:** `If you've lost your creative genius, watch this.md`, 2026-03-28).

| Criterion | What it requires | Why it matters in Koe's model |
|---|---|---|
| Unsolved for you | A real challenge; something you don't yet know how to do | An open problem turns the subconscious into a magnet for ideas |
| Matters to you | Genuine emotional investment, not paper appeal | Pattern recognition is powered by emotion; a project you don't care about won't "activate the radar" |
| Shareable | It takes form in reality: words, visuals, code, a conversation, a business, a meal | Grounding abstract thought tests its worth; connects to publishing for feedback |

Each criterion maps onto a function described above. "Unsolved" is the condition for the magnet; "matters to you" is the condition for the lens (a goal you do not care about filters weakly); "shareable" is the condition for the 2026 emphasis on publishing and for the later transformation of projects into products. The high-paying degree is a pointed example: it is the archetypal society-generated goal, and the criterion explains why it fails to produce the effects of a self-generated one.

A 2023 method fills in the structure once the project is chosen. "The goal is the what, the vision is the why, and the project is the how." A project has a goal, a process and priority actions to knock out daily, and three questions guide it: what can you do daily to move the needle; what aspects of your goals must you educate yourself on; how can you document progress so you stay motivated to return tomorrow? For each goal, in a notebook, write four things. *Milestones*: tangible, with realistic timelines. *Variables*: each factor that helps achieve the goal (for health: nutrition, training, sleep; for business: product, traffic generation, content). *Principles*: the priority actions that move the needle. *Skills*: those you must acquire to actualize the goal. And he urges against just watching and learning without building (**Source:** `The Cure To A Mediocre Life (Become Multidimensionally Jacked).md`, 2023-08-30).

The what–why–how distinction places the project precisely within Part IV. The vision (Chapter 7) is the why; the goal hierarchy (Chapter 8) supplies the what; the project is the how, the place where both become operational. The four elements (milestones, variables, principles, skills) correspond to what a game designer would call checkpoints, systems, core moves and abilities, which is why Chapter 10 can rebuild the same structure as a game.

The idea of choosing projects for what they will teach you comes, in the corpus, from Dickie Bush. In their 2023 conversation Bush says he writes about whatever he is learning (for one year, that was learning to write, which he hated in school but recognized as powerful). For the next three to five years he is "simply on a search for what are the projects I could take on such that the byproduct of starting that thing is tons of new skills that I don't currently have." His criterion is strict: "any time I'm starting a new project I say, is that going to guarantee that I learn a bunch of new things? If it's not, I don't take it on." Writing online was a forcing function to learn basic marketing and sales. His next project (a physical "shipyard," involving real estate, legal work, running a gym and reception, and documenting it on YouTube) means "I need to learn 10 other things; this sounds like the perfect thing that I could spend three to five years on" (**Source:** `Dan Koe & Dickie Bush On One-Person Businesses, Creative Workflows, and Lifestyle Design.md`, 2023-03-16). The heuristic is Bush's, and the term *forcing function* reaches Koe through him; Koe later generalizes it (the one-person business as a forcing function for every area of life, Chapter 28). It is a useful complement to Koe's own criteria: "unsolved for you" asks whether the project contains a challenge; Bush's test asks whether the challenge is broad enough to make you learn many things at once.

#### The Clarity Catalyst

In 2024 Koe gives the learning function of the project a name: the *Clarity Catalyst*. In February he introduces it as the first step to learning anything fast: "creating a Clarity Catalyst, and your Clarity Catalyst is a project, it's an outline, it's a pattern recognition intention; you are intending to have something that you are applying all of your everyday situations to; it becomes your vessel" (**Source:** `The Fastest Way To Acquire High-Value Skills (And Finally Change Your Life).md`, 2024-02-11). The three equivalent terms in that sentence (project, outline, pattern-recognition intention) show that the Clarity Catalyst is not a new object but a new name for the function the previous subsections described: a project, seen as the thing that tells pattern recognition what to look for.

In July he specifies its components. His "giant leaps in understanding," he says, came from writing his book, building his products, writing newsletters and creating his videos (the last requiring a new skill stack from him and his editor). The common factor was a big project, and a big project has five features:

| Component | What it does |
|---|---|
| Scope | Sets the size of the challenge; "the bigger the better for creativity" |
| Outline | Lets you note ideas from everyday life that fill it in; "you notice more in life" |
| Milestones | Give direction and clarity on the next steps |
| Real-world deadline | "Forces you to act or else your survival takes a hit" |
| Experimentation | Trial and error, turning failures into lessons |

(**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md`, 2024-07-21.)

Together, he argues, these create "the perfect environment for a flow state": pattern recognition from novelty and dopamine, clarity from milestones, challenge from deadlines, experimentation from learning. The video he was presenting, he says, was all he thought about for eight to twelve weeks. His instruction is to determine what you want to learn and create a real-world project that you will publish for others; if you are unsure what project to choose, look at what others have done.

His examples show how the abstraction lands. If you want to learn Photoshop, the project could be a piece of digital art or a social media graphic. If you want bodybuilding or a better body, the project is your body, your nutrition regimen and your training program: you experiment with new protocols until the patterns fit you, "until entropy takes hold and you have to do it again, because this game never ends." The last clause connects the Clarity Catalyst to Chapter 5's entropy and to Chapter 10's infinite game: even a successful project is not finished, because the order it creates decays and must be rebuilt.

The deadline deserves comment because it is the component most clearly borrowed from survival. A deadline from the real world (a client, a launch date, an audience waiting) recruits the survival machinery of Chapter 3 to the service of a chosen goal: "or else your survival takes a hit." This is the same move as the author's deliberately excessive rent, mentioned in 9.2, and as the "tactical stress" of Chapter 10: survival pressure, normally the enemy of creativity (it narrows attention, as 9.2 showed), is used on purpose and in measured doses to force action.

The evolution record notes that the Clarity Catalyst is one name in a series. In 2022 the same core appeared as "become a builder" and "learn < build < teach"; in 2023 as a six-step process and a "Mastery Method"; in February 2024 as Clarity Catalyst → learn as you build → teach; in July 2024 as the expanded Clarity Catalyst described here; in 2025–2026 under a cybernetic frame (learning as an output process). The names, number of steps and theoretical frame change; the core (a project, learning when you hit problems, teaching what you learned) stays the same. Chapter 14 tells that story in full.

#### How to start a project

Koe gives a six-step procedure for starting a project in two 2025 videos, in nearly identical wording; the corpus treats the two as one unit, and the account below uses the union of both.

1. *Choose something to build that moves the needle toward what you want in life.* In one version the choice comes from the life plan; in the other, from your "pain and gain story," the anti-vision and vision of Chapter 7.
2. *Create a note or document and brain-dump everything that comes to mind.*
3. *Save three to five sources of inspiration you want to emulate.* For a YouTube video project, save three to five videos of which you would want to create "similar iterations"; the same applies to Photoshop art, tweets, a book or a course. "You start by pulling certain parts of those things into your own project, so you're not necessarily starting from scratch; you're just not starting with a tutorial." This is "intelligent imitation… you're trying to pull from multiple sources to create your own thing," and as you hit sticking points, "that's when you learn best."
4. *Study those sources and break down their structure or characteristics.*
5. *Outline the project* into sections, milestones, images, inspiration and what you need to know to complete it.
6. *Have a place to capture ideas,* "preferably somewhere you don't lose the ideas or forget about them" (Apple Notes, Todoist, Notion and the like).

And then: "So, now that you're ready to start, don't start learning. Just start the project" (**Source:** `How To Learn Anything 10x Faster Than Anyone With AI.md`, 2025-03-09; `A Full Dopamine Detox Protocol To Reset Your Life In 30 Days.md`, 2025-10-26).

The reasoning behind the order is visible once each step is linked to a function. Step 1 sets the lens. Step 2 creates the first, imperfect outline, the experience anchor. Steps 3 and 4 apply the *intelligent imitation* of Chapter 1 (imitation is inevitable, so make it conscious and plural): the sources give you a structure to start from without the passive dependence of a tutorial. Step 5 turns the brain dump into a magnet with milestones. Step 6 makes sure that what the magnet attracts is not lost. The closing instruction is the practical center of the method and of Chapter 14's "build to learn": learning is postponed until the project produces a sticking point, because only then does the learning have something to attach to.

A shorter 2024 version, given as guideline two of a daily routine, reduces project setup to two steps. "Step one is to create an outline: brain dump everything you know that should be built in the project; you will add to this outline as you build. Number two is to create milestones, so break the project down into manageable goals you can achieve in 1 to 4 hour work sessions, depending on your current responsibilities." The project falls under the vision, and "you continue building projects until you reach that vision" (**Source:** `Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md`, 2024-02-18). The one-to-four-hour milestone is the operational link between the project and the 4-Hour Workday of Chapter 12: a milestone is sized to fit one session of focused work, so that each day ends with something finished. The phrase "until you reach that vision" restates the 2026 line that the life you want lies at the end of a series of projects.

#### One meaningful project, one hour, one day at a time

Koe's most compressed formula for the whole of this section is an opening line he used in 2023: "one meaningful project, one hour of focused work, one day at a time, without waiver — that's the secret you're looking for." Later in the same video he details the three components. *One meaningful project*: turn your goals into projects and pick the one that solves the most prevalent problem (the "most urgent problem" definition of purpose from Chapter 8). *One hour of focused work*: trained like a muscle, scheduled in a high-energy block, usually the first hour of the morning. *One day at a time without waiver*: persistence and iteration, with momentum built from small wins (**Source:** `Focus Is A Superpower (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md`, 2023-07-11).

In 2024 he reuses the formula, with a fourth element, as the opening of a video on focus: "Block out one hour a day, work on one meaningful project, aim for one vision for your future, take it one day at a time. You don't need more motivation, you need more clarity. You don't need more time, you need more focus" (**Source:** `Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md`, 2024-02-18). In 2025 the same opening returns, word for word except for one addition: "Block out one hour a day *minimum*" (**Source:** `The Deep Work Routine That Changed My Life.md`, 2025-05-18). The corpus treats the 2024 and 2025 versions as one unit with the "minimum" nuance added. The four "ones" divide the problem cleanly: a daily time commitment (one hour), a single vessel for the work (one project), a single direction (one vision), and a horizon short enough to be managed (one day).

The two contrasts in the 2024 version (clarity rather than motivation; focus rather than time) are the practical conclusions of Chapters 5 and 6. Motivation is a fluctuating state; clarity is a structural property of a mind with an ordered goal hierarchy, and a project is how that hierarchy becomes concrete. Time is not the binding constraint for most people; attention is, and an hour of real focus on one project does more than a scattered day.

In mid-2025 he adds a new argument for the formula. Choose one project that acts as a building block for the life you want: a creative project leading to independent work, or a portfolio project leading to a career change. Given AI, he recommends that everyone at least *try* entrepreneurship, while acknowledging that many don't want to: it hedges against replacement, and it lets you control "the good, true and beautiful" in your work, which you cannot do when someone else assigns it. Spend one hour of deep work a day on a project that can change your professional life, and put it on your calendar (**Source:** `You Need To Be Extreme If You Want Your Life To Change.md`, 2025-06-29). The *building block* (his term in this sense) and the two project types (creative, portfolio) make the formula usable by someone who intends to stay employed: the portfolio project is explicitly a path to a better career, not only to a business. The AI argument is developed in Chapter 36.

The emphasis on *one* project has a history of its own. In 2022 Koe advised "pick one thing and focus on it… but not forever"; in 2023 he criticized "focus on one thing" for ignoring the many things that make that one thing possible, and qualified it as valid only if the one thing is a massive goal requiring many skills; in 2024 he encouraged exploration ("shiny object syndrome is kind of encouraged") during a phase of curiosity; and by the end of 2024 he had settled on a formulation that reconciles these: "you don't focus on one thing, you focus on one mission, which requires you to learn many things." The evolution record classifies this as a refinement: the object of focus moves from an activity to a mission. "One meaningful project" should be read in that light. It is one *project* (a mission-sized container), not one *skill* or one *task*; inside it, many things are learned.

#### From project to product

The last step of this section takes the project into the market, which prepares the transition to 9.4. In 2022 Koe coined the slogan "projectize yourself, then productize yourself." Many people do not realize that "products start off as projects." If you are the project and you put that project on the public market (social media), "you become a product that other people want to invest in with not only their money but their attention." You don't launch a product when it's perfect, which is impossible; entrepreneurs launch MVPs (minimum viable products), which he defines as "a project that delivers enough value to charge for," and then build and improve over time. He summarized the video in one sentence: "you are your life's work; your minimum viable product was created at birth; start, build, teach" (**Source:** `Learn New Skills FAST (How I Remember Everything I Learn).md`, 2022-11-27). The claim that your MVP was created at birth is a provocation with a precise meaning: you do not need to become perfect before you start offering value and improving publicly, because you already are a viable starting version.

**Complementary context:** the minimum viable product is a term from startup methodology (popularized by Eric Ries's *The Lean Startup*) for the simplest version of a product that allows a team to learn from real users. Koe adapts it in several directions: the minimum viable offer, the "minimum viable vision," and here the person as MVP.

The corpus records a later shift in what Koe calls the MVP. In 2022 it is the person or the project; by 2025 he says "social posts are the new MVPs," meaning each post validates an idea that can then be developed into a newsletter, a product or software. The evolution record treats the two as compatible: the first describes the attitude of starting imperfect; the second, the mechanism of validation (Chapter 26).

In 2024 he states the relation between projects and products as a distinction. Projects are how you achieve your life goals, build a solution to a problem, and guide experimentation, education and skill acquisition. "Profitable products provide a transformation": they solve a problem to help someone get closer to a goal. He returns to the attentional argument: "If you aren't building a project every single day that pushes you toward your vision for the future, you are playing a losing game, and your attention automatically gets drawn to the projects of others," who need you so they can make money, survive and build more projects. His examples of products that come from projects: a program to get in shape, a planner for productivity, a Kortex or Notion system for writing content, taking notes or tracking progress; templates, he adds, are "always beneficial to include" (**Source:** `The Death Of The Personal Brand (& The Future Of Creative Work).md`, 2024-10-06). He links this to his product "Mental Monetization," a name the lexicon records with several meanings in this period.

The full progression from personal problem to product appears in 2023, as the product pillar of his one-person business model, which he calls *public personal projects*. Under the philosophy of solving your own problems and selling the solution, the sequence is: (1) identify a problem in your life; (2) turn it into a personal project for experience; (3) turn it into a minimum viable offer; (4) start selling it for $500–1,000 as freelancing or consulting; (5) build your audience along the way with content; (6) increase the depth and complexity of your offer; (7) productize it when ready, so it sells while you sleep. He estimates the whole progression at about one to three years. If the goal is money or career, learn a skill such as email marketing; if it is attraction, confidence or getting in shape, go to the gym and fix your nutrition. Once you make progress, talk about it: articulating it publicly reveals knowledge gaps (you cannot explain certain points, or you are challenged online), which forces more learning; then teach it for free or for a small price. And price must track value: "the value that you ask for, the money, has to reflect the amount of value that you can give"; if you are not an expert with many results, you cannot charge a high price, so start lower and work up (**Source:** `The One Person Business Model 2.0 (Turn Yourself Into A Business).md`, 2023-06-25).

This sequence is the business form of everything in 9.1. Step 1 is the shallow problem; step 2 is the lesser purpose turned into a project; steps 3–7 are the movement from status to contribution, compressed into a business plan. The time estimate (one to three years) is one of many in the corpus; the evolution record lists horizons ranging from sixty days for a first $1,000 to five years for a million-dollar year, and treats them as different milestones rather than contradictions (Part X develops them).

In the same video he explains why projects in particular, rather than study, are the right vehicle. Projects force you to apply what you learn; they are tangible; they are your result; you can iterate and get better; you can reflect on them and make things easier for the next person. For email marketing, for example: write an email sequence for your own brand, or for an affiliate product so that you have something to sell, or practice on your favorite books or products. Or create a product around your interest (interested in productivity? create a planner without prior experience), because "the way you get results is just by putting something on the market" and refining it. From that portfolio piece you create something better; "you can't improve what you haven't created" (**Source:** `The One Person Business Model 2.0 (Turn Yourself Into A Business).md`, 2023-06-25).

The last sentence is the principle, and a 2023 metaphor gives it an evolutionary scope. Channel emotion into building: "that's how you achieve a goal." You can just start building a house; the first one sucks; you build another and it gets better. That is how you actually learn to build a house that you can pass down to others, improving it until it is strategic and others need not go through the same trial and error. "That's what evolution is": solving problems so they become easier to solve. For any metaphorical house (a business, a relationship) there is plenty of good advice to study, but you practice it by building something of your own (**Source:** `Get Mad At Where You Are In Life (A Deep Explanation).md`, 2023-06-11). The lexicon records this as his *philosophy of building*. Its last move is the one that leads into 9.4: the point of building the house well is to be able to pass it down. A project that is improved until it can spare others the trial and error has become a contribution, and the vehicle that carries it to others is what Koe calls the life's work.

### 9.4 Life's Work: Business as the Vessel

#### Business redefined as life's work

At its largest scale, the project becomes what Koe calls the *life's work*. He uses the phrase, a common English expression, with a specific sense that shifts slightly across the corpus: what you choose to do with your life; how you spread the impact of the value you develop; and, most often, the business that serves as the vehicle for both. The definition that anchors the section appears first in 2022 and is repeated almost verbatim in 2024. "Stop thinking of business as a billion-dollar company. Business is what you do; business is your life's work; business is the vessel to create your ideal lifestyle while impacting the world at large." Money is "a modern byproduct of living with purpose and passing down your teachings." Business is "self-actualization and self-transcendence" (**Source:** `The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself).md`, 2022-10-23; `The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md`, 2024-02-06).

He places business beside contemplative practices as one method among several. Just as there are mindfulness, spirituality and Zen techniques for actualization or enlightenment, and you are not supposed to become ideological or dogmatic about any one method, business is *his* method: exposing himself to the world, practicing mindfulness, clarifying his thoughts with writing, and putting out a meaningful message. The comparison is deliberately provocative, and its logic is the one 9.1 developed: if depth is reached through the shallow rather than around it, then the pursuit of money, done consciously, can be a spiritual discipline rather than its opposite.

The key word is *vessel*, which recurs throughout the corpus with several related meanings (the lexicon lists the body, the business, the brand, the newsletter and the internet as vessels at different times). In this section the sense is consistent: a vessel is a container that carries something else. The business is not the purpose; it carries the purpose, the way a boat carries cargo. This is why the definition can call money a byproduct: the cargo is the life's work, and money is what the boat earns by carrying it.

The framing is present from the very beginning of Koe's public work. In the opening statement of his podcast in December 2021, he defines the show's mission as helping listeners "optimize your human experience through holistic personal development, mind, body, spirit, with business as your vessel for freedom, fulfillment, and the impact of your life's work." The target listener is a "growth-minded individual" with skills, interests or passions who has tried to turn them into income but struggles to make it "sustainable and predictable" (**Source:** `Justin Welsh Shows You How To Start & Grow A One-Person Business.md`, 2021-12-20). Business here is not the end but the vehicle through which personal development produces three outcomes (freedom, fulfillment, impact), and the three domains of personal development (mind, body, spirit) are those that 9.3 turned into projects.

In 2023 he ties the vessel explicitly to the evolution of purpose described in 9.1. "My philosophy is the same: business is an extension of the self, being a creator is an extension of the self, personal brand, same thing; it is a vessel for your purpose," because purpose means being part of something greater, contributing (for example, to a community by providing value in exchange for value). Those not living with purpose are not contributing to something greater. And: "your purpose evolves from superficial to metaphysical as you solve the superficial, shallow problems in your life," and business reflects that evolution (**Source:** `Self Improvers Are Creating Their Own Careers (The New Economy).md`, 2023-04-16). The *extension of the self* (his phrase) explains why the business changes as the person does: if it is an extension of you, it must evolve "from superficial to metaphysical" along with your purpose, passing through the levels of 9.2.

#### Choose a vessel, and design the life around it

If business is a vessel, the first practical decision is which vessel to choose, and Koe's criterion is control over time. Explaining how he came to work four hours a day, he says: "I'm allowed to create that lifestyle because I chose a vessel that [allows it] with time. I worked a job as well, but choose a vessel that allows you to control and create your own lifestyle over time. So if you don't have that, that's step one; that becomes your identity: the job is the problem, and you're going to have to work at it until you can get out of it." His own path illustrates the point: wanting to work four hours a day is what led him to freelance work and to understanding the power of social media, and people do not see that power now "because they don't have the identity that isn't an employee mindset" (**Source:** `Disappear For 2-4 Hours A Day (The Millionaire Productivity Routine).md`, 2024-02-18).

The passage applies Chapter 8's definition of purpose (the most urgent problem) to the job itself. For someone without a vessel they control, the job is the problem; working on getting out of it becomes the purpose, and the identity reorganizes around that purpose. The phrase "I worked a job as well" matters: Koe does not tell people to quit; he tells them to make the exit the project.

A vessel you control brings a responsibility that an employer used to carry. "If you work for someone else, a manager manages you. When you work for yourself you can sleep until 1 p.m., but are you going to get your work done?" Lifestyle design is therefore a core aspect of the one-person business: you have to decide how to systemize your work, structure your days for both maximum productivity and maximum creativity, and design the life you want through trial and error, experimentation, self-reflection and understanding the problems that block your quality of life (**Source:** `The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md`, 2024-02-06). *Lifestyle design* is a common phrase that Koe uses in his own sense: not copying someone else's routine but building your own by experiment. Part V is, in effect, the manual for the self-management this sentence demands.

The life's work also needs space, and that space has to be defended. In *The Art of Focus* Koe writes that "the concept of a life's work doesn't cross the minds of the mindless masses." Your life's work is an inherent part of your vision: how you spread the impact of the value you develop over time, and with the internet "it has never been so feasible." But you must create space for it through intentional lifestyle design: "if you don't take the time, it will be taken from you." In a world that encourages eighty-hour weeks, high-pressure environments and little rest and recovery, "you must fight back." He repeats his 2023 claim that four-hour workdays, singular focus and meaningful work are becoming the norm in small pockets of the internet, and points to ancient Romans and Greeks, Steve Jobs, Charles Darwin and other visionaries as people who attributed their success to short working hours combined with restful activities such as long walks. Most of the world's most impactful creatives, he says, worked jaw-droppingly little, "because they understood that focus is finite and creativity demands a lack of narrow focus on work." Rest opens focus so the mind registers new ideas and solutions (**Source:** `The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md`, 2024-01-18). The historical examples are offered as illustrations rather than as evidence, and the corpus record notes that some of them may reach him through Alex Soojung-Kim Pang's *Rest*. The connection to 9.3 is direct: the default mode network that lets the project "munch on problems" at rest only does its work if there is rest, and a life without space for rest has no space for a life's work either. Chapters 12 and 13 take this up.

#### Business as a spiritual endeavor

Koe repeatedly makes a claim that sounds paradoxical: that business is among the most spiritual things a person can do. In 2023: "Business is arguably the most spiritual endeavor you can take. Business is how you train your spirit. It is how you get better at contributing to humanity, something greater than you, spirit." His favorite framing is the one-person business, "turning yourself into the business." And he quotes Alan Watts: "sensible people get paid for doing what they enjoy doing" (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (You Can't Escape The Matrix).md`, 2023-09-05).

"Spirit" in this passage has a definition attached: it is "something greater than you," and business is spiritual because it trains you to contribute to that. This is not the definition of spirit used in the contemplative traditions he mentions elsewhere; it is closer to the "contribution" level of 9.2. The claim, then, is that business is a practice of the contribution stage, and that practicing contribution is what training the spirit means.

The Watts quotation does a specific job in Koe's argument. In 2023 he uses it to legitimize a definition: "Business is how you channel your interests, skills and expertise to impact others at scale. And as Alan Watts would say, sensible people get paid for doing what they enjoy doing" (**Source:** `Your 20s Are Meant To Build (How To Not Waste Them).md`, 2023-04-20). The quotation answers an objection the reader may have carried from 9.1: that combining enjoyment and income is somehow impure. Watts, a figure associated with spirituality rather than commerce, is cited precisely because his authority comes from the other side of that divide.

**Complementary context:** Alan Watts (1915–1973) was a British writer and speaker who popularized Zen Buddhism and Taoism for Western audiences. The corpus record shows that Koe uses him in several ways: as legitimation (this line, which becomes his thesis of "getting paid to play"), as a model of articulation, and occasionally as a counterexample.

In 2025 Koe gives the spiritual claim a training metaphor taken from the gym of 9.1. "Entrepreneurship and making money are deeply spiritual because you experience more negative thoughts, negative emotions, negative experiences that you can work through." If you are stuck meditating all day, or worried about minor irrational things, "you're not actually putting weight on the bar. You aren't bench pressing 315 pounds with your mind. You're lifting a five-pound dumbbell because you're upset that someone cut you off in traffic." That is not a high enough weight to build more mental muscle and capability (**Source:** `I'm 28. Here's How To Get Ahead Of Most 20 Year Olds.md`, 2025-07-27). The *weight on the bar* metaphor turns spirituality into a matter of load. Spiritual growth, on this account, is the capacity to work through negative experience, and it grows like a muscle under progressive overload (the principle Koe recognized in his business in 9.1). Entrepreneurship supplies heavy weight; a life organized to avoid difficulty supplies only light weight, however much meditation it includes. The metaphor is also a criticism, consistent with the "status hat" of 9.1, of spirituality pursued as an escape from difficulty.

In 2023 he places business in a cosmological frame. "Evolution is about solving problems in the macrocosm, personal growth is about solving problems in the microcosm, business is about solving problems to bridge the gap between the two. Business is your contribution to humanity. It is how you fulfill your life's purpose of raising your consciousness or awareness and letting that impact the world like a ripple in water" (**Source:** `Micro Education Businesses Are The Future & How To Start With Zero Dollars.md`, 2023-09-17). He offers this as context for why the levels of awareness from marketing (Chapter 32) apply "to everything." The *macrocosm/microcosm* distinction (his use, appearing once) makes business the hinge between individual development and collective evolution: you solve your problems (microcosm), you package the solutions so others can use them, and the sum of such contributions is what evolution does at scale (macrocosm). The ripple image says the same thing visually. This is interpretation and worldview, not a testable claim, and Koe presents it as his frame.

The same year he coins the most compressed version of the whole idea, admitting that it packs "a book's worth of information in one sentence": "holistic entrepreneurship is the meta vessel for any man or woman balancing the chaos in their life with the masculine pursuit of self-generated goal-oriented building." He frames it as his non-static personal philosophy, "not something that you have to do," and goes on to explain the entrepreneurial landscape in five points (**Source:** `Get Mad At Where You Are In Life (A Deep Explanation).md`, 2023-06-11). Each phrase of the sentence condenses a part of the system. *Holistic* refers to the four domains (mind, body, spirit, business) of 9.3. *Meta vessel* says that this vessel contains the others: entrepreneurship is the container in which the projects of all domains are carried. *Balancing the chaos* is Chapter 5's entropy. *Self-generated goal-oriented building* is the project, defined by self-generated goals (9.3) and by building. "Masculine" is used here in a symbolic sense (an orientation toward goals and construction, explicitly attributed to "any man or woman"), not as a statement about gender.

#### What business is: helping others climb the mountain faster

Koe's simplest definition of business is a metaphor from 2022. Five people stand at the foot of a mountain, "life being infinite mountains." They look up, and each draws a different path to the first peak; beyond it there are infinite mountains to climb. "Once you reach that peak you can look down and you can help people navigate that path up in a better way. That's what business is": helping people solve their problems, or climb the mountain faster, to reach the desired outcome you have already reached (**Source:** `How I Turned My Life Into A Video Game.md`, 2022-12-11).

The image gathers the chapter's threads. The mountain is a problem; its peak is a solved problem, which is to say a fulfilled purpose in the Chapter 8 sense. "Each draws a different path" means there is no single right way, which is why the project must be your own. "Infinite mountains" is the infinite game, and the peak visible only from the previous peak (the image Koe uses in 9.3 for goals) appears again here. And business is defined not as selling but as guiding, which is the contribution level of 9.2 in its most concrete form: you can help others only up the mountains you have climbed. This is the principle "solve your own problems and sell the solution" that Chapter 28 develops.

#### The Creator philosophy: personal evolution, then collective evolution

Koe gives this whole outlook a name in 2023: "The Creator philosophy is not a business model but a way of life." In essence, it means improve yourself, and then improve others who want to be helped, from the lens you distilled information from or from your own experience (**Source:** `The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md`, 2023-03-12). The definition has two steps, and their order is the order of 9.1: self first, others second. The qualifier "others who want to be helped" sets a limit: the Creator philosophy is not a mission to fix everyone.

In 2024 he spells out the way of life as a sequence: use the internet as a creative extension of yourself; learn skills that cannot be taught in schools; solve your own problems for *personal evolution*; get results that cannot be achieved in employment; put your personality on the internet to attract people like you; help them solve their problems for *collective evolution*; and earn meaningful independent income doing so. "This is not another dogmatic business model, this is a way of life." He adds that it is complex: it requires a complex mind and a lot of education and skill acquisition, not a job of repetitive tasks that "makes you a robot again" (which, he says, is why he built a sixteen-week program around it) (**Source:** `Zero To $1 Million As A One-Person Business (Working 2-4 Hours A Day).md`, 2024-02-25). The pair *personal evolution / collective evolution* is his (the lexicon records it only here). It maps the two halves of the Creator philosophy onto the microcosm and macrocosm above: personal evolution is solving your problems; collective evolution is helping others solve theirs; business is the bridge. The commercial reference at the end is part of the source; what matters for the argument is the claim that a way of life of this kind cannot be reduced to a repetitive procedure.

The idea is older than its name. In 2022 Koe described the meaning of life itself in the same terms: "That's what life is all about, that's what evolution is all about, that's what raising the collective human consciousness… is all about": develop yourself, become conscious of the roadblocks, failures and lessons, so others can reach that stage faster, and keep building. He generalizes the pattern beyond business: "it's all people helping those that are under them in terms of the developmental stages," in corporations or anywhere else. And he draws a practical consequence: to reach new stages and make more money, "you can't expect people to just give it to you"; you have to dive into the unknown, figure it out, and create clarity for those under you (**Source:** `How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md`, 2022-06-25). The evolution record classifies the progression from "raising the collective consciousness" (2022) to "Creator philosophy" (2023) to personal and collective evolution (2024) as a renaming of the same core. Chapter 40 returns to the collective side.

The same year he gave the idea its ethical shape, in a passage that completes the "selflessness requires selfish values" of 9.1. We are here, he says, to self-actualize and transcend: to pass down lessons and help others get there faster. His message is to improve yourself, help others improve with your prior experience, and let both reap the benefits. If enough people self-actualize and transcend with business as their vessel, more people open up to creative problem solving and use their minds for something beyond themselves: "selflessness after you solve all of your selfish needs." Creativity, innovation and discovery through the pursuit of curiosity will take us into the next phase of evolution. He adds, self-deprecatingly, "maybe I'm delusional" (**Source:** `The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md`, 2022-12-18). "Selflessness after you solve all of your selfish needs" is the temporal version of the 2022 tweet: selfish values first, selflessness after. The "maybe I'm delusional" is worth preserving because it marks the status of the claim: a hope about collective evolution, not a prediction.

He gives the most personal version of the idea in 2023, under the name *conceptual* or *spiritual reproduction*. Having a child is fulfilling, he says, because "you're creating a self, you're creating someone that's going to go on and impact the world." To find that fulfillment early in life, you can become a creator, a writer, "someone who contributes to humanity through business," using your mind to help others, and "by doing so you change identities across the globe." He hopes he has changed viewers' lives, "and by doing so a part of me is inside of you," admitting that it is a strange thing to say. This is why writing, speaking and building a business are so rewarding: the sense of connection and fulfillment that comes from "impacting lives at scale" (**Source:** `How To Reprogram Your Mind & Build Your Intelligence.md`, 2023-09-24). The idea extends the law of conceptual survival of Chapter 3 to reproduction: if identities are made of ideas, then passing on ideas that others adopt is a way of reproducing on the mental plane. It explains, within the system, why contribution feels fulfilling rather than merely dutiful.

The Creator philosophy also has a caution attached, which Koe gives as an example right after the "selfish values" tweet. You can be selfless and give money to a homeless person, but be a bit skeptical: are you doing it because you care about that person, or to receive a good feeling? He is explicit that he is not saying you shouldn't give; he is saying "know your own motives and become conscious of your own thought processes and not lie to yourself" (**Source:** `Why You Can't Be Yourself (The Power Of Being Alone).md`, 2022-11-13). The example protects the contribution stage from the same corruption that 9.1 found in spirituality: giving, like spirituality, can be worn as a status hat. The remedy is not to stop giving but to see clearly why you give.

#### Do you have to start a business?

On one question the corpus contains statements that seem to contradict each other, and they should be laid side by side.

In February 2024, as seen in 9.3, Koe says your project "should probably be a business," that "entrepreneurship is modern survival," and that if you do not have a vessel you control, the job is the problem your identity must work to solve.

In June 2024 he says the opposite in form. He recommends choosing the problem you want to solve and the solution you want to provide, and adds: "I don't even recommend starting a business." Instead, treat "any skill you learn, any problem you solve and any solution you create as value that can be given to other people as your life's work." Improving yourself (personal and professional development) and being online documenting that journey is a "quote unquote business": you are pursuing a good life, talking about it, and earning from it (**Source:** `Ideas Are The New Oil (How More People Get Rich In The Digital Age).md`, 2024-06-09).

In July 2024 he cites two authors and draws the strongest conclusion. "If your vision is big, audacious and truly impactful, you won't reach it working for someone else." Steven Kotler "harps on how an MTP, or massively transformative purpose, is key to achieving the impossible"; Robert Greene "preaches about discovering your life's task." Koe's conclusion: "entrepreneurship is the only logical option for long-term thinkers" (**Source:** `Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md`, 2024-07-21).

**Complementary context:** Kotler's "massively transformative purpose" (a term he shares with Peter Diamandis and Salim Ismail) names a purpose large enough to organize years of effort and to attract others. Robert Greene's *Mastery* describes discovering one's "Life's Task," the work one is uniquely suited to. Koe uses both as equivalents of his own "life's work" and his "vision"; the conclusion about entrepreneurship is his, not theirs.

In October 2024, as seen in 9.2, he says that your life's work, at some point in the job → career → calling evolution, "will require you to start a business," and that if calling it "independent work" helps you swallow the pill, you should.

Finally, in a January 2025 conversation, his guest Sahil Bloom argues that purpose can be found in providing for a family from a factory job, without "burning the boats"; Koe does not contest it in the passage recorded.

The evolution record of the corpus judges this a contradiction of formulation more than of substance. What Koe rejects in June 2024 is "starting a business" as an act separate from documented personal development, the image of a company founded on an idea; what he affirms elsewhere is that a life's work eventually takes the form of a business, because value given to others in exchange for income *is* a business, whatever one calls it. On that reading, the June statement says "don't start a business in the conventional sense; let your development become one," and the October statement says "eventually you will have one, so don't let the word stop you." The two can also be read as addressed to different stages: the beginner documenting a journey, and the person at the calling level. The author does not reconcile them himself, and the reader should not pretend he does. There is also a difference of register: the strongest statements ("the only logical option") are rhetorical and addressed to people with "big, audacious" visions; the softer ones (and the guest's view) leave room for purpose inside employment. Chapter 28 returns to the question under the heading "entrepreneurship is for everyone."

#### The internet as the vessel, and the brand as only a vessel

If business is the vessel of the life's work, the internet is the vessel of the business, and Koe insists on keeping the hierarchy straight. In 2025 he argues that the internet is the most accessible place for creators: no money, no right location, no right person, network or job are needed; you post your thoughts and can get in front of almost anyone. Stop seeing the internet, audience-building and social media as something apart from you: they are "a channel, a vessel to distribute your life's work." He speculates that the internet was "subconsciously created" to let us connect with anyone because we are social creatures, and that to complete your life's work you need connection with the people who benefit from it. Doing this forces you to learn the right skills, the *meta skills* that will not be replaced: agency, storytelling, marketing, sales, writing (not "being a content writer"), illustrating the story, and orchestrating your vision with AI, all as one person. "It's not about cringeworthy content templates and how to grow to a billion followers in 6 months" (**Source:** `The creator economy is dying thanks to AI.md`, 2025-02-16). The claim that the internet was "subconsciously created" for connection is speculation, and he presents it loosely; the operative claims are the accessibility of the channel and the skills that using it forces you to acquire. *Orchestrating* (his term in 2025) means using AI to learn and execute a larger vision while remaining the decision maker; Chapter 36 develops it.

In 2026 he states the hierarchy as "the only question that matters." Having changed your environment and chosen a vessel (content or code; he recommends content, and notes that even code eventually requires content), answer: "what is your life's work?" "We're not building a personal brand because that is just the vessel to do the life's work." He lists Jordan Peterson, Andrew Huberman, Alan Watts and Marcus Aurelius as people who all have personal brands (even Watts, who was not trying and is no longer alive) but who are deeply aligned with a purpose and a vision and use social media as the best tool available now, *permissionless leverage* (Naval Ravikant's phrase for code and media, which work without anyone's permission). That may change: historically the tool was the newspaper, radio, television or a book publisher (though even with a publisher you still have to do the work), and in the future it might be something else entirely; he jokes about Mars. For a beginner with no capital, social media is the obvious choice. And their brand is not "corporate hooks, templates" but who they are: mind, worldview, beliefs, interests and experiences in public for people to explore (**Source:** `How To Become So Valuable AI Can Never Replace You.md`, 2026-06-28). The argument inverts the usual order of the creator economy: the brand is not the goal that the life's work serves but the vessel that serves the life's work. Part IX develops the brand on that basis.

Jordan Peterson is Koe's recurring example of the distinction, and he uses him twice. In 2024: Peterson "isn't a content creator, he isn't an influencer, he isn't a personal brand"; none of those is his title, role or identity. He tours, writes books, leverages social media as a base and uses all tools at his disposal to further his life's work. He isn't worried about the latest content trend "because his mind outperforms any of those"; the quality of his ideas and critical thought set him apart, "regardless of your opinion on Peterson's highs and lows." The conclusion Koe draws for his audience: you did not start this path to become a personal brand; you started for freedom, autonomy, meaningful income, and the ability to write, speak and create about "the interests that spark a fire in your soul" (**Source:** `The Death Of The Personal Brand (& The Future Of Creative Work).md`, 2024-10-06). In 2025 he extends the example: Peterson writes books, gives talks, has writing software, and founded Peterson Academy "because schools wouldn't cut it." The internet and social media are simply the best tools now to advance his life's work, and they will probably change, but "true creators will adapt." If social media went to nothing, Peterson would not say his work was done. Whether the future is intergalactic space, virtual reality or a return to the Stone Age (when, Koe says, everyone built and traded, had a role and therefore a purpose), the answer is the same: become a creator, solve problems and distribute the solutions. "Create the internet you want to be a part of, create the content you want to see in the world, create the products you would buy, use and benefit from" (**Source:** `The creator economy is dying thanks to AI.md`, 2025-02-16). Peterson is used as an illustration of a structure (life's work first, tools second), not as an endorsement of his views; Koe's own qualifier ("regardless of your opinion") marks that.

The same priority appears as advice in the 2024 video on the death of the personal brand. Koe's lesson for those who want to follow him is: "create the content you want to see in the world and build the product you want to see in the world." Marketing, sales and writing come secondary: they "come under that vision" and help actualize it, but they are not the driving force. The reason he gives is a reading of demand: people are "starving for depth on the internet." They think it is filled with toxicity and memes, but they cannot pull themselves away. For him, social media has been "a library of value and information" that let him learn and do almost anything. The video's aim, he says, is not how to become a personal brand but "how to stand out with unique ideas and create your life's work" (**Source:** `The Death Of The Personal Brand (& The Future Of Creative Work).md`, 2024-10-06). The ordering (vision first, skills under it) is the goal hierarchy of Chapter 8 applied to the creator's skills: marketing is a sub-goal, and a sub-goal that becomes the top goal produces exactly the "cringeworthy content templates" he criticizes.

A homelier example shows that the life's work need not be intellectual. If you love beer and it is your life's work, it makes sense to start a brewery, make the best beer, pass it to customers who pass it along, and have everyone happy at your place. That is a worthy thing, Koe says, even though alcohol is involved: you are spreading a positive message and atmosphere, and "you are seeing your work for more than just a means of income." Likewise, if you love philosophy or self-development, which have benefited from the creator economy, the question is how you will spread that passion through your writing rather than treat it as income alone (**Source:** `Distraction Is The Greatest Trap Of The 21st Century (How To Avoid).md`, 2023-01-22). The brewery generalizes the life's work beyond online education: what defines it is not the medium but the relation between the work and the passion it spreads.

#### The time it takes: the only quick fix, built brick by brick

The final objection Koe addresses is time. In 2023, to the question "won't this take a lot of time?", he answers: yes, obviously; zoom out. If a formal education and degree take four years and climbing the ladder to a reasonable salary takes another ten, it will take "a few months, years" to build your own thing. Ironically, "the uncertain or the uncomfortable path, or doing your own thing, is the only quick fix out there," where "quick" means quick on an *expanded time horizon* (his term): it could take two years to reach $10,000–$50,000 a month. People love the certainty of four years of college, a break, a status-based company, climbing from $50,000 to $100,000 a year, getting capped, and eventually starting their own thing anyway, "when you could have just done it from the start" (**Source:** `The Non-Needy Networking Process (How To Make High Value Connections).md`, 2023-02-12). The argument is a comparison of time horizons, an application of the zoom-out technique from Chapter 6: the conventional path feels faster because each step is certain, but its total duration is longer, and it often ends where the uncertain path would have started. The income figures are his estimates and, like the other horizons the corpus records, should be read as illustrations of order of magnitude rather than promises.

The 2022 version of the same answer uses an architectural metaphor. You get out of feeling lost, anxious and uncertain by "building, actively constructing a better life for yourself, stretching towards something, and along the way building brick by brick." "These words are intentional: building, constructing; you don't build a house in a day." How long does it take to build a mansion? To see anything above ground? To lay a foundation, "maybe six months, I'm not a construction guy," so for six months you will not see results, nothing you can show others, "because you have to lay the groundwork, you have to de-program, you have to chisel down and rebuild back up" (**Source:** `How To Stop Feeling Lost, Anxious, Or Uncertain About The Future.md`, 2022-10-16). The *brick by brick* image (the lexicon records it as a common phrase with his sense) explains the emotional difficulty of the early period: the foundation is invisible, so progress cannot be shown to others or, often, to yourself. The phrase "de-program" ties the foundation to Part I: the first months of building your own thing are spent partly undoing the conditioning that made the default path feel natural. The six-month figure is explicitly a guess ("I'm not a construction guy"); its role is to set expectations, not to measure anything.

Read together with the progressive overload of 9.1 and the house built and rebuilt in 9.3, the two metaphors give one picture of time in the life's work: growth that looks flat for a long time and then, looking back, turns out to have been "an exponential line."

#### Making the immaterial material

Koe closes *The Art of Focus* with a restatement of the whole path, and it serves as a summary of how this chapter fits into the larger system. Understand and experience the patterns of the universe. Experience the full range of the human experience. Align your decisions with the perspective of your ideal self. Pursue a hierarchy of goals and solve the true problems in your life from an elevated level of mind. Use your creative ability to build holistic projects that contribute to humanity. Hunt for ideas and build with your own two hands. "Make the immaterial material, turn dirt into gold, become a fountainhead of value in a world that is desperate for depth." At the root: "leave your dent in the world through the art of focus" (**Source:** `The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md`, 2024-01-18).

Each clause can be located in the book. The patterns of the universe are Part III's entropy and information. The ideal self is Chapter 4's future self. The hierarchy of goals and the true problems are Chapter 8. The elevated level of mind is Chapter 4's level of mind and Part XIV's maps. The holistic projects that contribute to humanity are this chapter's projects at the contribution level, carried by the vessel of a life's work. "Make the immaterial material" names what a project does at every scale, from a brain dump turned into an outline to a vision turned into a business: it takes something that exists only in the mind and gives it form in the world, which is also the third of Koe's criteria for a good project (shareable). And the *fountainhead of value* (his phrase, used with slightly different emphases between 2023 and 2024) is the person at the center of the concentric circles of 9.2: someone through whom value flows outward, the "perspective vessel for reality" that distributes as well as receives. Chapter 10 now turns this structure (goals, projects, rising challenge, feedback) into the frame of a game, and asks how to play it so that it produces flow rather than strain.

### Exercises

1. **Trace your own portals.** List three goals you pursued in the past that you would now call superficial (a body, an income, a purchase, a relationship status). For each, describe what happened after you reached it or abandoned it: did the shine wear off, did you continue with "a different why," or did it reveal a deeper problem? Using Koe's distinction between using a material goal as a *portal* and simply moving to the next object, decide which of the three functioned as a portal and what, specifically, made the difference.

2. **Locate yourself on the levels of purpose.** Using the survival–status–creativity–contribution framework, identify the level at which you operate in two different domains of your life (for example, money and health). Then apply the "reset" principle: in which domain have you recently started something new and fallen back to survival? What would leaving your current level look like in each domain, according to the entry and exit criteria Koe gives, and what in your current situation might trap you there?

3. **Test the "transcend and include" argument.** Koe claims that someone who identifies as world-centric without caring for themselves and their group is "smart but dumb," and that spirituality pursued without "superficial work" becomes a "status hat." Construct the strongest counterexample you can: a person or tradition for whom contribution or spiritual practice came first and self-development followed. Does your example refute the principle, show a limit to it, or turn out to fit it on closer inspection? Defend your answer.

4. **Audit whose project frames your attention.** For one ordinary week, note which project occupies the first one to two hours of your day and how many hours go to projects assigned by others. Then, applying the argument that "your mind literally interprets the world based on the goal that it has," identify two things you noticed during the week only because of a project (yours or someone else's) and one thing you probably missed because you had no project to tie it to.

5. **Design a project with Koe's tools.** Choose one domain (mind, body or business) and outline a project for it using (a) the three 2026 criteria (unsolved for you, matters to you, shareable), (b) the four elements of the 2023 project outline (milestones, variables, principles, skills), and (c) the five components of the Clarity Catalyst (scope, outline, milestones, real-world deadline, experimentation). Where the three frameworks overlap, note it; where one demands something the others ignore, explain what it adds. Then write the first milestone so that it fits a single one-to-four-hour session.

6. **Adjudicate the business question.** Koe says in one place "I don't even recommend starting a business," in another that "entrepreneurship is the only logical option for long-term thinkers," and in a third that your life's work "will require you to start a business." Write a short argument stating the most coherent reading of these positions, and then identify one kind of person (by situation, stage or temperament) for whom his strongest version would be bad advice. What would the levels of purpose and the job–career–calling triad recommend for that person instead?

7. **Compare enjoyment and fulfillment in a real case.** Koe distinguishes clarity that comes from society-generated goals (which can produce enjoyment) from clarity that comes from self-generated goals (which produces fulfillment). Think of someone you know who enjoys a job they did not design. Using the job–career–calling definitions, the five flow drivers and the concept of "borrowed clarity," analyze what their situation provides and what, by Koe's account, it lacks. Then identify what evidence would show that Koe's distinction does not apply to them.

8. **Examine a metaphor's limits.** Choose one of the chapter's central metaphors (the drifting boat, the mansion built brick by brick, the five people at the foot of infinite mountains, or 315 pounds on the bar of the mind). Explain what mechanism it is meant to convey, then describe a situation in which taking the metaphor literally would lead to a poor decision.
