## Chapter 30 — From Service to Product: The Roadmap by Stages

Chapter 29 left the reader at the door of the business: a route of entry, a minimum viable offer, a first client, permission to launch something imperfect and a sixty-day plan. That is where most people start, and Dan Koe's own beginning looked exactly like that. The question this chapter answers is what happens next, because the starting point that Koe recommends contains a trap that becomes visible only after it has worked. A service sold to clients brings money fast, but it ties income to hours, multiplies bosses and eventually reproduces the job the person left. Koe's name for that outcome, "a new 9-to-5," is the hinge of the chapter.

The chapter depends on several earlier blocks. From Chapter 5 it needs psychic entropy, because Koe explains the paralysis of beginners as a gap that lacks a path. From Chapters 8 and 12 it needs the goal hierarchy, the levers and Koe's Law (the rule that work should fit inside about four hours a day), because the stages of the business are stages in which different levers move. From Chapters 15 and 19 it needs progressive overload and "you are the niche," because productizing, in Koe's sense, means turning one's own path into a product. From Chapters 25–28 it needs the audience and the minimum definition of a business (audience plus product), because the whole roadmap is the story of how the relation between those two halves changes over time.

The chapter proceeds in seven steps. Section 30.1 explains why client work becomes a new 9-to-5 and what Koe means by productizing a service or productizing oneself. Section 30.2 presents the roadmap: the developmental stages of a creator, what each stage requires, the trap of each stage, and a parallel framework Koe calls the "smart progression." Section 30.3 explains his preference for media-based (educational) products before software or physical products. Section 30.4 gathers his warnings against copying business models and against getting locked into a paradigm, including his repeated description of jobs and beginner models as stepping stones. Section 30.5 treats the business as lifelong iteration and persistence. Section 30.6 collects the numbers, thresholds and timelines Koe gives, and explains why he presents them as framing devices rather than promises. Section 30.7 applies the roadmap to the writer and shows how Koe describes the same process once AI enters it.

### 30.1 The New 9-to-5 and the Move to Productize

#### The offer path in four moves

Koe's most compact statement of the route from service to product appears in October 2023, in a video on making a million dollars a year as a digital writer. Asked what he would do if he started again, he lays out four moves.

The first is to start a freelance business. Without a large following, a beginner has to do more manual work to land clients, but a writing client pays "for a higher price than something like a course," so freelancing produces an income before an audience exists. He specifies what to sell: social media content, copywriting, emails and similar deliverables, with one caveat, "just don't offer plain old writing." The caveat matters because the rest of the section explains why undifferentiated skill becomes a commodity. The second move is to wean off manual outreach: as the audience grows, it brings clients in, and the hours once spent prospecting are freed to create a product. The third is to turn the freelance service into a product. One can keep freelancing, but a digital product, a course or even a physical product "allows you to earn without manual labor." The fourth is to "do whatever you want": "at this point you should have the mental and financial resources to build and monetize whatever you want; enjoy the freedom that a proper skill stack and experience provides."

**Source:** How To Make $1 Million A Year As A Digital Writer.md

Two months earlier, in August 2023, Koe had given a longer version of the same path as his personal method: (1) start a personal brand as the foundation; (2) solve your own problems in the real world, which revolve around health, wealth and relationships; (3) distribute your findings, opinions and beliefs through writing; (4) start with a freelance or coaching service and get results; (5) sell a physical or digital product that requires less time once the audience grows; (6) "make a hefty income in two to four years with persistence and iteration"; (7) and then expand your vision and build whatever you want to get there, "which could be software, spaceships, whatever you want." He adds that this is the process he teaches, with templates, in his program Digital Economics.

**Source:** How To Escape Wage Slavery (Entrepreneurship Is Modern Survival).md

The longer version shows what the shorter one assumes. The service is not the first step but the fourth: before it come the brand, the solved problems and the writing that distributes them. That ordering connects the roadmap to Chapter 28's philosophy ("solve your own problems and sell the solution"): the service is how a person who has solved a problem gets paid while the audience is still small, and the product is the same solution packaged so that it no longer requires the person's hours. The last step ("spaceships") is deliberately open. Koe does not describe the product as the end of the path; he describes it as what makes the next, larger vision affordable. Section 30.3 develops that idea as "media first, software later."

The economic logic behind the third move appears in a December 2023 passage. As readership grows, "it doesn't make sense to focus on low leverage activities like client work." If you do not plan to hire a team and become a manager, "the logical option is to create a digital product that requires minimal effort with high profit margins. This is how solopreneurs make millions." The reasoning is a scaling argument: solopreneurs understand that "if they gain one follower they can gain 1 million," and with a product that does not require labor to fulfill, they can turn those million eyes into multiple millions of dollars. The argument rests on a distinction that runs through the whole chapter. A service scales with the founder's hours; a product scales with the audience. Once the audience grows faster than the hours available, the service becomes the bottleneck.

**Source:** How Intelligent Creators Will Make Money In 2024 (The Build Teach Earn Method).md

#### "A new 9-to-5": the diagnosis

The central concept of the section is a warning Koe has repeated, under several names, from 2023 to 2026. In January 2023, explaining the origin of the 4-Hour Workday, he says that if you keep landing clients without productizing and systemizing, "you've built yourself into some form of a new nine to five where you have a bunch of clients you're making a good amount of money but there's still a cap": a limit on what you can make "without just working 16 hours a day." He presents this as the concrete meaning of the rule from Chapter 12 that more than four hours of work should be perceived as a problem. The four-hour limit is not only a lifestyle preference; it is a diagnostic. If the business needs more hours to grow, its model is the problem, and the limit forces the founder to evolve and systemize. He adds a practical constraint: one cannot take unlimited clients, because one must still be able to deliver and to do other things.

**Source:** The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md

In March 2024 he tells the same passage as autobiography. Seeing the cap as a problem made him move in a new direction: he could not take unlimited clients and still deliver, so he took on fewer clients, productized his service and sold that product to the audience he had built, which "required much less work."

**Source:** Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md

The term *new 9-to-5* is Koe's coinage, and it carries a precise meaning: a client business that has not been productized, which reproduces the two features of employment he criticizes in Chapter 2, a ceiling on income and a lack of control over one's time. The variants he uses for the same diagnosis are worth recording because they appear across years and videos: "a second 9 to 5," "a self-made 9-5," "a glorified 9-to-5," "the new digital nine to fives" and "a new 9 to 5 called client work." They are not different concepts; they are renamings of one observation.

#### Many bosses, invoice to invoice

The diagnosis has a second layer, which concerns control rather than income. In May 2024 Koe says he wants to show "what to focus on so you don't get trapped in a new 9 to 5 called client work." Many people become freelancers or agency owners "just to realize that that's not what they wanted at all, because now they just have a new boss"; in fact, "5, 10, 20, however many clients they have, that's how many bosses they have." His stated goal is "full control over how much you work, how much you make and the fulfillment you derive from both."

**Source:** How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md

In June 2024, inside a promotion for his course Mental Monetization, he compresses the point into a confession: when he started freelancing he did not realize he was "going from one boss to 10 bosses." In the same passage he calls a digital product "the highest form of leverage," because it lets one get out of client work or avoid it altogether, and he notes that many creatives do not know how to monetize at all, some not even through freelancing.

**Source:** Ideas Are The New Oil (How More People Get Rich In The Digital Age).md

In February 2025 he adds the emotional texture. Most people start the way he did: client work, some success, then the time gets eaten up, "and worst of all, you realize how much you hate working on other people's projects." You left your job because you hated the work; you wanted to write, explore your interests and focus on your craft. "But now you're back in survival mode, working from invoice to invoice instead of paycheck to paycheck." The phrase *survival mode* has a specific sense in Koe's vocabulary (living to cover the basics, without room for development), and the parallel "invoice to invoice / paycheck to paycheck" is the point: the form of dependence changed, the dependence did not. The passage ends with the promise that motivates productizing: once you have a product that does not require your time or labor, "$100,000 is just a matter of persistence and iteration," and "if you can make $1, you can make 1 million," provided the product can reach that scale as one person.

**Source:** A Full Guide To Making Your First Profitable Product (Beginners, Take Notes).md

#### Skill for someone else, art for you

Why would someone hate client work even when the work itself is what they enjoy? Koe's answer, from May 2022, is one of his more precise psychological claims. Even when you do what you like, design for example, working on that project for someone else as a freelancer "does not have the same neurochemical effect" as working on a project you love. He compresses it into a formula: "it's skill for someone else compared to art for you."

**Source:** If You Want To Secure Your Future… Follow This New Career Path.md

The formula extends the distinction between intrinsic and extrinsic goals from Chapters 1 and 10: the same activity changes its effect depending on whose goal it serves. A designer drawing a client's logo and a designer building their own project perform the same motor actions, but only the second is pursuing a self-generated goal, and only the second enjoys the autonomy and purpose that Koe (following the flow literature) lists among the intrinsic drivers. The reference to a "neurochemical effect" should be read as Koe's interpretation of the flow model rather than as a measured finding; he does not cite a study. The practical implication is what matters for this chapter: client work can be enjoyable as a skill and still feel like employment, because it is employment in the relevant sense. It serves someone else's goal.

#### Koe's own story: the glorified 9-to-5

The diagnosis is built on Koe's biography, which he retells in several videos with slightly different emphasis. While at a "cushy" job at a web design agency, he kept learning how to freelance, seeing from the inside how projects were pieced together. Freelancing picked up over six to eight months, and after roughly nine months at the job he quit, feeling secure. "But freelancing is really just a glorified 9-to-5 job. You're still working for other people on projects that aren't yours, probably projects that you don't care about, that you don't have any creative direction in." In the May 2026 telling, he first says this is beside the point and then corrects himself: "Actually, not really. That's why I started on social media."

**Source:** How To Grow An Audience If You Have 0 Followers (It's Only 2 Habits).md

The June 2025 telling explains why the move to social media was not a single decision. While procrastinating at the job, he had landed freelance web design clients because web design was what he understood. After making it work he realized "I had just built myself into a second 9 to 5." Starting a personal brand "wasn't one decision": a hated job, then hated freelancing, after trying so many things, "my brain was primed to make this decision." Then he discovered what social media could do. People posted their knowledge and interests, and he felt he could write in a similar way; "they didn't have to constantly reach out to clients because their content attracted the clients"; and web designers were selling digital products that required "zero effort kind of beyond creating the product." The model checked every box. Yet he had already tried YouTube and built some audience with digital art, "but had no idea how to monetize that."

**Source:** How To Build A Better Personal Brand Than 99% Of People.md

The detail about the earlier digital-art audience is easy to skip and important to keep. It shows that the audience alone was not the answer either; what changed was the combination of an audience with something to sell that did not depend on hours. Section 30.3 returns to this when Koe criticizes building an audience on visuals without education.

A third telling, from November 2023, frames the same events as an evolution "from time to mind." When he started, the logical option was to freelance with a skill: it seemed beginner-friendly and cost nothing to start, which was the promise of people teaching freelancing online, "and it's true." But after years of manual effort, the joy he got from building the business slowly declined as responsibilities increased. He could take only a certain number of clients in limited hours. "Unless I wanted to stay at my self-made 9-5 I had to solve creative problems, I had to evolve."

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

#### Three evolutionary moves

In the same video Koe reconstructs how he got out, as three moves, each of which removes a specific constraint of the previous model.

| Move | What he did | Constraint it removed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Began writing online to attract clients | "The time suck" of manual outreach (cold email, calls, messages) |
| 2 | Built a digital product that could sell while he slept | His "absolute dependence on client work to survive" |
| 3 | Pivoted the freelance offer into a consulting offer | Half the client-work time: "I could charge more and do less because I was helping someone rather than doing it for them" |

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

The table makes visible a logic that the narrative tends to hide: the moves are not a fixed sequence but a series of answers to whatever the current bottleneck is. Outreach was the first bottleneck, so content replaced it; dependence on clients was the second, so a product replaced part of the income; delivery time was the third, so consulting (helping) replaced doing. The third move anticipates a distinction Chapter 33 develops in full, between "done for you," "done with you" and "do it yourself" offers. Moving from doing to helping is moving one rung toward the product.

In June 2024 he tells the same transition as the birth of a "one-person media company." He started freelancing to control his time; after making it work through trial, error and failure, he realized he still did not control it: "I had 5 to 10 bosses instead of one, they were called clients." Then he saw social media's opportunity, which was to build an audience either to attract clients or to sell products. He acted as a one-person media company with the tools available: he wrote on Twitter and in his newsletter and repurposed the writing to YouTube, Instagram and every other platform. "It starts with writing," adding video where needed. To get out of client work he built digital products: ebooks, courses, templates, planners, systems, and by 2024 software. He presents the one-person business (also called a creator or a one-person media company) as an example of how generalists thrive in this age, which links this chapter back to Chapter 20.

**Source:** Change Your Life In 365 Hours (The New Rich Focus On These Tasks Daily).md

#### The freelancer as commodity: the new rat race

A second line of argument, older than the "new 9-to-5" label, explains why the freelancer's position weakens over time and not only why it feels like a job. In October 2022 Koe describes the typical escape from employment: people go freelancing, "learn a skill, sell a skill," and "get stuck in a new rat race that they have created for themselves": I need clients to survive; they will pay me this much. "You're a commodity because you're a specialized robot that performs one specific task that other tools are starting to automate out." He was a freelancer and pivoted out because he realized that one has to become an expert, "and that does not mean being a specialist." The same passage was re-edited, almost verbatim, into his long compilation of February 2024.

**Source:** The Rise Of The Value Creator (A Career Path For Polymaths & Self-Improvers).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The distinction between *expert* and *specialist* is Koe's, and it connects to the critique of specialization in Chapter 20. A specialist performs one task; an expert understands enough of the surrounding domain to solve a problem. Koe's example is web design. It is "an incredible first option" to start making money, but if you want to charge $3,000–$10,000 for a service (to generate wealth and take fewer clients for more time), you have to understand that "the low ticket freelance jobs are being automated out." He can build "an absolutely beautiful website for ten dollars" with software; web designers today use software plus their mind and creative ability to apply it to someone else's business, which usually lacks the time to do it.

**Source:** The Rise Of The Value Creator (A Career Path For Polymaths & Self-Improvers).md

The highest-paid done-for-you freelancers, he continues in the 2024 compilation, do not rely on software templates as the core of the service; they understand a range of skills and build a solution for a particular business. His example is a coach who has trouble generating leads. The coach needs a landing page (web design), copy for it (persuasion, marketing, copywriting) and an understanding of the business as a whole (an opt-in that leads into an email sequence with follow-ups). That well-rounded service fits a specific business and is impossible to outsource to a single piece of software. Humans build tools and solutions, and "if you are not a builder then you are going to be built over or you are going to build for someone else."

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The *builder* line is a word Koe uses in a sense of his own: not a construction worker or a programmer but anyone who assembles skills into a solution and owns it. Read with the rest of the section, the claim has two halves. Against automation, the specialist loses to software and the solution-builder does not. Against the new 9-to-5, even the solution-builder who sells only hours remains "building for someone else," which is why the roadmap does not stop at high-ticket freelancing.

Koe gives an extreme case of the specialist's fragility in March 2023: short-form content agencies. He predicts that half of the people doing short-form content marketing will be out of a job within six months, because they understand only "a little piece" of marketing. He lists the questions they cannot answer. Do they understand long form? How does short form fit into the funnel? What is the quantifiable end result, views (which most clients do not care about) or cash? If it is cash, the agency needs a unique system, templates or strategies for turning short form into leads or cash, because short form is usually shallow and does not nurture a lead into a high-paying or loyal customer (he specifies he is not talking about direct response). Short-form agencies do well when they serve a creator or brand that already has a converting funnel, where their content drives a little more traffic. "You're not the reason behind their success, and you need to understand the reason," so that you can iterate your offer to get results for anyone.

**Source:** The Best Online Business To Make $1 Million In 3-5 Years.md

The six-month prediction is a forecast, not a reported outcome, and the corpus does not revisit it. What survives the prediction is the diagnostic question, "are you the reason behind their success?", which applies to any service. A service that cannot answer it is a commodity, whatever it costs.

#### Known paths and the ideology of the freelancer

Two further warnings explain why people stay inside the trap. The first is about where beginners look for work. In February 2025 Koe describes someone with multiple interests, developing taste and wanting more agency, who does not know where to start. If you are young, "the only places you know to look are like freelance markets and job boards," because you have not learned how to do your own thing. "Those are known paths, that's no different from a job. If someone can give you the work, if you aren't hunting for the work, if you aren't attracting the work, then it's replaceable, and you're probably not going to get paid as much as you deserve." So instead of a place to look or a tactic, he offers guidance toward "creating your own path," which Section 30.4 develops as the metapath.

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

*Known path* is another of Koe's terms with more than one sense. In most of the corpus it means the assigned life path (school, job, retirement) analyzed in Chapter 2; here, in the plural, it means the established marketplaces of freelance work. The two senses share a criterion: work that is given to you is work for which you are replaceable.

The second warning is about identity. In February 2024 Koe says people get trapped in "business ideologies": they start as freelancers, that is all they know, they idolize freelancing above other models, and they complain when they cannot escape the *feast-or-famine cycle* (periods of too much work alternating with periods of none), with just enough income to survive. Because they identify as freelancers, their mind cannot open to opportunities that would leverage their skill set better. "They left their 9-to-5 to pursue freedom and created a new 9-to-5 for themselves." The same applies to stage one of the roadmap (Section 30.2): hold "the intention of evolution" so that you do not get trapped in a client business, complaining about a lack of time and not focusing on content and digital products.

**Source:** Zero To $1 Million As A One-Person Business (Working 2-4 Hours A Day).md

This is the identity model of Part II applied to a business model. Chapter 3 described identity as a network of goals that defends itself; Chapter 4 described how it changes. A freelancer who has made "freelancer" part of their identity will defend the model the way any identity is defended, by not perceiving alternatives. Koe's remedy, the "intention of evolution," is the same remedy Chapter 4 gives for identity in general: hold the next version of yourself as a goal before the current version hardens.

#### An earlier, gentler position: freelancing for corporations

The firm rejection of client work is not the only position in the corpus, and the chapter would misrepresent Koe if it presented it as such. In April 2023, answering the objection "What's wrong with offering my skills to corporations?", he says there is nothing wrong with it, but asks why not develop skills that fit your unique interests and enjoyment and then reach out to big corporations as a freelancer, with no income cap, possibly making ten times more. People prefer to apply for jobs using what the "social matrix" taught them (school trains you for a job that pays the school to teach the curriculum). As a freelancer who treats yourself as a business, "you aren't a slave to productivity metrics"; you control your lifestyle and can change anything. And people who try entrepreneurship and dislike it do not realize that "you get to create your own job; you don't have to do it like all other entrepreneurs do."

**Source:** Self Improvers Are Creating Their Own Careers (The New Economy).md

Here freelancing is the alternative to a job, not a disguised job. The change across the corpus is traceable. In 2023 freelancing for corporations is presented as superior to employment, and starting with a service is recommended because the beginner controls the outreach. From 2024 to 2025, client work is described as a new 9-to-5 with as many bosses as clients, "invoice to invoice," and freelance marketplaces as known paths. In March 2026 Koe once again recommends the client route for beginners, adding "I hated client work, but it was a necessary step," on the grounds that it is easier to sell a $1,000–$5,000 service to one person than 100–200 subscriptions. The reason he gives for the middle phase is his own experience ("I didn't realize I was going from one boss to 10 bosses"). The positions are compatible if read by stage: client work is good for starting without an audience and bad as a destination, and every version asks the person to build an audience in parallel and to evolve toward a product. What changes is emphasis, not the underlying sequence.

**Source:** Self Improvers Are Creating Their Own Careers (The New Economy).md; Ideas Are The New Oil (How More People Get Rich In The Digital Age).md; How To Build A $1M One-Person Business Faster With AI.md

#### Fulfillment time limits income: productize before the traffic spike

Koe gives a further, more structural reason to productize early, and it is the most counterintuitive argument in the section, because it treats success as a risk. In December 2022, and again almost verbatim in the February 2024 compilation, he states: "Your income, lifestyle and freedom are limited by the amount of time your products take to fulfill." He situates the claim in a forecast about work: with "a projected 62% of jobs being threatened by automation in the next decade," the highest paid will be those who leverage their mind (creative ability, knowledge, experience), a point he connects to his "$100,000 product in your head" video (Chapter 33). Then comes the argument. If you build an absurd amount of distribution but have only a time-based service (freelance, agency, coaching) and have not productized as you grew, "you're going to have a bad time." An influx of traffic, such as a mention on a podcast or an appearance on one, brings more potential clients than you can handle. Pivoting out of client work or hiring are both reasonable responses, but from the lens of a one-person business it is better to productize and control your time: build a product that sells, fulfills and maintains itself while you sleep.

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The argument inverts the usual worry. Beginners fear not having enough demand; Koe warns about having demand the business cannot absorb. A service business fails at the moment of its greatest opportunity, because its capacity is fixed by hours, while a product business can absorb a spike because each additional buyer costs almost nothing to serve. The practical conclusion is about timing: the product should exist before the spike, not after.

The automation figure needs a caution. Koe gives no source for "62% of jobs," and in the same 2024 compilation he cites a different figure, "60% of new jobs have yet to be created." The two numbers measure different things and neither is sourced; they function as rhetorical framing rather than as evidence, and the argument about fulfillment time does not depend on them.

#### Scale the service or productize

Once the service works, Koe sees a fork. In March 2023 he describes it as the difference between "an employee business (a regular business) and a one person business." Many people in his network scale a high-ticket coaching offer: they hire coaches, train them and automate. His preferred method "at least in this information space" is to productize: grow large by talking about broad, beginner-level topics and whatever you want without niching down too much; sell a tutoring, coaching or consulting offer; then package it into a course or several courses; and scale through low-ticket sales. "A lot of people don't like that, but a lot of people don't have my results. Sorry, not sorry."

**Source:** The Best Online Business To Make $1 Million In 3-5 Years.md

By February 2025 the tone has softened. If you want to stick with client work and hire a team to scale to $1M or more, "be my guest... that's actually a very good option. Many people do that with great success." But even then, he asks, why not create a product, first so that you get more warm leads for the client work, and eventually so that you have another revenue stream that comes in without additional work.

**Source:** A Full Guide To Making Your First Profitable Product (Beginners, Take Notes).md

The two passages record a real change of emphasis. In 2023 the scaled service is a legitimate alternative Koe dislikes; in 2025 it is "a very good option." What remains constant is the recommendation of a product alongside whatever else the business does, now justified not only by leverage but by marketing: a product brings warm leads to the service. This is the first appearance in the chapter of an idea Section 30.3 develops: the educational product as the foundation of any business, not only of the creator business.

#### Paid in the learning insights: the guests' versions

Koe's interviews add two methods for the passage from service to product, and both are attributed to guests rather than to Koe. In January 2025, John Hugh, co-founder of the creator platform Stan, gives what he calls the "surest bet" for a sustainable business: start by booking coaching calls or consulting. Successful Stan users, he says, think: "I don't know what to productize yet; I'm going to do the hard work to really understand my customer and specifically really understand what they will pay me for." His steps: (1) put up a paid call (the price in the transcript is ambiguous, "a 30," and he says it "doesn't have to be a lot of money"); (2) feel the thrill of a stranger paying you; (3) understand that "you're getting paid in the learning insights"; (4) book your first five clients and go through the journey of transformation with them, to understand why they buy and what their exact pain point is; (5) pattern-match across five to ten clients: "three out of five of these clients came to me for X specific reason; this is now what I'm going to productize." His rationale is friction: making a product (course, guide, ebook) "takes effort," so it is better to "get started today, make some money, get some easy wins and then use that to scale over time." His examples include a web designer who wants to productize later, career coaching and fitness training.

**Source:** How To Grow An Audience With Zero Followers & Build A Profitable Startup.md

*Paid in the learning insights* is Hugh's phrase, and it reframes the early service. Its main payment is not money but information about what customers will buy, which is exactly the information a product requires. The method converges with Koe's own: the service is a research instrument for the product.

In a December 2021 interview, Justin Welsh offers an even earlier step. If you enjoy a group of people, "the next thing that you should do is you should talk to them and you should understand their problems. Talk to them for free. Ask them to jump on a call for 15 minutes." His sample question is addressed to account executives: what is keeping you from moving into a sales manager role? Once you understand the challenges, "you can very easily create solutions or hypothesize solutions," either "I think this is the way to solve that problem" or "I know very specifically that this is a way."

**Source:** Justin Welsh Shows You How To Start & Grow A One-Person Business.md

Welsh's version costs nothing and precedes even the paid call; Hugh's version gets paid while learning; Koe's version (Section 30.2) uses a minimum viable offer and then a high-ticket service whose results become the product's social proof. The three are compatible stages of the same research process: free conversations, paid conversations, delivered results.

Koe adds a case that shows the path as a career rather than a single transition. In July 2024 he cites Jose Rosado, who "made a full-time income selling profile banners when he first started and then transitioned into web design and made multiple six figures if not way more than that by now, and then transitioned again into digital products, because the one person business model favors your evolution; it favors you starting and doing whatever you want in the future." The case is Koe's illustration, not an interview, and its figures are his estimate.

**Source:** The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md

#### What "productizing yourself" means

The phrase that names the whole movement is *productize yourself*. Koe uses it in two related senses. The first is the one developed so far: turning a time-bound service into something that sells and fulfills without the founder's hours. The second, which he emphasizes in the February 2024 compilation, is broader. He is "not talking about B2B info products" but about productizing yourself: treating yourself as the niche, writing to yourself, building for yourself and monetizing that way. Traditional marketers dislike the approach, he says, but given his results, and given that "big players" ask him how he does it, it "doesn't have to be as complicated" as copywriters, marketers and salespeople make it. He claims to make "10 times more" than when he followed "the copy and sales hacks out there," by being authentic while still using that skill set to write compelling posts and sales pages, from an authentic lens, talking to a target customer he knows well because the customer is a former version of himself.

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

This second sense ties the chapter to Chapter 19 ("build, write and sell to yourself"). The product is not a generic information product for a market the founder has researched from outside; it is the founder's own path, packaged. The "10 times more" is a personal comparison without a published baseline and should be read as such; it belongs to a broader shift in Koe's thinking, documented in Chapter 32, from direct-response tactics to authentic voice while keeping the psychological principles of persuasion.

In the same compilation he summarizes what everyone who has productized themselves has in common, in three points: (1) they improved their quality of life through personal and professional development; (2) they learned the necessary skills and interests along the way to increase their quality of life; (3) "they started posting online regardless of how basic they thought their information was."

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The third point answers the most common objection of beginners, that what they know is too basic to be worth publishing. Koe's observation is that the people who succeeded published anyway, and Chapter 26's argument about serving the person one or two steps behind explains why it works: basic information is valuable to whoever does not have it yet. The three points also show that the product comes last. Development first, skills along the way, publishing throughout; the product is what these produce once they have accumulated.

### 30.2 The Roadmap by Stages

#### Why a roadmap: the Gap

Before giving his roadmap, Koe explains why beginners need one, and the explanation reuses the vocabulary of Chapter 5. In October 2022, opening a video addressed to "high value" people, he observes that too many people dream of starting a billion-dollar company or becoming "the next Jeff Bezos or Elon Musk," because that is what they see. He assumes that if you dream of that, you have not started a business. The distance between where you are and where those people are causes overwhelm "because you don't have a way of getting there." Having a way of getting there is what Koe calls *clarity*; "the lack of clarity is what causes overwhelm and anxiety and just psychic entropy," a term he glosses as disorder of the mind in general, "not a mental disorder." The one-person business model is presented as the alternative path that removes the gap. The passage was re-edited in the February 2024 compilation.

**Source:** If You Are High Value, Start A One-Person Business.md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

*The Gap* is Koe's coined term here, and its definition is precise: the distance between your current position and someone else's, which produces overwhelm because no path connects them. *Psychic entropy* is a term from Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's work on flow (Chapter 5), which Koe uses mostly without attribution and in a loose sense.

Later in the same 2022 video he applies the idea to himself and to a second symptom, procrastination. People see him with 240,000 Instagram followers and think it will take forever to get there, "which it will," but that does not mean he saw no results along the way. "You're paying attention to the Gap, and that's what's causing the overwhelm, anxiety and procrastination, rather than the first step in front of you," which is just starting and doing. He asks the audience how they perceive him (the "cool lighting," the authority) and insists he is "a regular ass dude"; the perception creates a gap that is not real. A few minutes later he applies the same logic to the fear of market saturation: you think there is too much saturation because you have not started; you are guessing what is in the gap rather than finding out for yourself.

**Source:** If You Are High Value, Start A One-Person Business.md

The argument has three layers. The first is causal: overwhelm comes from missing clarity, not from the size of the goal. The second is attentional: looking at the gap instead of the next step converts ambition into procrastination, which is Chapter 6's claim that attention determines what the mind can act on. The third is epistemic: much of what the gap seems to contain (insurmountable competition, extraordinary people) is a projection, because it has not been tested. The roadmap that follows is Koe's remedy at the first layer. It does not shrink the goal; it fills the gap with intermediate stages, each of which has a visible next step.

#### Everything goes through developmental stages

The principle behind the roadmap is general. In March 2023 Koe states that everything goes through developmental stages: your community evolves, and so do you; you are not static. (He adds, in his blunter register, that if you stick to one niche your purpose and fulfillment are "fucked," a point that anticipates the smart progression at the end of this section.) Business is no different: beginner, intermediate, advanced. Trying to make $100,000 or $1,000,000 from the start causes overwhelm. The remedy is to break the goal into manageable chunks and "focus on the levers that you have to move as a beginner, which are going to be different at the intermediate stage and different again at the advanced stage." He adds a warning about teachers: be mindful of who you learn from and what lens they teach from, because someone teaching from an advanced level will cause anxiety, overwhelm and a lack of results, since you will focus on the wrong things.

**Source:** The Best Online Business To Make $1 Million In 3-5 Years.md

He uses the gym to illustrate it, in a passage that appears in January 2023 and again in March 2024. The process "is going to change with time," because mastery and skill acquisition come "in stages of beginner, intermediate, advanced and possibly even more in between." A beginner lifter does not do the same workouts as an intermediate or advanced lifter. In business, a beginner is not hiring employees but acquiring skills: learning direct outreach and direct response marketing, learning to grow on social media, figuring out what to talk about, creating a niche. The intermediate stage is more about maintaining (he refers to "stage one through three creators" in his one-person business roadmap). "Every stage will have its own lever moving actions that you need to take on a daily basis." He connects this to his Power Planner, which breaks a vision into yearly, monthly, weekly and daily goals with priority tasks.

**Source:** Society Is A Pyramid Scheme (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md; Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md

*Lever-moving actions* is Koe's term (he alternates "actions," "tasks" and "levers" almost interchangeably) for the daily priority tasks that move a goal forward, the levers of Chapter 8's goal hierarchy. The stage principle adds a qualification to that chapter: the levers are not fixed properties of a goal but depend on where the person is. The same goal of financial independence has different levers at zero followers and at a hundred thousand. This is also an application of Chapter 10's challenge–skill balance: a stage-inappropriate lever produces anxiety (too hard) or boredom (too easy).

#### The 99% mistake and the scope of the roadmap

Koe introduced the roadmap in a December 2022 video titled "The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake)," whose content he repeated in the February 2024 compilation. The mistake of the title is copying. There are different stages, and people should not look at what others are doing and expect to do the same, because "a 100,000 follower account that's making a million dollars a year is not going to be doing anything remotely similar to what you should be doing at the beginning." Different stages have different opportunities.

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

He also limits the roadmap's scope. It is for *one-person creator businesses*: people who want to talk about their interests, build an income doing so, hire few or no employees and unlock the freedom technology gives the individual. It is not for freelancers unless they want to build an audience. And if you have a goal of your own that the video does not align with, "don't let flashy screenshots or other things distract you from the goal": watch for awareness, but keep your goal. In the 2024 compilation the roadmap is accompanied by a promotion of Digital Economics, by then self-paced, which Koe describes as a Notion "command center" with templates to create a brand, write content, systemize it, create a product or service profitable from the start with zero experience, and promote and automate promotion, all within two to four hours a day.

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The scope note is consistent with the rest of the system. The roadmap is a map for one particular game, and Chapter 10's rule applies: the game is chosen by the player's goal. A screenshot of someone else's revenue is a goal assigned from outside, exactly the kind of goal Chapter 1 warns about.

#### Three developmental stages of a creator

The framework itself distinguishes three stages by the leverage available. As with everything in life, Koe says, there are developmental stages, each with opportunities aligned with the skills, experience and expertise one has. "A stage one creator can't send out an email and make $50,000 on demand," nor DM anyone and get a reply; you must put in reps and slowly build authority and leverage. He adds an explicit caveat: "these are all made up; there can be different stages," or different aspects; they reflect the most common patterns he has noticed in contact with thousands of creators. Each stage has its own tasks and its own trap, and the priority of content shifts across them.

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The caveat is part of the framework and should be kept with it. Koe presents the stages as a pattern he has observed, a heuristic map, not as a law of business; the follower thresholds are approximate and, as Section 30.6 shows, his own numbers for them do not always agree.

| Stage | Name | Approximate threshold | Main focus | Content priority | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Low leverage creator | From zero to about 10,000 followers | High-performing content, templates, short-form growth and validation | About 80% growth content | Many followers, zero loyalty, no product |
| 2 | Medium leverage creator | Around 10,000 followers | Direct response marketing, a high-ticket offer, branching into long form | Adds authority and authenticity (long form) | Another 9-to-5: the high-ticket client cycle |
| 3 | High leverage creator | Around 80,000–100,000 followers and above | Productize, network, refine and diversify, long-form synthesis | Doubles down on best ideas and diversifies | (The stage most never reach; its requirement is described rather than a trap) |

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The corpus does not preserve all of the steps of each stage (Koe announces "three things to do" per stage; the material reconstructs the first step of stage one, the second of stage two and the first of stage three, plus the traps). The subsections below develop what is recorded and do not invent the rest.

#### Stage one: high-performing content, and the trap of loyalty

Everyone starts at stage one, Koe says, unless they have prior business experience or a strategy already laid out. Social media growth is a practicable skill, not luck. The first focus is high-performing content. You cannot post whatever you feel like because it is clever or artsy; you have to understand what leads to growth. "If you aren't growing it's not the reader's fault because you feel like they lack understanding; it's because you lack the ability to make your message understandable to them." The study method he gives is concrete: filter the videos of your favorite YouTube channel by "most popular" and ask why those did best; study the top tweets; follow accounts you aspire to be like and compare which of their content performs better; use tools such as Tweet Hunter or TwX; note the common patterns, understand why they work and emulate them.

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The sentence about the reader's fault transfers responsibility in the direction Chapter 23 requires: engagement is a property of how the message is structured, not of how intelligent the audience is. The study method is the reverse engineering and pattern recognition of Chapter 15 applied to content.

The trap of stage one is the opposite error. If you get stuck there, "you end up as an account with a lot of followers but zero loyalty," as most people warn: if you disappeared, nobody would ask "hey, where did Dan go?" Such creators become obsessed with engagement and never bother to create a product or service.

**Source:** The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The two halves of stage one are in tension, and the tension is deliberate. The beginner must learn what performs, which means studying engagement; but engagement pursued for itself produces an audience that does not care about the person. Loyalty, in Koe's sense, is what makes an audience into the first half of a business (Chapter 28's "audience plus product"), and a product is what converts it.

#### Stage two: a plumber for a business, and the high-ticket client cycle

Stage two begins around 10,000 followers. At that size, Koe argues, the audience is too small to sustain an income from a low-ticket digital product, so the move is to create a coaching, consulting or freelancing service and work up to $2,500–$5,000 by stacking specific skills and applying them to a problem in a specific business. "It's like being a plumber but for a business": you find a problem because you understand the business as a whole, you create an offer specifically for that problem, you reach out, you present the perfect solution to the perfect person, and you get them results. Then you turn that system into a product, with the results as evidence, so that by stage three (80,000–100,000 followers) you can monetize through products alone or reduce client time. If you do not know where to start, use the *minimum viable offer* from Chapter 29: charge $500–$1,000 and work up; "you only need to get past a certain point before you know what to do next." Between stages two and three, he says, you should be able to land about four clients at $2,500 a month, which is $10,000 a month, through direct outreach. That matches a tweet of his that became a shorthand: "10k at 10k followers." In the 2024 version he adds two plugs: Digital Economics as a plug-and-play system, and Modern Mastery, a $5 offer with marketing, sales and direct-response education.

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

*Plumber for a business* is Koe's image for the high-ticket offer, and the image carries the method. A plumber is not paid for the hours spent with a wrench but for finding the leak and fixing it; the diagnosis is the value. The image connects to Section 30.1's distinction between specialist and expert: the plumber understands the house as a system. It also explains why stage two is the bridge to stage three. The plumber who fixes the same leak in many houses has, without intending to, developed a system; the product is that system, documented.

The trap of stage two is the new 9-to-5 of Section 30.1, now located on the map. If you get stuck in stage two, "you pretty much build yourself into another nine to five." Koe sees people hovering between 10,000 and 30,000 followers who do nothing product-related, do not grow or maintain growth with high-performing topics, and are caught in a cycle: reach out to a prospect, get a client, have no time for anything else, repeat. The stress is constant, because losing a client means being down $2,500 a month, and there is no time to build an audience while searching for the next client.

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The *high-ticket client cycle*, as Koe names it, is self-reinforcing. Each client consumes the time that would have built the audience; without audience growth, the next client must come from outreach; outreach consumes more time. The cycle can be broken only by protecting time for the activity that does not pay this month, content and product, which is why the 4-Hour Workday of Chapter 12 is not a separate topic from the business roadmap.

#### Stage three: the high leverage creator

Most people never reach stage three, Koe says, because it is "impossible without survival mastery, ego development and multi-disciplinary study." People get locked into the "focus on one thing" mindset, which he concedes is not bad at the beginning, build themselves into the high-ticket client cycle and do not pursue complementary skills or interests that would individualize them and reduce market saturation. His own example: he incorporates spirituality and philosophy into business videos, and "I guarantee you that's why a lot of you watch this"; it is what makes him stand out to those who like his content.

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The three requirements are not defined in the passage, but each points to an earlier part of the book. *Survival mastery* seems to mean having the basics of income and life handled, which Chapter 9 describes as the first level of purpose; *ego development* refers to the developmental model of Part II and, at its most elaborate, Part XIV; *multi-disciplinary study* is the generalist of Chapter 20. The claim that complementary interests "reduce saturation" is the argument of Chapter 27 that each perspective desaturates a market: no one else combines the same interests.

The first step of stage three is to productize, network and leverage the results of the high-ticket service. By now, Koe says, you have iterated your system or curriculum until it gets consistent results and you are confident enough to put it into a product, with a curriculum teaching people how to use it, distributed without requiring your time. His advice: "take your system and method for getting results, reposition it towards a broader audience, beginner level" (you now have a large audience to leverage), and "use your results as initial social proof to have a six figure plus launch." Networking, which "you should have been doing all along," now targets people with 100,000 to a million followers. His example is a cross-platform exchange: with 100,000 followers on Twitter and a wish to grow on Instagram, reach out to an Instagram creator with 100,000 followers and offer, "I'll help you grow on Twitter if you help me grow on Instagram with the validated ideas I have." That, he says, is what he did, and why he now has more followers on Instagram than on Twitter.

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The repositioning step contains a reversal that is easy to miss. In stage two the offer is narrow and high-priced (one problem, one business); in stage three the same method is repositioned for a broad, beginner-level audience at a lower price. The narrowness was a condition of the service, not of the knowledge. Chapter 19's "broad catch, specific sale" describes the brand side of the same move, and Chapter 33 describes the product side.

#### The skill sequence that accompanies the stages

In November 2023 Koe gives a sequence of skills to study as the business progresses, framed explicitly as progressive overload (Chapter 15). Each step has its subject of study: (1) build your profile, studying social media and graphic design for the banner and profile picture; (2) start writing content, studying copywriting, content writing, marketing and post structures; (3) ideate a product, studying marketing, sales and offer creation; (4) learn to promote, studying email marketing, promotions and copywriting again. Then a warning: do not get complacent. If you do not seek a higher challenge once the first stops giving good results, you will get bored, and "boredom leads to thoughts of quitting because you feel like there is something better you could be doing with your time."

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

The warning is the challenge–skill model of Chapter 10 applied to quitting. People often explain abandoning a business by difficulty; Koe locates a large share of abandonment at the other end, in boredom, when the current level is mastered and no new challenge has been chosen. The roadmap's stages are, among other things, a schedule of new challenges.

#### How the stage framework changed

The framework of follower-based stages is not Koe's only version, and the versions are better read together. In December 2022 the stages are defined by leverage and follower counts (low, medium, high). In March 2023 they become beginner, intermediate and advanced, each with its levers. In 2024 a third version appears inside Koe's Law (Chapter 12): three stages defined by the delivery model (client work, then a hybrid or group format, then productized) all kept within about four hours a day. The criterion changes from followers to the fulfillment model and time. Koe gives no reason for the change, and the 2024 versions contain small discrepancies of detail (for example, the writing time of stage one is given as "1 hour/day" in one video and "1 hour per week" in another). The three frameworks describe the same progression from low to high leverage, seen from three angles: audience size, skill level and delivery model.

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The Best Online Business To Make $1 Million In 3-5 Years.md; Zero To $1 Million As A One-Person Business (Working 2-4 Hours A Day).md; The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md

#### The smart progression: from a first niche to de-niching

In July 2023, in a video titled "Niche Down Is Terrible Advice (For Smart People)," Koe offers a parallel roadmap focused on authority rather than revenue. He calls it "the smart progression for authority and growth," and it has six steps: (1) determine two to three topics that lead to your vision; (2) practice top-of-funnel writing and growth; (3) start a newsletter as a vessel to build your projects; (4) create a lead magnet as your first niche; (5) sell a minimum viable service offer to gain experience; (6) de-niche: "evolve, transcend and include."

**Source:** Niche Down Is Terrible Advice (For Smart People).md

The first step contains a criterion for choosing topics that goes beyond interest. Break the two or three topics into subtopics and ideas to write about, but first determine what you are leading followers toward: your vision for the future, or your next goal in health, wealth, relationships or happiness, and from there the skills and interests that will take you there. His example: "Anyone can start working on their finances, but one person is going to be more interested in starting the e-commerce business or the personal brand or budgeting or just a physical small business like opening a coffee shop. That is your skill or interest to get you to that goal, and that is unique to you."

**Source:** Niche Down Is Terrible Advice (For Smart People).md

The example shows how the smart progression reconciles "you are the niche" (Chapter 19) with the need for focus. The niche is not chosen from a list of markets; it emerges where the person's next goal meets the specific route they find interesting toward it. Two people with the same goal (better finances) become different niches by their route.

The last step is the one that gives the progression its name. By now, Koe says, you have authority in one niche, the one in which you built the lead magnet and the service, yet you can still talk about whatever you want, because foundational content exists and the lead magnet can be plugged into it. To deepen authority and open room for new interests, you "complete your brand actualization pyramid": turn your freelance or consulting service into a cohort-based course and get results at that level; productize the cohort into a digital product and get results; then "repeat the process of lead magnet, service, cohort and product until you have a holistic synthesis of content, products and services."

**Source:** Niche Down Is Terrible Advice (For Smart People).md

*De-niche* and *brand actualization pyramid* are Koe's terms, and "transcend and include" is borrowed from Ken Wilber's developmental model (Chapter 38): each stage keeps what the previous one achieved and adds a wider scope. Applied to the business, the first niche is not abandoned but becomes one module of a larger synthesis, and the service → cohort → product sequence repeats in each new area. The smart progression and the three-stage roadmap describe the same movement from service to product, but the smart progression adds a second axis: after productizing one area, the creator expands horizontally into new interests, which is how a "holistic synthesis" accumulates.

There is a tension here that the corpus does not resolve. Koe's public position is that one should not choose a niche, yet the smart progression begins with a "first niche," and other passages recommend "one focus area plus a lot of noise," growing with broad topics "without niching down too much," or "one main skill or interest" as a starting point. The two can coexist if read by function: an initial focus serves monetization and the beginning; breadth serves the brand and the long term. Chapter 19 treats the niche question in full.

### 30.3 Media First, Software Later

#### Media matters more than code for beginners

When the product arrives, what kind of product should it be? Koe's answer is consistent in its order, though not in its wording: educational, media-based products first; software, physical products or anything else later, financed by the first.

The most developed argument comes from June 2024 and is framed as a disagreement with Naval Ravikant.

**Complementary context:** Naval Ravikant, an investor and essayist whose ideas Koe adapts more than any other source's, describes "code and media" as forms of *permissionless leverage*: products that can be copied at almost no marginal cost and do not require anyone's permission to create, unlike capital or labor. Chapter 25 presents Koe's use of the idea.

Koe says he thinks the media side is more important than the code side, sensing that Naval leans toward code. No-code tools let everyday individuals leverage media; on social media one can attract tens or hundreds of thousands of people, and "even 10,000 people is a lot." Creating a knowledge product (a course, a book) costs nothing; you do not need a publisher, since Amazon KDP prints on demand ("absolutely terrible royalties," but an option). By contrast, he notes, his team pays a lot every week for the development of Cortex, his software. If you do not want to code and lack the manpower for a big application, go the media route: build courses until you generate cash flow, then evolve into something that demands more cash. As a beginner, "I don't think it's wise to become a startup idea junkie," given high failure rates; start with what is fully in your control. One can usually iterate to replacing one's income with media (courses, books, knowledge work, posts, an audience), and then "get startup obsessed." The asymmetry is the decisive argument: with an audience, monetization is almost guaranteed (sponsorships, for example); with code alone, you can build the app, but without marketing know-how or an audience you cannot get users.

**Source:** Change Your Life In 365 Hours (The New Rich Focus On These Tasks Daily).md

*Startup idea junkie* is Koe's label for the beginner who chases startup ideas instead of what is in their control, and it echoes Chapter 28's warnings against startup ideas. The divergence from Naval is one of emphasis for beginners, not a rejection: Koe does not deny that code is leverage, only that it is the right first leverage for someone without capital, a team or an audience. His own position on code moves over the years (Section 30.7 records how AI changes it), but this ordering survives the changes.

#### The order: media-based products, then software, then whatever you want

In February 2025 Koe restates the order as a heuristic for beginners without capital. A product can be built with relative ease: a book, ebook or guide; a cohort-based course around your interest; software built with AI-assisted coding and learning; or drop shipping, which he says is not "the best model to go after," though some do it well. If you lack the money for physical products or locations, digital products ("media or code": podcasts, books and courses on one side, software and apps on the other) cost little to create, though they require skill, and the cost of software varies. "I like media based products first, then go into software, then go into whatever you want." Once you have launched a few media products around your interests, "you now have data on what works and you have some money to invest."

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

The sentence states two reasons for the order, and both matter: data and money. Media products are cheap enough to test, and the tests produce information about what sells; their profits then fund the expensive build. The same reasoning appears in September 2023, in a video about "micro education businesses": you can start "a hyper profitable education business with the knowledge in your head," and "you don't need to build a software to make this work." You can build software around the profitable education product later, to enhance the business, "because you'll have the money to invest in one."

**Source:** Micro Education Businesses Are The Future & How To Start With Zero Dollars.md

In September 2024 Koe turns the order into a validation method, which he calls the testing stage of business. Digital products can be built fast, which suits the stage at which you do not know what to sell, whether your software idea will work or whether an e-commerce business "that you're going to invest a ton of money in" will work. "You validate it first with content": a tweet does well, then a thread does well, then a newsletter, then a YouTube video, then a lead magnet, then a digital product, "then you turn that into a software or an e-commerce product or something of that nature, because you know it's going to do well and you have the money to build those things." His own case illustrates it: his education product on writing "is now turning into Cortex"; "one's education, one's a software, but they're very similar, and one wouldn't exist without the other."

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md

The *content ladder*, as this sequence can be called, is the "a tweet is the new MVP" idea of Chapter 24 extended upward: each rung is a cheaper test of the same idea, and only ideas that pass the cheaper tests earn the more expensive ones. It also gives "media first, software later" a precise meaning. The order is not a ranking of media above software; it is a ranking of tests by cost.

#### "The most holistic option": digital products and services

In the same September 2024 video Koe says that, although he will not give a specific business model (Section 30.4 explains why), he will give "what I think is just the best option, the most holistic option that allows you to pivot; it doesn't trap you in one narrow specific topic; it is very meta." "It's not starting an agency, it's not becoming a content creator, although that's what it's going to sound like." It is "the accessible low-cost route so that you can earn cash flow and then invest in other business ventures," and "in the age of the internet that means digital products and services." Coding software is a good option for those who can code; for those who cannot: "courses, coaching, templates, workshops, live events etc. You package up your knowledge and you sell it."

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md

The word *holistic* here carries the meaning of "pivotable." The option is the best, in Koe's view, not because it is the most profitable but because it does not lock the person into a topic or a model, which is the subject of Section 30.4.

#### The beginner business model and the big boy business model

Some people feel uncomfortable selling coaching, consulting, freelancing, courses, templates or ebooks, which Koe calls "digital products that are hyper profitable." In December 2024 he answers the discomfort in two ways. First, this is a *beginner business model*, so that you can pivot into a "big boy business model" later. Second, he believes education products are "the most impactful products, because education is the starting point of everything": you are in your current situation because of your conditioning and education. The creator economy, he adds, is the new school system, in which people find someone they resonate with and buy education from them if they share goals.

**Source:** Build A One-Person Business As A Normal Person (From $0 To $10K).md

The two answers point in different directions, and the reader should notice it. The first treats education products as a stage to pass through; the second treats them as the most impactful product there is. The second answer connects the business to the diagnosis of Part I: if conditioning and education produce the default path, then education is also what can produce a different one, and selling it is a form of the "decentralized school" Chapter 36 describes. Koe holds both views at once: the education product as a starting point for the founder, and education as a permanent function in the economy.

#### Margins and the economics of education

The economic case rests on margins. In September 2023 Koe says he knows "how a 10 million dollar digital product business is more profitable than most 50 million dollar physical product businesses." He expects an education product to operate at a 95% profit margin, and later in the same video says information products have "a 95 plus profit margin," which he reads as "a sign that the economy will continue to favor the education market."

**Source:** Micro Education Businesses Are The Future & How To Start With Zero Dollars.md

The figures are Koe's statements about his own and his peers' businesses, not audited data; the 95% margin is plausible for a product with near-zero marginal cost, but the comparison with physical businesses is offered without numbers. What they establish for the argument is the structure: when the cost of serving one more customer is close to zero, revenue and profit converge, and a smaller business can out-earn a much larger one with costs of goods.

#### Every business should have a foundation of education

From the margins Koe draws a stronger claim. In the same 2023 video he says he is "moving toward the belief that every single business should have a foundation of education," an information product "to distribute positive behavior change at scale." Its margins are "a sign that the economy will continue to favor the education market," and "technology and evolution have led us to this point, and who are you to question mother nature." The phrase *positive behavior change at scale* is his definition of what an education product does, and it anticipates Chapter 31's definition of value as behavior change.

**Source:** Micro Education Businesses Are The Future & How To Start With Zero Dollars.md

In September 2024 he extends the claim to businesses that are not educational at all. "I actually believe that most businesses, especially physical product businesses and software businesses, should have an education product alongside that, not only to boost profit but to increase the use of what it is that you are selling and create this philosophy and lifestyle around it." He adds a test: "If you can't provide the education, then maybe it's not a product or service that people actually need."

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md

The test is the most interesting part of the claim. A product people need solves a problem; a problem can be explained; the explanation is education. If no education can be built around the product, the product may not solve a problem anyone recognizes. The "mother nature" line, by contrast, is rhetoric: an appeal to the direction of technological evolution, not an argument.

In February 2025 the foundation becomes a sequencing rule for any product. "If you want to even build a software or build a physical product, just start with the education product first," to "get the right angle out." His example is a planner: if you want to sell one but do not know what to put in it or how to market it, "create a digital planner first, sell that, iterate on it until it's good, and then turn it into a physical one," so that you know it will do well.

**Source:** A Full Guide To Making Your First Profitable Product (Beginners, Take Notes).md

The *right angle* is the marketing angle (Chapter 32): the framing of the problem that makes the right people buy. The education product discovers it cheaply.

In the same video he compares the education product with AI-built software for the absolute beginner. AI is advancing so that most people can build an app (tools such as Lovable and Bolt guide you through it), but it still requires learning some programming and how to create and market an offer. Koe does not think it is as good as an education product for an absolute beginner who does not want to learn another skill. "That's why I recommend writing so much": you need neither video editing nor programming to start. It is a way to begin so that you can iterate, improve the product and change its shape over time, and build software afterward if you want. "We're doing something that is extremely quick to build and test, so you understand what sells. You make money, then you can make it a lot better over time."

**Source:** A Full Guide To Making Your First Profitable Product (Beginners, Take Notes).md

#### Low-hanging fruit, and the problem with selling looks

Two further heuristics belong to "media first." The first is addressed to artists. In October 2023 Koe observes that "when you go and look at most artists, they usually just have a bank of tutorials and premium tutorials as a course, and that's how they monetize." Artists think "I have to monetize my music, I have to monetize this and this and this"; his advice is to "just start with the low hanging fruit, like get a product out there and monetize, so you can free up time to actually spend refining and growing your business."

**Source:** How To Build An Audience With Zero Followers (What They Don't Tell You).md

The advice separates the art from the education about the art. The art may be hard to sell; teaching how it is made is easy to sell, and the income buys time for the art. This is the "media first" order applied inside a creative practice.

The second heuristic explains why education beats appearance as a basis for income. In July 2023 Koe says the market is "shifting away from shallow promises and good looks as a way of building an audience: it's less about 'oh look at me' and more about 'look at you, here's what you can do.'" In fitness, a field he follows closely in his free time and on podcasts, creators without a way of educating bank on the algorithm, on AdSense, or on coaching sold through their looks rather than their persuasive ability. He has heard many times that when such creators gain weight to a healthy body-fat percentage and feel better, their income drops sharply, so they must get shredded again and be unhealthy to make money. Those who educate, write and create information products make more, and sustainably. The same applies to visuals and art without philosophy, depth or education, which was his own problem as a digital artist: he sold art without teaching how he made it.

**Source:** The Value Creator (A New Internet Career Path For Intelligent People).md

The fitness anecdote is reported hearsay ("he's heard many times"), not data, but its logic is the chapter's: an income tied to something the founder cannot sustain (a body-fat level, a look, an algorithm) is as fragile as an income tied to hours. The confession about digital art closes a loop opened in Section 30.1, where his early art audience had "no idea how to monetize." What was missing was the education layer.

#### How the position on software and info products moved

The "media first" order is stable across the corpus, but what counts as the media product and how soon software follows both change, and the changes are part of the record. In 2024 software is for "those who code," and the route for everyone else is media. In February 2025 software built with AI-assisted coding already appears among the easy products, though still after media and with a higher chance of failure. In January 2026 Koe says information products have reached "the final stage of market sophistication," that "static courses don't cut it" and that one must understand "why info products are dying," while in the same video he also says they are "not dead" and must be transcended and integrated; the next level is an "implementation tool" built with AI on top of a course's knowledge base. By June 2026 he says that "information products are abundant" but "I don't think they will die," and that "small-scale software will be the new info product." The reasons he gives are market saturation, the fact that most buyers do not finish courses, AI generating information and lowering the cost of software, and paradigms that last less time. The reconciliation the corpus supports is that what is "dying" is the average static format, while education as a function remains too foundational to be fully commoditized, and the course remains the knowledge base on which the tool is built. Section 30.7 develops the AI side.

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md; A Full Guide To Making Your First Profitable Product (Beginners, Take Notes).md; How I'd build a one-person business (if I started over in 2026).md; If You Have Multiple Interests, Start A One-Person Business.md

### 30.4 The Metapath and the Traps of Paradigm

#### The metapath: a path of paths

The previous section gave a default product order. This section explains why Koe refuses, most of the time, to turn that order into a business model, and what he offers instead. The central concept is the *metapath*, a term he coins in February 2025. "Step one: choose a metapath. A metapath is a path of paths." He illustrates it with a mountain: "we need to climb this mountain, but there are multiple different ways to climb the mountain, and the way that you climb the mountain is unique," some taking longer, some shorter. "The greatest mistake you can make is boxing yourself into a specific model, like starting an agency business or an e-commerce business or some kind of other niche model like Amazon print on demand." Those models are great to learn from, "but only to acquire relevant skills and knowledge to advance along your metapath." Do not operate as "I'm this agency owner and that's all I do": "you're a CEO, you're the director of your life," and an agency could be a way of getting where you are going, "but if it's not, then you're probably on the wrong path."

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

The metapath reverses the relation between person and model. In the ordinary picture, a person chooses a business model and becomes what it requires; in Koe's picture, the person has a direction (the vision of Chapter 7), and models are temporary vehicles chosen for what they teach. *Director of your life* is the same idea at the level of identity: the role is to choose and coordinate paths, not to be any one of them.

Later in the same video he names what makes the metapath possible. "Being a creator is the metapath. That's how you can make any business model work." You can be a creator and an agency owner, an e-commerce store owner, a teacher running a cohort, or a software builder. "Elon Musk is a creator. All the people that you look up to understand the power of sharing their voice and message online to attract people to their life's work."

**Source:** How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

*Creator* has its own meaning in Koe's vocabulary (Chapters 18 and 21): not a job title or a content producer but someone who identifies problems, synthesizes, builds and shares, a way of being that the internet currently channels. In that sense, being a creator is not one model among others but a layer underneath all of them: distribution under one's own name, which attracts customers to whatever the business sells. Calling Elon Musk a creator is consistent with that definition, though it stretches the word well beyond its common use.

#### No copy-paste business models

From the metapath follows Koe's refusal to hand out models. In September 2024: "I'm just not a fan of giving out copy-paste business models, because you don't learn anything that way; you can find them anywhere." "I encourage you to experiment with those things and try multiple different ones, like you would try multiple training programs, so that you can create your own with the principles." He gives only principles, so that you can notice "the important things that you should actually double down on." And the comparison that justifies the refusal: "If I were to just come in at this point in the video and be like 'here's the business model that you choose' and you were to go with it, that's the same thing as just going to school because your parents told you to."

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md

The comparison ties the business roadmap back to the diagnosis of Part I. A copied business model is an assigned goal: someone else's path, adopted without the experiment that would make it one's own. The gym analogy explains the alternative. No one designs a good training program without trying several; the program one ends up with is built from principles discovered by experiment (Chapter 15's self-experimentation).

In March 2024 Koe states the same refusal as the final step of a process for people who feel lost: "The final step is to distribute your value, because that's how you earn a creative income doing what you want, based on the interests, skills and everything else that you experimented with to learn." "I'm not interested in giving you this step-by-step business model, because that's not what the creator economy is. It's the one that you create, it's the one that you discover, it's based on your interest and your skills in multi-disciplinary study. Your success is dependent on being a polymath and Renaissance man."

**Source:** If Your Life Is Spiraling Out Of Control, Here's The Solution To Feeling Lost.md

*Distribute your value* is half of a pair Koe uses in 2024 (create value, then distribute it), and the passage places the business at the end of a process of experimentation, not at its beginning. The polymath claim belongs to Chapter 20.

The refusal coexists with a recommendation, and the reader should see both. In the very video where he rejects copy-paste models, Koe gives "what I think is just the best option" (digital products and services, Section 30.3). In February 2025 he proposes the metapath and, in the same video, "media-based products first, then software." Later in 2025 he says the type of first business does not matter, that one can pick something popular on YouTube, and that "they all work; the first one is just a prototype." The movement goes from "digital products are the best option" toward indifference about the first model; the reason he gives is that what you will be doing in two to three years will be different and can be learned only by doing. The tension resolves if the recommendation is read as a default for people without capital and the indifference as protection against paralysis. Koe does not resolve it explicitly.

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md; How To Thrive With Multiple Interests.md

#### Zooming out: the business as nested holons

In January 2023 Koe gives a structural picture that supports the metapath. Applied to business, Ken Wilber's notion of *holons* (wholes that are also parts of larger wholes, Chapter 38) yields a nested series: you are a whole that includes your interests; your interests are part of you and also part of your niche; your niche is part of a market; markets are parts and wholes of social media. If you are struggling in any of these domains, there is a problem you can pinpoint; then zoom out to see the bigger picture of how it connects with other things. That is why "multi-perspectival understanding and open-mindedness is so important": it "allows you to solve creative problems by connecting the dots."

**Source:** Society Is A Pyramid Scheme (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md

The picture gives the metapath a diagnostic use. A problem located at one level (no sales) may have its cause at another (a niche that does not fit the person's interests, or a market that does not exist on the chosen platform). Someone locked into one model sees only that model's level; the zoom-out of Chapter 6 lets the founder move between levels. *Multi-perspectival understanding* is Koe's term for this capacity.

#### Paradigm traps: religion, diet and business

The opposite of the metapath is the *business paradigm*, another of Koe's terms. In June 2023 he compares it to belief systems in other domains. Just as people get locked into a religious paradigm or a nutrition paradigm such as the carnivore diet, they get locked into a business paradigm such as freelancing and client work, thinking they cannot pivot or change what they do every day, "simply because that's what you're supposed to do," what they were taught, without questioning it or creating a new way of doing business. "Nobody knows what they're doing; people are just giving away methods here and there so that you can try them." Fulfillment comes from building your own way, "a lifestyle that is of your own design," by actually being creative.

**Source:** The 4 Hour Workday (How Creatives Work Less & Earn More).md

In July 2023 he adds the historical dimension. People do not realize that business, like any other domain, evolves. If you get trapped in a paradigm ("cold outreach is the best, cold email is the best, this network marketing strategy is the best") and do not see the power of personal brands and the emergence of the creator economy, "you're eventually going to get left behind, because you can't change your beliefs around it." As with religion, nutrition and health, new things emerge and we understand human existence better, so we must adapt by learning continuously.

**Source:** Niche Down Is Terrible Advice (For Smart People).md

The paradigm trap is Chapter 1's "closed mind" (an ideology that has become identity) transposed to business methods, and it explains the freelancer ideology of Section 30.1. Koe's remedy for freelancers specifically, from November 2023, is educational: "If you're looking for more, then you have to break out of the freelancer mindset by learning and educating yourself on a potentially new business model that you want to evolve into." *Freelancer mindset* is his name for the identity that keeps the freelancer inside the new 9-to-5.

**Source:** Turn Your Knowledge Into A Business (How To Productize Your Mind).md

#### Beginner models as stepping stones

The concept that organizes Koe's view of every intermediate stage is the *stepping stone*, a common phrase he uses with a specific sense: a stage valuable for what it teaches and dangerous if mistaken for a destination. In August 2023 he applies it to business models: "I'm not here to demonize nine to five jobs; I see them as a stepping stone. I also see beginner level business models as a stepping stone, like freelance work, agency work, coaching businesses and sometimes digital products. They're all a stepping stone in your life's work, and they can easily enslave you. Not all people that do this are slaves, but the people that don't pivot and evolve beyond them start to become slaves to them." Later in the same video he calls freelance and agency work "the new digital nine to fives."

**Source:** How To Escape Wage Slavery (Entrepreneurship Is Modern Survival).md

The inclusion of "sometimes digital products" in that list conflicts with other passages, where digital products are "the highest form of leverage," "the most holistic option" or a model in which "it is impossible to fail." Koe gives no reason for the difference. The reading that makes both true is the one Section 30.5 supports: the risk lies not in the product but in stagnating with a single product, "stagnation equals death." A creator who launches one digital product and stops evolving has built another stepping stone and stopped on it.

The same concept governs Koe's view of jobs, which he restates many times because, as he says, people keep hearing it as hostility. In October 2023: "You don't need to go to school to get a high-paying job, but that job can be used as a launchpad for building your own thing." He talks badly about jobs a lot, "but I'm not against them; I had one; I see them for what they are... a stepping stone." *Launchpad* is his declared synonym.

**Source:** How To Make $1 Million A Year As A Digital Writer.md

In June 2023 he states his complaint precisely: most 9-to-5s "inevitably come with a cap on both your income and your development." "I see nine to five jobs as a stepping stone; they breed complacency and are dangerous for your psyche." "Challenge is what makes life fun and interesting"; if you halt your evolution by never pursuing something more, you lose purpose and fulfillment. He acknowledges an exception: many people have their own goals and can achieve sustained happiness in a 9-to-5, and he is not speaking to them. As a creator he is "supposed to" get his beliefs across and have a niche audience; viewers are supposed to disagree on some things; he speaks to those with "that desire for more."

**Source:** The 4 Hour Workday (How Creatives Work Less & Earn More).md

In March 2024 he nuances a harder claim (that the employer "controls your entire life"): some employers "are more lenient than others," "there's degrees to this," and some people have more control over their lives than others. "There's nothing wrong with a temporary job, but it's just that, a stepping stone," as long as you do not fall into "this comfortable, robotic and replaceable routine that is the nature of employment." In August 2024 he is at his most positive: jobs are "an absolutely incredible stepping stone." You go in, learn skills, meet people, and when you are ready (ideally before responsibilities trap you) and know you are meant for more, you leave and do your own thing, because you now have the skill. He also explains why he keeps clarifying: people attached to their jobs hear "you can do something else" as hatred of people or of jobs.

**Source:** Working Hard Won't Make You Rich (Do This Instead).md; Minimalist Productivity Work Less, Do More.md

In July 2024 he gives an estimate that frames the choice. "I don't hate jobs, I just see them for what they are, which is a stepping stone" to evolve beyond: you grow until you hit a cap, progression slows, and "if enjoyment is found in progress and you no longer progress," the job has become a stepping stone. His estimate, which he presents as opinion rather than data, is that 90% of people actively hate their jobs; 9% think they enjoy them but do not understand their potential, so in reality they hate them too; and 1% have jobs that allow all the flow drivers (curiosity, passion, purpose, autonomy, mastery), a number of jobs that will not change. Unless you bank on joining the 1% while still limiting your income to a salary, "the wisest thing you can do is just bet on yourself": becoming an entrepreneur unlocks control over your enjoyment and takes you into the unknown.

**Source:** Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md

In May 2025 the wording is at its harshest. "This is also why a 9-to-5 job is a great stepping stone, but is often a death sentence. You learn and progress until you're stuck doing the same thing over and over again. That's not a meaningful way to live. It psychologically castrates you and bleeds into all other areas of your life."

**Source:** How To Gamify Your Life (And Reinvent Yourself... Fast).md

Across these passages there is no chronological turn but an oscillation of tone, from "incredible stepping stone" to "death sentence," around a stable core: the job is a phase of skill acquisition; the problem is staying once progress stops. The 90/9/1 split is an impression, not a measurement, and the flow drivers come from the flow literature (Steven Kotler's version, which Koe cites early and later uses without attribution). For this chapter, the point is that the same logic applies to every stage of the roadmap: the job, the freelance business, the high-ticket service and the single digital product are all stepping stones, and each becomes a trap when the person stops evolving on it. Chapter 2 treats the job critique in full.

#### Pivotable models versus pigeonholing models

Koe also distinguishes models by how easily they let the founder change course. In December 2024 he explains why he will not teach agencies or e-commerce but "the barebones of turning a skill or interest into an offer." The beauty of the barebones model is that you can pivot and scale as far as you want. With an SMMA (a social media marketing agency) or dropshipping, you are "pigeonholed in that; the only option is to either succeed or quit." With the skill-to-offer model, you pivot and change as much as you want; if your values or beliefs change, the business changes with them; and you can use it, especially the audience, to fuel any other business. Want an agency? Build this first, then pivot into the agency; the same for e-commerce or software.

**Source:** Build A One-Person Business As A Normal Person (From $0 To $10K).md

*Pigeonholed* is the opposite of the metapath: a model whose only exits are success and failure. The audience is what makes the barebones model pivotable, because it is an asset that survives the change of product.

In March 2023 he gives a time horizon for trend-driven models. If you chase quick money with the latest strategies, you have about three to six months before you need to pivot. You therefore need either a big-picture overview of business, or to give up, or to commit to becoming the best in that space. If you are going to do captions or ghostwriting, "it better be a 5 to 10 year long fucking game plan where you are the best in the business."

**Source:** The Best Online Business To Make $1 Million In 3-5 Years.md

The alternative is not presented as forbidden; it is presented as a different bet. A narrow service can work, but only as a decade-long commitment to excellence in it; what does not work is a narrow service treated as a quick win.

Pivoting has a reputation problem, which Koe addresses in November 2022 as one of eleven lessons from growing a seven-figure business: "Don't quit, pivot." Everyone worries about shiny object syndrome, but if you are still pursuing the same goal and pivot along the way (choosing a different skill, for example), you are not starting over or quitting. "You are allowed to start over from a better place of compounded knowledge." If you learn web design and can make only $500–$1,000 with that single skill, do not stop yourself from learning copywriting, SEO or design because someone calls it shiny object syndrome. Improving your service, or verging slightly from someone else's path, is good: you take one path, then pivot, and then you are unique.

**Source:** 11 Lessons From Growing A 7-Figure One Person Business.md

The criterion that separates a pivot from shiny object syndrome is the goal. Changing tactics under the same goal accumulates knowledge (*compounded knowledge*, Koe's term); changing goals every month does not. The last sentence of the lesson adds a benefit that connects to Chapter 27: the person who has pivoted several times has a combination of skills nobody else has, which is a form of uniqueness.

#### Models without soul

A further objection to many popular models is not strategic but existential. In March 2023 Koe argues that modern business models pay no regard to meaningful goals or critical thinking. "Drop shipping a fancy little flashlight has no soul"; creating a basic journal with a print-on-demand strategy just to get in on a little game is the same. Starting an agency with a skill you do not care about, to sell to people you do not care about, in order to pull in cash: "there's nothing there." He says it is fine to do these things, but the viewer is watching because they want to contribute to people's lives.

**Source:** The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md

In July 2024 he traces the problem to outdated advice. "Traditional branding, marketing, content and just offer creation is not really going to help you if you go and learn from someone who's been doing this before the creator economy, or just kind of outdated advice; it can possibly lead you in the wrong direction," meaning "something that you don't actually care to do, you're only doing it to make money": "learn this business model, learn how to start an agency... that you don't really care to do, but you're going to do it anyways because that's the only path you know to make money."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (Avoid Learning These Skills).md

The position evolves within a narrow range. In 2023 these models have "no soul" but are "fine to do," and in the same period Koe says they are not "all bad" and can be "a decent stepping stone." By December 2024 the tolerance has become an order: build the skill-to-offer business first and, if you want, pivot into an agency or e-commerce afterward. He says he uses such models "to frame a discussion." Read together: as a stepping stone or a later expansion, yes; as a long-term identity chosen only for money, no. The objection is the one Chapter 28 makes about the life's work: a business that does not express the founder's goals cannot become their life's work, and therefore cannot sustain the motivation the long path requires.

#### The quickest fix is the longest path

The deepest of these warnings concerns time. In March 2023 Koe addresses the viewer who has tried quick-fix business models for three to six months, or however long it takes to see some results, and is not content: the work has not become fulfilling, and fulfillment was "the entire reason you got into this." His promise: "there is a way to do what you want or do what you enjoy and pull in an income doing so."

**Source:** The Future Of One-Person Businesses (Take Advantage Now).md

In another video from the same month he explains what such content lacks. Posts like "The Best Side Hustles To Start In 2023" or "the best online business model in 2023" are among the most popular topics in the self-improvement space and get a lot of views and engagement, which, he admits, is partly why he makes his own version, "but with a new perspective." What they all lack is a big-picture overview of online business: they focus on tactics, methods, special strategies, "the flavor of the day outreach strategy" for landing more clients. He says there is nothing wrong with this and he watches some of it himself, but he wants to open minds to a different route.

**Source:** The Best Online Business To Make $1 Million In 3-5 Years.md

Then the principle. Every person is wired to want the quick fix; Koe did and still does, and has to remind himself that success does not come instantly, something he has proven to himself, so he has experience to bank on. Beginners lack that experience and get trapped in a loop: quick fix, quick promise, the latest best business model, then a cycle of failures. You have to go through the failures, "there's no escaping it." "But the quickest fix is the longest path," or rather accepting the longest path, rather than jumping to one thing, then another, then another, only to come back to the longest path and make it work.

**Source:** The Best Online Business To Make $1 Million In 3-5 Years.md

*The quickest fix is the longest path* is Koe's coined formulation, and its logic is a paradox of accumulation. Each quick fix resets the learning to zero, so the sum of quick fixes takes longer than one long path pursued from the start. The formulation sits close to Chapter 10's distinction between finite and infinite games and to "compounded knowledge" above: what compounds is the underlying skill, and only a path long enough lets it compound.

In August 2023 he applies the principle to a specific tactic. He is tired of hearing "email marketing will make you ten thousand dollars a month." First, email is writing, speaking, marketing and sales. Second, "email marketing is such a myopic way of viewing the skill set," and you will fail because you think you need only a specific tactic (write emails, land clients), when that business model "is going to go to the ground in the next five or ten years." You are not stacking the skills that let you make money in any situation "because you understand the root of all being."

**Source:** I Had To Learn These High-Income Skills (If I Wanted To Make Money).md

*Myopic* is used here with a precise sense: seeing a skill as an isolated tactic rather than as an application of root skills. The prediction about five to ten years is Koe's forecast. What it supports is the claim from Chapter 15 that principles outlast tactics.

In September 2023 he extends the trap to the whole field of monetization. Many fall into trying "to monetize the vain, superficial or rapid": they post half-naked pictures and lose their income when they have to gain weight and actually be healthy; "they study the algorithm rather than human nature"; they hope AdSense ("relying on someone else to create your income for you") will work long-term; they study "the best business models of 2023 instead of understanding just business as a whole and studying marketing, sales, writing and speaking," so that they do not lose income when the economy shifts. "It is better to realize early that intelligence is a more sustainable monetization lever; creativity, wisdom, development and character can be the source of your income if you protect your mind."

**Source:** How To Reprogram Your Mind & Build Your Intelligence.md

The passage gathers the section's warnings into one criterion. Looks, algorithms, platform payouts and trendy models share a property: the founder does not control them, and they change. Intelligence, creativity and character are controlled by the founder and compound. That is the sense in which Koe calls them a *monetization lever*.

#### Gurus and shallow ideologies

Koe frames many of these warnings as a dispute with the business-advice industry. In September 2023: "I don't agree with 99% of business gurus out there. Most of the hyper successful ones are old and out of touch; most of the young ones never question what the old ones say and forge a new path." He uses the statement to introduce his critique of the classic advice to "build a product for a starving market."

**Source:** Micro Education Businesses Are The Future & How To Start With Zero Dollars.md

In August 2024 he generalizes: "It's no wonder people don't stick to building a business. The business ideologies circling the space have dominated for too long. They are shallow and lack regard for human nature, psychology, and fulfillment." Things evolve beyond being shallow, and there are better ways to start a business now: creating your own niche that is "profitable, authentic, and something you actually enjoy doing."

**Source:** Don't Find A Niche. Become The Niche.md

The diagnosis explains abandonment by a mismatch: models that ignore what makes a person persist (meaning, autonomy, enjoyment) produce businesses people quit. The 99% figure is rhetorical. Koe is aware of a reflexive risk in his own position. In January 2026 he asks himself, on camera, whether he is "just promoting this dogma," and answers that the more he breaks it down, "the more it seems like the one true way." He concedes that objections such as "not everyone can do this" hold some truth and reformulates the claim from "everyone should" to "anyone can, the ambitious people who want to," adding that this path "has more opportunity than most of those things, and it's all within your control," but he keeps the thesis. A reader may notice that his critique of dominant business ideologies applies, at least in principle, to the creator-economy model he promotes; he acknowledges the tension without resolving it.

**Source:** If you have multiple interests, do not waste the next 2-3 years.md

### 30.5 Iteration and Persistence

#### Persistence over consistency: a correction made on camera

If the roadmap has stages and every stage is a stepping stone, what carries a person across them? Koe's answer is a pair of words, persistence and iteration, and he arrived at it by correcting a more familiar word, consistency, in public.

In March 2023, listing the three things that determine a person's future, he names the third as "the ability to persist and iterate." "Persistency... and iteration is greater than consistency and repetition." In the same video he corrects his earlier wording of the second determinant from "repetitive focused action" to "persistent focused action," "because we don't want to always rely on motivation." You can do things well even when you do not feel like it, his examples being washing the dishes properly or taking out the garbage when your mother told you to: you can move your body in a specific way to get a specific result out of reality regardless of how you feel.

**Source:** Your Future Will Be Determined By 3 Things.md

In July 2023 the correction happens mid-sentence. "Focus is a currency, and when you invest it in your own education, work, rest and success, the results won't be immediate." Then: "consistency is a requirement — actually, scratch that — consistency is overrated, persistency is underrated." Persistence and iteration are what projects require to lead to a better future; one should show up every day intending to build a project that lives into the future.

**Source:** Focus Is A Superpower (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md

In January 2024, in the official summary of his book *The Art of Focus*, the idea becomes a numbered principle, set beside two others that explain it. Principle 19: "Iteration and persistence is better than complacency and consistency." Principle 20: "Reality is not black and white, but the extremes get the most attention; the magic is found in the gray area that can only be found by questioning everything." Principle 21: "Our perspective determines the positive or negative interpretation of situations; the truth seeker must hunt for kernels of truth to cultivate a more holistic perspective that allows for more positive interpretations."

**Source:** The Art Of Focus – Official Book Summary By The Author Dan Koe.md

*Focus is a currency* (focus as something invested, whose returns are delayed), *persistency*, *persistent focused action*, *gray area* and *kernels of truth* are all Koe's formulations. The pairing in principle 19 is the key to the distinction. Consistency is paired with complacency because repetition without change stabilizes the current level; persistence is paired with iteration because continuing only makes sense if what continues is being improved. Principles 20 and 21 supply the attitude iteration requires: the willingness to question what is not working and to look for the partial truth in negative feedback rather than treating it as all or nothing.

The change is documented across the corpus. Earlier, Koe described a system as "a process of doing the same thing every day" and praised repeating "boring fundamentals" like grinding in a video game (Chapter 12). From 2023 repetition stops being sufficient, and iteration and persistence are added, with the explicit reason of not depending on motivation. In August 2024 he again says "consistency is key" when choosing a routine that fits one's life, and in October 2024 he says he does not believe consistency is the path to success: it maintains progress but does not create it, and stagnation is death. The positions can coexist: repetition builds the base and sustains a phase; iteration is what improves it. For the business roadmap, iteration is the engine, because each stage is reached by changing what the previous stage repeated.

**Source:** Your Future Will Be Determined By 3 Things.md; Focus Is A Superpower (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md

#### Small wins as the test of the right path

Persistence, in Koe's account, is not willpower applied to an unchanged plan. In July 2023 he asks why anyone would keep playing a game that is never fun or shows no progress. This is mostly in the individual's control. People say "you just need discipline, stick it out"; but "if you're not seeing small wins, then you're not doing the right things." He gives three examples of doing things in the wrong order. Someone trying to lose weight who reads a book on hyperthyroidism before learning the principles of training and nutrition. Someone starting a business who buys a course on client acquisition with Facebook ads before learning the principles of traffic and offers. Someone dating who learns "game" before the principles of social, intimate or business relationships. Principles overlap across domains, from bodybuilding to business; progressive overload, for example. You do not try to increase income by $100,000 a month in one month; you increase it by $5,000, then it picks up, "and before you know it you're bench pressing a financial 315 pounds for 10 reps."

**Source:** Focus Is A Superpower (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md

The passage gives persistence a feedback condition. *Momentum*, built from stacking small wins, is what makes persistence sustainable, and the absence of small wins is diagnostic: it means the person is working on specifics before principles, the error Chapter 15 describes. This keeps the doctrine of persistence from becoming stubbornness. One persists in the direction and iterates on the method until the method produces small wins. The financial bench press is the progressive overload of Chapter 15 applied to income, and it explains why the stages of Section 30.2 are not shortcuts: each adds a manageable increment of load.

#### Iterative products: monetization is never one-and-done

Applied to the business, persistence and iteration become a doctrine of products. In August 2023, as step five of a process for high-income skills, Koe introduces *iterative products*. Most creators fail because they treat monetization as one-and-done. "Your products and services evolve as you do; they build off of each other. You can't create a better product if you never launch the first. Stagnation equals death." If something is not selling, "it's your problem, it's not the market's"; if you kept learning and refining your skills, you would see the opportunity to improve your products. "All of this business stuff is just persistence and iteration for life."

**Source:** I Had To Learn These High-Income Skills (If I Wanted To Make Money).md

"It's your problem, not the market's" places responsibility where Section 30.2's stage one placed it for content ("it's not the reader's fault"). The phrase *stagnation equals death* recurs in Koe's work as a general principle (Chapter 10's infinite games); here it explains why a single digital product can become a stepping stone that enslaves (Section 30.4).

In September 2024 he gives the method of iteration as four variables to test. "The final piece of the puzzle is the delicate balance of iteration and persistence; this is where most people fail." "There are four points that we talked about; each of those points provide data. If something isn't working, you need to test something new."

| Variable | What to test | Koe's criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing angle | "New marketing angles: use personal experience to market how your product changed your own life and gauge the response" | The audience's response |
| Product effectiveness | "How effective your product is at solving the problem in others' lives" | "Improve it until people can't stop talking about it" |
| Audience strategy | "The ideas, content and strategies you use to build an audience" | "If you aren't growing it's because you haven't done this; your skill isn't high enough" |
| Promotion | "How, when and how often you promote your product each week" | Until you make the amount of sales you want |

"This whole business thing doesn't stop when you decide on one marketing angle, one product, one strategy for audience growth and one way to promote." "You can't fail if you have all the right pieces and experiment until they work."

**Source:** You Won't Look At Money The Same Again (How To Build Meaningful Wealth).md

The table turns "iterate" from an exhortation into a procedure. Each variable produces its own data, and a failure can be localized: weak response to the angle, weak results in buyers' lives, weak growth or weak sales from too little promotion. This is the self-experimentation of Chapter 15 applied to a business.

In July 2023 he gives a shorter diagnostic that maps the same variables onto two skills. "If you don't get your content in front of people, you won't know if your ideas are valuable; if you get your content in front of people and it doesn't do well, then it wasn't valuable, you need to write better. If you don't work to get your products in front of people, you won't make money; if you get your products in front of people and no one buys, your product isn't perceived as valuable, you need to sell better." "Perceive is the key word; marketing and sales are perception." Then he connects it to "you are the niche": write, speak, market and sell to the identity you target, which is you. "A valley girl won't resonate with meathead bodybuilding advice; a soccer mom probably won't resonate with philosophical musings on business. Your language dictates who you attract."

**Source:** The Value Creator (A New Internet Career Path For Intelligent People).md

The diagnostic has a strict logical form: exposure first, then judgment. Without exposure there is no data; with exposure, poor performance means the value or its perception was insufficient. The emphasis on perception anticipates Chapter 31 ("value is perception"), and the line about language anticipates Chapter 32.

#### "Impossible to fail"

The strongest version of the iteration doctrine appears in February 2025. With an audience and a digital product, Koe argues, iteration has no structural limit, which is not true of almost any other kind of product. In the past you spent a great deal of time and money on product development, manufacturing and distribution, then launched, and statistics circulate according to which "something like 95% of businesses fail within their first year." With an audience and a digital product, you can pivot, test ideas daily in content, turn an idea into a marketing strategy, build a landing page, add payment and launch preorders; if it does not work, scrap it and retry "an infinite amount of times." "In other words, it is impossible to fail if you persist and iterate." And when you later turn the product into a startup or a physical product, you already have so much data that success is far more likely.

**Source:** A Full Guide To Making Your First Profitable Product (Beginners, Take Notes).md

"Impossible to fail" should be read with its conditions: an audience (which supplies exposure), a digital product (which makes each attempt cheap) and persistence plus iteration (which keep attempts coming and improving). Under those conditions, Koe's claim is that failure becomes an intermediate result rather than a final one. The "95%" first-year failure statistic is quoted loosely ("something like") and without a source; widely reported figures for first-year business failure are much lower, so it should be read as rhetoric for the contrast, not as data. The argument does not need it: the point is the cost of each attempt, not the failure rate of others.

#### Launch every quarter

How often should one iterate at the level of products? In December 2023 Koe gives a cadence. "You can and should create a product or service to launch and sell at least every quarter for 2 to 3 years. Don't take that lightly." Sell one thing, then a quarter or half a year later create something new "from a new found place of experience so that it sells better," and the more you do it, the more it compounds. He says he has sold something new every quarter and every time saw "an exponential leap" in authority, audience growth and sales: "that's the exact difference I've noticed between myself and other accounts who make one tenth of the progress in that time." "Evolution is automatic; the people that prevent their personal evolution is themselves." Lean into the thought "I have to build something new if I want to get to the next level," because building forces you to evolve and learn.

**Source:** How Intelligent Creators Will Make Money In 2024 (The Build Teach Earn Method).md

The comparison with "accounts who make one tenth of the progress" is Koe's observation, not a measured study, but the mechanism he proposes is clear. Each launch is a forcing function for learning (Chapter 28's business as forcing function), and each produces a new piece of content, a new reason for attention and a new body of results, so authority accumulates in steps.

The cadence also solves a problem many creators raise: "I want to talk about my interest here, but it doesn't relate to the main thing I want to sell." Koe's reply is a question: "Do you ever plan on evolving or becoming more profitable or more free or having more leverage?" Then "every quarter launch something new under an interest that you actually want to build authority in." When that happens, you will have to educate your audience: much of your content shifts to the new topic, "from a beginner lens so that anyone can understand it and get on board." He refers to his earlier video on the minimum viable offer for how to create the new offer.

**Source:** How Intelligent Creators Will Make Money In 2024 (The Build Teach Earn Method).md

This is the smart progression's "de-niche" (Section 30.2) seen from the calendar. Each quarter can open a new area, and the audience is brought along by teaching it from the beginning.

In June 2024 Koe describes the end state of iteration as a revenue structure. He is not a fan of building one or two digital products and calling it a day, because he favors iteration and evolution: if your one product is weak and not your full potential, you settle for whatever it makes. Make success "almost guaranteed by continuously launching, improving and iterating until you have the perfect combination of digital products that create a high base income." Then monetize further with *micro products*: workshops or events at a low price, one or two calls, launched every one to two months, for predictable revenue spikes on top of the base.

**Source:** Ideas Are The New Oil (How More People Get Rich In The Digital Age).md

*Base income* and *micro products* are Koe's terms for the two layers. The base comes from a stack of iterated products that sell continuously; the spikes come from small, time-limited offers. The structure connects to the "offer stack" of Chapter 33.

#### Overnight success takes ten years: what the guests add

The corpus also contains persistence arguments from other people, and they should be kept distinct from Koe's. In January 2025, John Hugh of Stan says that having met "a couple dozen billionaires or very successful famous people" (some of whom made him think "wait, what"), the one trait he respects in all of them is this: "the only thing that matters is that you just persist." "Just show up every single day and never ever give up, and I promise you you'll look back in five years" successful. His co-founder Vitali closes the interview with two quotes he lives by: "nothing is impossible" and "your level of confidence defines your level of achievement."

**Source:** How To Grow An Audience With Zero Followers & Build A Profitable Startup.md

In February 2025 Koe cites Jeff Bezos: "all overnight success takes about 10 years." He draws a single lesson from it: stop comparing yourself to people who have been in the game five to ten years and expecting to match their skill and results after one month of learning from them.

**Source:** Don't Quit - How To Get Ahead Of 99% Of Personal Brands.md

The Bezos line is the time dimension of the Gap of Section 30.2. The Gap is not only between where you are and where someone else is, but between your first month and their tenth year.

In November 2025, in a conversation about Kortex (the software company Koe co-founded, renamed Eden; Chapter 37 tells its story), his co-founders add two lessons from building a startup. Ari, introduced as "the most cracked engineer," says that technically he is not "super cracked"; "the thing that really makes a difference, especially in startups, is just persistence." His first startup attempt began with zero web or front-end knowledge, and he chose to build a text editor, "an insane first project to take on." The biggest lesson from the conversation (attributed probably to Ari, possibly to Matt; the transcript is ambiguous) is that people often say "this is too hard, you shouldn't do it," but "until you actually try, giving up at that point is meaningless"; "even Eden wouldn't exist if we kept to that advice." When they told venture capitalists, founders and investors that they wanted to build something "better than Google Drive," the reaction was "how are you going to do that? It's too hard. Why would you do that?" Several months later: "I actually think we have a better product. So, it's possible. Just have to try."

**Source:** Kortex The $3 Million Dollar Mistake.md

Matt, the product co-founder, generalizes the point into a view of risk. "There's no point in not taking risks," he says, then corrects himself ("a poor way to frame it"). Everything successful in his life came from a big risk: going part-time at university against his parents' advice to take low-paid content jobs (which led him to meet Koe), risking "public humiliation" by pitching in front of fifty strangers on a cohort call, and flying to Japan to rebuild the product while the team said it was not a good idea. "Success is just like a series of perhaps well-calculated risks, but like a series of big risks," and "anybody who makes it big does things that seem conventionally like a bad idea," though you know it is a good idea that can lead to something great.

**Source:** Kortex The $3 Million Dollar Mistake.md

These are the guests' principles, drawn from their own experience; Koe presents them without adopting them as rules, and the startup context is different from the one-person business (Chapter 37 explains what changes). They also sit in tension with other lessons from the same period. In the same Kortex conversation, the team says they should have scrapped some things much earlier, and Koe admits, "I spent too much time justifying that... we should have called it quits earlier"; in December 2025 he says he has quit "10 times as many goals" as he has set and that "you should quit more goals than you set," because otherwise one cannot narrow in on the correct goals. The tension is real, and the corpus offers its own resolution: persist in the mission, the direction or the validated channel; do not cling to a particular implementation or to a goal that experimentation has shown to be wrong. Vitali's condition points the same way: persistence is warranted when the belief behind it is grounded in data and insight. Iteration is precisely the practice of changing implementations while persisting in direction.

**Source:** Kortex The $3 Million Dollar Mistake.md; How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md; How To Grow An Audience With Zero Followers & Build A Profitable Startup.md

### 30.6 Numbers, Thresholds and Timelines

#### Why Koe gives numbers: belief before action

Koe gives more numbers about income than about almost any other topic, and he is explicit about why. The numbers are not forecasts for the reader; they are tools for changing what the reader believes is possible. In May 2024 he says the aim of a video is to "give you a sense of control over your career and life" and to "prove to you that it's possible to make $1 million as one person, as a creator, with simple logic and math, because the first step to action or even seeing the opportunity is belief." Without belief, the opportunity does not even register.

**Source:** How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md

The claim rests on the perceptual model of Chapter 6: the mind registers what fits its goals and beliefs, so an outcome believed impossible is not pursued, and the paths toward it are not noticed. In the same video he addresses skeptics directly: "If you want to build a $1 million one-person business, people have done it, it's possible; you don't need to project your limiting beliefs on me about how people can't do it or how it's only special." "You have an internet, you have a keyboard, you have a brain"; with those three, "hopefully you can improve them all or the skill associated with all."

**Source:** How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md

In March 2026 he adds the disclaimer that governs every number in this section. "We're going to set the goal of 1 million dollars... just so we can frame it. I'm not saying you're going to make that. I'm not saying you're going to make anything. There's no amount of knowledge that I can just give you right now that will immediately make you a million dollars. It doesn't work like that. Stop looking for videos like that." The breakdown matters "because you need to at least believe it's possible before you can do it." In the same passage he limits the scope of the AI material that follows (Section 30.7): "we're not going over crazy agent workflows here, we're just going over normal AI use," because "you need to learn the fundamentals before you can actually get into the crazy AI agent stuff."

**Source:** How To Build A $1M One-Person Business Faster With AI.md

The $1M goal is therefore a *framing device*: a number large enough to force the business to be thought of as a system, and concrete enough to be divided into daily quantities. The section should be read in that spirit.

#### The $1M math

The division is simple and Koe repeats it in several videos. In March 2026: "A million dollars divided by 12 months is 83,333 dollars per month. That number divided by 30 days is 2,777 dollars per day." He lists ways to reach the daily number: "sell 18 $150 products a day, like a course"; "sell 111 $25 subscriptions a day" (for example, starting on Substack); "go the freelance route and land one $5,000 client every other day," which he calls "a pretty high price point for just starting out in freelancing, but it illustrates the point"; with coaching, consulting or services, "land one $10,000 client every four days"; "or you can have a combination of both," such as "one to two clients a week and a few other product or subscription sales a day."

**Source:** How To Build A $1M One-Person Business Faster With AI.md

| Route to $2,777 a day | Volume required |
|---|---|
| $150 product (e.g., a course) | 18 sales a day |
| $25 subscription | 111 sales a day |
| $5,000 freelance client | One every other day |
| $10,000 coaching or consulting client | One every four days |
| Combination | e.g., one or two clients a week plus a few product or subscription sales a day |

**Source:** How To Build A $1M One-Person Business Faster With AI.md

The next step converts sales into traffic. "At a 2.5% conversion rate on a landing page, meaning 2.5% of the people that go to the landing page actually buy the thing that you're selling, you need about 720 people to visit that page a day to make 18 sales on a 150 dollar product." Alternatively, one could have "a post go viral once or twice a month, which I wouldn't bet on, but is becoming more and more of a thing as the social media platforms are becoming more interest-algorithm related." "Even if you're just starting off, if you can create good content, you can post your first reel [and] it'll go mega viral," but "most people don't know how to create content, so that's usually not going to happen unless it's based on luck."

**Source:** How To Build A $1M One-Person Business Faster With AI.md

A February 2025 version of the same math adds an estimate of what traffic is achievable. Getting 720 people to a page converting at 2.5% "is pretty doable." It takes a while, but beginners writing on Twitter wisely can get 100,000 impressions in their first month, and one TikTok or Reel can go viral for a million views. Get 720 people to a page once a day at 2.5% conversion, "split up as much as you want." "Not easy by any means, but it's feasible when you understand the moving parts of business."

**Source:** A Full Guide To Making Your First Profitable Product (Beginners, Take Notes).md

The value of the exercise is not the final number but the chain. Revenue becomes daily sales; daily sales become daily visitors; daily visitors become a content and distribution problem. Each link names a lever (price, conversion, traffic), and the levers correspond to the four variables of Section 30.5. The 2.5% conversion rate is an assumption Koe uses for illustration; actual rates vary widely with the offer and the audience. The impression figures are his estimates of what is achievable, not guarantees.

#### Your standard is how much you make

Koe adds a psychological mechanism that explains why a large number can help rather than overwhelm, which might seem to contradict the Gap of Section 30.2. In July 2024: "If you have the goal of making a million dollars a month and you aren't there yet, that shines a light on the potential problems in your business that you can then see and fix and move forward and get closer to that goal; and as you move forward, new problems emerge that you start to think, 'oh, that's why I'm not hitting that number,' and then you eventually achieve it." "The reason you don't make as much money as you want to make is because... your standard is how much you make. If you raise your standards higher and actually mean it, then that is what identifies problems, and then once you solve those problems, then you reach the standard."

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Be Relevant In 10 Years.md

*Your standard* is Koe's term, and it applies Chapter 6's thesis that problems exist only relative to a goal (and Chapter 3's claim that standards define what is perceived as a problem). A higher standard makes problems visible that a lower standard leaves invisible. The difference from the Gap is the qualifier "and actually mean it" combined with a path: a goal held as a standard, with its problems being solved one by one, organizes attention; a goal admired from a distance, without a path, disorders it.

#### The $1 Million Creator in eight steps

In May 2024 Koe embeds the money question in a broader sequence he calls "the $1 million Creator." The steps are: (1) choose a vision, not a skill; (2) stack the future-proof skills (marketing and sales, writing and thinking, entrepreneurship); (3) solve problems with experimentation; (4) become a value creator; (5) monetize your mind, not your time; (6) turn it into $1 million, through the levers of writing, promotion and iteration; (7) rest, because work contracts the mind and rest expands it; (8) "[expletive] being busy," that is, stop work that does not move the needle toward your vision. He notes that only "two, three points" are directly about making money; the rest concern "the skills, mindset, beliefs, habits associated with that."

**Source:** How Smart Creatives Work Less & Earn More LEVERAGE.md

The sequence is a compressed map of this book. Vision (Chapter 7), skill stacks (Chapter 35), experimentation (Chapter 15), the value creator (Chapters 18 and 35), monetizing the mind (Chapter 34), rest (Chapter 13) and the refusal of busyness (Chapter 12) surround the two money steps. That proportion is Koe's point: income is the output of a person built in a certain way, not a separate project.

#### Follower-to-income benchmarks, and why they do not agree

The second family of numbers relates audience size to income. In December 2022, describing stage two of the roadmap, Koe says it begins around 10,000 followers, when "you should feel you have the growth game down." He advises not waiting until then to monetize, but says this is "where real money comes in." Then he shares his own numbers: "at 500 followers I had a 3K month, at 10,000 followers I had a 10K month, at 50,000 followers I had a 50K month," and at 200,000 a $200,000 month (he corrects himself mid-sentence), "because of Black Friday." He shares them "as a baseline," hoping "it breaks some people out of their limiting money beliefs." About two years earlier, he says, he thought $100,000 in a month was insane, until he saw a trustworthy person he talked to frequently, doing the same thing he did, making $100,000 a month; that "blew his mind." He wanted $10,000 a month and was happy with it, "but it's fun to push more in business." The passage is repeated, almost word for word, in the 2024 compilation.

**Source:** The One Person Business Roadmap (99% Make This Mistake).md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

The story about the peer is the mechanism of the previous subsections in miniature: a belief changed by seeing someone similar achieve something, and with it the set of outcomes Koe considered possible.

In October 2023 he discusses a ratio he read in someone else's post: one can make 50 cents to $1 per follower per month. He qualifies it: this is "only possible with your own product or service... not possible with banking on the algorithm or using something like AdSense." "You need to create an independent source of income... not rely on the algorithm or AdSense as your boss like you would a job... you don't want another boss," a corporation deciding your income by your views and how much you cater to it. The ratio held for him "to a point": in his first three years he made about $1 per follower per month (10,000, then 50,000, then 100,000 dollars a month), with $800,000 in his third year, and about $3.1 million in revenue so far in the year of the video. But with 2.8 million followers, he does not see himself making $2.8 million a month: "maybe I've just gotten lazy with how I promote... then again that could very well be a limiting belief." His conclusion: "If you slowly build a decent readership, launch a new product and get 80% of the things right, I find it hard to believe that you couldn't make a million dollars."

**Source:** How To Make $1 Million A Year As A Digital Writer.md

The ratio is adapted from an unnamed post, and Koe's own figures show where it breaks: it holds in the early years and fails at scale. His two explanations for the break (laziness in promotion, or a limiting belief) are offered as possibilities, not as a diagnosis. The insistence on "your own product" connects the ratio to Section 30.3's argument against income based on algorithms or platform payouts.

In December 2023 he gives a different illustration. If you have 10,000 followers and make $3,000 a month, you know you can reach 100,000 and make $30,000 a month; at a million or more, or by optimizing your offer stack and value ladder, you could make $100,000 a month at 100,000 followers; and "if you know what you're doing, you can have 5,000 followers and make $100,000 a month; I see people do it all the time."

**Source:** The Future Of Work Is Play (How To Create A Digital Career).md

The three sets of numbers are not consistent. The 2022 numbers imply about $1 per follower per month; the December 2023 illustration implies about $0.30, and then detaches income from audience size altogether. The difference has an explanation in the sources themselves. The 2022 figures are Koe's personal history; the 2023 ratio is a rule he tested and saw break; the December 2023 figures are an illustration of leverage whose outcome depends on the offer. Read together, they support one conclusion that none of them states alone: the audience sets an upper range, but the offer (its price, structure and promotion) determines where in that range the income falls.

#### How much is enough

Koe also gives thresholds for what is sufficient. In May 2025 he says most people can live fine on $5,000 a month; with a family, $10,000–$15,000, "that's three clients a month" at $5,000, which he admits is a cliché in online business, "but it's true." A $10 subscription with 500 buyers yields $5,000 a month in recurring revenue, though it takes longer because more units must be sold (and he does not recommend subscriptions for beginners, pointing to a separate Q&A video). "500 people on an internet that has four to five billion people, there isn't that much to ask for." Over a year one can build an offer stack, a high-ticket product, a low-ticket subscription and something in between, and then one needs about one high-ticket buyer a month, ten subscription buyers and ten product buyers. Given how attention flows (people constantly follow new people; fluctuations invisible to outsiders occur naturally), "there's really more than enough attention to go around."

**Source:** The Future Of Work (How To Become AI-First).md

The passage reframes the $1M math at a human scale. The point of the "500 people" line is proportion: the required number of buyers is tiny relative to the internet's population. Chapter 34's "enough" develops the idea that the goal is sufficiency and autonomy, not maximization.

#### How little competition there is

In July 2025 Koe adds a claim about competition. "If you actually commit to it, which 99% of people don't, then you will probably make 10 to 100K a month," provided you can improve, iterate and not quit after two weeks. "Those who are not in the game just don't understand how little competition there actually is": the successful online businesses you see on social media are "like 10 to 50 people... maybe a 100, maybe 200," out of seven to eight billion; your echo chamber is "so incredibly small, it's actually mind-boggling." The only successful online business people are those who do not care about competition and know they can grab their piece of the pie.

**Source:** I'm 28. Here's How To Get Ahead Of Most 20 Year Olds.md

The figures are impressions from inside Koe's network, not counts, and the "10 to 100K a month" is conditional on a commitment he says 99% of people do not make. The claim's function is to counter the saturation fear of Section 30.2: what looks like a crowded field is a small, visible cluster that the algorithm shows repeatedly to people interested in it.

#### The labor theory of value and asymmetric income

Why are such numbers hard to believe for people coming from employment? Koe's answer, from October 2024, names an economic idea. People who have only worked jobs (nothing wrong with that, he adds; he is "not one of the guys" saying quit your 9-to-5) understand only "the labor theory of value": I do this much work, I get this much every week or two. "It's not asymmetric," and it is not how the world works: "you're not getting paid in accordance with the value that you provide." Once you are paid by value, it takes time for the mind to shift to "I can send one email out" and make large amounts depending on time, skill and product. He first says $500,000 to $1 million and then corrects himself: "I can make $10,000 in an email."

**Source:** How Smart Creators Will Grow An Audience From Zero In 2025.md

**Complementary context:** the labor theory of value is a doctrine of classical political economy (associated with Adam Smith, David Ricardo and, in a different form, Karl Marx) according to which the value of a good derives from the labor required to produce it. Koe uses the term loosely, for the employee's expectation that pay is proportional to hours worked, and contrasts it with income tied to perceived value (Chapter 31). His use is a reinterpretation for his own purposes, not an engagement with the economic theory.

The concept explains the resistance the earlier numbers meet. A mind calibrated to hourly pay reads "$10,000 from an email" as implausible, because it measures income by the hours behind it. The mind shift Koe describes is the business version of the identity change of Chapter 4: the new numbers become believable only after the person has experienced, at least once, being paid for value rather than time.

#### Timelines

The third family of numbers concerns time, and here too the figures differ by video. In May 2023 Koe says being a creator "has been a three-to-five-year thing" for him, including a two-year gap of "not really taking it seriously," during which he made his first $10,000; then income rose slowly: about $100,000, $150,000, and $800,000 "when I finally figured this out," and higher since. His advice is to "just stick it out... take action and be consistent for what would be getting a four-year degree, but it's not like you're not getting paid along the way if you offer a product or service."

**Source:** I Made $800,000 In One Year Writing 2 Hours A Day.md

In March 2023 he says he had some prior freelancing experience but started his "creator journey" personal brand about three years earlier, which led to his current results. If you do it optimally and approach it from experience, give yourself three years to hit big numbers, which does not mean you cannot make a good amount in the first year. "$1,000,000 is a lot of money; you don't need that much. Give yourself five years to make $1,000,000 in this space; I bet you can if you commit to it."

**Source:** The Best Online Business To Make $1 Million In 3-5 Years.md

In April 2023, promoting Digital Economics, he relays that someone told him they would have to go through the course repeatedly while executing for a year. "Business takes time": "a year of going through and executing on a course I believe is invaluable; of course it can take less time, but expect a year to see some decent results and to make some decent money."

**Source:** Self Improvers Are Creating Their Own Careers (The New Economy).md

Section 30.1 already recorded the August 2023 figure, "a hefty income in two to four years with persistence and iteration," and Section 30.5 the cadence of launching every quarter for two to three years.

| Milestone | Timeline Koe gives | Source (date) |
|---|---|---|
| Decent results and decent money from executing a course | About one year | 2023-04 |
| "Big numbers" | About three years | 2023-03 |
| $1,000,000 | About five years | 2023-03 |

The figures look inconsistent but measure different milestones (first decent income, large income, $1M, a launch cadence), and Koe does not present them as contradicting each other. The "four-year degree" comparison is the most useful frame: a multi-year commitment comparable to the one most people accept without question for a degree, with the difference that a product or service pays along the way.

#### What not to worry about yet

The last threshold is administrative. In December 2024 Koe notes that many people ask "how do I actually start" because other things come to mind with the word *business*. For a long time, he says, do not worry about building a website, forming an LLC or handling taxes if you have not made at least $10,000 to $50,000. He adds a caveat: he does not know tax or business rules outside the United States, so do your own research. In the US you do not need an LLC; you can be taxed as self-employed indefinitely; $10,000–$50,000 is a lot to you but minuscule to the IRS. "Remove those from your mind and let's focus on actually making money first."

**Source:** Build A One-Person Business As A Normal Person (From $0 To $10K).md

The threshold is Koe's rule of thumb from his own situation, not legal or tax advice, and the claim about audits is his opinion. Its purpose is prioritization: administrative setup feels like progress and protects the founder from the exposure of selling, which is the avoidance Chapter 28 describes.

### 30.7 The Writing Business and the Process with AI

#### Writers should not try to monetize their writing

The roadmap so far has been general. Koe's most frequent application of it is to the writer, because writing is the base skill of his system (Part VIII). His first claim about the writing business is counterintuitive. In October 2023: "Most writers try to sell their services on social media; most writers try to just sell their writing" (a paid blog, a paid newsletter, a paid community), "but that's so limiting." "Writing is both the traffic mechanism and the means to create an information product, or to create the education and the marketing around whatever product you want to sell, even if it's like a physical product." Writers "think that they have to monetize their writing rather than create a solution to the problems that they're talking about in their writing." Since "writing starts with a problem and creates steps and value to get to a solution," the writer should sell "a product that the people that are interested in those interests are already buying." "When you know how to write, market and sell, you can earn an income writing about whatever you want"; creatives generally "don't know how to market and sell."

**Source:** How To Make $1 Million A Year As A Digital Writer.md

*Traffic mechanism* is Koe's term for whatever brings people to see a business, and here he gives writing two roles at once: it attracts the audience and it constitutes the product (the course, the guide, the sales page). Selling the writing itself, as subscription content, uses only the first role and caps income at what readers will pay to read. Selling the solution to the problems the writing discusses uses both. The argument is Section 30.1's distinction between service and product applied to the writer: writing sold as writing is a commodity; writing that leads to a solution is a business.

A related heuristic, from January 2024, explains why writing in particular. If you are a creator, focus on writing rather than posting shirtless pictures (he has in mind fitness creators), "because you need to monetize your intelligence, not your looks; that way you can pivot with the times and you can build a sustainable income." What he teaches, he specifies, is "high impact digital writing," not academic or technical writing, "not how to write a research paper."

**Source:** This High-Income Skill Will Make You Irreplaceable (Full Guide).md

*Monetize your intelligence* (in other videos, "monetize your mind, not your time") is the positive form of Section 30.3's and 30.4's warnings about looks and algorithms: an income based on thinking can follow the thinker into new topics, which is what "pivot with the times" means.

#### The minimal model: newsletter, account, solution

In June 2022 Koe describes a writing-based business that requires very little: (1) a newsletter, (2) a Twitter account and (3) "a creative solution or potential solution to iterate on," which he glosses as "something to talk about." He explains why the newsletter comes first. Traffic platforms such as Twitter, Instagram and short-form video are "very shallow," algorithm-based; you can game the algorithm and grow fast, but "there's no depth, there's no authority, there's nothing there that will inherently make people buy from you." You can sell from social media, and many do, but he wants to set the reader up to develop themselves and write long-form to make sense of their ideas. The newsletter is where services and products are plugged in; it can be weekly or monthly. He adds that his own long YouTube videos serve the same purpose, packaging what he learns, and that "the newsletter is the foundation of these videos."

**Source:** How To Make Money As A Creative On The Internet.md

The three points correspond to the three parts of Chapter 28's minimal business: traffic (the account), trust and depth (the newsletter, which Chapter 24 calls the hub) and an offer (the solution). The word "potential" in the third point matters. The solution does not have to be finished; it has to be something the writer can iterate on in public, which is the content ladder of Section 30.3.

#### Nine steps (2022), six steps (2024)

Koe gives a step-by-step version of the writing business in August 2022 and repeats it in the February 2024 compilation. The steps are: (1) choose a topic you cannot shut up about; (2) brainstorm a unique perspective; (3) write 1,000+ words on the topic in a structured way; (4) set up a blog or newsletter platform and post it; (5) repurpose the actionable, step-by-step section into a Twitter thread (and from there an Instagram carousel); (6) learn how to grow on a platform, through branding and through getting eyes on your profile by networking; (7) turn the impactful ideas from the long-form piece into individual tweets (with his PPP framework), so that "you have a perpetual cycle of content"; (8) develop your own writing system; (9) branch out to other platforms when you are ready and have validated ideas. He frames this as what one can do with one to two hours of writing a day, assuming one has a product or service, or an idea for one that can be built and fueled with the audience. With belief, an abundance mindset and the understanding that the internet is infinite, he says, such a writer can earn a higher monthly income than "one of the world's highest paid doctors," because the doctor has a cap. (In the spoken recap of both versions he misnumbers the steps, saying "five" twice; the body lists nine.)

**Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Thrive In The Next 10 Years.md; The One-Person Business Model (How To Productize Yourself Full Guide).md

*Perpetual cycle of content* is Koe's name for the loop that steps 3, 5 and 7 create: long form generates short posts; short posts that perform are expanded into new long form (Chapter 24's ecosystem). The comparison with the doctor restates Section 30.1's distinction between time-capped and leveraged income; it is a rhetorical contrast, not a claim about actual earnings.

In January 2024 he gives a simpler version in six steps: (1) choose the topic you cannot shut up about, without overthinking it ("pick a topic and practice writing"), and, if you are already a creator, use your *topic tree* (Chapter 22); (2) brainstorm your unique perspective; (3) write 500–1,000 words on the topic per week; (4) deconstruct and simplify the ideas into daily posts; (5) learn how to get your writing shared; (6) monetize your experience: with writing, a growing audience and traffic, create a product or service (he points to his video on seven digital career paths for options).

**Source:** This High-Income Skill Will Make You Irreplaceable (Full Guide).md

The change between the versions is a simplification. The weekly volume drops from 1,000+ structured words to 500–1,000; the topic tree enters; and in the 2024 version threads on X can stand in for a newsletter at the start. Koe gives no reason for the revision, but the later version is the current one and the two are compatible.

#### Two even simpler starts

Two other passages give entry points for people who have not yet started writing at all. In August 2023 Koe says that to become a good writer one needs psychology, to structure a message that "captures, holds and spreads attention," and that psychology is best learned by studying marketing and sales, which he calls applied psychology. His path: (1) choose a topic you would be interested in making your full-time work; (2) block 30 minutes to learn about marketing, sales or your interests; (3) do not skip a day of learning, whether from videos, podcasts, books or internet content; (4) take notes on the ideas that stick out; (5) start posting your ideas on Twitter/X or Threads; (6) study social media as a skill, so that you can grow. Over three to six months of actually trying, you should gain enough followers to be confident it can become full-time. "If you can gain one follower, you can gain a million": learn to replicate the process and make it efficient (if you gained 500 followers in three months, repeat and improve). With enough readers, create a product or service to monetize; by then the marketing and sales knowledge has compounded.

**Source:** You Aren't Successful Because You Care Too Much (How To Stop).md

In March 2025 the entry is framed as learning in public. (1) "Write a newsletter once a week to summarize what you've learned and... teach what you're learning." Many people start newsletters or social accounts this way; Sahil Bloom, who has millions of followers on Twitter, had for a long time a bio along the lines of "exploring my curiosity and sharing what I learn along the way," but "your bio doesn't matter here; what matters is the content: is it interesting, are you teaching it?" (2) Replicate formats you see, for example: "I learned about this insane concept this week, it's called the Zeigarnik effect, and it's the best way to overcome procrastination; here's what you do." Give credit when needed; weaving concepts with personal experience helps you learn more. (3) Write posts on X, Threads or LinkedIn about your opinions, beliefs, personal experiences and what you are learning and building, then "tack on social media as a skill you need to learn the same way." (4) Add a 30–60 minute writing block every morning. If the blocks add up to too much, split them across days: learn on Monday, build on Tuesday, write on Wednesday, walk every day. And use AI while writing, for example by asking: "You are an expert at crafting engaging social posts; what's the best way to start a post on this topic that I'm learning?"

**Source:** How To Learn Anything 10x Faster Than Anyone With AI.md

**Complementary context:** the Zeigarnik effect, used here only as an example of a post topic, is the finding associated with the psychologist Bluma Zeigarnik that unfinished tasks tend to be remembered better than finished ones; Chapter 6 discusses Koe's use of open loops.

The writing time recommended varies across the corpus (30 minutes, 30–60 minutes, one to two hours a day, two hours, two or three days a week), but the principle does not change: a block of morning writing as the base of the business. The figures for beginners are lower than Koe's own routine, and he attributes the drop in time needed partly to growing efficiency as skill increases.

The sequence of these versions (nine steps in 2022, six in 2023 and 2024, a weekly newsletter and a short daily block in 2025) shows the roadmap becoming simpler as it is repeated, not changing direction. Every version keeps the same order: topic, perspective, writing, distribution, growth as a learned skill, and a product once the audience exists.

#### The Writer's Bootcamp: the roadmap as a program

Koe also packages the writing route as a program. In October 2024, promoting the Writer's Bootcamp (run under Cortex, beginning October 28), he says it promises to help participants master digital writing in five weeks, with everything needed to build an audience, which he calls important "as a creative worker going into the future of work," or to develop a skill that opens new career options (freelancing, marketing, writing a book). There is also a curriculum-only version for people who dislike cohorts. Within the bootcamp, "we actually use your anti-vision and your vision as your brand vision, so that's how you turn yourself into the niche."

**Source:** It's actually pretty easy to focus 12 hours a day (if you do this).md

The source gives only the program's premises, not its method. The one detail it does give links two parts of the book: the anti-vision and vision of Chapter 7 become the brand vision of Chapter 27, which is how "you are the niche" (Chapter 19) is made operational.

#### The same process, enhanced by AI

The last question the chapter must answer is whether AI changes the roadmap. Koe's answer in March 2026 is that it does not change the process, only its speed. He asks whether anything has changed since his earlier popular videos on the one-person business, given that "AI's taking over everything," with agents and new features launching daily. "You do the same thing that you would have done before, but now you use AI to enhance that process." "The core difference between starting a one-person business a few years ago and now is just AI." He lists what remains: "You will still generate traffic, you still write content, you still write emails or newsletters, you still create a product or service, you still create a customer avatar, you still formulate a compelling offer, you still build a landing page, you still write persuasive copy for that page, you still put the offer in front of people and see if they buy, and then you iterate from there."

**Source:** How To Build A $1M One-Person Business Faster With AI.md

The list is the whole roadmap compressed into one sentence, and its last clause ("then you iterate from there") is Section 30.5. What AI changes is described in the same video: "To build a business with AI, you just implement AI in the correct places so that you can do things faster, with higher quality, and with less guesswork. AI in this sense is a catalyst to avoid a lot of trial and error." "You can still build a business the old way, that's fine; it'll just take a bit longer, especially if you're not a person who is high agency or knows how to iterate or refine, or not just accept the output of AI as law."

**Source:** How To Build A $1M One-Person Business Faster With AI.md

*Catalyst* is a common word Koe uses here in a specific sense: AI shortens the trial-and-error loop without replacing it. The condition he attaches is important. The benefit goes to people with *agency* (in his redefinition, the ability to act and iterate without permission, Chapter 35) who treat the AI's output as a draft rather than a verdict.

He then addresses whether courses and coaching still make sense when AI can answer questions. "You don't really need a course or a coach anymore, although those things are still valuable, because there's specific knowledge that you probably wouldn't think to look up with AI or even do with AI, and the AI probably isn't going to give you that out of the box." "Coaching is still going to be a thing just because the default response of the AI usually isn't that good. Getting specific knowledge from the source of someone who's done something is still superior, and if it costs 25 to 100 to 200 bucks, big whoop."

**Source:** How To Build A $1M One-Person Business Faster With AI.md

The argument preserves the education product of Section 30.3 under new conditions. AI supplies generic answers; what it lacks is *specific knowledge* (Naval's term, Chapter 20), the knowledge acquired by doing something, which is what a good course or coach transmits.

#### Code democratized, and the products that follow

In June 2026 Koe describes how AI alters the media-versus-code balance of Section 30.3. "Now AI has become a thing, and the ability to code or program, another form of leverage, has become democratized." You probably will not build the next Notion, Salesforce or social platform without a development team, "but most average people can absolutely build a small-scale digital product that they can sell, accompanied by a full marketing funnel that they can have the AI build out." Not everything should be written by AI: "you have to impress your personal taste on it so that it sounds like you, and so that it actually works." Building a prototype is faster than ever: "you can do the thing that used to take a team of 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 people as one person." This was always possible with social media and information products, he notes, recalling his older formula (build an audience, write content two hours a day, create a digital product, sell it in your content), "but information products are abundant." He does not think they will die.

**Source:** If You Have Multiple Interests, Start A One-Person Business.md

*Taste* (Chapter 18) is the variable that keeps the person necessary: AI can build the funnel, but only the founder's judgment makes it sound like them and work for their audience.

In January 2026 he answers an objection to building on AI. His shared prompts, he says, could easily become micro software: create an interface and set the prompts as system instructions. Rather than a general ChatGPT, one could build a hyper-specific chat or tool that helps people learn, practice and do the thing taught in the information product. The objection: isn't building a "ChatGPT wrapper" cheating? "By that logic building anything on the internet is cheating." Typeform, a billion-dollar company, is in his description an HTML wrapper; Cursor is a GPT wrapper; any information product is wrapped by the platform it is built on. If AI is the next cloud (which he believes), every piece of software will be a wrapper. For one person, savvy with resources and using third-party tools, a wrapper is a great way to go.

**Source:** How I'd build a one-person business (if I started over in 2026).md

*Wrapper* is a common software term used with Koe's own emphasis: it describes any product built on top of a more basic technology, and the argument removes the stigma by generalizing it. His characterizations of Typeform and Cursor are simplifications for the argument.

In May 2025 he had already described the endpoint of this line of thought. He imagines (as an "endgame" for Cortex) sharing a workspace containing all of one's knowledge plus custom prompts and AI workflows, so that people not only learn what is in one's mind but can do things with it. He could create "a clone of myself" with the knowledge base and the AI workflows for how he makes YouTube videos, writes books or stays focused. No digital products do this yet, he says; the digital product world is static: a course in which you learn passively and do not actually do the thing, whereas with AI you can. "A prompt that you refine and reuse is a reflection of your understanding of the task. It is an externalized clone of a creative process that no longer needs to sit in your head or in a static SOP document."

**Source:** Harsh Truth You Don't Need To Grind 247 To Be Successful.md

*Externalized clone of a creative process* is Koe's coinage, and it extends the meaning of "productize yourself" from Section 30.1. The first productization packaged a service into a course; this one packages a process into a tool that performs part of it. Chapter 33 treats the broader idea that "systems are the new product."

#### How far one person can go

AI also raises Koe's estimate of the ceiling of a one-person business. In December 2024 he cites Sam Altman, the founder of OpenAI, saying in an interview that he has a group chat with friends "betting on who's going to be the first $1 billion one-person business," because there are already one-person businesses making $1 million and $10 million, and surely others making much more. Koe adds that this was not possible in the past; it is possible now because of technology, social media, the internet, the ability to connect with anyone and attract an audience to who you are, and digital products that take no extra effort, labor or capital to build and distribute.

**Source:** Build A One-Person Business As A Normal Person (From $0 To $10K).md

The Altman anecdote is reported secondhand and is a bet, not a prediction. It belongs to a sequence in which Koe's estimate of the ceiling rises with each technological stage: in 2023 "nobody's really pushed the boundary past five, ten million as one person"; in mid-2024 one-person businesses making "$10 million a year"; in late 2024 the billion-dollar bet; and in 2026 a reinterpretation of the pre-AI model as "a comfortable lifestyle business" after which "you would eventually have to hire a team." The reason Koe gives is that AI lets one person do more. Chapter 37 examines the limits of the one-person model from the other side, through his experience of building a team.

#### A beginner's progression into a future of AI

Koe's February 2025 progression for a beginner's first product brings the chapter's threads together under the conditions of AI. "The best progression to follow going into a future of AI" has five steps: (1) build an audience as a free, high-leverage traffic source; (2) become an authority in your interests or expertise; (3) start a *personal system digital product* that you can test fast, "that's the secret... that's what separates you"; (4) persist and iterate until you reach $100,000 or more; (5) since you then have so much data and so many results, turn that system into something like software or a physical product. Build the audience where attention is right now: social media today, "tomorrow it could be intergalactic space"; it is free, accessible, you can write and grow for free, and it is a skill. Authority comes before the product: "you aren't Marcus Aurelius. From the start, these philosophical quotes that you're posting aren't going to do anything until people care about you." Then a digital product generates cash flow, and with cash flow and an audience, building a startup or a dream business is not far off; or one can simply live as one wants and focus on one's craft.

**Source:** A Full Guide To Making Your First Profitable Product (Beginners, Take Notes).md

The progression restates the whole chapter in order. The audience and authority are stages one and two of Section 30.2; the personal system digital product is "productize yourself" (Section 30.1) in its system form; persisting and iterating to $100,000 is Section 30.5; and turning the validated system into software or a physical product is "media first, software later" (Section 30.3). The ending keeps the openness of the August 2023 path: the product is not the destination but what pays for the next vision, or for the freedom to stop expanding. The Marcus Aurelius line gives the reason authority precedes the product: borrowed wisdom has no pull until the person posting it has earned attention in their own right.

There is a tension between this progression and other passages in which Koe recommends launching immediately, before having an audience, or monetizing from zero followers (Chapter 29). The corpus supports holding both: authority is built while selling something small, a service to start and a product to scale, a first product launched quickly to learn and a later, data-driven product that is iterated. What the progression adds is the endpoint, which in the age of AI is a system that can become software.
