## Chapter 38 — Maps of the Development of Consciousness

Throughout this book, Dan Koe has spoken of "levels": a level of mind that dictates which problems you can see (Section 4.7), a higher self you can identify with, vertical as opposed to horizontal growth (Section 17.5), a business that evolves through stages (Chapter 30). This chapter opens the box those words come from. It presents the developmental maps Koe studied, the way he adapted them, and the model he eventually built out of them, Human 3.0. It is the most theoretical chapter of the book and the one where the line between what Koe takes from others and what he contributes himself matters most, because most of the raw material is borrowed.

Four sources do most of the work. The American philosopher Ken Wilber supplies the scaffolding: the holon, the idea that development "transcends and includes," the difference between dominator and actualization hierarchies, the four quadrants of the AQAL model, and the distinction between states and stages. The psychologist Susanne Cook-Greuter supplies the nine stages of ego development, from the impulsive child to the "unitive" sage. Spiral Dynamics, the model of Don Beck and Christopher Cowan, supplies the image of the spiral and the division into tiers. Abraham Maslow's hierarchy of needs appears in the background as the simplest version of the pattern. On top of these, Koe adds his own vocabulary ("units of mind," "lines, levels and altitudes," "glitches," "false transformation," the three levels of social media content) and, in 2025, a synthesis of his own.

Three cautions govern the presentation.

First, the material spans 2023 to 2026, and the maps change from video to video. Koe moves from expounding Cook-Greuter's nine stages (2024) to condensing them into four "macro stages" (2025), then into a three-level model, Human 3.0 (late 2025), and then into a five-level scale of thinking (2026). The numbering of the stages is inconsistent across tellings, and Koe admits it. The chapter reports each version and the changes between them rather than merging them into one tidy scheme that the author never presented.

Second, Koe's stance toward the hierarchy itself shifts. In 2024 he says that "a higher perspective is more truthful than not"; by late 2025 he calls the stages "lenses of perception... not necessarily better than one another." Both positions coexist for a while. The chapter shows the tension where it appears.

Third, the percentages that accompany the stages ("75 to 80%," "15 to 20%," "1%," "50% at the conformist stage," "80 to 90% in Human 1.0 and 2.0") are the author's approximations or figures he attributes to the models without naming a study. They measure different things and do not always agree. They are reported as what they are: rhetorical estimates.

The table below gives a first orientation to the succession of maps. Each is developed in its own section.

| Period | Map Koe uses | Number of levels | Where in this chapter |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | Wilber's holons, transcend and include, dominator vs actualization hierarchies; "units of mind" | Not a stage scale | 38.1 |
| 2022–2024 | States vs stages (first as his own idea, later attributed to Wilber) | Baselines, not numbered | 38.5 |
| Nov 2024 – Mar 2025 | Cook-Greuter's ego development: preconventional, conventional, postconventional, plus unitive | Nine (with ten labels in one telling) | 38.3 |

### 38.1 Holons, Hierarchies and Transcend-and-Include

Every stage model in this chapter rests on one prior claim: that reality, and the mind within it, is organized in nested levels, each of which grows out of the one below and contains it. Koe takes that claim from Ken Wilber. Before following the stages themselves, it is necessary to understand where the claim comes from, how Koe met it, what its basic unit (the holon) is, and what follows from it: two kinds of hierarchy, a rule of development called "transcend and include," and a general pattern by which order emerges from chaos.

#### How Koe found Ken Wilber, and what he kept from Wilber's fall

Koe tells the story of his encounter with Wilber twice, in January 2023 and again, almost word for word, in a long compilation published in March 2024. Early in 2022 he came across "American philosopher Ken Wilber," "touted to be the most conscious man alive." He picked up several of his books, among them *A Brief History of Everything* and a title the transcript renders as "Cosmic Consciousness," skimmed others, and was "instantly hooked": "his teachings on holism, consciousness in general and just his integral theory really made a lot of philosophical concepts click for me." **Source:** Society Is A Pyramid Scheme (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md; Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md.

**Complementary context:** Ken Wilber (born 1949) is an American writer whose "integral theory" attempts to combine Western developmental psychology, contemplative traditions and systems thinking into a single framework. *A Brief History of Everything* (1996) is a popular, question-and-answer version of his larger *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality* (1995). The title "Cosmic Consciousness" belongs to an older book by Richard Maurice Bucke (1901); the transcript does not allow us to tell whether Koe was referring to Bucke's book, to a Wilber title, or misnaming one, so the reference is left as it appears.

Two details in Koe's account set the tone for how he uses Wilber. The first is lineage. He learned that David Deida, author of *The Way of the Superior Man*, and Mark Manson, author of *The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F\*ck*, had both been Wilber's students; Deida used what he learned to explain modern sexuality and masculinity. The second is a fall. Manson, who was "an adamant student," was supposed to attend a Wilber seminar or workshop when, "out of nowhere," Wilber "kind of turned on his audience," said "weird questionable things," and disappeared from the scene. Manson wrote about it in a blog post titled "The Rise and Fall of Ken Wilber." Koe's summary of that article is a single sentence: "even the most conscious beings are still human, we still have egos." Wilber, he adds, is now back on niche podcasts, which he recommends as "a lot more stimulating than mine," because "I haven't reached that level of thought or articulation yet." **Source:** same.

From the episode Koe extracts a principle of his own about how to learn from teachers: "the medium and the message are separate." Despite Wilber's questionable remarks, "there's still a lot that we can learn from him." The same holds for Alan Watts, who "was the victim of alcoholism"; people have different views on alcohol, "but the point is is that the medium and the message are separate and that even spiritual people are human too and they have their vices and they're battling with them." Koe adds a personal opinion that is easy to miss: in his view, "Alan Watts's alcohol problem still aligned with his philosophy." His recommendation is to research these things yourself, follow curiosity, question, and "don't form hard set beliefs on these things that most people would label as bad because we live in a world of relativity and there are no absolutes except for the absolute." **Source:** Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md.

The principle matters for the whole chapter, because it is the attitude Koe takes toward every source that follows: borrow the map, do not worship the cartographer. It also anticipates a point he will make about the highest stages of development. If the "most conscious man alive" can still act from ego, then reaching a high stage does not make anyone immune to falling back. That idea returns in Section 38.5 as regression under stress. Note also that "medium and message" is a phrase Koe uses elsewhere with a different meaning (writing and speaking as the medium, psychology and persuasion as the message, Chapter 21). Here it means separating the messenger, with his vices, from the teaching.

#### "Hierarchies are the foundation of existence"

When Koe wants to "open the can of human nature," he starts with hierarchies and stages of development. His opening claim is blunt: "Hierarchies are the foundation of existence." Even people who say that hierarchies, or the patriarchy, are bad "are still ranking the hierarchy is bad which is a hierarchy within itself." He recommends *A Brief History of Everything* to anyone who wants to understand patriarchy and matriarchy from "a universally large perspective" across history, and adds the opinion that most people who scream about it today do not understand what is going on. **Source:** Niche Down Is Terrible Advice (For Smart People).md.

The argument is a small piece of logic worth making explicit. To reject hierarchy is to judge that a world without it is better than a world with it, and to judge one thing better than another is to rank. A rejection of all ranking cannot be stated without ranking. Koe does not use this to defend any particular hierarchy. He uses it to shift the question from "hierarchy or no hierarchy" to "which kind of hierarchy," which is where Wilber's distinction between two kinds comes in (below).

#### The holon: a whole that is also a part

The building block of Wilber's picture is the holon. Koe spells the word out (H-O-L-O-N) and defines it with Wilber's formula: "a holon is the unit of everything; it is both a part and it is a whole." An atom is a whole, but it is also part of a molecule. The molecule is a whole, but part of a cell. Going all the way up, humans are wholes but part of society; society is part of a culture; culture part of a nation; the nation part of the world; the world part of the galaxy; the galaxy part of the cosmos; the cosmos part of God, "and so they span in infinite directions." This, he says, helps picture what "some people call relativism or relativity where everything is connected." **Source:** Society Is A Pyramid Scheme (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md; Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md.

Holons do not form a single chain. A human is part of society, but also part of this room, of a family, of a set of friendships. Wood is part of a tree, of a chair, of a house. A holon is a node in many wholes at once. **Source:** same.

In 2025 Koe returns to the same idea without naming Wilber, under the expression "whole parts": reality is composed of "not just parts, not just wholes, but whole parts." He lists several sequences: atom, molecule, cell, organism, environment; letter, word, sentence, paragraph; matter, life, mind, soul, spirit; and the "techno-economic base" of society, from foraging to horticultural, agrarian, industrial and informational. **Source:** You Need To Be Extreme If You Want Your Life To Change.md. The vocabulary and the sequences closely resemble Wilber's, but in that passage Koe does not credit him; Wilber is quoted later in the same video, not for this concept. This is part of a broader pattern described below: over the years, Wilber's ideas enter Koe's own vocabulary and lose their attribution.

#### Existence is relationship: ideas and emotions as holons

What joins holons? Koe answers with Alan Watts: "as Alan Watts would say, existence is relationship and you're smack in the middle of it." The connections between holons are relationships, and the multiplicity of chains (the human in society, room, family; the wood in tree, chair, house) is the visible form of that web of relations. **Source:** Society Is A Pyramid Scheme (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md; Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md.

Then he widens the concept beyond material things, and this is where it becomes a tool rather than a cosmology. "Ideas are also holons, thoughts, emotions, they are holons; they are part of a whole and a whole in themselves." Holons, "or really anything, ideas, figments of consciousness, units of thought is what Alan Watts would call them," are "the building blocks of creative problem solving." The mechanism Koe proposes is this: "this is how humans make sense of the world: we create limits or distinctions," which he here calls holons, and how these distinctions work together is "very context dependent or goal dependent." **Source:** same.

The phrase "goal dependent" connects the holon to the central thesis of Part III of this book: the mind is a goal-seeking mechanism, and the goal determines what is perceived (Section 6.5). The same reality can be cut into different holons depending on what you are trying to do. A carpenter sees wood as part of a chair; an ecologist sees it as part of a forest. Neither cut is the "true" one; each serves a goal.

Koe offers a practical example of why noticing holons is useful. With an emotion, you can ask "how can you follow that relationship to its source and understand it on a deeper level so that you can overcome that emotion if it's like a negative one." An emotion is a whole (it has its own texture) and a part (it belongs to a chain of causes: a thought, a situation, a belief, an old experience). Following the chain is a way of understanding the emotion instead of being carried by it. **Source:** same.

#### An exercise in philosophical contemplation: following honey

Holons, Koe says, are "best understood through philosophical contemplation, just letting thoughts trigger thoughts and going down a rabbit hole of curiosity and discovery." He demonstrates with a jar of honey, and presents it as a practice anyone can do alone: choose a holon and use your mind to follow it. From honey you can go to the process of making honey; to the bees and pollination; to the beekeepers and whether they earn a living wage; to whether harm is done to the bees; to the manufacturing and distribution process; to how honey affects human health when consumed; "and onward toward infinity." The key instruction is the last one: "you can take any single one of those words in that list, even the bullet points themselves, and just start connecting dots." Each word in the list becomes a new node, a new holon whose relationships can in turn be followed. **Source:** Society Is A Pyramid Scheme (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md; Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md.

The exercise looks simple, and it is, but it trains a specific capacity that Koe needs for later parts of his system: the habit of seeing any object as an intersection of relationships rather than as an isolated thing. It is the same capacity that, applied to a topic, generates content ideas (Chapter 22), and that, applied to a problem, reveals that it is "blocked" by something in another domain (the cross-quadrant unlocks of Human 3.0, Section 38.4).

#### Units of mind: Koe's name for holons

In a July 2023 video Koe introduces his own term for the same building blocks. "The fabric of reality is composed of units of mind; that's what I call it in my book that's coming out soon, *The Art of Focus*." He lists the alternatives explicitly: Alan Watts called them "units of thought," Leo Gura "figments of consciousness," and Ken Wilber "holons," "his word for the building blocks of reality." **Source:** Niche Down Is Terrible Advice (For Smart People).md.

Koe prefers "units of mind" because of a philosophical commitment he names: mentalism, the view "that all is mind." His reasoning is a short genealogy of language. Before human consciousness emerged, there was no mind to label, interpret and create language. An animal does not call the objects in his studio "a boom arm, a mic, a camera, a computer"; humans "constructed, layered and evolved" language over time to distinguish and make sense of reality. Animals, mostly in the present moment, see everything as form, information and energy; as Alan Watts would say, "it's a happening." The units into which we cut the world are therefore units made by a mind, which is why he calls them units of mind. **Source:** same.

**Complementary context:** "mentalism" here is used in the sense of philosophical idealism, the view that mind or consciousness is the fundamental reality. The phrase "all is mind" is the first of the seven principles in *The Kybalion* (1908), a Hermetic text. Koe does not cite *The Kybalion* in this passage, so the connection is only a likely background for the phrase.

He then gives units of mind three characteristics, which together restate Wilber's holon in the language of a creator.

The first: units of mind "are hierarchical and intimately connected with the world." His examples mix nature and media on purpose: atom to cell to molecule (the order as he states it), letter to sentence to paragraph to book, content to product to brand to community to social media, short-form content to medium form to long form, a room to an apartment to an apartment complex to a city. "Everything is a hierarchy." And "any of those units are vertically and horizontally connected to everything by a proximal relationship": a short-form post on bodybuilding connects to the gym, to nutrition, to the fork in your hand, to the micro-ecosystem in your gut. **Source:** same.

The second: units of mind "are whole parts," both a whole in themselves, like a human being, and part of something greater, "like humans desiring a purpose greater than themselves." The application follows immediately: "a whole personal brand or creator or online business is nothing without its parts," and most people neglect the need for each part in order to evolve, or the need to collect parts and push toward the next whole they will evolve into. **Source:** same. This is the holon reading of a principle developed in Part IX: a brand is an emergent whole made of posts, products, a newsletter, a voice. Neglect a part (say, the product) and the whole stalls.

The third characteristic, that units of mind "emerge, transcend and include," is Wilber's rule of development, to which we now turn.

The renaming is explicit and dated: "holon" becomes "units of mind" in July 2023. It does not stick. In later years Koe returns to "holons" (2024) and to "whole parts" (2025), and "units of mind" appears in the corpus only in that one video.

#### Transcend and include

Holons are hierarchical in a specific way: "they transcend and include one another." Something emerges, "and then it transcends and includes what is under it." **Source:** Society Is A Pyramid Scheme (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md (2023); repeated in Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md (2024).

Koe's main example is the human being. A human transcends and includes the animal. We include physicality and the survival nature, but our survival is "also conceptual," not only physical: we defend ideas, beliefs and identities the way an animal defends its body. This is the root of what Koe calls the law of conceptual survival (Section 3.4), here placed inside Wilber's framework: conceptual survival is animal survival, transcended and included. And something new emerges at the human level: "spirit emerged," and "humans have the ability to sense it." Koe defines spirit as "pretty much the connection of minds through the intangible," and illustrates it with a sports team's team spirit: a shared energy going into a game that helps the team win, so that a low-spirit team against a high-spirit team can see the game swayed "in any direction." **Source:** same. "Spirit" is therefore an ordinary word used in a specific sense: not a supernatural substance, but the intangible connection between minds. The same sense returns, enlarged, in the spirit quadrant of Human 3.0 ("the domain of meaning and connection," Section 38.4) and in Chapter 40.

In November 2024 Koe states the rule as a law of all evolution: "all evolution, especially the stages of development, are transcend and include: you transcend the last stage and you integrate its principles or its truths." He draws two consequences that keep the rule from being a mere slogan. First, "to think that you are just transcending survival mode and you will never... have to utilize that again is false." Second, to think that your childhood does not affect what you do and think now is also false: "you have to integrate it and leverage it." Development does not erase the earlier stages; it reorganizes them. **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md. (In that video he reuses the rule at the top of the scale: people at the unitive stage have integrated all past truths, which is why they can adopt the ego as a lens to act; see Section 38.3.)

In October 2025, now speaking of his own Human 3.0 levels, Koe turns the rule into the image of a toolbox: "You never leave any given level... You transcend and include. A level one perspective becomes a tool in your toolbox when you transcend to level two and so on." Being "wholly level three" does not mean you will not get angry, act out, be human or do stupid things, "because you are still part level one and level two." With a knife to your throat, you tap into level zero or level one thinking, pure survival instinct: "you're going to turn into an animal for a second," fight, flight or freeze. **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

And in December 2025 he pushes the image one step further. The stages are "tools in the toolbox... lenses of perception... not necessarily better than one another, but... larger, higher, more inclusive... more complex." Spiritual people praise the unitive stage, but "sometimes the unitive stage doesn't let you solve a certain problem." Higher stages keep all lower stages "in the toolbelt" to pull out when needed. **Source:** How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md.

Read in sequence, these passages show two movements. One concerns attribution: in 2023 the rule is presented as Wilber's; in 2024 it is used without explicit credit; in 2025 it is simply part of Koe's language. The other concerns meaning: the rule moves from a statement about the structure of reality (holons contain holons) to a practical statement about the self (nothing you were is lost; it becomes a tool). The second movement prepares a significant change in how Koe values the stages, from "higher is truer" to "higher is more inclusive," discussed in Section 38.3.

#### Two kinds of hierarchy: dominator and actualization

If hierarchies are the foundation of existence, why does the word have such a bad reputation? Koe's answer distinguishes two kinds. "Everything is structured as a hierarchy and there are two types." The first are "dominator hierarchies, which is what gives the word hierarchy a bad reputation": "pyramid scheme-esque structures." The second are "natural hierarchies or actualization hierarchies, which is an order of increasing wholeness, such as particles to atoms to cells to organisms, or letters to words to sentences to paragraphs: the whole on one level becomes a part of the whole of the next." **Source:** Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md.

Society, he argues, mimics the hierarchical structure of nature, but, depending on the context, it falls under the dominator definition, and so do its parts: public schools, government, corporate jobs, some religious institutions. They are "hierarchies within hierarchies." **Source:** same. This is the theoretical root of the critique of institutions developed in Chapter 2 (Section 2.5 in particular), where the pyramid of school and employment is read as a dominator structure.

The distinction has a history in the corpus that should be made visible, because Koe's account of it changes in two ways.

The first change is attribution. In March 2023, in a conversation with Dickie Bush, Koe presents the distinction without any source, as his own way of seeing things. A dominator hierarchy is a pyramid scheme; the alpha ape who gets the females and his choice of food; a corporate job structure with fewer people at the top and more at the bottom, where "just by sheer probability... 99% of people aren't going to be able to get to the top of that ladder." The actualization hierarchy is the creator economy: "it evolves... it transcends, includes, and it keeps evolving... anyone can get to the top." He adds an important admission: "I can't really prove this." He believes that technology, consciously or not, is solving base human problems so that people can make a living doing what they enjoy. Later in the same conversation he applies the frame to self-investment: "you're just investing in the pyramid scheme of yourself but under the actualization frame." **Source:** Dan Koe & Dickie Bush On One-Person Businesses, Creative Workflows, and Lifestyle Design.md. In July 2023 and March 2024 he ties the same distinction to Wilber and *A Brief History of Everything*. The author gives no reason for the change; the later passages simply name the source.

The second change is definitional. Alongside the "pyramid" definition, a July 2023 passage gives another. Dominator hierarchies "give hierarchies a bad reputation and make people want to dismantle the patriarchy," and they occur "when a unit within the hierarchy attempts to dominate," like a cancer cell that replicates until it kills its host, or that dismantles the hierarchy by killing a lower level of it. "The cancer cell has to be healed and reintegrated in order for the hierarchy to continue healthy growth and evolution." **Source:** Niche Down Is Terrible Advice (For Smart People).md.

The two definitions are not the same. The first describes a shape (a pyramid with a narrow top that most people cannot reach). The second describes a process (one part of a healthy hierarchy grows at the expense of the whole). They can be read as complementary: the second explains how a natural hierarchy degrades into a dominator one, and suggests what its cure would be (healing and reintegration rather than destruction). But Koe does not reconcile them, and the reader should not assume he meant one when he said the other.

| | Dominator hierarchy | Actualization (natural) hierarchy |
|---|---|---|
| Definition 1 (2023-03, 2024-03) | Pyramid-scheme structure; few at the top, most cannot climb | "Order of increasing wholeness"; the whole on one level becomes part of the next |
| Definition 2 (2023-07) | A unit within a hierarchy that attempts to dominate (the cancer cell) | The healthy hierarchy that the cancer cell threatens |
| Examples | Pyramid scheme, alpha ape, corporate ladder, public school, government, some religious institutions | Particles → atoms → cells → organisms; letters → words → sentences → paragraphs; the creator economy |
| What to do | Heal and reintegrate the dominating unit | Keep evolving: "it transcends, includes, and it keeps evolving" |

The cancer cell gives Koe a second, subtler lesson, aimed at creators. In the creator economy, "many intermediates despise simple platitudes or cliches or just like quote-like advice," and they enter a bitter phase of rebelling against the nature of business. That rebellion "is good, but like a replicating cancer cell you must take your discoveries and be reintegrated into the hierarchy so that you can advance to the next stage, or you destroy your business like I've seen time and time again because you just get upset." "Advanced creators see those things as a pillar of growth." **Source:** Niche Down Is Terrible Advice (For Smart People).md.

The principle is Koe's, and it applies transcend-and-include to a career. The intermediate who discovers that "post consistently" or "solve a problem" are oversimplifications has learned something real. But the discovery becomes destructive when it turns into contempt for the basics. The advanced creator integrates the discovery and returns to the platitude with a deeper understanding of why it works. This pattern (belief, rejection, integration) appears in Chapter 17 as a general description of how thinking matures; here it gets its theoretical basis.

Koe adds a further characteristic that makes the creator economy, in his reading, an actualization hierarchy. "Units of mind emerge, transcend and include." By nature, fewer creators reach the highest level (he names MrBeast and Joe Rogan: "there can only be so many people up there"), much as in a corporate ladder. But the creator economy "is not corrupt" (it can be, but rarely, he says) and it is decentralized, so people cannot dominate one level of the hierarchy, "maybe for a time until they evolve." The number of creators with 100,000 followers is increasing dramatically, and that number alone is "more than enough to make more than enough money." As the creator economy evolves, new levels are created "through emergence, transcendence and inclusion": new highs, and "new ways of development horizontally." **Source:** Niche Down Is Terrible Advice (For Smart People).md. The claim that the top is open to "anyone" is his belief, which he himself said in March 2023 he could not prove; the growth in mid-sized creators is offered as an observation, without a figure or source.

#### Wilber's graphic: matter, body, mind, soul, spirit

In a July 2023 video on focus, Koe shows an illustration he takes from *A Brief History of Everything*: "you can notice some connections here, right, between physics, biology, psychology, theology, mysticism, and how those align with spirit, soul, mind, body, matter." He adds one interpretive remark: in the mental plane, or the plane of soul or spirit, things get increasingly more complex but give more creative ability. **Source:** Focus Is A Superpower (Take Back Control Of Your Life).md.

The graphic was shown on screen and cannot be reconstructed with certainty from the transcript. The most likely pairing, inferred from the order in which Koe lists the two series (one forward, one backward), is the following; it should be read as a reconstruction, not as a verified copy.

| Level (Wilber's "Great Chain") | Discipline that studies it |
|---|---|
| Matter | Physics |
| Body (life) | Biology |
| Mind | Psychology |
| Soul | Theology |
| Spirit | Mysticism |

**Complementary context:** this is a version of what Wilber calls the "Great Nest of Being," his reworking of the traditional "Great Chain of Being," in which each higher level transcends and includes the lower ones. The point of the image, in Koe's use, is the same as that of the holon: each level is built on the one below and opens possibilities (here, "creative ability") the lower one did not have.

#### Order from chaos: dissipative structures and new wholes

The holon describes the structure of reality at a given moment. Koe also uses it to describe how new levels come into being, and here he combines Wilber with a scientist he cites in a single video, the chemist Ilya Prigogine.

The occasion is a practical question: how do you reach the point of obsession with your work if you are lost or simply do not care? Koe's answer begins with physics: "evolution creates order from disorder." The second law of thermodynamics says natural processes move toward greater disorder over time; entropy is the measure of that disorder, randomness or chaos. "That's only half the story." Prigogine (the transcript renders the name as "Ilia Priagene"), in a book Koe names as *The End of Uncertainty*, argues that the arrow of time is not an illusion but a fundamental feature of reality. When a system is pushed far enough from equilibrium, it can become unstable, and "this instability can lead to a spontaneous emergence of new complex ordered patterns called dissipative structures." His example: water pouring chaotically from a faucet; at some point, going down the drain, it forms a whirlpool, an ordered structure emerging from disorder. **Source:** You Need To Be Extreme If You Want Your Life To Change.md.

**Complementary context:** Ilya Prigogine (1917–2003) received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1977 for his work on the thermodynamics of systems far from equilibrium. The English title of his 1996 book is *The End of Certainty*; Koe's "End of Uncertainty" is likely a slip. A dissipative structure maintains its order by continuously exchanging energy and matter with its environment; the whirlpool exists only while water keeps flowing.

Koe then joins Prigogine to the holon. Reality is composed of whole parts, and the sequences (matter → life → mind; atom → molecule → cell → organism) show "evolution creates order from chaos": as matter became more complex, life emerged; as life progressed, mind emerged. "As anything becomes more developed, complex, disordered or messy, a new whole emerges as the ordered structure for it." **Source:** same.

The third step applies the pattern to the mind. Entropy applied to the mind gives psychic entropy, the tendency of the mind toward disorder, randomness and chaos (Section 5.2). So "when you feel lost or your life falls into chaos, you are presented with an opportunity for a higher version of yourself to emerge," like a dissipative structure or a new whole. Koe insists this is "not abstract philosophy" but directly observable, and points to his own story (told in Chapter 4): things became so complex and disordered that he fell into a rut, until, on his birthday in 2021, he got so fed up with his progress that he had no choice but to flip the script and do the complete opposite. **Source:** same.

The argument has three links (a law of physics, a pattern of emergence, a psychological application), and only the first is presented as science. The second is a framework (Wilber's, uncredited in this passage), and the third is Koe's interpretation, supported by his own experience. The analogy between a whirlpool and a person who reorganizes their life is a metaphor that explains, not a mechanism that has been demonstrated.

The passage also marks a change of emphasis in how Koe treats entropy. In many earlier videos entropy is "an enemy we are all battling against" (Chapter 5), and in 2024 he describes success as "entropy management": release it when life is boring, constrain it when it is overwhelming (Section 5.5). Here, in mid-2025, high entropy becomes the precondition of transformation. Koe attributes the shift to his reading of Prigogine ("that's only half the story"). The two views can coexist: daily order protects focus, and a period of chaos can be the moment a higher order forms. But the emphasis has moved.

In October 2025 Koe generalizes the pattern into a definition of life itself: "Since life, development, and evolution follow a general unfolding toward more chaos or complexity, and ordered structures are created to contain that chaos, we can call the process of life, especially your personal life, problem-solving." His image is the seed. A seed unfolds into a flower, many times more complex, and needs resources from its environment to develop itself. It has a natural desire to grow, and through evolution it solved the problems that killed other plant species. We may not think the seed consciously solves problems, but we label its behavior that way because we have language and seeds do not. So "there is a natural flow of life toward greater complexity. Complexity introduces problems, and to constrain the entropy that stems from complexity, an ordered structure must emerge through creation." He admits this is "starting off a bit deep" and recommends rewatching the passage a week later, because the pattern "can be noticed and observed across all planes of reality." **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

"Creation" here is a common word with Koe's own sense: the process of molding, controlling and ordering consciousness, the act by which an ordered structure is brought into being to contain complexity. This passage opens the video in which Human 3.0 is presented (Section 38.4), and it functions as its foundation: each level of the model is a new ordered structure, and moving between levels is solving the problems that complexity introduces.

#### History as the fifth dimension: the master pattern

In February 2026, building his framework of "five-dimensional thinking" (presented in full in Section 38.2), Koe assigns the fifth dimension to history. "In all previous examples, we never really tapped into the power of history." "If you understand how things develop and evolve over time, you can predict, not entirely accurately, but in a more accurate way, how to go into the future, especially with your own actions so you can set yourself up for some kind of success." The emphasis is on understanding, not memorizing: "We're not trying to know here, we're trying to understand. We're not trying to memorize facts about certain events in history. We're trying to understand the general patterns that unfold." He announces "five things" to know (the master pattern, how individuals evolve mentally and physically, how societies evolve physically and mentally, and applications), but the transcript does not cleanly enumerate five, so the exact list cannot be reconstructed. **Source:** How To Think Like A Strategic Genius (5-Dimensional Thinking).md.

The first thing, the "master pattern," is transcend and include. Koe sets it up with a disagreement: a materialist says reality is atoms; a mentalist says it is qualia (subjective experience); zooming out, "they're both correct because they're in separate perspectives." Throughout history, reality is composed, "on a very general metaphorical level, of whole parts." "With each evolution of something, when something new emerges, it transcends and includes the thing before it." His sequences: matter → life → mind; word → sentence → paragraph → chapter → book → library; machine → computer → AI; human → house → city → state → country → continent → planet → solar system → galaxy. **Source:** same. Here, too, Wilber is not named; the attribution is inferred from the vocabulary and from Koe's use of Wilber's framework throughout the same video.

The new element in this version is a rule of failure: "If you transcend and you don't include, then something's wrong. And if you remove one of the parts from the whole that allowed that thing to exist, then the whole self-destructs." "This is why everything is connected in the way that people talk about." **Source:** same. A whole depends on its parts. A mind that despises the body it rests on, a business that despises the basic skills it was built from, a civilization that destroys the biosphere that sustains it: in each case, a higher level has transcended without including.

Koe's example is the debate between environmentalists and technologists, which he reads as a tension within transcend-and-include. Environmentalists worry about climate change "because if the biosphere goes to crap, then humanity goes down with it": removing a part makes the whole self-destruct. Technologists say, in his paraphrase, "we don't really care about the environment right now because we're eventually going to create the technology that allows us to make the biosphere better," thinking in terms of acceleration and progress, while environmentalists think of "doing things differently right now so that we can slow down the use of the resources." **Source:** same. Koe does not settle the debate; he uses it to show that the master pattern gives both sides a vocabulary: the environmentalist defends "include," the technologist defends "transcend."

One terminological change should be noted. In March 2025, Koe had used "fifth dimension of thinking" for something else: the "cognitive dimension" of the construct-aware stage, the capacity to see how the mind constructs meaning (Section 38.3). In February 2026 the fifth dimension is history, and the fourth is the four quadrants: "This isn't the fourth dimension of time. That's actually the fifth dimension." He does not explain the reassignment. The two uses belong to different frameworks and are not necessarily incompatible, but the reader who meets "fifth dimension" in Koe's work needs to check which one is meant.

### 38.2 Macro Stages, AQAL and the Dimensions of Thinking

Section 38.1 gave the structure of reality as Koe borrows it from Wilber: nested wholes that transcend and include. This section turns to the maps that apply that structure to people. It begins with the general claim that the mind evolves through predictable stages and with the status Koe gives to the models that describe them. It then presents Wilber's AQAL model and its four quadrants, the way Koe uses the quadrants to answer "what do I do with my life," his condensation of the stage models into four macro stages, and finally his 2026 framework of "lines, levels and altitudes," in which the quadrants and history become the fourth and fifth dimensions of thinking.

#### The mind evolves through predictable stages

The premise of every map in this chapter is stated most clearly in a December 2025 video: the life you want lies within a specific level of mind, "because the mind evolves through predictable stages." At birth you are "this little survival sponge," soaking up beliefs in order to feel safe, and you can easily get trapped in a stage. **Source:** How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md. "Survival sponge" is Koe's image for the starting point of development: the child does not choose beliefs, it absorbs whatever makes it feel safe. This connects the stage models to the diagnosis of Part I: conditioning (Section 1.2) is what fills the sponge, and a stage is where it can set.

Koe names the models that document the pattern: Maslow's hierarchy of needs, Susanne Cook-Greuter's nine stages of ego development, and Christopher Cowan and Don Beck's Spiral Dynamics, "each built on the others, more comprehensive and multi-domain." And he states their epistemic status carefully: they are not pinpoint accurate; they offer a big picture, "it's like orienting generalizations," for seeing and identifying patterns. He points to his "Human 3.0" newsletter as the cornerstone article on the subject. **Source:** same.

An October 2025 passage gives the same premise in different words. His three macro levels (low, mid and high consciousness, the levels of Human 3.0) were "adapted from various models in developmental psychology like Spiral Dynamics" and "the nine stages of ego development from impulsive to unitive." These models show that "our mind, which is our values, beliefs, and worldview that influence how we think and make decisions, evolves through predictable stages over time." He adds a detail from Spiral Dynamics that justifies the three-level design: development alternates between a focus on the self and a focus on others, as when someone rejects the religion they grew up in to embrace individual atheism. In that video the author of the ego-development model is not named. **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

"Mind" here has Koe's specific sense, and it is worth fixing because it governs the rest of the chapter: not the brain, not intelligence, but "our values, beliefs, and worldview that influence how we think and make decisions." A stage of development is a configuration of that mind. This is also what he elsewhere calls "level of mind" (Section 4.7).

**Complementary context:** Maslow's hierarchy (1943) orders human needs from physiological and safety needs through belonging and esteem to self-actualization. Spiral Dynamics, developed by Don Beck and Christopher Cowan in the 1990s from the research of the psychologist Clare Graves, describes value systems ("memes") identified by colors (beige, purple, red, blue, orange, green, yellow, turquoise) and divided into a "first tier" and a "second tier." Susanne Cook-Greuter's model of ego development extends the work of the psychologist Jane Loevinger, who measured stages of ego development through sentence-completion tests. Wilber draws on all three. When Koe says the models are "built on" each other, this is the lineage he is pointing to, though he does not trace it in detail.

#### Survival, identity, progress: reading the levels in an audience

Koe applies the stage models most often to a practical question in marketing: what moves a given person. In a July 2026 video he describes three "tensions" that drive attention and buying (developed in Section 32.4): survival, identity and progress, "and they each build on top of each other. People kind of evolve through the stages." The pattern is the one documented by "Maslow's hierarchy, the stages of ego development, spiral dynamics, developmental psychology in general": people at different "levels of mind" have different things that tap their psychology. First they need safety and comfort; second, belonging and status; once those are solved ("preferably you're helping them do" so), third, "they crave deeper meaning, purpose, and experience." **Source:** Learn This Skill If You Want To Win In The Next 2-3 Years.md.

Two qualifications make this more than a pyramid of needs. People can desire any of these at different times, and "AI can't just pin down exactly where you are," which is what makes reading levels a hard skill: "you're essentially able to read people's minds when you get it right." And development is not one-way: you may want meaning, but under great stress or after losing a lot of money you regress to survival, where survival tension hits harder and you become "more susceptible to exploitation." **Source:** same. The last remark is a warning as much as a technique: the same knowledge that lets a marketer speak to someone's level lets a manipulator exploit someone who has regressed. Regression is treated at length in Section 38.5.

#### Integrative meta-frameworks and the observer

In an August 2026 video on strategic thinking, Koe recommends two "integrative meta-frameworks" as objects of study. Wilber's integral theory "maps reality across quadrants, levels of development, states of consciousness, and types." Spiral Dynamics is "a model of value systems development through tiers. It models how humans construct meaning at different stages, which for a strategist is studying reality because it's constructed by the observer." **Source:** The Art of Strategic Thinking (How to Become Wildly Successful at Anything You Do).md.

The last clause is the reason these maps belong in a book about creating your life. If reality, as experienced, is constructed by the observer (an idea Koe develops in Part I with the interface theory of perception and in Section 3.3 with the chain worldview → perspective → perception), then a map of how observers construct meaning at different stages is a map of the different realities people live in. For a strategist, a marketer or a writer, studying the stages is studying the territory.

#### AQAL: Wilber's "map of all knowledge"

The most extended presentation of Wilber's model comes in a July 2025 video. Koe introduces Wilber as "an American philosopher" who "made it his life's work to synthesize all knowledge and experience into one comprehensive theory of everything." The result is AQAL, "all quadrants, all levels," which Koe calls "a grand synthesis of the world's great philosophies, scientific discoveries, and psychological patterns" and "the map of all knowledge": "all knowledge and experience mapped into four interconnected quadrants." AQAL contains four quadrants, levels of development within each quadrant, and "even further than that... lines of development and states and stages of consciousness." According to Koe, Wilber arrived at it "through decades of his own study and three years plus of sabbaticals for writing books and researching." **Source:** This mental model will make you think like a strategic genius.md.

Koe is careful to report Wilber's own reservation: the model is not the end-all-be-all but "a work in progress," and Wilber encourages readers to understand it, build on it and poke holes in it. Koe's own claim is stronger: the model will "drastically shorten your path," whether you are figuring out what you want in life, becoming an enlightened monk or building a billion-dollar company. Of his first reading of *A Brief History of Everything* he says he "felt like my mind had expanded into a new dimension of thinking." **Source:** same.

The whole discussion is framed by a Wilber quotation that Koe places near the start: "I don't have to agree with everything you say, but I should attempt to at least understand it. For the opposite of mutual understanding is quite simply war." **Source:** same. Koe uses it as the ethical premise of the model: understanding multiple perspectives is the antidote to conflict and to "stupid thinking" (Section 17.1). The four quadrants are, in this light, a discipline of understanding.

#### The four quadrants

The grid is simple to reconstruct from Koe's verbal description. Two distinctions cross: interior versus exterior (left versus right side) and individual versus collective (top versus bottom).

The upper left is the individual interior: "your personal thoughts, emotions, beliefs and consciousness... what happens in your personal experience in your head." The upper right is the individual exterior, which "correlates with" the upper left: "your behaviors, your actions and your physical brain state." If an emotion lives in the upper left, "the map for that" in the upper right is "the brain waves that come from emotions"; this is "the scientific objective quadrant." The lower left is the collective interior: "the shared culture, values and group consciousness," distinguished from the upper left in that culture is "shared thoughts, emotions, language, values." The lower right is the collective exterior: "systems or structures or social institutions... the social world." **Source:** This mental model will make you think like a strategic genius.md.

| | Interior (left) | Exterior (right) |
|---|---|---|
| **Individual (top)** | Upper left: thoughts, emotions, beliefs, consciousness; personal experience | Upper right: behaviors, actions, physical brain states; "the scientific objective quadrant" |
| **Collective (bottom)** | Lower left: shared culture, values, language, group consciousness | Lower right: systems, structures, social institutions; "the social world" |

The utility Koe claims for the grid is methodological: when facing any challenge, "you can systematically examine each of the four quadrants or perspectives to get a big overarching picture." **Source:** same.

He gives a negative example to show what is lost when only one quadrant is used. If you think about "what do I want to do with my life" only "from an upper right perspective, like a scientific perspective, you're probably going to end up with a life that's rather shallow and nihilistic. But if you bring in the internal world and shared values and the social system that we're operating within, you get a much much much bigger picture and you're not setting yourself up for failure and more problems." **Source:** same. The claim is Koe's opinion. Its logic is that the upper-right quadrant can describe what you do and what your brain does, but it has no vocabulary for what matters to you (upper left), what your culture has taught you to want (lower left) or what the world currently makes possible (lower right). A life planned from behavior and measurement alone has no meaning built into it.

#### Running the quadrants on "what do I do with my life"

Koe turns the grid into a set of questions to be written down, four per quadrant. The exercise is his own application of Wilber's model, and he presents it in a specific order: the inner world first, then actions and capabilities, then the cultural context, then systems and structures.

For the first quadrant, the inner world: What are my core values and what truly matters to me? What activities make me feel most alive and energized? What fears or limiting beliefs are holding me back? What does my intuition tell me when I quiet the mental noise? **Source:** This mental model will make you think like a strategic genius.md.

For the second, the individual exterior, actions and capabilities: What are my natural talents and developed skills? What does my behavior reveal about my true preferences? What were some of my favorite things as a child? What concrete steps am I taking, and what life is that leading to? Koe singles out the fourth as "extremely important... This single decision will determine most of your life. And if you just think about this question for like 7 days straight, you'll have a lot more clarity on what you should do with your life." The role of this quadrant is corrective: taking an objective look at actions and capabilities lets you "weed some of those things out" from the answers to the first, interior quadrant. What you say you value and what your behavior shows you value are not always the same. **Source:** same.

For the third, the collective interior, the cultural and social context: What do my parents or religious leaders expect of me in terms of their idea of success? How do my friends influence my career aspirations and life path? How does the language I speak and the digital culture I am exposed to influence my opportunities? What shared values am I drawn to and what do I find meaningful? Koe says "this is where it really starts to make sense as to how powerful this can be," because you "peel back the layers" of how parental or religious conditioning shaped your interior answers. **Source:** same. The third quadrant explains the first: some of the "core values" you wrote down may turn out to be your parents' expectations. This is the diagnosis of Part I (assigned goals, the Matrix as an inherited network of beliefs) turned into a questionnaire.

For the fourth, the collective exterior, systems and structures: What are the current opportunities for jobs, careers or entrepreneurship in today's world? How does the education system or the internet shape my path? What technological or social trends are creating new opportunities? What systemic barriers or advantages do I face? Koe adds three notes of his own. Thinking through the education system and the internet is why he wanted to "pursue something meaningful on the internet since I was like age 16." On AI, "you may be hesitant... it may be a fad, it may not be," but it may be "a very logical thing to learn," and he encourages running all four quadrants on the topic of AI itself. On barriers: "for most people with the internet, there aren't many barriers and there are many advantages." **Source:** same.

The result, Koe argues, is that "you turn the question 'What do I do with my life?' into a comprehensive map that reveals how your authentic self, actual capabilities, personal calling, economic realities, and cultural context all interplay." "You have no freaking excuse now." Even if you still do not know exactly what you want, you will have more clarity "because you took the time to actually think." He lists the kinds of realizations the exercise produces: that you value creativity over financial security (or the opposite); that fear of disappointing your parents is hindering authentic desires; that you procrastinate on some tasks while heavily pursuing other interests; that technology lets you create a career path that was not available in the recent past. And he adds, "That's only the first half of this model." **Source:** same. The second half is "all levels."

#### All levels: the four macro stages

The "all levels" half of AQAL "refers to the developmental stages of consciousness that individuals and collectives can evolve through." Koe's reason for introducing it is a claim about perception: many people think certain problems are impossible or not for them "because they're not operating at the level of consciousness or level of mind that would allow them to actually spot the problem." Levels "illustrate the dominant value systems of an individual or culture": "these levels show what people, cultures or systems hold as central or important," and these values "influence the decisions that are made and how problems are perceived and solved." **Source:** This mental model will make you think like a strategic genius.md.

This is the goal-as-lens thesis of Part III applied to a whole stage of development. A goal filters what you perceive (Section 6.5); a stage is a set of dominant values, and therefore a set of goals, that filters what problems you can even see. Some problems are not hard for a given stage; they are invisible to it.

Koe tells his audience that he has covered Spiral Dynamics and the nine stages of ego development in earlier videos, and "for the sake of this video" he condenses them into four macro stages. Most people reside in the first three; very few ("for the sake of this video we're going to call these people geniuses") reside in the fourth. **Source:** same.

The premodern stage: individuals, cultures or systems "follow established authority and tradition. There is only one right way and that usually involves obeying a god or conforming to what is right or true without question." The modern stage: they "value science, individual achievement, and competition"; it is found in universities, corporate structures and self-help teachings, and it is "dogmatic about the fact that success should be based on merit and results." The postmodern stage: they "value relativistic thinking, or in other words everyone's truth is valid. So include everyone and ensure they are equal." Each of these stages, Koe stresses, has good and bad parts. **Source:** same.

The critical feature shared by the first three is a limitation: "they can't hold multiple perspectives. They are right and you are wrong." This makes it hard to think outside one's own stage and hard to solve both personal and systemic problems. "This is why smart people are incredibly dumb." The fourth macro stage, second tier, is "able to look backward and pull truths from all prior perspectives." **Source:** same. The second-tier thinker is developed further in Chapter 18 (Section 18.4) as the "synthesizer."

| Macro stage | What it values | Typical settings | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Premodern | Established authority and tradition; one right way, often obeying a god | Traditional religion and culture | Cannot hold multiple perspectives |
| Modern | Science, individual achievement, competition; success by merit and results | Universities, corporations, self-help | Cannot hold multiple perspectives |
| Postmodern | Relativism; everyone's truth is valid; inclusion and equality | (Koe gives no setting in this passage) | Cannot hold multiple perspectives |
| Second tier | Pulls truths from all prior perspectives | Very few people ("geniuses" for the sake of the video) | — |

Note the paradox in the postmodern row. A stage whose central value is that "everyone's truth is valid" is still, in Koe's account, unable to hold multiple perspectives. The resolution is implicit in his phrasing: the postmodern stage is relativist as a doctrine (it holds "everyone's truth is valid" as the one right view, and judges premodern and modern views as wrong), whereas the second tier can actually use the truths of each stage, including the ones relativism rejects. Koe does not spell this out; the reading follows from his description.

**Complementary context:** the premodern, modern and postmodern triad is a staple of Wilber's writing, where it corresponds roughly to Spiral Dynamics' blue (traditional), orange (modern) and green (postmodern) value systems, with "second tier" beginning at yellow. Koe's condensation follows that mapping without making it explicit.

The purpose of the macro stages, in this video, is the question of what you want out of life. Koe's method has three moves: "once you understand what stage you're in, you're able to zoom out a layer, see what you value the most (authority, science, equality) and then you can work to move beyond that to incorporate multiple perspectives so that you actually understand the problems that are happening in your life." He applies it to relationship and business problems. And he names the worst outcome: "The worst thing you can do is get locked in one of these lower levels and only view one perspective as the one right way." **Source:** same. "Zoom out a layer" is Koe's phrase for seeing your own stage from outside; it is a specific use of the zooming movement of attention described in Section 6.3.

#### Lines, levels and altitudes

In February 2026 Koe presents what he calls his "actual framework" for thinking, built up dimension by dimension: "We're going to call this lines, levels, and altitudes." The first dimension is lines of thinking (domains); the second is levels (how you think about each line); the third is altitude (the average across lines and levels, visualized as a skill tree). He then adds the four quadrants as the fourth dimension and history as the fifth (Section 38.1). The graphics he shows on screen (a novice-to-advanced width for each line, stacked levels, an interconnected multi-domain graph) cannot be reconstructed exactly from the transcript; what follows is built from his narration. **Source:** How To Think Like A Strategic Genius (5-Dimensional Thinking).md.

The framework is Koe's, assembled on top of Wilber, Cook-Greuter and Spiral Dynamics. Its purpose is the same as that of the rest of the video: "genius thinking," which Koe defines as the ability to keep thinking (Chapter 17).

**Lines.** "You can think of the width of this, the novice to advanced, as one line of thinking." A line is "you learning, let's say, marketing or astrophysics or politics or social dynamics or religion, literally learning anything... when you attempt to learn domain-specific knowledge. This graph applies to one domain, and you are increasing the width of that line of thinking. It's like you're gaining experience in a video game." **Source:** same. A line is horizontal growth in one domain, the "filling the cup" of Section 17.5.

**Levels.** The second dimension is "how you think about each line." Koe says the levels are extracted from cognitive development research ("see Ken Wilber, see Susan Cook-Greuter, see Spiral Dynamics") and applied to thinking about specific and multiple domains. He describes five.

Level 0, instinctual: when you are born, "you just act out of pure survival. You react to stimulus without thinking at all."

Level 1, conformist: "black and white thinking. You follow rules and obey authority without questioning. You adopt others' perspective": your parents' religion, their idea of a career. For parents formed in the industrial age that means "go to school, get a job, retire at 65."

Level 2, individualist: "critical thinking emerges and you construct your own model," often still based on your parents' perspective. You may choose your own major, an art degree your parents disagree with, but "you're still on the default path that society set for you."

Level 3, synthesist (a label Koe says is his own): "you see your model as one among many. You hold contradictions and use perspectives as tools rather than law." "Who knew that everyone is right in one way or another."

Level 4, generative: "you create original perspectives that didn't exist before, or you come to ideas without outside influence." **Source:** same.

Many people never reach level 3. They remain "Bible thumpers... across multiple domains" (level 1), or they rebel and consider their own way law (level 2). And the levels are not strictly sequential: you can be at level one "completely" and still "tap into level four occasionally." The goal is to maximize the levels in order to "increase our surface area of thinking." Koe insists on the empirical basis: "These aren't just things that I've made up... This has been documented." **Source:** same.

| Level | Name | How you think | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Instinctual | React to stimulus out of pure survival, without thinking | — |
| 1 | Conformist | Black and white; follow rules and authority; adopt others' perspective | First tier |
| 2 | Individualist | Critical thinking; construct your own model, still on the default path | First tier |
| 3 | Synthesist | Your model is one among many; hold contradictions; perspectives as tools, not law | Second tier |
| 4 | Generative | Create original perspectives, or arrive at ideas without outside influence | Second tier |

The tiers come from Spiral Dynamics, but Koe reassigns them. Levels 1 and 2 are "first-tier thinking": "overly dogmatic... I'm right, you're wrong, or my group is right, your group is wrong," and they fight a lot. Levels 3 and 4 are "second-tier thinking": "you start to reject dogma, and you start to actually seek truth for once, because your truth isn't the only truth. You understand that the ultimate truth lies somewhere in the middle, and usually you don't come to absolute truth. You come to trade-offs." He closes the point with Thomas Sowell: "solutions don't really exist, but trade-offs do." **Source:** same.

**Complementary context:** Thomas Sowell, an American economist, developed the contrast between "solutions" and "trade-offs" in *A Conflict of Visions* (1987), where he associates the belief in solutions with an "unconstrained" vision of human nature and the acceptance of trade-offs with a "constrained" one. Koe uses only the maxim.

Koe explains what changes from level to level with a criterion he applies to all of them: how far you keep thinking. "Genius-level thinking is your ability to continue thinking. Because at each level of thinking, your ability to navigate that space to both known and unknown ideas continues to expand. When you're born, you don't really think. When you're a conformist or level one, you think until you reach a point where you have the answer, then you start defending the answer. When you're an individualist or level two, you do the same thing, but with your answer. And when you reach synthesist, you can think far and wide, but your ability to create new lines of thought is not fully developed yet." **Source:** same. The criterion makes the levels operational. The difference between level 1 and level 2 is not how much you know, nor whether the answer is yours, but that in both cases thinking stops at an answer and turns into defense. Level 3 keeps thinking past the answer; level 4 can open lines that did not exist.

He also warns, in the same passage, that the level is not stable: "You can be at level four, but in a particularly stressful situation, you can regress back down into lower levels... to level one and be very conformist in one domain of your life when you're stressed and you're going through it, or you're in an argument with someone and you're really not thinking." **Source:** same. This warning, and its earlier versions, are gathered in Section 38.5.

Koe illustrates movement through the levels within a single line with his own field, marketing. "Within one line, I'm at level one. I study Alex Hormozi or my own videos, and I think 'Oh, this is the one true way to do things.'" Then, being "in the space longer," you advance to level two: "I have my own way of doing things because I've experimented so much... I'm not just copying what another person does, I'm doing things my own way, which gives me a competitive edge." Then, having pursued other interests (psychology, fitness, personal development), you develop "a deeper sense of pattern recognition": you understand the people you work with, how the world and culture work and how to apply marketing to them, and "it also further removes me from dogmatic thinking, which frees me up to think even more and become more creative." **Source:** same. The example shows something the bare table does not: the step from level 2 to level 3 in one line is enabled by width in other lines. That is what the third dimension captures.

**Altitude.** "Altitude of thinking... is the average of how you think about each line or level." The graphic condenses the lines, adds more domains and interconnects them at various levels. "It helps to visualize thinking as a skill tree": in a video game you put points into traits so you can do more, take on higher challenges, have more fun and keep playing. "Higher-level traits can only be unlocked once you meet a specific requirement of lower-level traits... you can't access something like level three dexterity when you haven't already put enough points into, let's say, level three strength." **Source:** same. "Altitude," in this framework, is Koe's term with his own definition: an average across domains. (**Complementary context:** Wilber also uses "altitude," for the overall level of consciousness, which may be where Koe found the word; Koe does not say.) The skill-tree image is the mechanism of the explanation: development in one domain is gated by development in others.

The consequence is a kind of plateau that cannot be diagnosed from inside the domain where it appears. Koe describes a business person who "takes it pretty far," makes a lot of money, and then hits a plateau: "I've studied everything, and I can't overcome this plateau." "You can't see the problem because you haven't developed other domains of your life. The problem here could be your inability to lead a team. Your emotional intelligence is so abysmally low that you don't even know that you're causing problems within the company that's causing it to stall." His conclusion is stated as belief: "In order to be a true leader, you have to develop yourself in all domains of life." Even those who reach the top without doing so see other domains suffer, which is "not something that I want." "You have one life. Maybe. I don't know. Maybe AI changes that, but I want to do cool things in this life." **Source:** same. The same idea becomes, in Human 3.0, the "cross-quadrant unlock patterns" (Section 38.4), and it underlies the ideal of being "multi-dimensionally jacked" developed in Chapter 40.

#### The fourth dimension: four perspectives of reality

Within the 2026 framework, the four quadrants return as the fourth dimension. "This isn't the fourth dimension of time. That's actually the fifth dimension." The quadrants "increase your ability to think by the power of four, because there are four dimensions of reality. And these dimensions are perspectives... you can kind of adjust the lens on your camera to see it in four different ways." **Source:** How To Think Like A Strategic Genius (5-Dimensional Thinking).md.

The description shifts slightly from the 2025 version. The inner world is the mental (psychological and cultural); the outer world is the physical. The individual inner is thoughts, emotions, beliefs, consciousness. The collective inner, culture, is group beliefs, value systems, ideologies. The individual outer is visible appearance, behaviors, physical brain states "or other measurements." The collective outer is systems, structures, social institutions. **Source:** same.

What Koe adds is a diagnosis of expertise. Most people and most experts look through only one lens: "The neuroscientist only sees the brain states, while the mystic only sees the vibes, or the therapist who only sees the behavior, or the sociologist who only sees the social systems, or the philosopher who only sees the meaning, which is the culture." Genius thinking is enhanced by the ability to see all of them. **Source:** same. Each specialist is not wrong; each is confined to a quadrant. This is the same structure as the macro stages, now applied to disciplines: the limitation is not the perspective but the inability to hold more than one.

He then demonstrates on a problem that many viewers bring to him: "the world is becoming corrupt and meaningless." From the collective inner: "How does society control attention and market their ideas to the masses?" From the individual outer: "How can your behavior change... to have a positive impact on society? What would be the steps to reaching such a point of influence with your marketing or other things that you can leave your mark?" From the collective outer: "What does the job market look like right now? Is a job an actual path to achieving such a thing? What about AI? Can I utilize the technology available to me to create that impact?" From the individual inner: "Am I thinking about this the right way or is there more? What am I missing and how does this make me feel? Does it make me feel motivated or inspired or hateful?" "It's all kind of merging together here." **Source:** same. The demonstration shows the main effect of the method: a complaint ("the world is corrupt") becomes a set of questions, each of which points toward an action or an inquiry.

#### Your own center of gravity

The framework invites a temptation Koe names and redirects. In debates and on social media you can diagnose the other person's kind of thinking: if someone is "super polarizing or dogmatic," you can assume they are "thinking stupidly and they're just trying to defend their own beliefs"; "if they sound like a TV or social media itself or the news, you can assume that they're just regurgitating this pre-programmed line of thought. They're not thinking at all." But "the most useful thing here is to just identify where you are at, where your own center of gravity is so that you can identify your own blind spots and see past them. You don't need to compete with someone else on how well you can think. You simply need to think better." **Source:** How To Think Like A Strategic Genius (5-Dimensional Thinking).md.

"Center of gravity" is Koe's term for the level from which you habitually think. Since levels vary by line and fluctuate with stress, the center of gravity is where you tend to return. The heuristic is the ethical counterweight of the whole chapter: stage models are easy to turn into weapons for ranking others, and Koe's own instruction is to turn them on yourself.

### 38.3 The Stages of the Ego

The most detailed map Koe ever presents is not his own. It is Susanne Cook-Greuter's theory of ego development, which he expounds at length in a November 2024 video ("How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People"), summarizes in March 2025, and retells in a compact "80/20" version in December 2025. This section follows the stages in order, keeping the three tellings distinct where they differ, and then addresses two questions the stages raise: how long development takes, and whether the stages are really levels at all.

#### The source and how Koe filters it

In November 2024 Koe says he will "dissect" ego development theory in relation to intelligence. He links a PDF of Cook-Greuter's dissertation, "90+ pages," "very insightful... definitely worth reading." The graphics he shows come from a site whose name the transcript garbles (rendered as "slow.com" or "slow SL o ww.co"); the stage graphics cannot be reconstructed beyond what he narrates. He also recommends studying Spiral Dynamics and Wilber's AQAL alongside it: "when you connect the dots between all three it's pretty incredible how your mind starts to think." **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md.

He is explicit that his version is filtered. He leaves nuances out "because cybernetics: we are writing towards a specific goal and how we get there is going to be determined by the goal," whereas the goal of Cook-Greuter's paper is to explain every aspect accurately. **Source:** same. This is the cybernetic principle of Section 6.4 applied to his own teaching: the goal of a video (here, helping viewers develop their thinking) determines which parts of a source make it in. The reader should take Koe's account as a goal-directed adaptation, not as a summary of the theory.

**Complementary context:** Susanne Cook-Greuter is a developmental psychologist whose 1999 Harvard dissertation, *Postautonomous Ego Development*, extended Jane Loevinger's model of ego development into the later stages, which she named "construct-aware" and "unitive." Her stage names vary between publications (for example "self-protective" and "opportunist" for the same early stage, "individualist" and "pluralist" for the same postconventional stage), which explains part of the variation in Koe's labels.

In March 2025 Koe presents the same material as "a concept by Susan Cook Greuter, the nine levels of ego development... one of her research papers," and states the core claim in one sentence: "at each level of development your mind interprets reality according to the goals, beliefs, etc. of that stage." **Source:** How To Take Back Control Of Your Life With Strategic Thinking.md. A stage, in other words, is a goal structure, and a goal structure is a lens. That sentence ties the whole of Cook-Greuter's model to the identity model of Part II, where identity is defined as a network of goals that filters perception (Sections 3.2 and 3.3).

#### Three groups and their proportions

Koe groups the stages into three categories, plus a final one.

The preconventional stages (about 5% of the population): symbiotic, impulsive and opportunist. In the 2025 summary he skips them, because most people grow out of them by age 10 to 12. At these stages you depend on others to fulfill basic needs, there is no distinction between self and other, and you think others are there to fulfill your needs. **Source:** How To Take Back Control Of Your Life With Strategic Thinking.md.

The conventional stages (about 75 to 80%): conformist, expert, achiever.

The postconventional stages (about 15 to 20%): pluralist, strategist, construct-aware. And, as "a final bonus," the unitive stage (about 1%). **Source:** same; How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md.

Two notes on this scheme. First, the count does not add up: Koe speaks of nine stages but names ten labels (three, three, three, plus unitive as a "bonus"). How he counts nine is ambiguous in that telling. The December 2025 list (below) does contain nine names, but different ones. Second, the percentages are approximate by Koe's own description, and they sit alongside other figures he gives elsewhere. In a December 2025 video he says that "in the spiral dynamics and nine stages of ego development models, they show that around 50% of the population is at the conformist stage of development, meaning half the population lacks the cognitive development for genuine agency." **Source:** The Most Important Skill To Learn Right Now.md. The models' authors and the underlying studies are not named, and the 50% figure for a single stage does not obviously agree with "75 to 80%" for the conventional group as a whole (it can, if the conformist stage is the largest of the three conventional stages, but Koe does not say so). The figures across Koe's work (90%, 95%, 99%, 80 to 90%) measure different things and are best read as estimates that convey proportions, not as data.

The 50% claim is used to support an argument developed in Chapter 35: agency is rare because it requires a level of development that many people have not reached. Here it is enough to note the shape of the inference. If the conformist stage defines identity by group membership and obedience, then "genuine agency," acting on self-generated goals, is structurally difficult from inside it.

#### Lower stages cannot see higher ones

The November 2024 video opens with a claim that frames everything else: people who score well on IQ tests usually land in the conventional stages of psychological development. And "lower stages can't understand higher stages." Koe's image is a mountain: think of a higher stage as a point of view; from the top you can see everything below, but from the bottom you cannot see the view from the top. **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md.

He immediately adds the qualification that he will repeat throughout: "lower and higher" do not mean "bad or worse"; "it's a distinction." But the consequences he draws are harsh. High-IQ people at a lower stage are still in "the bottom 80%" of "quote unquote intelligence": like most of the population they are "broke, unhealthy and angry at the world," and especially lonely "because very few people can match their intellectual capabilities." "They can think through complex problems, but they can't solve the bigger, more holistic problem of creating a rich life." **Source:** same.

The argument separates two capacities that are usually merged under the word "intelligence": the capacity to solve a complex problem that has been given, and the capacity to see and solve the larger problem of how to live. The first is what IQ tests measure; the second depends on the stage, on how many perspectives the mind can hold. This is why, in Koe's account, "smart people are incredibly dumb" (Section 38.2): their processing power is high, but it runs inside a single perspective.

The word "intelligence" did not survive long in this role. A month later, in conversation with Devon Eriksen, Koe said "maybe intelligence wasn't the right word for me to use, but perspective," and Eriksen proposed "effectiveness" instead (Section 3.3). The map of stages stays the same; what changes is the name of the capacity that expands as you move through it. The title of the 2024 video and the phrase "intelligence can only expand to the level of your mind" (below) keep the older word.

#### The preconventional stages

The preconventional stages "usually occur in early childhood": symbiotic, impulsive and opportunist. They are "characterized by egocentrism... they can't adopt another person's perspective"; the child has not yet been socialized, or is toward the end of that process. A newborn depends on parents who are, from its point of view, "just giving machines... survival machines": "me, me, me." In kindergarten you realize "oh, I'm not the only person on this Earth, I'm not the center of the universe," and by age 10 you are usually out of these stages. **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md.

#### The conventional stages: monkeys copying monkeys

The conventional stages comprise 75 to 80% of the population, "and this is where most high IQ people test." Koe's image for them is his own: "We can think of the conventional stages as monkeys copying other monkeys in the attempt for status and becoming the top of the food chain." He adds an observation from his own experience: "when you talk to someone who is in a conventional stage... you're usually talking to a TV or a YouTube channel or a social media feed. You aren't talking to the actual person; you're talking to their regurgitated conditioned beliefs from their environment." **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md.

In 2025 he turns this into a heuristic for recognizing the conventional stages: "When you talk to them it's like they're kind of regurgitating religious beliefs, or it's like you're talking to a TV or a mainstream news channel, or you're like 'haven't I heard all of this before in one of those popular self-help books.'" The main characteristic: "they haven't developed the ability to expand beyond a popular worldview and think for themselves." And the consequence for anyone trying to achieve something: you fail because you expect to do the exact same thing as the monkey who achieved your goal "without critical or deep thinking or solving complex problems, and it just backfires, because that's not how the mind works." **Source:** How To Take Back Control Of Your Life With Strategic Thinking.md.

The last point connects the conventional stages to the treatment of imitation in Section 1.4. Imitation is inevitable; what distinguishes the conventional stages is imitation without understanding. Copying the visible behavior of someone who succeeded, without the thinking that produced it, fails because the behavior was a solution to that person's problems, from that person's level, and does not transfer.

**The conformist.** "In the conformist stage their identity is defined by their relationship to a group." Koe's examples: high school students focused on popularity; corporate employees who stick to the rules, like attending all mandatory meetings; military or religious adherence. "Conformists obey the authority and conform to the group." The stage is easily observed in "Bible thumpers": the ideology of their religion "is law and that's all that comes out of their mouth"; they have no logical argument for or against it; "that's their mind." **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md. In 2025 he generalizes: the stage is not limited to high school students or Bible thumpers; it applies to anyone who "just conform[s] to the group and can't think for themselves," and it shows in decisions based on what the group does. **Source:** How To Take Back Control Of Your Life With Strategic Thinking.md.

**The expert.** The expert stage "is characterized by self-authorship, personal growth, tackling complex problems, self-reflection and complex problem solving." It includes professionals, engineering types, skill-centric people and "scientific materialists who think everything can be explained through modern science or reductionism." The defining weakness: "They are good at doing tasks but bad at knowing if they are doing the right task. They can't see if what they're doing is important for mankind." "The experts are the know-it-alls... they're still in a singular perspective and they think that that perspective is absolute truth." **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md. In 2025 Koe adds a second weakness and a cause. Experts are bad at identifying whether they are working on the right task, and also bad at shifting focus once they realize they are working on the wrong one; the cause, in his view, is that they are "still chained to a specific ideology or belief system from their past that they were conditioned into." **Source:** How To Take Back Control Of Your Life With Strategic Thinking.md.

**The achiever.** "Most of Western education and schooling are geared toward creating achievers. This is the pinnacle stage of where people can end up in their mind; this is the definition of Western success": a lot of money, a beautiful partner, a nice car, a nice house. "It's the American dream embodied as a perspective." At this stage there is "a shift from employee mindset to entrepreneur mindset, a shift from 'I have this individual skill' to 'how can I use this individual skill to utilize it in society.'" In Koe's own field, it is "the dominant paradigm of social media": business, self-help, masculinity and relationship accounts, the "health, wealth, relationships, happiness accounts," such as Hamza or Hormozi, achievers "that have the occasional glimpse into postconventional stages." **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md.

The 2025 summary adds an estimate and a nuance. Koe estimates that about 80% of the educational content on YouTube and social media is focused on this stage. Even if teachers like Alex Hormozi are not solely at this level, people who consume "how to make money" content "out of stress and survival to attain some high level of status rather than some deeper meaning" operate here. And "there's nothing wrong with this... everyone goes through these stages, everyone pursues status at some point, everyone pursues vanity." The value lies in recognizing "I'm here and there is something more." He sums up the attitude with a gym saying: "you go to the gym for vanity and stay for the therapy." **Source:** How To Take Back Control Of Your Life With Strategic Thinking.md. The saying condenses the logic of transcend and include in an everyday form: the shallow motive (vanity, status) is not to be despised; it is the door through which a deeper one (health, self-respect) enters. Koe uses the same logic, as "behind every shallow pursuit there is a deep reason," for social media (Section 38.4).

The numbering of these stages is inconsistent in the corpus. In 2024 the conformist is "stage 3," the expert "stage 3/4" and the achiever "stage 4"; Koe notes that "the numbering is a bit off." In 2025 the achiever is "the third conventional stage." In the December 2025 list, the names change again (below). The structure of the conventional group (group, then skill, then achievement) is stable across tellings; the numbers are not.

#### From tier one to tier two

The step out of the conventional stages is, for Koe, the decisive one. In the postconventional stages "we start to encounter systems thinking: that wholes are greater than the sum of their parts, that reality is more like an organism than a machine that can be dissected and studied." **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md.

"The big step between tier one conventional and tier two postconventional thinking is... it's multi-perspectival. It's not just about your business model, self-help ideology or religion; you begin to notice the properties that compose them all, like how Christianity and Buddhism point to the same identity of reality, God, even though they would disagree on that, because lower stages don't understand higher stages, but higher stages understand lower." He adds a caveat about the second example: "non-ideological Buddhists are a different story, but most hippies blindly adopt those beliefs without understanding." And he draws a consequence for motivation: "The higher you are on a mountain, the more you can see the multiple paths you can take to get there, so you have the desire to help more people." **Source:** same.

The image of the mountain now has two uses. Seen from the bottom, it explains why lower stages cannot understand higher ones. Seen from the top, it explains why higher stages are drawn to teaching: from above, the different paths up are visible, and one can point others to the path that suits them. The claim that Christianity and Buddhism "point to the same identity of reality" is Koe's interpretation, offered as an example of multi-perspectival seeing; he presents it as something a tier-two thinker notices, not as a thesis he argues.

#### The postconventional stages: success itself evolves

The postconventional stages are "the top 15 to 20% of the population," with a caveat Koe repeats in a casual register: these are stages people go through; being born preconventional does not make anyone worse; "if that doesn't tickle your peach, then don't consider it higher, lower." **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md.

What characterizes them is that "each stage has different goals and worldviews, meaning their idea of success evolves, so the system their mind runs on evolves with it." The mind is a goal-seeking system (Part III); a new stage installs new goals, and with them a new definition of success and a new system. Koe then connects development to entropy: "If we would consider an efficient system to be ordered, we can understand why chaos, discomfort and pain are a part of development." You order your mind, realize something new, must change perspective or embody more, are exposed to chaos and the unknown, and need to create order out of it. **Source:** same. This is the same pattern as the dissipative structure of Section 38.1, stated a year earlier and without Prigogine: development passes through disorder because each new stage is a reordering.

In 2025 Koe states the practical stakes: reaching the pluralist, strategist or construct-aware stage is what lets you view situations from multiple perspectives "to pull the best information in and make the right decisions." **Source:** How To Take Back Control Of Your Life With Strategic Thinking.md.

**The pluralist.** In the pluralist stage "a fourth-person perspective is discovered"; pluralists "begin to step outside of the system they grew up in, the status quo, and question their beliefs and values." Koe's most prevalent example is "a hippie who has a vision of everyone becoming a hippie, but they can't yet see the development others have gone through, so that vision falls flat." It is the evolution "from Bible thumper to atheist to a hippie who first discovers spirituality, but they still haven't looped back or integrated the truth from the Bible thumper." **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md. The 2025 summary gives two other examples: a religious person who becomes an atheist or a hippie, and a corporate executive who realizes that grinding on work he does not enjoy for status is not worthwhile. **Source:** How To Take Back Control Of Your Life With Strategic Thinking.md.

The pluralist's failure is a failure of transcend and include. The pluralist has transcended the conformist's certainty but has not included its truth; the vision "falls flat" because it skips the development that others would have to go through to share it.

**Complementary context:** "fourth-person perspective" is a term from Cook-Greuter's work: the first person is "I," the second "you," the third the objective observer of "it," and the fourth the capacity to step outside one's own culture and see it as one system among others.

**The strategist.** Koe says he resonates with this stage and guesses he has spent the past few years in it ("you never actually know"). His account has several components.

Intuition over logic: "Strategists realize that intuition is more powerful than logic and rationality found in conventional stages"; to think something has been formally proven "is silly and close to impossible," and the reductionist worldview does not work.

Systems with emergent properties: "the whole has emergent properties that can't be found in the parts." His example is dissecting a dead pig in biology class without taking into account its ecological or zoological context; to reach the highest truth, everything in the universe would have to be taken into account.

The observer in the equation: "there is the observer as a part of that hypothesis": "my mind at a specific stage of development with specific goals is going to interpret the findings different from the next person; that is extremely important."

Exploration over achievement: "Exploration and discovery start to become more important than achieving a goal or making a lot of money, which can often lead to achieving more goals and making more money, counterintuitively."

Vision and principles: the strategist "has a vision of what the world needs to move forward in the evolutionary process and understands the generalized principles of reality."

And a summary of orientation: "The strategist is a value creator. Their main concern is self-development, other development, self-actualization, integration and owning their shadow. Truth can be approximated, but higher is better: a higher perspective is more truthful than not." **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md.

Several of these components connect to ideas developed elsewhere in the book. "Value creator" is one of Koe's central terms (Chapter 31 and Section 38.4); here it names the orientation of a whole developmental stage. "Exploration over achievement," with its counterintuitive payoff, is the logic of the life as a game and of purpose as exploration (Chapters 9 and 10). The observer in the hypothesis restates the idea that reality is "constructed by the observer" (Section 38.2). The 2025 summary condenses the stage to two traits: the realization that "intuition is more powerful than logic and rationality," and the priority of exploration, discovery and self-actualization over goals and money, with a vision of what humanity could be. **Source:** How To Take Back Control Of Your Life With Strategic Thinking.md.

The sentence "a higher perspective is more truthful than not" deserves attention, because it is the strongest statement of hierarchy in the corpus and Koe later softens it (see "Stages or tools?" below).

**A case: where Koe places Jordan Peterson.** In the same video Koe offers an opinion about a public figure, presented as his own reading. In his eyes, Peterson "falls mostly between the construct-aware and the strategist stage," but his "doubling down on Christianity and what seems to be a regression" means he may not be there anymore, or he may have "this conventional stage shadow." Unless, Koe adds, Peterson uses it "as an act to introduce and help more people develop": attracting conventional-stage people with the Christian worldview so that they come to see it through a more metaphorical, higher lens. Koe ends with an open question: whether people can develop without first turning atheist or agnostic, as Peterson did, turning instead from hardcore Christianity to "not so hardcore Christianity" and then looping back to a more holistic view. "I don't know." **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md.

The case is useful because it shows the difficulty of reading stages from outside. The same behavior (a public return to Christianity) can be read as regression, as a residual "conventional stage shadow" (Koe's term for the conventional-stage traits a more developed person still carries), or as a strategy for reaching a conventional audience. Koe does not decide among them, and his own heuristic (Section 38.2) suggests why: the most useful thing is to locate one's own center of gravity, not other people's.

**The construct-aware.** Koe explains this stage with a metaphor of his own. "If the mind were a computer, construct-aware people would understand that conventional stages play one app or video game on that computer; they're on one site or browser. Postconventional stages can start to navigate the full desktop; they can play the most meaningful games and even create their own." The construct-aware "gain this programmer mindset; you understand the inner workings of the computer itself." If reality is the display, conventional people operate "within one browser tab or one app that they refuse to leave." **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md.

The construct-aware "tap into a fifth dimension of thinking, the cognitive dimension: they can see how the mind constructs reality and meaning." They see that most people find solace in a perspective but cannot zoom out to a larger whole, that "they can't adopt a meta perspective that houses other perspectives." At this stage "the generalized principles of reality really start to click": unity and division, creation and destruction, entropy and (as the transcript has it) wealth. **Source:** same. The 2025 summary repeats the key traits: the cognitive dimension, the capacity to "see how the mind constructs meaning," to integrate more perspectives and to think from a more abstract layer. **Source:** How To Take Back Control Of Your Life With Strategic Thinking.md. (As noted in Section 38.1, "fifth dimension" means something else in Koe's 2026 framework.)

| Stage group | Computer metaphor |
|---|---|
| Conventional | One app, one game, one browser tab they refuse to leave |
| Postconventional | The full desktop: choose the most meaningful games and create their own |
| Construct-aware | The programmer: understand the inner workings of the computer itself |

The metaphor anticipates the video-game roles of Human 3.0 (NPC, main character, programmer, Section 38.4), which reuse the same three-step structure.

The stage has a trap, and Koe speaks of it from experience. The construct-aware think from "a new abstract layer that can bring them pain, as they are constantly battling with the question of 'so what? What does this supposed mental masturbation do to help me?'" "They often get so lost in abstractions or that meta perspective that they can't ground it in reality or connect it to their decision-making right now." Koe says he has experienced this "quite a bit": "intellectualizing all day" without understanding the point, or thinking "that's just philosophy, it doesn't mean anything." **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md. The warning matters for the reader of this chapter, which is itself abstract. The test Koe implies is whether the map changes a decision. A map that only produces more maps has fallen into the trap.

#### The unitive stage

Koe calls the stage beyond the construct-aware "transcendent," and names it "the unitive stage." These stages are "the top 1% of psychological development and intelligence... because intelligence can only expand to the level of your mind." It is as far as his video goes, and "I think it's the furthest studied," though Ken Wilber has hypothesized further stages. He adds an observation about collective development: as population stages rise over time (in tribal times everyone was at lower stages; "most people are in the conformist stage now"), more people reach higher stages. He places Alan Watts and Terence McKenna here: "they tap into Source, it's just infinite intelligence, you can just tell that by the ways that they speak." He muses that a tier list of philosophers by stage would be fun to make, and states plainly that he is not there yet. **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md.

"Source" is Koe's word here, not Cook-Greuter's; he uses it for the "infinite intelligence" he hears in Watts and McKenna. The placement of particular people at particular stages is his opinion, based, as he says, on how they speak.

He then reads a passage from Cook-Greuter's description of the stage: "reality is now regularly experienced as the undifferentiated phenomenological continuum, or the creative ground of unified consciousness. Every object, word, thought, feeling and sensation, every theory, is understood as a human construct, separating out, creating boundaries where there are none. The quest for meaning and connection is an essential aspect of the human condition; giving names to experience and making distinctions is necessary for human growth, study, interaction and communication, but at the source there is nothing to distinguish." **Source:** same.

The quotation closes a loop opened in Section 38.1. There, holons were "distinctions" humans create to make sense of the world, "context dependent or goal dependent," and "units of mind" were units cut by a mind that labels. At the unitive stage, the person experiences directly what the theory of holons said in the abstract: the boundaries are constructions, useful and necessary for living and communicating, but not present "at the source."

Koe adds his own comments: to reach this stage you must go through all the others; it takes many years and much pain; you need the intention to move through stages "rather than getting trapped in one specifically," and it helps to know each stage's traits so you can notice them and work toward them. **Source:** same.

He then lists traits of the unitive stage, adapted from Cook-Greuter, with his own commentary woven in:

1. Truth is immanent in the universe but cannot be grasped by logic or rationality.
2. They adopt a universal or cosmic perspective as the organizing principle and steady place from which to derive meaning.
3. Most definitions of enlightenment are achieved.
4. They metabolize experience in a radically open way.
5. "They understand the need for a personal ego to survive, while also recognizing the illusion of the desire for permanence. The ego is useful as a perspective or lens when needed."
6. Dualities and conflict are witnessed without tension or desire to change them; they live in a creative ground and lean into humanity "fulfilling their destiny of evolution."
7. Life is seen as a "temporary and sometimes voluntary separation" from the creative ground.
8. They are not passive: "the non-personal stance allows for more conscious, powerful and direct action when it is needed." **Source:** same.

Koe's commentary on the fifth and seventh traits is where his own view comes through. He contrasts the unitive person with the early-postconventional hippie who decides to "go off and live in the woods, become a monk... absolve myself of purpose." Unitive people, having transcended and included every earlier stage, "know that they can adopt the ego as a lens to act and help and contribute to mankind." The seventh trait he reads through Alan Watts: coming down from the higher perspective to adopt an ego in order to act is "the middle way, the path of the bodhisattva," which Watts talked about. And the eighth corrects a conventional misreading: the hippie assumes enlightenment means passivity, but the non-personal stance allows for more powerful action, not less. **Source:** same.

**Complementary context:** in Mahayana Buddhism, a bodhisattva is one who, having approached enlightenment, chooses to remain in the world of ordinary existence in order to help others reach liberation. The "middle way" is the Buddha's path between sensual indulgence and extreme asceticism. Watts popularized both ideas in the West. Koe's use, the ego adopted as a lens for action, is a modern reading of the bodhisattva ideal, consistent with how Watts presented it.

The unitive stage gives Koe the resolution of a tension that runs through his work: the tension between the spiritual teachings that dissolve the ego (Section 2.7, The Power of Now) and the practical system of goals, businesses and ambition that fills most of this book. The resolution is not to choose one. The ego is a lens; at the highest stage it is picked up when there is something to do and set down when there is not. This reading returns in Chapter 40, where spirituality is defined as a lens rather than an identity.

#### The nine stages in the 2025 version

In December 2025 Koe retells the stages in what he calls an 80/20 version, with a different set of names and an example for each. The stage names and their structure come from ego development theory; the examples are Koe's. "Repetition is important for important things," he says, acknowledging that he has covered the stages before. **Source:** How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md.

| # | Stage | Core shift | Koe's example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Impulsive | No separation between impulse and action; black-and-white thinking | A toddler hits when angry |
| 2 | Self-protective | The world is dangerous; you look out for yourself | A kid hides report cards, lies about chores, figures out what adults want to hear |
| 3 | Conformist | You are your group; its rules feel like reality | Cannot fathom voting differently or having a different religion than your group |
| 4 | Self-aware | You notice an inner life that does not match the exterior | Sitting in church unsure you believe, not knowing what to do with it ("that is me in my past") |
| 5 | Conscientious | You build your own principle system and hold yourself accountable | Leaving your family's religion after careful study and adopting a defensible philosophy; a career plan with milestones, believing right efforts yield right results |
| 6 | Individualist | You see that your principles were shaped by context and hold them loosely | Political views tied to where you grew up; career goals really about your father's approval |
| 7 | Strategist | You work with systems while aware of your own involvement | Leading while questioning your blind spots; doing politics knowing your perspective is partial |
| 8 | Construct-aware | All frameworks, including identity, are useful fictions | Spiritual beliefs held metaphorically; "the map is not the territory"; watching yourself play founder or thought leader with gentle amusement |
| 9 | Unitive | The separation between self and life dissolves | Work, rest and play feel the same; "no one left who needs to become something. Just presence responding to what arises." |

Comparing this list with the 2024 telling shows how unstable the labels are. "Self-protective" replaces "opportunist." "Self-aware" and "conscientious" appear where the 2024 version had "expert" and "achiever." "Individualist" appears where the 2024 version had "pluralist." The strategist is "stage 5" in 2024 and seventh in this list. And "individualist" is also the name Koe gives, in other frameworks, to a level that corresponds to the achiever (Human 2.0, and level 2 of thinking): the same word names different stages in different maps. The underlying sequence (impulse, self-protection, group, inner doubt, self-made principles, awareness of context, systems, constructs, unity) is coherent; the reader should simply not expect the names and numbers to match across videos.

The 2025 list also gives the clearest statement of a point the earlier tellings only imply. Stage 4, self-aware, is the beginning of the exit from conformity, and Koe places himself there in his past: sitting in church, unsure whether he believed. The examples for stages 5 and 6 then describe, in order, the construction of a personal philosophy (leaving a religion after study) and the realization that this philosophy too was shaped by circumstance (views tied to where you grew up, goals tied to a father's approval). The sequence is the "belief, rejection, integration" pattern of Chapter 17, laid out as stages.

#### Development is a spiral, and it takes years

Two features of the stage models qualify any reading of them as a ladder.

The first is shape. The horizontal and vertical graphic Koe shows in 2024 reflects the colors of Spiral Dynamics ("red, amber, orange, green, teal"), and he draws an image from it: "think of a spiral, right, rather than circles." You "sometimes loop back around to the same problems or the same... patterns in that cycle of development," like déjà vu, so it feels as if you are not making progress, "but you're an entire stage above where you were." **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md. The spiral explains an experience that otherwise discourages people: the return of an old problem. It is the same problem, but met from a stage above, with more perspectives available. The image will return in Chapter 39 as the cyclical structure of the chapters of life.

**Complementary context:** the color sequence Koe names (red, amber, orange, green, teal) is the color code Wilber uses for his own stages in *Integral Spirituality* and that Frederic Laloux popularized in *Reinventing Organizations*. It overlaps with, but is not identical to, the colors of Spiral Dynamics proper (where the corresponding sequence would be red, blue, orange, green, yellow, turquoise). Koe attributes the colors to Spiral Dynamics.

The second is time. If you have failed to achieve anything worthwhile, Koe says in 2025, it may be because you are trapped in one of the lower conventional stages, conforming to a group without knowing it, and the mind makes it very difficult to release that grasp (he recalls the lobster that must shed its shell to grow, from Section 4.3). "The really unfortunate thing here is that this isn't a get-rich-quick type thing; it takes years to develop through these stages, so you may not be able to achieve your goals this year or in two years." But "even if it takes years, it can be accelerated." **Source:** How To Take Back Control Of Your Life With Strategic Thinking.md. The accelerators he proposes (among them a daily meditation practice he attributes to Wilber) are treated with vertical development in Section 17.5. The point here is the warning: stage development is slow, and the mind resists it, because a stage is an identity and identities defend themselves (Section 3.4).

#### Stages or tools? The objection and Koe's shift

The stage models carry an implicit ranking. Koe says repeatedly that "lower and higher" do not mean "worse and better," but he also says that "a higher perspective is more truthful than not," that lower stages cannot understand higher ones, and that "intelligence can only expand to the level of your mind." In 2024 both messages coexist in the same video.

The clearest challenge to the ranking came from a guest. In December 2024, reacting to Koe's video on ego development, the novelist Devon Eriksen (author of *Theft of Fire*) said: "I don't see these as levels you move through, I see them as tools you add to your toolbox." His example is vivid. Even someone at "the highest level of development," a "philosopher king," if you stick his head in a horse trough and hold him down, will act at "ego development level one: I want air, I'm going to fight you." And "that is not a regression or a failure, it's just he's pulling his earliest tool out of the toolbox because that's what's appropriate to the situation." **Source:** The Most Important Skill To Learn In The Next 10 Years With Devon Eriksen.md. The objection fits Eriksen's broader view of human development: having a toolbox, expanding it, and expressing whatever capacity helps achieve what one wants.

Eriksen's view and Koe's 2024 view differ on two points. On the structure of development: Eriksen sees accumulation (tools added), Koe saw ascent (levels passed through). On what happens under threat: Eriksen sees appropriate use of an early tool, Koe would call it regression.

Koe never explicitly accepts or rejects the objection, but his later formulations move toward it. In October 2025: "Lower or higher levels aren't bad or good. They are points of development along someone's journey," and "a level one perspective becomes a tool in your toolbox when you transcend to level two." In December 2025: the stages are "tools in the toolbox... lenses of perception... not necessarily better than one another, but... larger, higher, more inclusive... more complex," and "sometimes the unitive stage doesn't let you solve a certain problem." **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md; How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md. Even the horse trough returns, transformed: in March 2025 Koe describes a "master mystic" with his head held in a bucket of water flailing "like an old caveman," and in October 2025 a person with a knife at his throat turning "into an animal for a second" (Section 38.5).

The shift is one of emphasis, not a reversal. Koe keeps the language of "higher" and "lower," keeps the claim that higher stages are "larger" and "more inclusive," and keeps the use of the stages as a developmental path. What changes is what "higher" means: no longer "more truthful" but "more inclusive," which does not imply "better for every problem." The two readings can apply to different questions. As a description of capacity, the higher stage holds more perspectives. As a description of fitness for a task, the right stage is the one that solves the problem in front of you, and sometimes that is the oldest tool in the box. Koe does not give a reason for the change; the chronology suggests that Eriksen's objection played a part, but Koe does not say so.

### 38.4 Human 3.0

By late 2025 Koe stops presenting other people's maps and presents his own. He calls it Human 3.0. It is announced in September 2025 as a model "recently merged together" from several theories, published free as a newsletter, and developed in full in an October 2025 video ("A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months)"). This section reconstructs the model from that video: why Koe built it, its structure, its quadrants and levels, the dynamics by which a person moves through it (traits, channels, glitches) and the failure mode he calls false transformation. It ends with the one application of the model he develops in detail, the three levels of social media content.

#### Why a new model: the flaws of the existing ones

Koe's justification starts from a diagnosis of the field. There are many models and gurus: Spiral Dynamics for psychology; Buddhism and Christianity for meaning; materialism and mentalism for the nature of reality; e-commerce and consulting for business; red pill and feminism for social dynamics. "All of them have their truths. They're just a perspective." But they share flaws. **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

The first flaw is isolation: most models are confined to one domain, like school teaching math, English and science "as singular siloed classes when knowledge is a web," or like dissecting frogs without considering the ecosystem. The second is what Koe calls a "hard pill to swallow": many spiritual teachers have frail bodies, many businessmen cannot maintain relationships, many alpha males are emotionally unaligned. Being developed in one domain is great, but "it is growing increasingly rare that a single person is truly self-developed." The third is age: even the multi-domain models, like the ancient Greek philosophies, were created before the internet, AI and technology changed everything. The fourth: "very few of them talk about money," which Koe finds surprising, since money dominates most people's minds and lives. **Source:** same.

The four flaws are the design requirements of the new model, stated negatively. Human 3.0 must cover several domains at once, must make the imbalance between domains visible, must speak to a world shaped by the internet and AI, and must include money. The second flaw also echoes the business plateau of Section 38.2: development in one domain, without the others, eventually stalls.

#### What Human 3.0 is

In the September 2025 announcement, Koe says he "recently merged together multiple theories" (the AQAL model, Spiral Dynamics, ego development, the flow psychology of Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi, performance science and the metacrisis) into one model called Human 3.0, published free as a newsletter, to be articulated in future videos. It is for those who want to develop themselves, "become multi-dimensionally jacked, not be a specialist, be able to do it all and live a meaningful life." He says he has spent "over 10, 15 years studying all of this." **Source:** The Death Of Social Media (& The Future Of Content Creation).md. That video does not explain the model's contents. The "metacrisis" is a concept Koe takes from Daniel Schmachtenberger (Section 2.9), whom he does not name in this passage; Wilber's name is also absent, though AQAL is his.

In October 2025 he gives the model's origin and purpose at length. Over 15 years he researched psychology, personal development, philosophy, social dynamics, technology, the internet, startups, money, religion and meaning; after writing online for five years he noticed "critical overlapping patterns that have begun to form a new philosophy for today's world." "That's where Human 3.0 comes in." The goal: "I want to synthesize everything I've learned into one comprehensive map to navigate the modern landscape," and to provide the knowledge, skills and principles to "escape mediocrity and actualize your highest potential." Its subtitle: "a map to reach the 1%, to reach your potential." **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

Koe is careful about what kind of thing the model is. It offers the big picture: "not a dogmatic set of actionable steps, but a set of guidelines for reaching your maximum potential," which lets you bust through mental, physical, spiritual and vocational plateaus. It "orients patterns found across human development" and builds on the scientific, psychological, spiritual and vocational models that "did the heavy lifting": "take the best parts of the world's greatest theories and apply them to the life of the individual." He provides a Human 3.0 knowledge base and a "metatype prompt" for use with AI, linked in the video description. **Source:** same.

The status Koe claims for his model matches the status he gives the stage models two months later: "orienting generalizations" (Section 38.2), not a precise map. The model is a synthesis; its originality, by his own account, lies in the combination and the application to the individual life, not in its components.

#### The structure: quadrants, levels, phases, traits

Koe shows the model as a graph whose layout is only partly reconstructible from the transcript. Its components can be reconstructed with confidence, because he names them and states how they nest: "so it goes traits to phases, phases to levels, levels across all quadrants." **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

| Component | What it is | How many |
|---|---|---|
| Quadrants | The four domains of life: mind, body, spirit, vocation | Four |
| Levels | Stages of development within each quadrant: Human 1.0, 2.0, 3.0 (colored red, blue, green on the graph; low, mid, high consciousness) | Three per quadrant |
| Phases | The steps by which you move from one level to the next: dissonance, uncertainty, discovery | Three per level |
| Traits | What must be acquired to advance through the phases: knowledge, experience, skill | Three |
| Channels | Ways to accelerate through a level (drawn as curly brackets on the graph) | — |
| Glitches | Tactics or technologies that force a channel or bust a plateau | — |
| Archetypes | Your level pattern within a quadrant | One per quadrant |
| Metatype | The synthesis of your four archetypes: your overall development | One |

The use of the model follows from its structure. You map your level in each quadrant (your archetype), then your overall development (your metatype), and then you aim toward "a 3.0-plus level metatype, one who has maximized all areas of life." The map "prevents partial thinking and doesn't reduce problems to one perspective." **Source:** same. The transcript names archetypes and metatypes but does not list the specific archetypes; they are not reconstructed here.

The phases (dissonance, uncertainty, discovery) are Koe's three phases of profound change, developed as the mechanics of identity change in Section 4.3: dissonance is "this isn't the life I want to live, but I don't know what life I do want to live"; uncertainty is the step into the unknown; discovery is finding what fits. In the same video Koe adds a distinction that places phases and traits on two axes: phases "represent vertical development, which is moving up a level or regressing back down," while traits are "horizontal development"; "you kind of need to fill the space of the level vertically and horizontally so that you can move to the next one." That distinction, which comes from ego development theory, is developed in Section 17.5.

#### The four quadrants: mind, body, spirit, vocation

The quadrants of Human 3.0 are Wilber's four quadrants, renamed as domains of life. They represent "the four fundamental perspectives, as Ken Wilber says," which allow a generalized map of reality: "so you have personal interior, personal exterior, collective interior, collective exterior." Koe refers viewers to his July 2025 video on AQAL. **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

| Human 3.0 quadrant | AQAL position | What it contains |
|---|---|---|
| Mind | Personal interior (upper left) | Thoughts, emotions, beliefs, the internal world; how you interpret the world |
| Body | Personal exterior (upper right) | Behavior and appearance, habits and actions; "how the world interprets you" |
| Spirit | Collective interior (lower left) | Your relationship to environment, community, culture, family, friends, colleagues and reality; "the domain of meaning and connection" |
| Vocation | Collective exterior (lower right) | Your relationship to systems, structures and social institutions: education, career, economy; "how you contribute to the world" |

The renaming carries interpretation. "Body" for the upper right is a narrowing of Wilber's quadrant (which includes all individual behavior and physiology) into the domain of health and appearance. "Spirit" for the lower left joins two things: culture and relationships on one side, meaning and connection on the other. This is consistent with the sense Koe gave "spirit" in 2023, "the connection of minds through the intangible" (Section 38.1), but it differs from the use of "spirit" as the top of a vertical chain (matter, body, mind, soul, spirit) in Wilber's graphic. And "vocation" for the lower right makes the social system into the domain of work and money, which is how Human 3.0 meets the fourth flaw: the lower right is where money lives.

#### Cross-quadrant unlocks

The most practical idea in the model concerns how the quadrants interact. Since growth is solving problems (the "life is problem-solving" thesis of Section 38.1), "life problems within a specific domain are often blocked by a lack of development in another." Koe lists six "unlock patterns":

- Body unlocks spirit: physical vitality creates energy for deeper connections and presence.
- Mind unlocks vocation: mental clarity reveals authentic career paths and creative solutions.
- Vocation unlocks body: financial stability enables investment in health and training.
- Spirit unlocks mind: community support provides the safety to question beliefs.
- Body unlocks mind: exercise and nutrition directly affect cognitive function.
- Mind unlocks spirit: self-awareness enables genuine intimacy and connection. **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

The implication is aimed at the typical viewer: an ambitious person focused only on money and business "can only get so far in business when other areas of your life fall down." **Source:** same.

The pattern is Koe's, and it is the Human 3.0 version of two ideas already seen: the skill tree of altitude, where higher traits are gated by lower ones in other branches, and the leader whose company stalls because of his low emotional intelligence (Section 38.2). The list is not exhaustive (it does not include, for example, spirit unlocking vocation), and Koe presents it as a set of observed patterns, not a complete causal model. It also anticipates the order "body, then mind, then business" that Koe recommends in Chapter 40.

#### The three levels

The levels of Human 3.0 are adapted, as noted in Section 38.2, from Spiral Dynamics and the nine stages of ego development.

**Human 1.0, the conformist.** Level one, "the conformist stage," is the low-consciousness way of thinking. People at this level value established authority and tradition. They are characterized by narrow-mindedness, black-and-white thinking and "believing there's only one right way, which often stems from childhood conditioning." **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

**Human 2.0, the individualist.** Mid-consciousness. The individualist rejects the norm and pursues their own goals; they desire to acquire status and to be perceived as valuable. "They are less narrow-minded, but now believe that their way is the one right way." Koe estimates that "80 to 90% of the entire population falls within those two stages." **Source:** same. (The figure is another of his estimates; in other videos he speaks of "95%" living robotically. These numbers are not reconciled.)

**Human 3.0, the synthesist.** High consciousness. The synthesist is able to adopt multiple perspectives, connect various patterns of reality and strategize new paths. "They understand that all perspectives hold truths that can be synthesized for more holistic and mutually beneficial results." They can display what may look like level-one traits, such as narrow-mindedness, "but it is an intentional choice to tune out noise." Reaching level three "just makes life that much better." **Source:** same.

The last trait of the synthesist is worth dwelling on, because it shows why a level cannot be read from behavior alone. A person who refuses to read the news, ignores most opinions and follows a single path with total conviction could be a conformist (who cannot see alternatives) or a synthesist (who has seen them and chosen to filter them out). The behavior is the same; the level behind it differs. Koe ties this to Wilber's pre/trans fallacy (Section 17.1): the confusion of the pre-rational with the trans-rational, because both look non-rational from the rational level in between.

| Level | Name | Consciousness | Defining traits | Belief about the "right way" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | Conformist | Low | Authority and tradition; narrow-minded; black-and-white thinking; childhood conditioning | There is one right way (the inherited one) |
| 2.0 | Individualist | Mid | Rejects the norm; pursues own goals and status; wants to be seen as valuable | There is one right way (mine) |
| 3.0 | Synthesist | High | Multiple perspectives; connects patterns; strategizes new paths; intentional narrowness to tune out noise | All perspectives hold truths that can be synthesized |

The correspondence with the earlier maps is visible but not exact. "Conformist" moves from the name of the first conventional stage in Cook-Greuter's model to the name of the whole of level 1.0, which absorbs premodern traits (authority and tradition) from the macro stages. The individualist of 2.0 gathers the achiever and the modern stage. The synthesist of 3.0 corresponds to the second tier. Human 3.0 is thus a coarser map than the nine stages: it sacrifices resolution for usability, and it multiplies the map by four by applying it to each quadrant. The two can be read as different zoom levels on the same territory: the nine stages as the fine map, Human 3.0 as the practical one.

#### Levels are per quadrant

The multiplication by four is the model's main departure from the stage models Koe started with. Leveling up happens per quadrant: "you're not just leveling up through all of the above unless you're trying to do that. Most people are leveling up a little bit in one." "Someone can easily be a level two or level three in body, but have a level one mind": "the typical meathead," a person who loves fitness "but is just dumb as a rock." Or someone can be very smart but spiritually underdeveloped: "maybe you get the Hitler scenario where they're very smart people, they just have zero morals." **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

The level descriptions take a different shape in each quadrant. In vocation (lower right), a 3.0 synthesist leverages AI to pursue their life's work, while a 1.0 conformist believes AI is purely evil, out of a lack of knowledge, skill and experience. In body (upper right), low consciousness means no understanding of nutrition, leading to obesity and sloth, while high consciousness understands how nutrients interact and tweaks diet to serve goals. In spirit, Koe's example of level 1 is again the "Bible thumper." **Source:** same.

This is the Human 3.0 version of "lines" in the 2026 framework (Section 38.2): development is uneven across domains, and a single label for a whole person hides more than it shows. The archetype (your level in one quadrant) and the metatype (the combination) exist to make the unevenness visible.

#### NPC, main character, programmer

Koe gives the levels a metaphor drawn from video games. Levels "represent your complexity of self in any given domain. The entire Human 3.0 graph is you." The more complex you are, by cultivating perspective and expanding consciousness, the more interesting life becomes, because you can choose the challenges you take on. "Level one is similar to an NPC or non-player character running on a script. Level two is the main character choosing their storyline, and level three is the programmer who can create new games that others also enjoy playing." **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

The metaphor condenses much of the book. The NPC is the figure of Part I: the person running a script written by parents, school and society (Section 1.4). The main character is the person who chooses goals and a storyline, the subject of Part IV. The programmer is the one who creates games that others want to play, which is, in Koe's terms, the value creator who builds products, content and businesses (Parts VIII to XI). "Complexity of self" is a term Koe adapts from Csikszentmihalyi (Chapter 10): the self grows more complex by taking on challenges and integrating what it learns. The structure mirrors the computer metaphor for the construct-aware stage (one app, the full desktop, the programmer; Section 38.3).

#### Higher is not better

Koe attaches a warning to the levels. "Lower or higher levels aren't bad or good. They are points of development along someone's journey." Everyone starts at level zero, "pure survival instinct," then grows and learns. Born into a good family, you have a head start; born into a bad family, you have "a lot of conditioning to undo yourself." "A person who is high consciousness is not somehow better, they're just more developed, and that development obviously has its perks." **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

"Level zero" is Koe's name for the starting point; it corresponds to the instinctual level 0 of his 2026 scale and to the "survival sponge" of December 2025. The remark about families matters for how the model should be applied to others: a person's level reflects, in part, a starting position they did not choose. The warning is followed immediately, in the same video, by the toolbox formulation of transcend and include (Section 38.1): you never leave a level, and the lower ones remain available.

#### Traits: knowledge, experience, skill

How does a person move from one level to the next? "The traits, knowledge, skill, and experience must be acquired before the next level reveals itself to you." The phrasing is precise: the next level is not chosen; it "reveals itself" once enough has been acquired, as an area of the map that becomes visible. **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

The common failure is imbalance: knowledge without experience, or experience without knowledge. Koe calls it "fat personal trainer syndrome": someone very knowledgeable about fitness who has not practiced or developed the skill or experience to reflect it in their own physique. **Source:** same.

The traits map onto the phases. In uncertainty, when you step into the unknown, "you need to learn": knowledge is what helps with uncertainty, because what you lack is clarity. In discovery, when you are clearer, "you need to practice. You need to build something of your own toward a goal you're trying to achieve." **Source:** same. The mapping is the Human 3.0 version of "build to learn" (Chapter 14): learning is necessary to step into the unknown, but it becomes development only when it is turned into practice and building.

#### Channels: accelerating through a level

Koe introduces a term of his own for periods of accelerated development. Channels, drawn as curly brackets on the graph, are "how you accelerate through your development." "You can think of a channel as an exciting quest in a video game, a rabbit hole of knowledge or skill": the times when you cannot stop researching a topic or working on a project and time passes surprisingly fast, like a flow state. **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

He gives examples for each quadrant, distinguishing channels of knowledge and of skill:

| Quadrant | Channel of knowledge | Channel of skill (or experience) |
|---|---|---|
| Mind | Following a line of thought that keeps you up at night and produces a plethora of ideas | Becoming immersed in a deep meditative state |
| Body | Finding a new diet, supplement or methodology and binge-watching all the content about it | Becoming obsessed with running or lifting for three months and making more gains than ever |
| Spirit | Finding a philosophy that clicks with your current phase | Mystical experiences, honeymoon phases, intimate moments |
| Vocation | Finding the perfect opportunity and being unable to stop learning the skills to start a business | Reaching maximum clarity that quickly leads to an exciting career change, product launch or creative stint |

(For spirit, Koe lists the examples without assigning them to knowledge or skill; the split in the table is an editorial arrangement.) You can tell someone is in a channel by how excited they are when they talk about it, like someone writing a book "where the words can't stop flowing." **Source:** same.

A channel is close to flow (Chapter 10), but the two are not the same concept. Flow is a state, a matter of hours. A channel is a period, weeks or months, during which a person repeatedly enters flow around one subject and advances rapidly through a level. In the vocabulary of Section 38.5, a channel is a sequence of high states that raises a baseline.

Koe gives a process for entering one:

1. Reach the dissonance phase after fully acclimating to your current level.
2. Once you are tired of where you are, and if you do not become numb to your problems, "use your distaste as fuel to push into the unknown."
3. Create an aim, vision or goal within a specific quadrant, such as body.
4. Begin acquiring knowledge and skill: learn and do.
5. Make mistakes and refine your aim.
6. Experiment enough until you find the channel you get sucked into. **Source:** same.

Then, Koe says, vision plus skill and knowledge provide the clarity "that allows them to shoot forward in progress." **Source:** same. Each step depends on the one before it, and the logic of the sequence is the logic of the book. The first two steps are the anti-vision mechanism (Chapter 7): dissatisfaction, if it is not numbed, becomes fuel. The third is goal-setting within a domain (Chapter 8). The fourth and fifth are build to learn and iteration (Chapter 14). The sixth acknowledges that channels cannot be summoned directly: they are found by experimenting until one "sucks you in." The process leads to a channel; it does not guarantee one.

#### Glitches, and why they are dangerous at lower levels

Koe introduces a second term of his own: "One aspect of Human 3.0 that very few models talk about, and I call them glitches." The name plays on "a glitch in the matrix": "if the matrix were the boundaries of level one and two until you create your own in level three," a glitch is a tactic for forcing yourself into a channel or breaking through a plateau, often by means of technology. **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

His examples: psychedelics can force a mystical experience; performance-enhancing drugs accelerate fitness progress (he names Lance Armstrong, The Rock and "Thor"); moving into an apartment you cannot afford creates a real deadline that forces you to learn and do until your side business works, "tactical stress, a tool to kick you into an intense season of progress." And: "AI is the most recent and widely available glitch that crosses into all domains, and it can be used to self-develop or self-destruct rapidly." **Source:** same. "Tactical stress" is a practice Koe has recommended before (Section 10.6); here it is classified as one glitch among others.

The passage that follows is a warning, and it is the most important part of the idea. "This is why taste and discernment is required. AI is only pure good or pure evil from a limited level one perspective." Even if glitches accelerate progress, "they increase in risk at lower levels of consciousness." Psychedelics taken without prior experience that would let you interpret the altered state: "you may go insane." PEDs taken from an uneducated state without risk mitigation: "you're going to die early of heart failure." And "AI psychosis and outsourcing your mind to AI is becoming more prevalent, not because AI is bad, but because it's like taking steroids": if you have not built a base, studied every area of nutrition, understood the interactions and trained for five-plus years (metaphorically speaking), AI will have nasty side effects. **Source:** same.

Koe concedes that many high-reward mechanisms come at a cost no matter how careful you are, and that for certain people with certain goals the risk can be consciously taken. "For most people, especially in level one, they're death sentences." His rule: "Max out your natural potential first, so you have ample experience and don't get one-shotted." **Source:** same.

The argument has a precise structure. A glitch is a shortcut through the phases of a level. But the phases exist for a reason: they build the knowledge, experience and skill that let a person handle what the next level brings. Skipping them means arriving with the power of a higher state (an altered consciousness, an enhanced body, an AI that thinks for you) without the capacity to integrate it. The risk is inversely proportional to the base already built. This is the same principle as the difference between a state and a stage (Section 38.5): a glitch produces a state; only development produces a stage.

The warning also qualifies one of Koe's recurring tensions. In many videos he recommends being extreme, ripping the band-aid off, burning the boats, and in others he recommends gradual change (Chapter 4). The glitch passage gives one criterion for choosing: the more developed the person, the more extremity they can absorb; for someone at level one, the extreme path can be destructive. It does not resolve the tension in every case, and Koe does not present it as doing so.

#### False transformation

The last dynamic is a failure mode, and Koe gives it his own name. "A false transformation is when someone puts on the mask showing that they are at a higher level of development when they really aren't, and by doing so, they prolong their development and growth even more." Acting higher than they are, "they don't actually solve the problems that are keeping them lower, and then those things start to collect dust and actually pull them down." **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md.

His examples cover several quadrants:

- **Aesthetic without function** (body): perfect gym selfies, but unable to perform basic athletic movements.
- **Spiritual materialism** (spirit): collecting practices, teachers and experiences as status symbols.
- **Title inflation** (vocation): startup people proudly calling themselves CEO of a company that has not made any money. (The transcript has "tool inflation," but the example makes "title inflation" the likely intended term.)
- **Tool obsession** (mind and vocation): believing that the latest productivity app or AI will solve fundamental work-ethic problems; "that one hurts," Koe says.

He adds that some people with fat personal trainer syndrome come to believe that "being fat is being healthy," and he connects the idea to the longevity trend, treated in Chapter 40. **Source:** same.

False transformation is the inverse of the trait requirement. The real path is to acquire knowledge, experience and skill until the next level reveals itself; the false path is to display the signs of the next level without acquiring what produces them. The signs then become a substitute for development, and the unsolved problems compound.

**Complementary context:** "spiritual materialism" is a term coined by the Tibetan Buddhist teacher Chögyam Trungpa in *Cutting Through Spiritual Materialism* (1973), for the ego's tendency to use spiritual practice to reinforce itself. Koe uses it without attribution.

The concept sits in apparent tension with an idea Koe defends elsewhere: the alter ego and the practice of acting in alignment with the person you want to become (Section 4.5), which he insists "isn't a mask... it's a vessel for identity change." Koe does not contrast the two, so the line between them must be inferred. A plausible criterion is the following: the alter ego adopts the qualities of a future self in order to act and solve the problems that stand in the way, whereas false transformation adopts the appearance of a higher level instead of solving those problems. The first uses the mask to do the work; the second uses it to avoid the work. This criterion is an inference, not Koe's statement.

#### Applying the levels: the three levels of social media content

In the September 2025 video in which he announces Human 3.0, Koe applies a level scheme to the field he knows best. To understand what to do on social media, "you need to understand the three levels of social media content." Creators can see which level they are in and try to move toward level three, "the highest ideal of becoming a creator or personal brand or just someone... trying to do something meaningful with their life" using media. He describes these levels as "one little small subset" of the levels of development in Human 3.0. He also clarifies his own position: his experience of social media is "overwhelmingly positive"; the video is for people who are tired of it "because they can't extract the value." **Source:** The Death Of Social Media (& The Future Of Content Creation).md.

Before naming the levels, Koe states the principle that makes the content levels a matter of development rather than talent. The most sought-after career among young people is YouTuber, and people imitate others, "but I would argue that there's something deeper there": "behind every shallow pursuit, there is a deep reason, but you have to uncover it." Someone who goes to the gym for vanity, or after a breakup out of low self-confidence, has a shallow pursuit, "but that's okay because it leads into something deeper": many people understand "in the back of their head" the value of being healthy and fit. Social media works the same way: people start because they see something meaningful in it, but then "your lizard brain either gets addicted to the content or you fall for specific level one traps," and you "throw the baby out with the bath water." **Source:** same. The principle is the social-media version of "you go to the gym for vanity and stay for the therapy" (Section 38.3), and it is the developmental reading of a shallow motive: not something to be ashamed of, but an entry point. "Level one traps" are Koe's term for the ways the platform captures someone at the first level: addiction to consumption, or the tactics described next.

**Level 1: trendjackers.** These are the typical influencers, personal brands or creators, "often the loudest." Every post is optimized for "an isolated dopamine hit," with zero memory of yesterday's post. They are masters of hooks, thumbnails and clickbait titles: "content factories." They post "thought McNuggets" ("fast food for the mind... chicken nugget ideas") and "fortune cookie philosophy, which can easily be confused for wisdom," "posturing as level three content." They chase trending topics (cold plunges, the carnivore diet), pranks, stances and rage bait, because views and likes are all they care about. They are highly skilled at the mechanics of attention, but they "strip all meaning, value, and usefulness for the sake of pure virality." "These are the people who can and will get replaced with AI": tools like Higgsfield already generate AI avatars, AI has access to trends, and imitating what is working "isn't really a creative challenge." Imitation is useful, Koe adds, but if it is the only thing you do, you may end up here. **Source:** same.

**Level 2: brilliant nobodies.** These are the "Substackers or YouTubers with 47 subscribers with arguably brilliant writing or videos": incredible ideas, very smart, much to offer, threads on extensive topics with no traction, authors with great books who complain they cannot sell, "and when you look at their marketing and sales, you know exactly why." They hate the algorithm and social media, and think they can do their own thing without learning or playing the game "because their thoughts are better than everyone else's." They use "authenticity" vaguely; they "don't actually care about helping other people," but about their ideas sounding good, for pride and ego. They complain about "idiots going viral." They are "too proud of level one tactics, but... too jaded to even comprehend a level three perspective," and quietly think they are better than everyone, "and if you're better than everyone, then there's no growth potential." Koe's verdict: "You have useful ideas, but you don't really exist." And: "You despise AI when you're the one who could benefit the most from it." (If you are smart but do not want to be a creator, he adds, that is fine.) **Source:** same.

**Level 3: value creators.** Level three creators "use social media as a tool to pursue their life's work. All of their content has a through line, a frame, a mission." They have something meaningful to share, but they understand that "if they don't capture attention, that meaning won't be transmitted." That does not require quick edits and rage bait: books can be absorbing "simply by telling a great story." Posting on social media still requires acquiring a lot of knowledge and skill to do it well. **Source:** same.

| Level | Name | Strength | Failure | Relation to AI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trendjackers | Mastery of attention mechanics (hooks, thumbnails, titles) | Strip meaning for virality; "thought McNuggets," "fortune cookie philosophy" | "Can and will get replaced with AI" |
| 2 | Brilliant nobodies | Useful, often brilliant ideas | Refuse to learn the game; hide pride behind "authenticity"; "you don't really exist" | Despise AI though they would benefit most |
| 3 | Value creators | Meaning plus attention; through-line, frame, mission | — (requires sustained knowledge and skill) | Use media, and implicitly AI, as tools for their life's work |

The correspondence with the developmental levels is suggestive, though Koe does not draw it line by line. The trendjacker resembles the conformist: copying what works, a script optimized by the algorithm. The brilliant nobody resembles the individualist: rejecting the norm, convinced that their way is the only right way, and stuck there precisely because of it. The value creator resembles the synthesist: holding both truths, the truth of attention and the truth of meaning.

That is why Koe's solution sounds paradoxical. "It's counterintuitive, but merging level one with level two is one potential solution to create a meaningful impact on civilization at scale." The chain of influence he invokes: "change starts on the individual level and culture influences mass behavior and media influences culture. Hint: you are the media in today's world." Turning against the very tool that can provide meaningful work "is not wise." **Source:** same. Level three is not a rejection of level one; it transcends and includes it. The value creator takes the attention skills of the trendjacker and the ideas of the brilliant nobody and joins them.

This marks a shift in Koe's position on attention tactics. In 2022 and 2023 he opposed value creators who educate to creators of memes, drama and commentary, called direct response "so shallow, so fast," and contrasted creators with influencers who "attract the masses with looks." By 2025 he requires the value creator to master attention: what is rejected is attention without meaning, not attention itself. The reason he gives is the one stated above: without attention, meaning is not transmitted.

For readers who suspect they are at level one, Koe offers a short list: pursue new interests; accomplish something hard; share your journey toward a goal; read long books; fix your attention span; create an aim for your brand that helps people improve; attempt to create something "meaningful yet comprehensible." **Source:** same. Each item counters a specific feature of level one. New interests and long books counter the shallow, trend-driven diet; something hard and a shared journey replace imitation with a real project; fixing the attention span counters the dopamine loop; an aim that helps people and something "meaningful yet comprehensible" combine the meaning of level two with the accessibility of level one.

And for level three he gives what he calls operating instructions: "Do something great. Document your path. Solve your own problems. Sell the solution. Find a crevice of reality that you deeply care about," and dedicate your life to helping others learn, grow and actualize. "Take your weird interests and make them interesting to other people." "Become a node in this new decentralized education system called the creator economy," offering free or modestly paid (courses, coaching) interest-based education, "the antagonist to the formal education system." **Source:** same. The instructions summarize, in seven sentences, the arc of Parts VII to XI: you are the niche, the "crevice of reality" (Chapter 19), writing about your own problems and selling the solution (Chapters 22 and 33), and the creator economy as a decentralized school (Section 36.2). Placed at the end of a section on developmental levels, they read as a description of what the highest level looks like in practice: a person who has developed enough to create games that others enjoy playing.

### 38.5 States, Stages and Regression

The maps of the previous sections describe where a person is. This last section describes how a person moves, and fails to stay, at a given place. Two ideas do the work. The first is the distinction between states and stages: a temporary condition of consciousness versus a stable baseline of development. The second is regression: the fall, under stress, from one's baseline to a lower stage. Koe uses the first to explain how growth actually happens (slowly, with occasional peaks) and illustrates it with his own income. He uses the second to qualify every claim about "being at" a stage.

#### Everything is a state of consciousness

Koe's most radical statement about states comes in a July 2024 video: "Reality is consciousness that takes different states. There is nothing but states of consciousness. All that is real is state. The denial of states of consciousness is done from a state of consciousness." **Source:** Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md.

The last sentence is the argument, and it has the same form as the argument about hierarchies in Section 38.1: to deny states, you have to be in one, just as to reject hierarchy, you have to rank. The claim is philosophical (it belongs with the mentalism Koe professes when he renames holons "units of mind"), and he presents it as such, not as a finding of neuroscience.

He then makes the concept usable. States of consciousness and states of mind are, "for the most part," synonymous. There are higher and lower states, "neither of which are better than the other, they just are," but higher states may be preferable at times, like getting into flow for deep work. Sleepy, drunk, high, anxious, dreaming, stressed, bored, overwhelmed: all are states on a spectrum of conscious versus unconscious and open versus narrow. **Source:** same. The two axes, conscious/unconscious and open/narrow, are the axes of the Focus Matrix (Section 6.3), now used to classify states in general.

Where do states come from? A March 2026 passage gives Koe's answer, in the context of a seven-day protocol for recovering creativity: "Your state of consciousness is a reflection of your behavior over time." The protocol is "very simple. Many people may scoff at it and think that it's too simple, and then they'll close their minds off to it, which is the opposite of what we're trying to accomplish." "While I say it's simple, it's actually incredibly difficult to do because you've accumulated these habits over time that are keeping you in this state... And so we need to undo that, which can be painful." And later: "people are going to think that they're above it, and that's an enemy of creativity. So no matter how simple you think this is, just... do it." **Source:** If you've lost your creative genius, watch this.md.

The principle connects the momentary and the cumulative. A state feels like something that happens to you (you wake up anxious, you feel flat); Koe's claim is that the states you habitually find yourself in are the sediment of your behavior. To change the state, change the behavior over time. This sits alongside an earlier claim of his, from 2022, that "habits aren't the most important thing" and that focus is the only thing with root control (the thesis of Chapter 6). The two are compatible if focus is the cause and habits are its sediment, but Koe does not reconcile them explicitly, and the weight he gives to habits varies from passage to passage. The remark about thinking oneself "above" a simple protocol also echoes the pre/trans confusion: the sophistication that dismisses a simple practice can be a sign of a higher stage or a defense of a lower one.

#### States versus stages

The distinction proper appears in two November 2022 videos, presented without a source, as Koe's own way of explaining growth. In the second and fuller of them, states are "a temporary state of consciousness that you can break into at any time": taking a psychedelic, reading a quote that eases your worries, feeling extra creative after writing a few words. Stages are "a permanent stage of consciousness, like reaching a new level in a game where you have access to the powers you could only previously receive with a power-up," like a baseline level of happiness. **Source:** The Matrix Is Real (How To Break Free With Your Mind).md.

The video-game image is the mechanism of the explanation. A power-up gives a character, for a few seconds, abilities it does not normally have; reaching a new level gives it those abilities permanently. A state is a power-up; a stage is a level.

Koe draws three consequences. First, stages take development over time and do not eliminate falling into lower states (like being stuck in a rut). Second, a state can outrun the stage: relative to the graphic he shows, a state is like being launched into the unknown, a peak mystical experience you cannot sustain "because you haven't gone through the stages to maintain it." Third, a stage comes from experience and exposure, by gradual steps; the goal is to reach new stages, expanding the mind by exposing yourself to new experiences in every facet of life, leading to a deeper awareness of what is possible. His business example: a business does not go from $0 to $1 million a month, but from $0 to $100 or $1,000, then to $5,000, "an exponential curve." **Source:** same.

In the other video, a week earlier, Koe applies the same distinction to authenticity. States are temporary: "you can be creative for like five minutes," or authentic for five minutes, but when everything goes unconscious you return to the stage or level you are at. If you slowly develop yourself to a higher baseline, a "stage permanence" of authenticity, creativity, productivity or calmness, then you can branch into higher states and slowly move the baseline up. And moving baseline authenticity up happens "by deconstructing and reprogramming your brain." **Source:** Why You Can't Be Yourself (The Power Of Being Alone).md. This passage belongs with the critique of "be yourself" in Section 3.5: you cannot simply decide to be authentic, because authenticity in a given moment is a state; to make it your default, you have to change the stage, which means undoing conditioning.

In July 2024 the distinction returns with an attribution. "Ken Wilber often speaks of the difference between states and stages": states are fluid, temporary modes of mind you oscillate between every day (tired at night, focused in the morning); "stages or levels represent your baseline of development": you can still reach lower states, but you come back to baseline quickly and hang around your stage. In the same passage Koe mentions another map: "David R. Hawkins developed a map for levels of consciousness or stages of consciousness." He uses the distinction to pose the questions of that video: what causes chaotic states, are we doomed to suffer them, and how do we raise our baseline level of consciousness to achieve more flow? **Source:** Life Is A Video Game (Here's How You Win).md.

The change between 2022 and 2024 is one of attribution only: the idea is the same, first presented as Koe's own, later credited to Wilber. Koe does not explain the change.

**Complementary context:** in Wilber's integral theory, states (waking, dreaming, deep sleep, and altered or peak states, including meditative and mystical ones) are temporary and can be accessed at any stage, while stages (structures of consciousness) are stable and are acquired through development; Wilber stresses that a person interprets a peak state through the stage they are at. David R. Hawkins (1927–2012), in *Power vs. Force* (1995), proposed a "Map of Consciousness," a logarithmic scale from shame to enlightenment. Koe mentions Hawkins's map only in passing and does not use its levels.

| | States | Stages |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | Temporary; you oscillate between them daily | Stable; the baseline you return to |
| How you reach them | Can "break into" them at any time (a psychedelic, a quote, a few written words, a peak experience) | Development over time, through experience and exposure |
| Video-game image | A power-up | A new level |
| Relation to each other | High states give a "peek" at the next stage | A stage does not prevent falling into lower states |
| Koe's examples | Creative or authentic for five minutes; tired at night, focused in the morning | Baseline happiness, authenticity, creativity, productivity, calmness |

#### How growth actually happens: slow stages, occasional states, and the leap

From the distinction Koe draws a description of growth. Expanding the mind happens in "slow and consistent stages with occasional states that bust into higher and higher levels," giving you a peek into that level, so you have something to work toward. **Source:** The Matrix Is Real (How To Break Free With Your Mind).md. The state is not wasted when it fades: it shows the next stage and becomes a target.

But Koe immediately makes room for discontinuity. There are times when intense growth makes sense, and they are usually a product of consistent growth: if you are not acting consistently, you never give yourself a chance at intensity. When you feel the pull to move across the country, drop everything, sell your stuff and live a new life, and you have been thinking about it "for a [expletive] year" without doing it, "maybe it's time, take the leap," because a stressful environment, pressure and something on the line (like moving to another country and fighting for survival) may be what you need to reach a new stage or baseline. **Source:** same.

The condition matters: the leap is justified by the consistency that preceded it and by the length of time the pull has persisted. This is one of the clearest places where Koe reconciles his two registers of change, gradual and extreme. Across his work he alternates between them without explicit correction: small, calculated taps on the identity (Chapter 4) and "rip the band-aid off," "you need to be extreme." Here the two are sequenced: consistency first, which builds the base; then, when the pull has lasted and the base exists, intensity, which can lift the baseline. The glitch warning of Section 38.4 adds the other half of the criterion: intensity without a base is dangerous.

#### A case: income as states and baselines

Koe illustrates states and stages with his own revenue, in what he calls a "humble flex." He made his first $300 selling web design. He got better, stayed consistent, and learned things through experience that "couldn't be taught in a course." Over time he hit a high month of $10,000, "but my baseline was still like two thousand dollars a month." He kept going: his first $50,000 month came with a baseline of $20,000. Recently (in November 2022) he had two $100,000 months in a row; his new baseline was unknown, maybe $50,000, possibly jumped exponentially. When he has a state like his first million in half a year, he says, his baseline will increase again. He hopes to balance around a million dollars a year: "probably too much, I don't know, we'll see." His conclusion: "You don't know until you accomplish it." **Source:** The Matrix Is Real (How To Break Free With Your Mind).md.

| Peak month (state) | Baseline (stage) at the time |
|---|---|
| $300 (first sale) | — |
| $10,000 | About $2,000 |
| $50,000 | $20,000 |
| Two consecutive months of $100,000 | Unknown; maybe $50,000 |

Two years later he returns to the pattern with a much larger peak. When he "shot up" to his first $700,000 month after a product launch, there was no way to sustain it: his previous high was maybe $200,000 to $300,000, and his baseline maybe $100,000. Trying to sustain $700,000 a month instead of going through the cycles needed to reach new baselines "is just not going to work out," and he cannot launch a product every month ("maybe I could, maybe there's a way"). He prefers "the cyclical nature." **Source:** It's actually pretty easy to focus 12 hours a day (if you do this).md.

The case translates the state/stage distinction into a business rule: do not plan around the peak. A launch month is a state; it shows what the next stage might look like, but the business has to grow into it through cycles. The idea links to Koe's "higher lows, not higher highs" and the cyclical structure of progress treated in Chapter 39.

The numbers deserve the caution that applies to all of Koe's income figures. They are revenue, not profit; they were given at different moments for different purposes; and other tellings in the corpus (for instance a 2024 description of $100,000 to $250,000 sustained with peaks of $500,000 to $750,000) do not line up exactly with these. They illustrate the pattern; they are not a financial record.

#### Regression under stress

If a stage is a baseline, how far below it can a person fall? Koe's answer changes over time, and the versions are best read in order.

In November 2024, in the ego-development video, he states the basic claim: "Stress alone can cause you to regress into the past version of yourself or into a lower stage of development." When you feel spiritual and open but enter a very stressful situation, you can regress "to a more dogmatic and singular perspective point of view, very reactive and negative and argumentative." Then he adds a qualification about the floor. He says "the higher you develop yourself, the lower you regress under a stress situation," but immediately clarifies the opposite meaning: "if you develop yourself very high over the years, then when you do get stressed you're still regressing to a rather well-rounded stage." **Source:** How To Become More Intelligent Than 99% Of People.md. The intended point, despite the slip of the tongue, is that the floor of regression rises with development. (In ego development theory this direction of movement is called "vertical down," as opposed to horizontal growth and vertical growth up; see Section 17.5.)

In March 2025 the picture becomes starker. When you are deep in a project you are stressed, and "when you're stressed your mind narrows further; you're kind of temporarily regressed into a lower state of yourself." Even at the highest stage ("construct-aware, unitive, master mystic"), "when your head is stuck in a bucket of water and held there, you're going into survival mode and you're going to kick and flail like an old caveman that's pinned by a beast." That, Koe says, is what being in the middle of a project is like, and it is why the premortem must be done beforehand, while the mind is still wide (Section 17.6). **Source:** How To Take Back Control Of Your Life With Strategic Thinking.md.

In October 2025, with the knife at the throat, the regression goes all the way down: "you're going to turn into an animal for a second," to level zero or level one, fight, flight or freeze (Section 38.1). In December 2025, under stress "you regress to a more conformist or survival stage because you feel threatened." **Source:** A Full Guide To Reinvent Your Entire Life (In 6-12 Months).md; How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md. And in February 2026, in the framework of levels of thinking: "You can be at level four, but in a particularly stressful situation, you can regress back down into lower levels... to level one and be very conformist in one domain of your life when you're stressed and you're going through it, or you're in an argument with someone and you're really not thinking." **Source:** How To Think Like A Strategic Genius (5-Dimensional Thinking).md.

The change is from a high floor (2024) to near-total regression under extreme threat (2025). Koe does not explain it, but the versions can be reconciled by distinguishing the intensity of the stressor. Everyday stress (an argument, a hard week, a project going wrong) brings a developed person down to a floor that is still relatively high. A threat to life brings anyone down to survival. Between the two lies the project premortem: the stress of execution is high enough to narrow the mind significantly, which is why the thinking has to be done before.

Regression also connects to two earlier points. In marketing, regression explains why people who wanted meaning can suddenly be moved by survival appeals after a financial loss, and why they become "more susceptible to exploitation" (Section 38.2). And in the debate over stages and tools (Section 38.3), regression is exactly what Devon Eriksen reinterprets: what Koe calls regression, Eriksen calls pulling the earliest tool out of the box because the situation calls for it. The two descriptions apply most naturally to different cases. Eriksen's fits the man in the horse trough, where survival behavior is the appropriate response. Koe's fits the person who becomes dogmatic and argumentative in a disagreement, where the earlier stage is not a useful tool but a loss of capacity. In the first case the lower stage serves; in the second it replaces.

The practical upshot, across all the versions, is humility about one's own stage. Koe's December 2025 remark captures it: he has been construct-aware "a few times," but does not know if he is fully there, because stages are like tools and lenses, and under stress everyone falls back. People mostly operate "80% of their life within a specific level," which slowly increases, with some domains branching into other levels. He assumes most of his viewers are between levels 4 and 8 of the nine stages: those near 8 watch as a refresher; those near 4 may not know what they want and feel meant for more, "and that's a good thing, because there is a pattern." **Source:** How to fix your entire life in 1 day.md.

The "80%" is another of Koe's approximations, but the idea it conveys is the practical conclusion of the whole chapter. A stage is a center of gravity, not an address. You spend most of your time around it, you visit higher states from time to time, you fall below it under stress, and it moves slowly upward if you keep developing. The maps of Wilber, Cook-Greuter, Spiral Dynamics and Koe himself are ways of seeing that movement. The next chapter turns from where you are on the map to how it feels to move through it: the happiness, struggle and cycles that make up the chapters of a life.
