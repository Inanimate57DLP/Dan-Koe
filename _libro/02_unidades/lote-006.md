- **titulo:** The Seven Liberal Arts of the modern world: logic, statistics, rhetoric, research, practical psychology, investment and agency.
- **desarrollo:** Devon Eriksen's list (read aloud by Dan Koe from Devon's post) of what he calls the Seven Liberal Arts of the modern world, each defined by what it lets you do: (1) logic — how to derive truth from known facts; (2) statistics — how to understand the implications of data; (3) rhetoric — how to persuade and spot persuasion tactics; (4) research — how to gather information on an unknown subject; (5) practical psychology — how to discern and understand the true motives of others; (6) investment — how to manage and grow existing assets; (7) agency — how to make decisions about what course to pursue and proactively take action to pursue it. ## U-006-004
- **tipo:** fuente-de-tercero
- **titulo:** Cicero's distinction: what we call education today is mostly training, the kind given to slaves; free citizens were taught how to learn.
- **desarrollo:** Devon Eriksen draws on Cicero ("I would highly recommend giving those bits of his writings a read"; he notes "we don't really have an organized version of what Cicero would have considered education"). In Rome, job training was given to slaves: a slave was "essentially a human machine," property who would spend his whole life doing one task, so you give him job training. "Job training is not education it is preparation to do a specific task." A free man, a Roman citizen with full rights (who could carry weapons, etc.), was expected to need to do many different things in his life because he acted in his own interest; he existed to benefit his society but also to serve himself, "and hopefully the two would align." Because neither teacher nor student could know what he would need to know, "you taught him how to train himself you taught him how to learn." Devon's adaptation: modern Western education is systematically training, because those who determine it ask "what's going to make this person a useful worker for the moneyed interests that I actually serve."
- **ejemplos:** Roman slave vs. Roman citizen.
- **cita:** "what we call education today is mostly actually training and that is the kind of education that in Rome would have been given to Slaves"
- **terminos:** education; training; job training
- **origen:** adaptada-de:Cicero (via Devon Eriksen)

## U-006-013
- **tipo:** framework
- **titulo:** Agency as "belief in the existence of hard problems": the three kinds of problems — easy, impossible and hard.
- **desarrollo:** Devon Eriksen: "I like to characterize agency as belief in the existence of hard problems." Three categories: (1) Easy — things we can do the moment we turn our hand to them with the intelligence, skills, knowledge and resources we already have. (2) Impossible — things you not only can't do with your capabilities and resources but where you don't know how to overcome the distance between you and them, so "there's no meaningful attempt"; no amount of motivational speeches will change it. (3) Hard — "you can't do this right now but you can learn if you keep trying." "The trick is to raise children who believe in a third category of tasks." Low agency, implicitly, is collapsing everything not easy into impossible. Teaching agency starts "on an individual parental level," because "if children have agency then even if there's something you miss they can address it themselves as an adult because they believe they can."
- **ejemplos:** Easy: going to the store to buy a carton of milk. Impossible: eating a bicycle ("your teeth don't work that way"), tunneling to Java through the center of the earth.
- **cita:** "the trick is to raise children who believe in a third category of tasks those are hard"

## U-006-015
- **tipo:** método
- **titulo:** How adults who went through school can rebuild agency: notice your risk aversion and train belief in success like a muscle with stepwise achievable goals.
- **desarrollo:** Devon Eriksen's answer to "what can people do to practice agency": (1) start thinking about your own emotional makeup; (2) try to notice ways you are risk averse — too afraid to do something where there's something you want on the other side; (3) realize that watching YouTube success and motivational videos is not it: "belief in your ability to succeed is like a muscle it has to be trained"; (4) be aware of this and make a plan setting goals that "you can't do right now but they're achievable in steps." The mechanism: each successful step trains the belief "that if you work at something you can't do now you will eventually succeed."

## U-006-018
- **tipo:** argumento
- **titulo:** You cannot know in advance what to learn; to follow a moving target you need a feedback loop and must learn as you move.
- **desarrollo:** Devon Eriksen's answer to whether it's possible to know what to learn: "the further into the future you have to look the harder it becomes to get everything right." He might know what he needs to learn in the next two months, have some idea for the next two years, and have no idea for the next 20 years — "I'm going to find that out as I learn what I need to learn in the next two years." So deciding at age five, at kindergarten, what a child needs to learn will be wrong "because the world is going to change." "In order to follow a moving Target you have to have a feedback loop." The way to structure learning is not "I make the best plan at the outset" — "my plan sucks cuz I don't know anything yet" — but "I need to get moving at learn as I go." What we should cultivate in children and call education is "the tendency to move and the ability to learn as we move." Dan adds: "time in the market beats timing the market."
- **ejemplos:** 2 months / 2 years / 20 years horizons; deciding a 5-year-old's curriculum.
- **cita:** "the tendency to move and the ability to learn as we move"

## U-006-070

- **titulo:** Advice to aspiring authors: the way to find out whether your work has value is to try to sell it yourself — it's not your decision whether it's good enough, it's theirs.
- **desarrollo:** Devon Eriksen speaks directly to aspiring authors: "I know you're afraid I was afraid too everyone is afraid... you stay afraid even as you start having success the fear is constant."
