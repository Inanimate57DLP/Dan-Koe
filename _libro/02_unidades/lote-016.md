## U-016-001
- **tipo:** término-acuñado
- **titulo:** "The delusion of hard work": you can work hard at anything without it being useful for the progress of humanity
- **desarrollo:** Dan opens by naming "the delusion of hard work": the belief that effort by itself entitles you to payment. You can work hard at anything, but that does not mean the work is useful "for the progress of humanity." You can put four years into a degree or ten years into climbing a corporate ladder and still not be paid anywhere close to what you want. Instead of "taking your future into your own hands," the deluded person whines and complains ("I deserve to be paid more," "I've spent 14 years working hard and this is all I get for it," no time for family, no money for vacation, "I slave away with no light at the end of the tunnel"). According to Dan, "the complainers of the world are missing one crucial piece of the puzzle": that pay follows value, not labor (see next units).
- **ejemplos:** Four years of work for a degree; ten years climbing a corporate ladder; the complainer who has worked 14 years and can't afford a vacation or time with family.
- **cita:** "you can work hard at anything but that doesn't mean it's useful for the progress of humanity this is the delusion of hard work"

## U-016-002
- **tipo:** argumento
- **titulo:** The "labor theory of value" (being paid for the amount of work you do) is not how reality works
- **desarrollo:** Dan names "the labor theory of value" as the belief that "you should be paid for the amount of work you do," so as to "feel as if you have jumped through hoops to feel like you deserve something." He rejects it: "that's not how reality works." It "does not make sense to pay someone based on the amount of work they do"; it "does make sense to pay someone based on the level of problems they solve." The reasoning: one year of hard work writing to become an author does not justify "100,000 units of currency to just be given to me." People only care if the writing is beneficial to them; "the amount of writing I did within that year doesn't matter," what matters is "the quality and results gotten from that writing." Dan does not attribute the term to any specific thinker in this transcript; he uses it as the label for the mindset he is criticizing.
- **ejemplos:** One year of writing to become an author does not entitle you to 100,000 units of currency.
- **cita:** "it does not make sense to pay someone based on the amount of work they do it does make sense to pay someone based on the level of problems they solve"
- **terminos:** the labor theory of value
