## U-014-132
- **tipo:** proceso
- **titulo:** How to write an essay: six rapid-fire steps
- **desarrollo:** It's supposed to be difficult, especially for non-writers; you just need paper or an app. (1) Write to discover, not to perform (most social engagement comes from packaging anyway, to learn later). Start with a concept, viewpoint, question, experience, thought, something that bothers you, or a topic; an essay begins with uncertainty and an open mind. (2) Write about what genuinely interests you; focus on a single main idea; use it to research and learn, go down rabbit holes, challenge everyone's point of view, don't accept one source as law. (3) Resist the template: you'll find how you like to structure as you improve — it's a skill — but for now just write; debate yourself; ask questions to keep writing going; then worry about structure and ask AI for help if you want. "Do the thinking first." (4) Ask "Do I actually believe this?" — easy to write what you already believe, but the point is to change what you believe; the most difficult part; resist acting like you're absolutely right. (5) Read essays and consume "syntropic" content: your sense-making capacity is shaped by inputs; the For You page won't feed it to you — actively search, curate, nurture your feed. (6) Build a body of work, not a content calendar: people follow for a body of coherent work; each essay compounds on the last; "AI can't replicate a coherent philosophy built through years of genuine thinking."
