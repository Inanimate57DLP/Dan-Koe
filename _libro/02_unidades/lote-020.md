## U-020-003
- **tipo:** framework
- **titulo:** Three steps that explain why the author retained so much from The Power of Now: intention, genuine curiosity, reducing the execution gap
- **desarrollo:** From self-reflection, the author identifies three steps as the reason for his retention: (1) having an intention — something to aim toward, held consciously or not, that makes the teaching fit your situation; (2) genuine curiosity — wanting to understand rather than closing your mind and picking apart small disagreements; (3) reducing the execution gap — stopping to practice something as soon as you read it, so theory is backed by direct experience. He presents these as a starting point ("a great way to retain a lot of what you learn") but says "we can do better than that," leading into the builder/teacher method and the later six steps. He deliberately puts this actionable advice at the start, noting that he usually drags on intros, and says the deeper material needs to be built up to.
- **ejemplos:** His reading of The Power of Now.

## U-020-013
- **tipo:** metáfora
- **titulo:** The ancestor and the bush: novelty is remembered only when it has something to be applied to
- **desarrollo:** An ancestor walking past the same bush over and over gets conditioned to see it doesn't aid survival — no fruit, no berries, "it was just a bush." When they notice something new, like berries growing, it catches their attention and they are much more likely to remember it next time if they need it for survival. A meaningful project redirects this survival-noticing mechanism toward something productive.
