## U-020-003
- **tipo:** framework
- **titulo:** Three steps that explain why the author retained so much from The Power of Now: intention, genuine curiosity, reducing the execution gap
- **desarrollo:** From self-reflection, the author identifies three steps as the reason for his retention: (1) having an intention — something to aim toward, held consciously or not, that makes the teaching fit your situation; (2) genuine curiosity — wanting to understand rather than closing your mind and picking apart small disagreements; (3) reducing the execution gap — stopping to practice something as soon as you read it, so theory is backed by direct experience. He presents these as a starting point ("a great way to retain a lot of what you learn") but says "we can do better than that," leading into the builder/teacher method and the later six steps. He deliberately puts this actionable advice at the start, noting that he usually drags on intros, and says the deeper material needs to be built up to.
- **ejemplos:** His reading of The Power of Now.
- **cita:** "having an intention genuine curiosity and reducing the execution Gap is a great way to retain a lot of what you learn but we can do better than that"

## U-020-007
- **tipo:** principio
- **titulo:** "The greatest skill one can develop is decreasing the time between idea and execution" — reducing the execution gap
- **desarrollo:** The third step. The author quotes his own tweet, which "popped off randomly." Some disagreed, saying other skills are the greatest; he concedes the point but says "in my situation in life that was the greatest skill." Applied to reading: when he came across something he could practice, he didn't keep reading — he put the book down and practiced. Example: reading outside with coffee, hitting advice about meditation, he stopped and meditated right there. Mechanism: because ideas were aligned and at the top of his mind, and he hadn't forgotten any of the nuances, he could notice whether the practices were working; he didn't "glance over" the outcome. It was easier to solidify knowledge because he "backed the theory with direct experience," which he flags as an important point.
- **ejemplos:** Stopping mid-reading to meditate while drinking coffee outside.
- **cita:** "the greatest skill one can develop is decreasing the time between idea and execution"
