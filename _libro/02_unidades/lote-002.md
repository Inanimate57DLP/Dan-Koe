## U-002-015
- **tipo:** término-acuñado
- **titulo:** Conceptual survival: once you identify with an identity, goal or label, your actions follow suit to make it survive
- **desarrollo:** Dan (writing about this privately for his book): Naval said somewhere "your purpose is your axis of suffering," so you must choose it wisely. Dan frames it as "conceptual survival": because humans conceptualize, "the principles of survival don't only apply to our physical nature but you can like note those patterns in the mental nature ... the mental plane of life." When you identify with an identity, goal or label, "your actions are going to follow suit in order to survive that identity." Applied to the four-hour workday: if he identifies "as a person who works four hours a day with an open mind," his actions follow to only work four hours, and "anything outside of that is going to be registered as a problem," which is the source of suffering — but that alone "makes the problems reveal themselves you can become aware of them," and actions follow "almost automatically" to solve them. The negative case: unconscious goals projected by society ("go to school get a job retire at 65") are adopted as kids without questioning, actions follow, and that is their suffering. The remedy: take that frame, figure out what you want, identify with those goals loosely — "firm beliefs held Loosely" — and it actualizes "as long as you're consistent with it." ## U-002-017
- **tipo:** método
- **titulo:** Identity lists: a pinned phone note where every action toward a desired identity is logged as evidence
- **desarrollo:** Dickie's method for behavior change. Problem: aspirational identity change ("I want to be someone who works four hours a day ... I need to be focused, organized") is hard because "it's not very easy to see tangible progress towards that." Steps: (1) Whenever he wants to start a behavior/change, make a list titled with the identity and pin it as a note on his phone. (2) Define what that identity entails. (3) Every action he takes toward it, write it in the note (e.g., "I plan my day the day before"). (4) Over time this becomes "a big list of evidence" supporting the identity. Mechanism: it "hacked the dopamine" — he takes the action because he knows he gets to write it down; it's "a new source of gamification," reinforcing the belief, and "that flywheel starts to spin more and more." Dan summarizes it: make a goal conscious, change behavior to actualize it, write down each action to reinforce it — Dickie: "you start to do it so you can write it down." Cadence: gather one item per week on each of six or seven identities; a year gives ~50 pieces of evidence. Benefit: "it becomes a source of truth" answering "am I really making progress on this," which otherwise stays vague. After six months he had about a hundred items. Started "this year" (2023).
