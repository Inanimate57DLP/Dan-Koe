## U-005-004
- **tipo:** principio
- **titulo:** Burnout is about loss of control, not about working hard.
- **desarrollo:** Justin explains his burnout: he took his first executive role at 33 as "a stretch hire," expecting to take the company to "a million, maybe two," and instead grew it to about $51 million. "As it got bigger and bigger and bigger, I lost control." His thesis: "burnout is about loss of control. It's not about working hard. I could work hard all day. But losing control is is what starts to really cause burnout." The implication (which reappears in his life philosophy and in Dan's comments on alcohol) is that regaining control, not reducing effort, is the remedy.
- **ejemplos:** PatientPop scaling from his expected $1-2M to ~$51M while he lost control.
- **cita:** "burnout is about loss of control. It's not about working hard."
- **terminos:** loss of control; burnout

## U-005-005
- **tipo:** historia
- **titulo:** Justin's December 2018 panic attack was the event that made him look at his life "through a different lens."
- **desarrollo:** In late 2018 (December), Justin was "really heavy," drinking too much, eating too much, not sleeping enough, and had a panic attack in which he "literally thought I was dying." His wife called 911 and EMTs came to the house. About two weeks later, in January, he told his co-CEOs he would step down, and he stayed another 8 months, during which he got into shape, reduced drinking and slept more. He frames the crisis as necessary: "that was the sort of the thing that needed to happen in order for me to look at my life through a different lens."
- **ejemplos:** Wife calling the EMTs; resignation announced ~2 weeks later; 8-month transition.
- **cita:** "that was the sort of the thing that needed to happen in order for me to look at my life through a different lens"

## U-005-006
- **tipo:** historia
- **titulo:** Dan: even light alcohol use costs a sense of control because brain fog can last up to a week.
- **desarrollo:** Dan relates to the bad-habits part of Justin's story. As a young guy who partied in college, he caught himself "trying to justify my alcohol consumption," which was "not a lot by any means" — maybe once every two weeks on the weekend. His realization: stopping "alone, it does give you more of that sense of control because like the brain fog and other things can last like up to a week, and you're just not making the progress that you could." He links this back to Justin's "control" framing of burnout.
