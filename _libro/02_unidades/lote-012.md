## U-012-001
- **tipo:** principio
- **titulo:** Obsession is the key to greatness: expose yourself to experience, follow curiosity, then make the obsession sustainable through work
- **desarrollo:** The author opens with a three-step prescription. First, expose yourself to more experience (explicitly "not distraction"). Second, let curiosity pull you down a "rabbit hole of discovery". Third, once you find the thing you can't pull yourself away from, work to make it a sustainable part of your life. Obsession is presented as the key to greatness, but it is not chosen in advance; it is discovered through exposure and curiosity, and only afterwards turned into something that sustains you (economically and practically). Later in the same video he restates this as a chain: curiosity leads to learning, learning leads to obsession, obsession leads to "a life that you never thought was possible".
- **ejemplos:** ninguno
- **cita:** "expose yourself to more experience not distraction and let curiosity pull you down a rabbit hole of Discovery"
- **terminos:** obsession, rabbit hole of discovery
- **origen:** propia
- **nivel:** fundamental
- **prerrequisitos:** distinction between experience and distraction
- **fuente:** The Future Of Work Is Play (How To Create A Digital Career).md, 0:00, 2023-12-31
- **tension:** ninguna

## U-012-002
- **tipo:** historia
- **titulo:** The author's childhood habit of observing people's actions to discern where they lead
- **desarrollo:** Since childhood the author was "obsessed with finding a better way of living". Everywhere he went he observed people's actions — what they put in their grocery baskets, their daily routine (go to work, go home, watch Netflix, fall asleep, repeat) — to discern whether doing those things would lead to the life they had, and whether he wanted that life. He asked what, how and why: what were they pursuing, how did they get where they are, why did they look that way. He admits there was some judgment as a teenager ("we all judge people it's okay") but says he wants to shift that from judgment to discernment. The method: with enough data from observation, decide "do I want that or do I not want that".
- **ejemplos:** People who "don't really push into the unknown": work, home, Netflix, sleep, repeat. A person picking up "a 1,000 calorie dessert drink for breakfast with a sandwich that leaked grease from its wrapper".
- **cita:** "I want to shift that from judgment to discernment"
- **terminos:** judgment vs discernment
- **origen:** propia
- **nivel:** complementario
- **prerrequisitos:** ninguno
- **fuente:** The Future Of Work Is Play (How To Create A Digital Career).md, 0:00–1:43, 2023-12-31

## U-012-004

- **titulo:** The author conditioned himself to see the 9-to-5 as the worst outcome because accepting it seemed to doom people to comfort
- **desarrollo:** Through questioning and observation he "conditioned myself to believe that getting a 9 to-5 job was the bane of my existence", "honestly the worst outcome". The reason: once people accept a job offer (exiting college, entering or in their late 20s) they seemed "doomed to a life of comfort" — "obviously not all of the time but 95% plus of the time". He frames this as a self-conditioned belief, i.e. partly a personal stance, and says the danger of employment goes beyond this.
- **ejemplos:** mortgage, spouse and kids taken on before financial capability
- **cita:** "they were quick to become an adult but were never able to reap the rewards of being one"
