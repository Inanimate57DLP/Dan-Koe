## U-017-001
- **tipo:** historia
- **titulo:** Misreading Tim Ferriss's "The 4-Hour Workweek" as "the four hour work day" planted an idea that shaped Dan Koe's life decisions.
- **desarrollo:** While in college trying to become a fitness YouTuber, the author came across the popular book title "The 4-Hour Workweek" by Tim Ferriss. He states he never read the book, but when he first saw the title he interpreted it as "the four hour work day." Since then, the idea of a four-hour workday stuck "in the back of my head" through college, business failures and everything up to the present. Along the way his subconscious mind was "munching on creative problems" because he perceived anything more than four hours of work a day as a problem. He claims that this alone changed the direction of his life and all the decisions he made. The point: a held idea, even one born from a misreading, acts as a standing problem-definition that the subconscious keeps working to solve.
- **ejemplos:** The misreading of a book title he never read.
- **cita:** "I perceived anything more than four hours of work a day as a problem and this alone changed the direction of my life"
- **terminos:** four hour work day
- **origen:** adaptada-de:Tim Ferriss (only the book title; the author explicitly never read the book)
- **nivel:** complementario
- **prerrequisitos:** ninguno
- **fuente:** The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md, 0:00, 2023-01-01
- **tension:** ninguna

## U-017-002
- **tipo:** principio
- **titulo:** Treat any work beyond four hours a day as a "problem" to be solved, not as a catastrophe.
- **desarrollo:** The author clarifies that "problem" does not mean "the end of the world." It means he knew he could achieve some form of success with four hours or less of work per day, so whenever his work extended beyond four hours his mind "naturally wanted to come up with solutions." The four-hour limit functions as a reference standard: crossing it is a signal to look for systemization, elimination, delegation or productization rather than simply accepting longer hours.
- **ejemplos:** Currently working around six hours a day; it does not affect him negatively, but he still sees it as a problem he will systemize at some point.
- **cita:** "I don't mean like it was the end of the world I just mean that I knew that I was able to achieve some form of success with only four hours or less worth of work per day"
- **terminos:** four hour work day, problem
- **origen:** propia
- **nivel:** fundamental
- **prerrequisitos:** U-017-001

## U-017-003
- **tipo:** método
- **titulo:** Start with a service-based business where you control outreach, then productize and systemize once the audience is large enough.
- **desarrollo:** When starting out (freelancing, starting an agency, learning a skill) it is "very wise to start a service based business first": something where you are in direct control of the outreach, not reliant on an audience or paid ads to drive traffic to a product. Sequence: (1) have a skill and create a valuable offer; (2) iterate on it over time until you can charge more and more; (3) reach out to people and land clients; (4) maybe build an audience along the way, which then fuels client acquisition; (5) once the audience is large enough, productize and systemize so you can still maintain a four-hour workday. The step 5 is what keeps the four-hour constraint alive.
- **ejemplos:** His own path: took on fewer clients, productized his service, and sold that product to the audience he had built, which required much less work.
- **cita:** "it gets to a point where you have an audience that's large enough to productize right and systemize so that you can still maintain a four-hour work workday"
- **terminos:** service based business, productize, systemize
- **origen:** propia
- **nivel:** intermedio
- **prerrequisitos:** U-017-002
- **fuente:** The 4-Hour Workday (Focused Work Changed My Life).md, 0:51–1:39, 2023-01-01

## U-017-007
- **tipo:** metáfora
- **titulo:** Business growth works like bulking and cutting: add work to reach a new high, then systemize back down to four hours at a higher baseline.
- **desarrollo:** Example chain: if you're making $10,000/month and hit a new high of $20,000/month because you saw an opportunity and added more work (say six hours a day), you then have to systemize and "come back down a little bit." So you keep moving your baseline up, so your four hours of work "are going a longer way" and making more money (or whatever the end goal is). Analogy: it's like bulking and cutting. When you bulk you eat in a caloric surplus and are in a state of growth — here business growth — and you "tack on a bit of fat in your business," the things that bog you down and make you work more. When the cutting season comes you cut back to, say, "15 body fat in business" and can sit comfortably while working four hours a day. You don't get stuck at one part of the journey; you evolve.
- **ejemplos:** $10k → $20k/month with 6 hours/day, then systemizing back; caloric surplus and cutting to 15% body fat.
- **cita:** "it's like bulking and cutting when you bulk and you're eating in a caloric Surplus ... you're going to tack on a bit of fat in your business"
- **terminos:** baseline, bulking and cutting, fat in your business, cutting season
- **origen:** propia
- **nivel:** intermedio
- **prerrequisitos:** U-017-002, U-017-005

## U-017-010
- **tipo:** concepto
- **titulo:** Depth of focus: the task positive network (external, productive) versus the default mode network (internal, creative).
- **desarrollo:** The second quality that fascinates him about humans is their "depth of focus." In psychology: the task positive network is active when you are focused on an external string of tasks — good for productivity. The default mode network is active when you take your focus off the external (like work) and bring it to the internal; this opens awareness and allows better ideas — or negative ideas — to fill your brain; good for creativity. The balance is between narrow external focus and more internal open focus.
