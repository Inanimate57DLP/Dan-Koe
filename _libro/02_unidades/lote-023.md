## U-023-003
- **tipo:** argumento
- **titulo:** It makes no sense to have complete faith in sensory perception because interpretation is backed by conditioning
- **desarrollo:** The author insists on "radical open-mindedness" and being open and critical with thinking as essential to creative work. The chain: (1) we take what we perceive with any of the five senses (sight, sound, taste, touch...) as truth; (2) but our sensory perceptions and the way we interpret them are backed by our conditioning: how we were raised, the environment we live in, the context or lens we view the world from, and the goals each individual is pursuing, conscious or not; (3) therefore everyone will interpret whatever they see differently; (4) closing your mind off to your one interpretation is what sparks arguments and debates "in the comment section", because of the inability to see beyond what is said, heard or seen. He adds that animals perceive differently than us (dogs hear frequencies we never will; some frogs see differently, "I don't know which ones"), and there may be more colors out there, "who knows". He invites the viewer to practice deep thinking while watching: not taking what you see as law.
- **ejemplos:** Working a job: you are probably unconsciously pursuing the goal of your boss (an example of goals shaping perception). Dogs hearing other frequencies; frogs seeing differently (he admits uncertainty about which frogs).
- **cita:** "our sensory perceptions and how we interpret things are backed by our conditioning"
- **terminos:** radical open-mindedness; conditioning; lens
