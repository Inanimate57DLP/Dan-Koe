## U-022-001
- **tipo:** definición
- **titulo:** Learned helplessness is a learned condition in which a person stops trying to escape a situation even when escape is available.
- **desarrollo:** The author opens by defining learned helplessness as the reason most people never achieve their goals. It is "a psychological condition where a person or animal learns to behave helplessly in a particular situation even when the opportunity to avoid or escape it is available." Mechanism: it occurs after an individual has experienced repeated aversive stimuli (pain or failure) that they cannot control. As a result they come to believe their actions have no effect on the environment and they give up trying to change their circumstances. The author uses it as the root diagnosis for why people "don't try anymore." Later in the video he reframes it: "you aren't helpless, you've just learned to be so" — i.e., the condition is learned and therefore reversible.
- **ejemplos:** "You probably have that friend who believes they just can't do anything" — you look at them and think "can't you see that this is possible if you look at it from a different angle."
- **cita:** "You aren't helpless, you've just learned to be so."
- **terminos:** learned helplessness
- **origen:** de-tercero:psychology (definición estándar citada por el autor, sin atribución nominal)
- **nivel:** fundamental
- **prerrequisitos:** ninguno

## U-022-002
- **tipo:** término-acuñado
- **titulo:** Selective skepticism: the automatic, programmed rejection of opportunities ("sounds like a scam", "I can't do that because", "what if it doesn't work").
- **desarrollo:** The author names "selective skepticism" as "a critical flaw in their self-development." When people are presented with opportunities to start a business, meet new people or change their life as a whole, their "unconscious automatic and programmed response" is: "sounds like a scam", "I can't do that because…", "but what if it doesn't work". Mechanism: their mind cannot see a potential future where they do well; their beliefs trap where their thoughts can go "in this small little bubble"; their attention "can't transcend the problem." Consequence chain: if your decisions create your future, and your mind can't see the good future, this only leads to a mediocre life. It is "selective" because the skepticism is applied to opportunities, not to the default path.
- **ejemplos:** People online presented with opportunities who respond "sounds like a scam."
