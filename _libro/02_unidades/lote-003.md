## U-003-001
- **tipo:** principio
- **titulo:** To get ahead of 99% of people in 6 to 12 months, first become brutally aware of what you don't want and of where you will end up if you keep doing the same things.
- **desarrollo:** The opening prescription for getting ahead "in 6 to 12 months" starts with two acts of awareness: (1) what you don't want, and (2) where you will end up if you keep doing the same things in life. The method for the second is to observe the masses and see where "mindless action" leads ("it's not pretty at all"). The observation must be deep: sit with your thoughts, think them through, and do not close your mind and accept surface appearances as reality. The author argues that "closed-minded surface level living" is precisely what got the person into their current situation, so the cure requires thinking deeper. He is explicit that he is not writing off vision or grand ideas ("those things are good"), and he asks the viewer not to label what he says as good or bad but to "test it through your direct experience."
- **ejemplos:** Observing "the masses" and the results of their mindless action.
- **cita:** "become brutally aware of two things the first is what you don't want the second is where you will end up if you keep doing the same things in life"
- **terminos:** mindless action; closed-minded surface level living; the masses
- **origen:** propia
- **nivel:** fundamental
- **prerrequisitos:** ninguno
- **fuente:** Get Ahead Of 99% Of People With Deep Work & Monk Mode.md [0:05–0:57] (2024-03-21)

## U-003-002
- **tipo:** heurística
- **titulo:** It is easier to know what you don't want (from experience and observation) than to know what you want (from imagination).
- **desarrollo:** The author states an asymmetry of knowledge: knowing what you don't want comes from lived experience and from observation of others, and is therefore easier to access and more reliable than knowing what you want, which comes from imagination, thinking and "grand ideas for your future." This is the epistemic justification for starting with the anti-vision before the vision. He does not dismiss imagination-based vision ("those things are good they are not bad"), but recommends grounding it in its negative counterpart.

## U-003-003
- **tipo:** término-acuñado
- **titulo:** Anti-vision: a written picture of everything you despise and do not want your life to become, used as a source of potent negative energy.
- **desarrollo:** The author is "very big on creating a vision for your future," but says the best way to do that is by also creating an "anti-vision": "a negative thing something that you despise something that will get you angry." When you read your anti-vision you "should feel uncomfortable... very uncomfortable with the possibility of where you could end up in life if you do not take control of your life," and that discomfort is what you channel.

## U-003-006
- **tipo:** método
- **titulo:** How to create the anti-vision and vision: 30 minutes with a notebook as a "mad scientist," starting with the anti-vision.
- **desarrollo:** Steps: (1) Sit down with a notebook for 30 minutes. (2) "Become a mad scientist" and "draw out everything your entire future." (3) Start with the anti-vision: write down every single thing you do not want in this life — what you do not want to look like, what you do not want your future to be, what you do not want your day to look like on an everyday basis. (4) From that, the vision becomes much clearer because "it's just the opposite of those things." (5) Check the emotional reaction: reading or writing the anti-vision should make you feel very uncomfortable. (6) With that discomfort, "laser in on one big goal" — your vision — which should be meaningful. (7) In the same notebook, create a plan and strategy for exactly what you will do day to day and week by week ("every single thing you're going to do for the next week"), getting "extremely specific" to "create the order so you do not be a victim of entropy."
- **ejemplos:** ninguno

## U-003-016
- **tipo:** argumento
- **titulo:** Self-consciousness arises from comparison: attention notices something, compares it with an idea of what should be, and the split attention drains mental energy.
- **desarrollo:** The way to end the suffering of anxiety and boredom is "learning to control your attention which is heavily influenced by the ego or the self." Mechanism: through attention you notice something; you become self-conscious "because you're comparing it to something"; you tend to highlight the differences between yourself and the thing compared; this "split in attention causes you to use precious mental energy" that could be focused on the task at hand. You end up comparing "ideas of what should be rather than accepting things as they are and refocusing your attention." Note that you don't need contact with an actual person to compare — an idea is enough.
- **ejemplos:** Seeing a pimple in the mirror: you don't need to be near someone with clear skin to have the idea and perception of clear skin, then the desire for clear skin that you can't have because of the pimple.
- **cita:** "you start to compare ideas of what should be rather than accepting things as they are and refocusing your attention"
- **terminos:** self-consciousness; ego; split in attention; mental energy
