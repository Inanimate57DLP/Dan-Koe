## Notes on the Corpus

This book is built from 164 of Dan Koe's YouTube videos, published between 2021 and 2026. They are spoken, often improvised, frequently supported by images on screen, and they reached this book through automatic transcripts. That leaves gaps no careful reading can close. The chapters flag each one where it occurs; this note gathers the main ones so the reader knows the limits of the reconstruction.

### Concepts the author relies on but never fully explains

Some of the words that carry the most weight in Koe's system are the ones he defines least.

- **Consciousness and awareness.** Koe uses "consciousness" for at least four things: attention in the moment, self-awareness, a stage of development ("level of consciousness") and the whole of which an individual is a part. He moves between these senses without marking the shift, sometimes within a single video (Chapters 2, 4, 5 and 40).
- **Energy.** Koe calls a system a goal plus a process plus "energy," and says order requires "invested energy." But energy is never defined beyond examples (clean food for the body; "focused prioritized action" for work), and in a 2024 summary the third component of a system becomes "problem" instead (Chapter 5).
- **Entropy.** The concept runs through the whole system. Koe uses the physical law as an analogy for minds, habits and businesses without saying where the analogy stops. The book supplies the physics as complementary context (Chapter 5).
- **Level of mind.** The term itself is defined: the height and openness of one's perspective. The levels are not. Koe never says how many there are or what distinguishes them, and the scale he once showed on screen cannot be recovered (Chapter 4, section 4.7).
- **Conceptual survival.** The law Koe coined changes status without explanation. It is a "law" in 2023, "just a concept, a metaphor" in 2024, and the root of all experience in 2026 (Chapter 3, section 3.4).
- **The known and the unknown.** These recur constantly as names for the comfort zone and for where growth happens, but they are defined only in passing (Chapters 18 and 39).
- **"Meta skill" and "the most important skill."** Writing, code and content, entrepreneurship, learning, making money and agency are each called the meta skill or the most important skill at different times. Koe himself eventually advises reading these claims metaphorically (Chapters 15 and 35).
- **Neuroscience.** Dopamine, the default mode network, the reticular activating system and the bits-per-second limits of conscious attention are used as explanations. They are presented loosely, often without sources, and are best read as images (Chapters 2, 6, 11 and 13).
- **Spiritual vocabulary.** "Karma," "Christ consciousness," "the Universe" and "the mystical" appear as metaphors whose exact sense is left open (Chapters 4, 7, 37 and 40).

### Inconsistent figures and biographical details

Koe tells many stories from memory, many times, and the details move.

- **"The masses."** The percentages 90%, 95%, 99% and "less than 1%" are rhetorical estimates of different things, and they do not reconcile with one another (Chapters 1 and 7). Other statistics, such as "95% of businesses fail in the first year" or "99% of people abandon their resolutions," are given without sources (Chapters 29 and 30).
- **Attention.** Conscious processing is put at 40–50 bits per second against an unconscious 11 million. In one passage this becomes "11 billion" and then "11 million" again (Chapter 6).
- **The cost of a job.** The share of life a job consumes is given as 70%, a third or 25%, because each figure counts something different (Chapter 2).
- **The failed businesses.** The number "seven" never changes, but the businesses that fill it do. The failure period lasts "four or five years," "two to three years" or "almost five years." His father's loan is $3,000 or $2,000. His agency salary is $50,000 to $70,000. His student debt is $2,000, $8,000 or $20,000 (Chapters 2, 4 and 37).
- **Income.** Revenue and earnings are rarely distinguished. The yearly figures do not always fit together (for example, $1.5 million by mid-2023 with a projected $2.5 million, against "around $4 million" for the same year), and predictions such as an "eight-figure failure floor" for his software company sit beside its later description as "the $3 million mistake" (Chapter 37).
- **Hours.** His daily writing ranges from 30 minutes to three hours, and his total working day from under four hours to sixteen, depending on the season and the video (Chapters 12 and 37).
- **Smaller lists.** The Eternal markets are sometimes three and sometimes four (Chapters 19 and 29). Time frames for change range from weeks to years (Chapter 11). Whether Koe calls himself "a writer" depends on the year (Chapters 18 and 37).

The book reports such variations side by side and does not silently choose one.

### Limits of the automatic transcripts

Automatic transcription garbles names, coined terms and numbers. Where context makes the intended word certain, the book corrects it and says so; otherwise it marks the reading as probable or leaves it open. Representative cases:

- **People.** Naval Ravikant appears as "Nal rant," "nval" and "poppy Nal" (Chapters 32, 35 and 36). Balaji Srinivasan appears as "Bala G" (Chapter 25), Ilya Prigogine as "Ilia Priagene" (Chapter 38), Elliott Hulse as "Elliot Holz" and Justin Sung as "Justin Sun" (Chapter 14), and Jocko Willink, as the source of "distribution equals freedom," as "Draco willing" (Chapter 25). Some creators' names remain uncertain (Chapters 12 and 33), and some people are given only a first name ("Paul," "Devon") and cannot be identified with confidence (Chapters 9, 17 and 18).
- **Terms.** Koe's Razor becomes "co razor" (Chapter 3). The Eternal markets become "internal markets" (Chapter 28). Fill, Empty, Use becomes "fill mtus" (Chapter 13). Cortex becomes "CeX" (Chapter 28), and the company's own name alternates between Cortex and Kortex before it becomes Eden (Chapter 37). The noosphere becomes the "new sphere" (Chapters 21 and 34). Norepinephrine becomes "neuropanephrine" (Chapters 10 and 11).
- **Numbers.** "$110,000" almost certainly stands for $10,000 (Chapters 12 and 33), and "$11,000" for $1,000 (Chapter 29). Other figures, such as "$752,000" for an early coaching offer or "$88,000" in a calculation of client income, cannot be recovered (Chapters 7 and 29).
- **Titles.** "Cosmic Consciousness" may refer to Richard Maurice Bucke's book or to a misremembered Wilber title (Chapter 38).

None of these gaps changes the shape of the system. But wherever this book says a structure "cannot be reconstructed" or a reading is "probable," the uncertainty lies in the source, and the book shows it rather than hiding it.
